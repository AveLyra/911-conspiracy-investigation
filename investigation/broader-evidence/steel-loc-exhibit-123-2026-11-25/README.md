## Source observations

The Library's [Witness and Response exhibition item list](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/911/911-object.html), entry **(123)**, describes a fragment of a **vertical structural column** from the World Trade Center Towers. It identifies the material as steel, credits a gift from **Metal Management Northeast, Inc.**, and names the Prints and Photographs Division. These are the institution's descriptions of the exhibition entry, not the actual gift instrument or proof of a particular tower or floor. The parenthesized 123 is the exhibition-list label; its relationship to any collection accession number is unverified.

The separately published [September 2002 Library bulletin](https://www.loc.gov/loc/lcib/0209/debris.html), paragraphs beginning “Highsmith helped connect” and “Approximately 5 feet,” describes a roughly **400-pound** steel section received by that division and estimates its dimensions and upper-story origin. A [2014 Library account](https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2014/02/curious-collections/) describes a 400-pound beam and its off-site storage at that time. Entry 123 is a plausible candidate for the object described in those articles, but the published entries do not provide a common unique accession number; **the join remains unverified**. Their reported similarities are not independent authentication of origin.

The bulletin separately describes a large steel beam carried cross-country for a memorial. Do not conflate that object or its transport with entry 123 without a specific record. Nor does any source here connect entry 123 to W14A, W14B, C12, an authenticated NIST specimen, or WTC 7.
