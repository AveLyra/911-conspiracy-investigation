## Quantities and custody

- **Donation and recovery.** The earlier LMDC record describes a **60-ton piece**. The Army photo caption says **about 24 tons** salvaged and its article says **24 tons used in total**. The reproduced Navy caption separately gives **48,780 pounds** and **approximately 10 percent lost** during heating. The shipbuilder's 2003 release describes **24 tons** in prospective bow-stem wording. Preserve all four descriptions under their original scopes. [LMDC A-1](https://www.renewnyc.com/attachments/content/pdfs/Coordinated_DOE_03-31-04.pdf#page=20) · [Army caption and article, extracted lines 19 and 25](https://www.army.mil/article/28473/commissioning_of_the_uss_new_york_nov_7) · [Navy caption reproduction, Description](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:US_Navy_030910-N-7542D-254_Workers_from_the_Amite_foundry_pour_molten_steel_recycled_from_the_World_Trade_Center,_into_the_mold_of_the_bow_stem_of_the_Amphibious_Transport_Dock_ship_USS_New_York_(LPD_21).jpg) · [Shipbuilder release, first paragraph](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2003/09/10/300413/189/en/Photo-Release-Northrop-Grumman-Melts-and-Casts-World-Trade-Center-Steel-for-Bow-Stem-of-New-Navy-Ship-USS-New-York-LPD-21.html)
- **Pour and set-aside material.** Navy Newsstand release **NNS030912-06**, released **September 12, 2003**, gives **twenty tons** melted and poured and an **additional 21,000 pounds** stored for future anchor-handling castings. NRL gives **more than twenty tons** and repeats **21,000 pounds**. The two accounts share language and the same reported use. The release identifies a specific follow-up component and leaves its manufacture and installation unverified. [Navy Newsstand mirror, extracted lines 72–86](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2003/09/mil-030912-nns03.htm) · [NRL, printed p. 7](https://www.nrl.navy.mil/Portals/38/PDF%20Files/2006_NRL_Review.pdf?ver=fqttsf-FEu12QI5ibNh0FA%3D%3D)
- **Casting and delivered component.** The trade association describes a **seven ton casting**. The Army and the shipbuilder's delivery release give **7.5 tons** for the bow stem. The Navy crest gives **7.5 metric tons**. Different weight stages or units could matter, but the available records supply no agreed basis for a mass balance or a piece-level match. [BIS, physical p. 215](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/effect-imports-steel-national-security-redactions-20180111.pdf#page=215) · [Army article, extracted line 25](https://www.army.mil/article/28473/commissioning_of_the_uss_new_york_nov_7) · [Shipbuilder delivery release, p. 1, “New York (LPD 21)”](https://investor.northropgrumman.com/node/10486/pdf) · [Navy crest, Shield](https://www.surflant.usff.navy.mil/Organization/Operational-Forces/Amphibious-Ships/USS-NEW-YORK-LPD-21/About-Us/Crest/)

The exact foundry, ship number, press release number and photo identifier narrow the search for intake and fabrication records. The material's member and building attribution, including any possible WTC 7 connection, remains unverified.
