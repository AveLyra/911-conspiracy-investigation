This updates the [printed-source comparison](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/4d544e5093bd693b6739fa7c16ecebd42f753801/investigation/broader-evidence/steel-loc-printed-photo-check-2026-11-25/README.md), which found the high-contrast scan and 2014 crop insufficient to establish image identity. ## Sources and actual test

The Library's [2002 article image](https://www.loc.gov/loc/lcib/0209/images/debris_3.jpg), linked as the steel-beam photograph in its [September 2002 article](https://www.loc.gov/loc/lcib/0209/debris.html), was directly retrieved November 25, 2026. The retained JPEG is 241 × 180 pixels, 55,143 bytes; SHA-256 `cff022f704b1ee8665fcc1ac5e5c83262ccb47a232f760ec087150f14fe82b87`. Its article credits the beam photograph to Rachel Evans.

The [January/February 2014 Library of Congress Magazine](https://loc.gov/lcm/pdf/LCM_2014_0102.pdf), physical PDF p. 3 / printed p. 2, was retrieved in the preceding check, SHA-256 `a1027fb166fc4c9b574b3a5a57140168fdf026f7fc03bae621ad093ac8537afa`. The rendered page was visually inspected again. Its cropped beam photograph also credits Rachel Evans.

The new color image and the 2014 magazine crop show the same distinctive arrangement: an opening near the left, a raised bent strip along the upper edge, and an irregular opening near the center of the visible section. Their viewpoint and layout are consistent with a **reused photograph**, subject to cropping and grayscale reproduction. This is a visual comparison, not a pixel-level identity test of the original photographic files. The earlier high-contrast reproduction remains insufficient on its own; the directly acquired article image adds visual detail.

## Interpretation and unresolved records

The repeated photographic view strengthens the association between the depicted images. It is **not an independent 2014 photograph**, a complete custody record, a verified match to exhibition item 123 or catalog group 2002:130, or proof of the piece's original floor or building. The 2014 article separately reports that its 400-pound beam was shown in the Library's 2002 exhibition and stored off-site at the time. Preserve that statement as its own source account, not evidence of current location. Neither image determines the cause of deformation or establishes a relationship to WTC 7, W14A/B or C12.
