# Saudi-assistance and FBI-source-handling review

This is a checked-source first tranche, not acceptance of the documentary assessment. Each S-ID separates the report’s content from an unresolved historical inference. All B/H inventory assertions remain claims to test; these source findings support only their stated narrower propositions.

## S001: A specific hold instruction, not merely a failure to pass a message

The DOJ OIG reconstructs a CIA-system draft CIR carrying Mihdhar visa/travel information, a same-day hold note attributed to the CIA deputy chief, and a later unanswered query about sending/remaking it. It reports that the draft was not formally disseminated to its FBI addressees.

Source: [original record](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/0506/chapter5.htm); Chapter V III.A.4; derived blocks P089–P099; IV.A P439.

Contrary evidence and limits: FBI personnel detailed to the CIA had accessed the visa cables/draft. Therefore “no FBI employee knew” is broader than the reported formal-dissemination failure. Witnesses generally reported no recollection; the hold purpose was not established.

Needed: Original draft CIR with version/access metadata; Original hold annotation and routing/addressee audit; The mid-January email and response search.

Next test: Acquire the exact draft/routing record and distinguish individual access from dissemination to FBI operational addressees.

## S002: Conflicting contemporary handling accounts remain side by side

The OIG describes an internal CIA cable saying the visa documents had been passed to the FBI, approximately three hours after the hold note. It found no witness or additional document corroborating that transfer.

Source: [original record](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/0506/chapter5.htm); III.A.6; blocks P104–P110.

Contrary evidence and limits: An incorrect statement, an undocumented transfer or other process failure are competing possibilities; the report did not determine why the cable said this. CIA/FBI interviews and access logs do not certify receipt by the named operational addressees.

Needed: Original internal cable and distribution list; Claimed delivery/receipt record; The identities/records underlying the transfer assertion.

Next test: Match the original cable statement to a recipient acknowledgment or a complete negative search with scope.

## S003: Later oversight nonproduction is a distinct event

The OIG states that it discovered the CIR title in February 2004 in a list of documents accessed by FBI detailees, after neither it nor the CIA OIG had previously obtained the draft; follow-up work delayed the review.

Source: [original record](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/0506/chapter5.htm); I–II background; blocks P021–P028.

Contrary evidence and limits: The OIG lacked independent access to CIA databases. The published account establishes a late-discovery problem, not the reason the document was absent or a demonstrated connection to the 2000 hold decision.

Needed: Oversight requests and search/production logs; The accessed-document list; Original receipt and subsequent interview records.

Next test: Compare the exact request terms, searches and production chronology rather than treating every omission as destruction.

## S004: FBI-asset tenancy and conflicting disclosures

The OIG reports that Hazmi and Mihdhar boarded with an FBI informational asset in 2000. It records inconsistent accounts about whether the asset told his handler about them, what identities were supplied and what follow-up occurred.

Source: [original record](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/0506/chapter5.htm); III.B.3; blocks P199–P211; notes 130–135 at P653–P658.

Contrary evidence and limits: The asset and retired handler were not interviewed directly by OIG, although the handler had spoken to FBI supervisors and congressional investigators. OIG notes an inconclusive polygraph and the San Diego FBI conclusion that the asset was not complicit. None should be dropped.

Needed: Contemporaneous asset contact reports; Complete original post-attack statements; Congressional interview/testimony originals and OIG interview requests.

Next test: Cross-reference dated contact notes against each later account and identify which assertions are independently documented.

## S005: Documentation criticism differs from a proved policy violation

The OIG criticizes thin annual bona-fides documentation and missing retrievable information, while saying no specific FBI rule required the handler to investigate or record the identities of these boarders.

Source: [original record](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/0506/chapter5.htm); IV.B.3; blocks P511–P527; conclusions P594–P596.

Contrary evidence and limits: The lack of a specific boarder rule and disagreement among other agents are contrary to a blanket “proved rule violation” characterization. The policy review still identifies a concrete oversight weakness.

Needed: Controlling contemporaneous asset policies; Annual source evaluations with supporting checks; Contact-report retention/indexing rules.

Next test: Inspect the actual evaluation and policy texts, separating a missing policy from failure to follow an existing one.

## S006: A documented payment/closure lead without an established purpose

OIG note 133 reports that the initially unpaid asset received $100,000 and was closed as an asset in July 2003.

Source: [original record](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/0506/chapter5.htm); Note 133; derived block P656.

Contrary evidence and limits: The note does not establish what the payment compensated, who approved it or why the asset was closed. A hush-payment or protection theory is a hypothesis, not a result of reading the amount.

Needed: Payment authorization and purpose; Disbursement voucher; Source closeout and approval records.

Next test: Obtain the payment/closeout authorization before assigning a motive.

## S007: Specific assistance and a repayment that changes the financial characterization

The 2016 EC reports translation, travel, lodging and financing assistance and labels coincident transfers anomalous. Separately, OIG reports a same-day reimbursement of the first rent/deposit advance; note 129 gives $1,558 and a nearby $9,900 deposit. The 2025 court also describes repayment.

Source: [original record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-J1_14-PURL-gpo159739/pdf/GOVPUB-J1_14-PURL-gpo159739.pdf); Physical PDF p9 / internal p8; OIG III.B.2 P196 and note 129 P652; Court physical p21.

Contrary evidence and limits: A reimbursed advance is not automatically an unrepaid gift or proof of the original source of the hijackers’ funds. Repayment does not erase logistical facilitation. The EC’s broader anomalous-transfer claim cannot be resolved using one summarized transaction.

Needed: Underlying bank statements/checks/deposit records; EC referenced financial-analysis document; Court Pls Ex550 FBI8057; KSA Ex91 FBI2808–09; Bayoumi deposition 737:3–17.

Next test: Match the actual debits, credits, timing, amounts and beneficial sources across the whole claimed transfer set.

## S008: Restaurant accounts conflict, with limits on what the witness could observe

The EC contrasts Bayoumi’s claimed overhearing of Arabic while leaving with BIN DON’s account of a front-window position and approach at a distance inconsistent with first hearing the speech. BIN DON is reported not to understand Arabic.

Source: [original record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-J1_14-PURL-gpo159739/pdf/GOVPUB-J1_14-PURL-gpo159739.pdf); Physical pp9–10 / internal pp8–9.

Contrary evidence and limits: A witness can report position and movement without knowing the Arabic conversation or an earlier plan. The original statements and their interview dates/references are not reproduced in full. Do not count their later summaries as new witnesses.

Needed: Original Bayoumi 2003 interview; Original BIN DON interview(s); Restaurant layout and contemporaneous corroboration.

Next test: Compare complete statements, opportunity to observe, sequence of questions and any contemporary account.

## S009: Later court record adds competing statements, not a settled harmonization

The court describes Bayoumi and Morgan giving two-minute versus thirty-minute conversation accounts. It also reports Morgan’s declared belief that the encounter was coincidental, and Bayoumi’s denied offer of help beside a quoted MPS account of offering contact/help.

Source: [original record](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/03-md-01570%2011182.pdf); Physical pp17–18; Pls Ex120 402:10–20, 725:14–726:16, 726:23–727:2; Ex112 97:15–98:4; Ex92 paragraph19; Ex450 193:11–16.

Contrary evidence and limits: BIN DON and Morgan must not be silently equated or counted as two independent witnesses without an identity/alias crosswalk. The court’s whole-record jurisdiction inference remains stronger than any one isolated inconsistency.

Needed: Complete declarations and deposition transcripts; Original MPS transcript/recording; Witness identity/alias chain.

Next test: Join the exact interview/deposition editions and witness identities; preserve two/thirty minutes and the coincidence belief even if a larger inference is supported.

## S010: Substantive preliminary judicial findings should not be minimized

The court’s August 28, 2025 decision made an evidence-based jurisdiction assessment supporting an inference of knowing or deliberately indifferent Saudi-linked assistance. It found the encounters more likely than chance and denied the renewed jurisdictional dismissal motion.

Source: [original record](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/03-md-01570%2011182.pdf); Physical pp6–7,40–45; especially40–42 and45.

Contrary evidence and limits: This was not mere acceptance of pleaded allegations. It also was not a final merits judgment that the Saudi government ordered the attacks; the opinion expressly reserves disputed credibility/meaning questions for trial. No present appellate status is claimed here.

Needed: Underlying exhibits and complete adversarial record; Later appellate mandate or current docket record.

Next test: Test the cited exhibits and competing explanations on their actual contents, while tracking the procedural standard separately.

## S011: The same court rejected dramatic video inferences

The court did not accept that the apartment gathering video established an attack-preparation welcome party or that the Washington video established terrorist reconnaissance; its assistance inference rested on other record evidence.

Source: [original record](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/03-md-01570%2011182.pdf); Physical p42, section C.

Contrary evidence and limits: That is an important negative result alongside the jurisdictional findings. This review has not independently examined the complete original videos or adopted the court’s description as a certified audiovisual analysis.

Needed: Pls Ex10K (MPS2023–059) and Ex10K-TR; Pls Ex10F original Washington footage and full metadata.

Next test: Obtain complete original recordings and transcripts before converting selected scenes into intent claims.

## S013: The EC’s telephone paragraph contains its own timing tension

Internal p14 first describes five Bayoumi–Sudairy calls as occurring while the hijackers were with Bayoumi in San Diego, then lists three in January before the described restaurant meeting and a later date printed only as 02/2000. Both descriptions are preserved.

Source: [original record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-J1_14-PURL-gpo159739/pdf/GOVPUB-J1_14-PURL-gpo159739.pdf); Physical p15 / internal p14; compare physical p9 meeting on/about02/01/2000.

Contrary evidence and limits: This could be scope shorthand or an editorial problem, but neither explanation was demonstrated. The narrative does not itself supply the call records or content. Do not silently fill the incomplete date with a guessed day.

Needed: Underlying call-detail records and analysis; Unredacted source reference if lawfully released; Event/location timeline and time-zone conventions.

Next test: Rebuild the dated call chronology from original records, preserving direction, missing entries and uncertainty.
