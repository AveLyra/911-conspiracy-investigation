## What the first source review establishes

The first tranche checks a DOJ Inspector General chapter, the released April4,
2016 FBI communication and the August28,2025 judicial decision. Its 14 S-records
make narrowly attributed statements, preserve contrary evidence and identify
the original records still needed. They are not 14 independent proof chains.
[Source review](SAUDI-FBI-REVIEW-v2.md) · [structured findings](source-findings-v2.json).

The useful leads are concrete: a held intelligence draft and a contradictory
handling account; its later discovery by oversight; conflicting FBI-source
disclosure accounts; assistance and financial transactions; and specific
restaurant, call-timing and exhibit questions. The OIG's reported lack of direct
asset/handler interviews and its note of a July2003 $100,000 payment and closure
warrant original-record follow-up. The payment's purpose is not established.
[DOJ OIG, Chapter V](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/0506/chapter5.htm).

Counterevidence matters: FBI detailees accessed the visa information even though
the draft was not formally sent to its FBI addressees; OIG reports the FBI's
noncomplicity conclusion for the landlord asset; and the rent advance was
reported repaid. The FBI communication's restaurant narrative is conflicting
testimony/analysis, not an acquired record of advance tasking. Its telephone
paragraph also contains a temporal tension and a date printed only as
`02/2000`; neither has been silently repaired.
[FBI EC, physical pp9–10 and15](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-J1_14-PURL-gpo159739/pdf/GOVPUB-J1_14-PURL-gpo159739.pdf).

The 2025 court made substantive preliminary findings supporting jurisdiction
based on knowing or deliberately indifferent assistance; describing its decision
as merely repeating allegations would understate it. It also left merits and
credibility questions for trial and rejected some dramatic video interpretations.
Those procedural limits are not a reason to erase its findings, or a final
judgment that Saudi Arabia ordered the attacks. The original cited exhibits are
not supplied merely by obtaining the opinion. Current appellate status is not
verified in this tranche.
[Decision, pp40–45](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/03-md-01570%2011182.pdf).

The two government-hosted FBI PDFs differ in size/hash. All17 tested page content
streams, image payloads, decoded RGB pixels and page boxes match. That limits
the demonstrated difference in this particular comparison; it does not establish
the files' edit history. GovInfo's abstract says November2013 while its title and
the EC say November2015. Both values remain preserved. Two hosts of the report
are one chain, not independent corroboration.

## Limits, corrections and continuation

Assistance, foreknowledge, deliberate nonintervention, demolition and later
concealment remain separate propositions. Circumstantial evidence can matter;
it needs accurate connections and an account of dependence. These source checks
do not establish a bridge to demolition or a coordinated attack-cover-up theory.
They also do not establish that every official account is correct.

No original bank records, CIA CIR, source interview, deposition, drawing exhibit
or film master was acquired in this tranche. Relevant pages were inspected;
rendering/OCR all pages is not a claim to have substantively read every page.
No native simulation or physical experiment was rerun. Interrogation dependence,
FAA/NORAD/Mineta, presidential warnings and financial/historical assertions remain
in the [ranked record plan](NEXT-RECORDS-v1.md), with explicit pending work.

Our own draft errors are preserved and corrected separately: misassigned
cross-reference rows, an incorrect parent link, a missing optional library,
sandbox acquisition/write failures and a PDF comparator that initially included
runtime object identities and mishandled a content-stream truth test. These are
our analysis/tooling errors, not evidence against an institution.
[Error register](analysis-errors-v4.json).
