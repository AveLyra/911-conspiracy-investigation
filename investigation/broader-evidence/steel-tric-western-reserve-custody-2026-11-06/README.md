# Ohio convoy follow-up: Tri-C's beam and the Western Reserve Fire Museum's earlier steel

November 6, 2026 UTC · STL02–STL03 / AUD011 / AUD016 / B188 / H005

A **2005 Western Reserve Fire Museum newsletter** records that the museum acquired **two pieces** of World Trade Center steel and gave one to Lake View Cemetery's monument, unveiled **September 11, 2004**. A **2006 museum newsletter** describes a piece from its collection displayed at Cleveland City Hall. A separate Tri-C account describes an approval for **three pieces** and a **2010 Ohio convoy**, with one piece reportedly going to the museum. The earlier holdings make the museum name insufficient for identifying a 2010 shipment item. [2005 museum newsletter, p. 6](https://wrfmc.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Bugle-NL200503.pdf#page=6) · [2006 museum newsletter, p. 2](https://wrfmc.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Bugle-NL200609.pdf#page=2) · [Tri-C account](https://www.tri-c.edu/news-and-events/news/world-trade-center-steel-at-the-heart-of-tri-c-memorial.html)

## What the records can test

The two-piece acquisition, single-piece display, reported three-piece approval and later loan describe different actions. The texts provide no defensible total of unique objects across these sources. A stable accession or tag number would distinguish the 2006 exhibit piece and 2019 loan from the reported 2010 donation. The college's dedication account establishes a dated installation description and identifies a commissioning program. The reference to the North Tower's shadow describes the design of the plaza. It provides no building attribution for the installed steel.

The earlier Hilliard report retains its separate claims of **three pieces selected**, **more than six tons**, and **seven tons**. Tri-C's three-piece approval has a different reported applicant. The two three-piece references cannot be joined by quantity. Neither the carrier nor either destination is linked here to a WTC 7 member or a NIST specimen.
