November 5, 2026. B129 / PD07. This follows DOT Document Request No. 2, item 20, which requested FAA/DOT material provided to NTSB or FBI and referred to a separate attached memorandum. The previously recovered October fax contains item 20. The current review found a related FBI authorization and its product list. ## What the authorization and transfer records show

- **AWA-820, July 18, 2003.** FBI's Office of General Counsel authorizes FAA to furnish the Commission with information identified on an attached product list. It states that disclosure is subject to the Justice Department–Commission nondisclosure agreement covering Moussaoui-related materials. It separately describes a Satori simulation available for viewing at an FAA lab. The released copy includes tables titled **NTSB Request List for September 11, 2001**, with columns for **received by AAT-200** and **to NTSB**. Preserve the table title alongside the FBI cover letter. The dates in the table, the date of authorization and any later Commission receipt are distinct. [AWA-820, physical pp. 1–2, 4, 7](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/34913653)
- **PIT-19, September 11, 2001.** The Pittsburgh facility memo reports furnishing FBI copies in four categories. It lists a pictorial of the last known Flight 93 radar position, graphic and numerical computer data reductions, and a list of 32 diverted aircraft. AWA-820 separately lists a Pittsburgh Arrival North tape for **1339–1351**, with **9/20** in the AAT-200 column and **9/21** in the NTSB column. These records name different items and recipients. The Pittsburgh memo is a contemporaneous sender-side record. [PIT-19, physical p. 1](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7603391) · [AWA-820, physical p. 7](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/34913653)
- **N90-160 and AEA-1110.** The former is a September 20, 2001 forward thanking staff for gathering data, under a subject referring to information due to FBI September 19. The latter is an October 4, 2001 New York transcript message that says a copy was sent to a regional official for onward delivery to FBI. The document mentions a TMU/DC attachment by filename. It supplies neither the attachment nor a recipient acknowledgment. [N90-160](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7603364) · [AEA-1110](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7599711)

The TMU/DC intervals are nested on paper, with the longer label beginning 10 minutes earlier and ending 2 minutes later. Different selections or copy generations and a description error are untested possibilities. Label arithmetic does not establish missing audio. Several New York inventory rows explicitly say they were listed again for sorting. Repeated rows and multiple archive filenames cannot establish a count of distinct recordings.

The body of ZNY-756 says the unedited copies were **requested**. The catalog's file description uses **provided**. The body of ZNY-757 describes computer-data tape duplicates, including one CORE tape explicitly described as blank. These are separate from the voice re-recordings. ZNY-755 is an acquired handwritten tape list with difficult extraction. The study made no full transcription or tape-by-tape identification from that list.
