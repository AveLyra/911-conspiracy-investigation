## A sequence issue, without a claimed explanation

A begins with breathing/description/cockpit/Flight12–11 material on3, followed by reservation and seat questions on4; similar topics occur again in its longer run on5–6. B begins with a number-three/cockpit/mace/hijack opening on11, then seat/name material and the longer overlapping sequence on12–14. The early A fragments therefore cannot all be assigned a unique one-to-one position in B from words alone.

The map preserves the early fragments and the later counterparts instead of deduplicating them. It does **not** establish replay, looping, a deliberate edit, two different calls, a master-file omission or what produced the displayed structure. Similar statements may be repeated in an actual conversation; playback/session handling, source coverage and transcription are additional untested possibilities. The first **Flight12** attributed to FA ONG in B11 is distinct in the printed sequence from the later AA/OP Flight12 exchange in B12. Matching that number alone would create an unsupported alignment.

## Agreement and the strongest competing reading

The seat/**3R**, name/number-three/**Flight11**, Boston–LosAngeles, later flight-number correction and medical-contact ending supply substantial ordered parallels. Both later access passages use **into the cockpit**, and both terminal medical-contact lines end at **get**. These agreements constrain a claim that the printed differences demonstrate wholly different calls. They still do not prove identical native recordings, authenticated voices or calibrated clocks.

An ordinary source-generation/transcription account could produce differences of wording, speaker labels and displayed coverage. That account is plausible, **not demonstrated**. Conversely, the paper differences alone do not establish fabricated or deliberately altered audio, attack participation, concealment or motive. Related transcriptions and their handling descriptions cannot be counted as independent authentication of the underlying call.
