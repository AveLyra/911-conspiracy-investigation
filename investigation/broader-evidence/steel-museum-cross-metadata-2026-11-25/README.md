# Steel catalog: Ground Zero cross dimensions and material descriptions

Research date: 2026-11-25. This is a bounded check of one public museum catalog entry, not a structural analysis or a chain-of-custody audit. [National September 11 Memorial & Museum, accession C.2011.179.131](https://collection.911memorial.org/Detail/objects/117560/rel/1). Retrieved HTML SHA-256: `6967aecaa1d6d6ee3802683552246f92effffcab19a9e59526f50c91607725ca`. Raw HTML lines below refer to that retrieved version; page content may change.

## Observations preserved separately

- Catalog dimensions: **16.3 ft X 8.5833 ft**; catalog metric dimensions: **41.402 cm X 21.801582 cm** (retrieved HTML line 294). Its historical note separately calls the column **17-foot-tall** (line 251). Neither measurement was physically verified.
- Description: “attached aluminum cladding on the right crossbeam” (line 250). Historical note: the horizontal arm was “draped with a piece of galvanized steel” (line 251). The catalog does not establish in these passages whether the two descriptions refer to the same piece or different attached material. Both remain unresolved.
- The museum reports the cross-shaped column and crossbeam were found in rubble of **6 World Trade Center** on **September 13, 2001**, and relocated near West Street on **October 3, 2001** (line 251). Its entry names the Port Authority as donor (line 294). These are the museum's accounts; a find location is not proof of the component's original building, and the catalog entry is not an unbroken transfer record.

## Reproducible comparison

Using exactly 30.48 cm per foot, the catalog's imperial values calculate to **496.824 cm X 261.618984 cm**, not the printed metric values. Separately, multiplying the same numbers by 2.54 yields the two printed metric values exactly. This is a pattern in the displayed values; the unit-conversion mechanism, authoritative dimensions and reason for the difference remain unverified. Precision reflects the displayed inputs, not physical measurement accuracy. The 17-foot note and 16.3-foot catalog field have different descriptive contexts; the source gives no precision or method to determine their relationship.

These inconsistencies affect reliance on this catalog's descriptive metadata. They do not establish the original dimensions, the material's composition, a WTC 7 attribution, suppression, intent, or an alternative collapse mechanism. Distinct attached materials, changes in description, and ordinary clerical errors are untested possibilities, not established explanations.

## Open records and tests

- Obtain dated accession and object-condition records, original measurements and units, scale photographs, and any revisions to the catalog. Ask whether the measured fields describe the same physical extents.
- Obtain primary recovery, transfer and provenance records; test any proposed building or structural-member attribution against marked identifiers and plans.
- Obtain descriptions, photographs or materials testing that establish whether the noted aluminum and galvanized steel refer to the same or separate attachments. A conversion or catalog comparison alone cannot settle composition.

The existing steel-preservation questions and source conflicts remain open. This catalog observation does not modify earlier evidence records.
