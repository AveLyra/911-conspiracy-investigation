# Flight 11 recording custody and briefing copies

November 6, 2026 America/New_York · **TEL03–TEL04 / AUD016-A1 / TC05–TC06**

A public reproduction of the Commission's Team 7 Box 17 FBI file describes three discs made from the recorded portion of the Flight 11 call to American Airlines' Southeast Reservations Office (SERO). The FBI report identifies the original-format recording as a Rockwell file and describes an evidence copy containing it. A different report describes making an analog tape for transcription. These are specific paths to the recorded segment, its copies and the underlying call-activity records. The acquisition here is the FBI paperwork, with the media and raw call records still open. The two transcript routes both print **Flight 12** during the agent exchange and **Flight 11** later in the same call (302-3005 p. **5**, lines **24–44** and CE-1024 p. **12**, lines **35–49**, and p. **13**, lines **40–45**). Both flight numbers and their order are retained. The repeated wording can arise from shared audio. It does not independently identify a different flight or establish which person introduced the earlier number in the source recording.

The reported **four-minute cutoff**, the airline index's **4:07** file length, the playback discussion of **five or ten** additional minutes, and the later interview's **perhaps 23 minutes** for the whole onboard-to-SERO call remain separate observations. The press of the button, the start and end of captured audio, and the duration of each person's participation need separate anchors. A rounded estimate, differing participation windows and different copy boundaries remain testable possibilities. The reported automatic cutoff is contrary evidence for treating the transcript endpoint alone as evidence of a deleted segment.
