## Source observations

The June 17, 2004 public hearing transcript, accessed November 25, 2026, is retained as HTML SHA-256 `7d94b247f92595c378db69b729a4dfcd17e31039f0253b12c2aae65f7e561905`. In the acquired file, lines 397–413 contain the exchange about NORAD records and tapes:

- Commissioner Ben-Veniste described difficulty obtaining information and staff discovery of tapes and other records. He initially attributed a lack of recollection about tape recordings to Arnold and Eberhart. Eberhart objected that he had not testified before the Commission on the day in question; Ben-Veniste apologized and corrected the attribution. He later said the reported statements were made to staff and identified Arnold and McKinley as the speakers. The source preserves the initial attribution and the correction together.
- Arnold said he had been unaware of a NEADS tape at the time and, based on what staff told him, the tape initially could not be run and a contractor later made it playable. He said his earlier lack-of-knowledge statement had been accurate to the best of his knowledge. These are his hearing account and reported understanding, not the contractor's records or an independent playback test.
- The 2005 IG report, physical page 13 / printed 5, says key NEADS positions were recorded, personnel encountered a transcription problem, and the tapes were not used for the press release or reporting until Commission staff reviewed them. That report gives an institutional finding; it does not by itself identify the particular tape Arnold described or what he personally knew.

## Interpretation and tests

The hearing gives an identified speaker's account and an on-record correction of an attribution. It does not prove when a particular person knew of a tape, why an individual tape was not used, that all recordings were inaccessible, or that the hearing and IG references concern the same physical tape. Preserve the participants' accounts and the IG's later findings separately; no inconsistency is adjudicated. In particular, inability to run a tape, difficulty transcribing it, knowledge of its existence and using it in a timeline are distinct questions. The staff's earlier initial attribution and its correction are not independent corroborating accounts.
