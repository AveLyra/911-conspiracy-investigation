# FAA knowledge-and-correction trail

Review date: October 26, 2026, America/New_York. Additive public research record; earlier findings and conflicting source records remain unchanged.

**The strongest new result is a recoverable drafting-and-noncorrection trail, with conflicting witness accounts beneath the Inspector General's summary.** We acquired a 4,380-page release containing interviews, reproduced emails and OIG working records. We also located a case-status screen recording the investigation as **Closed on December 12, 2006**. We have **not** obtained the native May 2003 answer drafts, their original transmission email, a final FAA correction, or a final administrative-action/no-action decision. Investigation closure does not supply those missing dispositions.

These records concern FAA's account of communications during the attacks and its later answer to the Commission. They do not establish advance knowledge, participation in the attacks, WTC 7 demolition, or a coordinated conspiracy. The concern with the most direct support is the handling of an answer after a material inaccuracy became known. The strongest objection to inferring intentional concealment is the witnesses' uncertain and conflicting recollections, their stated expectations of follow-up by others, and the OIG's limited intentional-falsity finding. Those points remain beside the noncorrection evidence.

## 1. Who prepared and transmitted the answer?

The archived hearing transcript credits **“Mr. Asmus and Ms. Schuessler.”** That wording is retained; it is not silently renamed. The subsequent interviews offer more specific but conflicting accounts:

- **Osmus, September 24, 2004:** she, Linda Schuessler and Laura Brown gathered in Brown's office; Schuessler called the liaison; Brown typed the answer on her computer while Schuessler described the conversation. Osmus first remembered only one or two sentences, then acknowledged the text shown to her was longer than she recalled. [Physical pages 3294–3298](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=3294); preserved excerpts P04–P05.
- **Schuessler, December 9, 2004:** she supplied information to **“Shirley and Lynne and they compiled the statement”** and later described them as the leads. [Pages 4056 and 4060](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=4056); P06.
- **Miller, February 10, 2005:** told investigators that the assertion she helped prepare it was **“incorrect.”** She said people associated her with Commission work from the beginning, although her involvement began later. [Pages 2679–2681](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2679); P07.
- **Brown, August 31, 2005:** confirmed working with Schuessler and Osmus and said she gave the statement to Garvey, who wanted to provide it to Ben-Veniste herself. This is Brown's account of the route, not the original transmittal. [Page 4325](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=4325); P16.

These identify useful custodians and files to seek. They do not establish which recollection controls, who approved each sentence, or whether the surviving read-in text equals every draft. The identities of the unnamed executives in the final OIG report are not assigned by matching these interviews to redacted roles.

## 2. When did people learn the answer was inaccurate?

The final OIG report says the liaison did not join the phone bridge immediately after the first impact, and that three executives learned of the inaccuracy but did not correct it with the Commission. Its page 10 preserves different awareness dates: potentially the read-in day, within weeks, and by summer's end. [Official report, pages 9–10](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cc2006085.pdf#page=9); P02.

The newly examined interviews preserve the uncertainty beneath that summary:

| Account | Wording/date claim retained | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Schuessler, December 2004 | **“maybe the following day”**; also refers to reflection that evening and a next-day conversation | Repeated memory qualifications; not a contemporaneous dated correction |
| Osmus, September 2004 | **“a couple of weeks later, perhaps”** | An approximate recollection |
| Brown, August 2005 | **“It was weeks after that”**, not shortly after | Does not fix a particular date; attributes information to Miller |
| Miller, February 2005 | Probably summer 2003; does not recall a specific QFR discussion or follow-up correction | Her account questions whether the uncertain recollection supplied a better timeline |
| Redacted liaison interview | Remembers clarifying that she was not upstairs then, but not who was present or when; could have been before or after May 2003 | Does not independently date the claimed initial drafting call or later clarification |

Sources: [3295](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=3295), [4056–4059](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=4056), [4330](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=4330), [2681–2689](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2681), [4133–4135](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=4133); P06, P08–P10, P14–P15. Do not turn these into a single established date of knowledge.

## 3. Expecting a correction is different from asking for one

Osmus said **“we knew”** the answer was inaccurate as presented and expected the liaison's Commission interview to clarify it. She did not know whether Miller had done more. The redacted liaison interviewee, asked about that specific point, replied: **“No one talked to me about clarifying that point with them. I would remember that, no.”** [Pages 3306–3307](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=3306) and [4138–4139](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=4138); P11–P12.

Miller did not recall transmitting a clarification and said there was no better timeline to supply; Brown deferred to Miller's Commission contacts; Schuessler did not know how correction had been handled. These accounts support a specific follow-through question. They do not, without more, distinguish responsibility being passed along, poor process, incomplete recollection, self-protection, or intentional concealment. Original clarification messages, the actual liaison Commission interview and the Commission's receipt/correction records would test those possibilities.

## 4. Preserve the event being timed

The liaison interviewee describes reaching the **building** about five or ten minutes after the first impact and before the second; separately she describes going **upstairs to the tenth-floor situation room** after the Pentagon impact. She also warns that she cannot give reliable clock times, although she recalls the sequence around the Pentagon impact. Miller recalls a conversation placing arrival **at the building** after the Pentagon impact. Osmus likewise uses “FAA building” in her later-arrival account. [Pages 4104–4112](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=4104), [2681–2682](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2681), [3295–3296](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=3295); P28–P31.

The liaison also calls the QFR's NORAD-contact description misleading: it suggested a hotline, whereas she recalls separate calls from available phones and denies immediate contact. [Page 4136](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=4136); P13.

**Building arrival, upstairs arrival, bridge participation and NORAD contact must remain separate fields.** The contradictory building-arrival accounts remain unresolved. The distinction between events does not itself reconcile the witnesses or prove that other FAA–military communications did not occur. Visitor/access logs, contemporaneous notes and call records would be better tests than assigning an exact clock time from later recollection.

Osmus also says she did not recall reviewing logs before the May 2003 answer, did not know the origin of the cited logs/timelines, and later reviewed presentation matters rather than accuracy. That is an individual source-validation limit, not proof that nobody checked any evidence. [Pages 3299–3300](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=3299); P38–P39.

## 5. Actual reproduced emails and draft-report targets

The September 27–28, 2004 **QFRs** thread includes Miller saying she had not handled anything for the record after the May 22 hearing, followed by an account identifying Osmus as the earlier lead and describing subsequent chronology production. These are 2004 retrospective emails, not the missing May 2003 drafting/transmission thread. [Pages 2495–2496](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2495); P17–P18.

A February 14, 2005 reproduced OIG email refers to a draft outline and results-in-brief, explicitly **not yet approved by headquarters**. November 14 and 21 emails refer to the wrapped attachment label **`draft_final_report_27a.doc`**, review of a near-final report and a proposed joint transmittal. The December synopsis later records that a joint DOD report would not be issued. These are specific version/review-chain targets. The DOC attachment, its tracked changes, approvals and native messages have not been recovered here. [Pages 2182–2185](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2182), [2188](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2188); P19–P21, V04.

The 429-page packet preserves more heavily redacted versions of the November 2005 and September 2004 threads. The 2021 packet exposes more body text at the corresponding pages. Both versions and their hashes are retained. **Different disclosure versions are not evidence that the historical email itself changed.** A redaction's validity or purpose is not adjudicated here.

## 6. Website corrections have their own version trail

The September 27, 2004 Mead-to-Blakey memorandum says the website error was reported to Brown, that the fact sheet had since been revised, and that the revision was **not identified as recent**; it requested a notation or errata. The attached printouts retain the August 12, 2002 heading:

| Preserved record | Entry retained |
|---|---|
| Original printout, footer September 24, 2004 | **0924:** AA77 suspected-hijacking notification to NEADS, followed by an open line discussing AA77/UA93 |
| Revised printout, footer September 27, 2004 | **0924:** general open phone line with agencies and military, sharing missing/suspicious-aircraft information |
| December 9, 2004 OIG briefing | Describes changed sites with **“9:20”** and **“(revised entry 10/21/04)”** |

[429-page packet, pages 168–172](https://www.governmentattic.org/6docs/DOT-OIG-FAA9-11_2001-2006.pdf#page=168); [4,380-page packet, page 2467](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2467); P23–P26, V05–V06. The 0924 and 9:20 records remain side by side; we have not reconstructed every intermediate publication version or selected one value. The printouts also retain other values, including AA77 impact **0940** and UA93 crash **1007**; those were not silently replaced with other chronologies.

This is evidence of a documented correction and a contemporaneous concern about revision labeling. It does not establish erasure of all prior versions, deliberate deception or correction of the separate Commission answer.

## 7. Closure is established more narrowly than final administrative action

The TIGR input sheet records case **042R0122001**, ROI date **8/31/06**, and close-investigation date **12/12/06**. A separate case-status screen says **Closed**, start **12/12/2006**, with an earlier Open entry dated **08/24/2004**. [429-page packet, pages 6–7](https://www.governmentattic.org/6docs/DOT-OIG-FAA9-11_2001-2006.pdf#page=6); V01–V02. These image-only details were not captured by ordinary PDF text search.

The December 12 quarterly synopsis retains **“Final report is pending”** beside an October 1 update saying the report issued August 31, 2006; its following page says **“Investigation is to be closed”** and **“FINAL UPDATE.”** We preserve all wording rather than normalizing the status history. [2021 packet, pages 2188–2189](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2188); P21–P22.

The actual final report recommends that FAA correct its response and **consider appropriate administrative action** for two then-current executives. That does not say action occurred. No final action, no-action rationale, completed QFR correction or recommendation-disposition file was located in the bounded public-source review. The 2006 semiannual report repeats the recommendations; targeted searches in the two 2007 semiannual reports did not identify a case-specific disposition. Direct downloads of those semiannual PDFs returned 403; web-tool access was limited to parsed text. These limits cannot prove the records do not exist. [Official report, page 12](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cc2006085.pdf#page=12); P03; [search scope](SEARCH-AND-METHOD.md).

## 8. Smaller discrepancies and source limits are retained

- **Referral date:** the synopsis says July 24, 2004; the reproduced referral letter is dated July 29, 2004 and bears an August 24 receipt stamp. Those are different recorded dates; this review does not supply a cause or assume all refer to the same act. P27, V03.
- **Interview cover time:** Osmus's cover prints **10:05 p.m.–11:01 a.m.**; the opening says **10:05 a.m.** This is a low-weight transcript inconsistency, not evidence of tampering or an attack-day timeline finding. P36–P37.
- **Release scope:** the 2011 OIG letter describes 4,845 responsive pages, referrals and a 426-page production; the 2021 letter describes 4,376 attached pages and a 255-page FAA referral. A separate 2011 FAA letter releases 255 pages and gives a July 6 request date, while the OIG letter cites October 5. Same-size referrals or a shared control number do not establish identical contents or resolve the request-date history. P33–P35.
- **Transcription and provenance:** Commission interview transcripts in the larger packet bear an “unofficial, uncorrected” notice. OIG interview covers say prepared from tape recordings. “ORIGINAL” stamped on a reproduced page does not authenticate native audio, the underlying emails or file history. No original tapes or native email containers were acquired.
- **Embedded-file inspection:** all three downloaded release PDFs expose no embedded-file attachment names through the inspected PDF name trees. This limited structural test does not establish that source attachments never existed, that no text reproduction is present, or that no content is recoverable by other methods. [Audit](EMBEDDED-FILE-AUDIT.json).

## What would strengthen or weaken the concern?

The most useful next targets are the **actual May 2003 draft/transmission chain**, contemporaneous messages showing **who learned which fact when**, the liaison's **original Commission interview and audio**, and the **post-August 2006 correction/action/disposition records**. A documented correction received by Commission staff would weaken an inference of uncorrected institutional concealment while remaining beside the OIG's contrary account. A reasoned no-action decision would address a different question from whether a correction occurred. Evidence of explicit instructions to preserve a known false account would strengthen an intentional-concealment hypothesis. Neither result is presumed.

The OIG reported insufficient evidence of knowing false notification statements or purposeful omission, and attributed the AA77 notification error to reliance on inaccurate chronologies. Schuessler separately denied an intentional effort to mislead and described the volume of data; the final report noted more than 6,000 materials supplied to the Commission. These are retained as adverse/alternative evidence, not adopted as a resolution of every discrepancy. P01–P03, P32. A personal expectation of correction is not proof that one was made, and failure to locate a disposition is not proof it was suppressed.
