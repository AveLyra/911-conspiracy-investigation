# Scooby report 7: January and July intelligence-routing source check

November 12, 2026. This checks selected claims in the recovered N14 report against the original oversight publications. Those publications are primary sources for their investigations and findings, but are retrospective descriptions of records; this pass did not inspect the native draft, cable, email, attachment, server or recipient logs. CIA’s original URL redirected to its reading-room landing page during this check. The prior project [source transcription](../cia-lead-verification-2026-10-26/SOURCE-EXCERPTS.md#cia-p079) identifies a hashed mirror of the CIA report and is used below with that limitation.

| Claim | Verification and limits |
| --- | --- |
| A draft for FBI headquarters and New York was held in January 2000. | [DOJ OIG, printed pp. 240–242 / PDF pp. 249–251](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/special/s0606/final.pdf#page=249), describes the visa-containing draft, intended recipients and Michelle’s instruction to hold it on the deputy chief’s authority. The report says CIA records did **not** show John accessing the draft itself, although he accessed related cables. A recorded instruction and absence of a draft-access event are distinct facts. |
| Some information reached the FBI, but the visa was absent from the documented update. | [DOJ OIG, printed pp. 244–245 / PDF pp. 253–254](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/special/s0606/final.pdf#page=253), infers the first Malaysia-meeting cable reached the FBI from close textual matching in the threat update; it says the update omitted the passport and visa. That is affirmative evidence of partial sharing, not proof that the held draft or visa information reached the intended field office. |
| An internal cable asserted the documents had been passed, but delivery remains unresolved. | [DOJ OIG, printed pp. 243–244 / PDF pp. 252–253](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/special/s0606/final.pdf#page=252), records the assertion and says the cable did not identify recipient or delivery method; the CIA OIG report, [printed p. 54](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/cia/DOC_0006184107.pdf#page=79), says its team could neither confirm nor refute delivery. The assertion is a contemporaneous lead rather than a receipt. |
| The draft was retained in February; a later database deletion does not date destruction of that particular draft. | DOJ OIG printed p. 242 records the drafter’s instruction to delete unrelated drafts but save this one. The [CIA OIG transcription, printed p. 53](../cia-lead-verification-2026-10-26/SOURCE-EXCERPTS.md), reports a January 13 Lotus-attached copy recovered in January 2004 and a late-2002 database deletion; it says the specific draft’s deletion date was unknown. This does not establish intentional destruction of this draft. Native retention, deletion and backup records remain open. |
| CIA OIG’s January 2004 recovery and DOJ OIG’s February 2004 discovery are separate statements, but their relation is unclear. | The CIA report’s account above says its team obtained the Lotus-attached version in January 2004. [DOJ OIG, printed pp. 227–228 / PDF pp. 236–237](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/special/s0606/final.pdf#page=236), says DOJ noticed an undisclosed draft title in February and that CIA OIG had not previously obtained that document in connection with its review. The statements are in tension if they concern the same copy and review; neither identifies the transfer chain sufficiently to resolve it. Do not assume either fabrication or reconciled duplicate copies. |
| A July 2001 request to send information was followed by delay. | [DOJ OIG, printed pp. 299–300 / PDF pp. 308–309](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/special/s0606/final.pdf#page=308), describes a July 13 request about the Khallad identification and says investigators found no evidence CIA supplied that information to the FBI until August 30. This is a later information stream and should not be conflated with the January draft. Its native message and complete delivery history were not independently checked here. |

The hold and identified sharing failures support scrutiny of decisions and responsibilities. The available accounts also preserve limited sharing, competing delivery assertions and an unreconciled 2004 discovery sequence. They do not establish the hold’s motive, a Saudi recruitment operation, or a connection to UMS or Mossad. Distinguishing error, compartmentation, source protection and intentional concealment requires native records and testimony tied to the decision.
