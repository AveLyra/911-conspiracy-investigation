## Structural steel and the remaining debris followed different described paths

- The October 11, 2001 **Dust Control Evaluation Summary** describes barge and truck transfer to Staten Island Landfill and staging at the top of the **1/9 landfill**. It says large items including **structural steel/red iron** were separated at staging. The summary says the red iron was loaded for transport to a specialized cutting operation for cutting and recycling. It then describes four existing shakers, manual inspection of large material, inspection of smaller material on three conveyors, and landfill disposal of inspected debris. The steel-separation step precedes the narrative's shaker and conveyor steps. The passage does not say what was examined before the steel left, who received it, or which building a given piece came from. [City PDF pp. 2–3, Bates 000150330–331](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000150329.pdf#page=2)
- The summary reports approximately **10,000–12,000 tons per 24 hours** and continuous work. This is a draft's estimate of site processing, without a daily series, receipts or category weights. The October 16 cover memo solicits comments on two working drafts and qualifies that some components may already be operating. Keep the October 11 process account and the October 16 qualification together. Neither supplies an implementation date for every step. The draft's four-shaker wording remains separate from the later court opinion's account that two named contractors were retained around October 14. The records address different questions, and the equipment in service and exact dates remain unverified. [Cover memo and estimate, PDF pp. 1–2](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000150329.pdf#page=1) · [District court opinion, factual background](https://openjurist.org/567/fsupp2d/529/wtc-families-for-a-proper-burial-inc-v-city-of-new-york-1454675)

## Building 7 area and record custodians

The **October 12, 2001 Version 4.0** landfill health and safety plan places “Building 7 operations” in the landfill exclusion-zone description, alongside debris storage and sorting areas. Its occupational monitoring section separately mentions workers and FBI staff in the “building seven area.” The plan identifies FBI and NYPD operational authority over the crime scene, Phillips and Jordan as construction manager, and EE&G as health and safety facilitator. It also describes private contractor workers performing structural-steel torch cutting. These assignments provide retrieval leads. The plan does not connect a torch-cut piece to the Building 7 area, any specific debris load, or a particular recycling destination. [City plan, PDF pp. 5, 8, 21 and 64](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000150335.pdf#page=8)

The later DOJ OIG account describes a separate area at Fresh Kills for debris identified as from WTC Building 7 and reports mixed rubble in its account of item origin. That independently identifies the work area as a research target. It does not validate the origin of individual pieces. The City plan points to **Figure 1** for the exclusion zone but says on its cover that Figure 1 was to be submitted separately. An approved map and shift, receiving and cut logs would be needed to associate areas and records. [DOJ OIG report](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/0403a/index.htm) · [City plan cover](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000150335.pdf#page=1)

## Release differences and contradictory cover wording

Both 73-page releases carry the October 12 Version 4.0 cover. In **000150335** the cover is on PDF p. 1. In **000150256** it is on PDF p. 73. The latter begins with the table of contents. Both scans say **REVIEW DRAFT** and visibly contain “has not been formally released and should be construed to represent a final document.” That final phrase conflicts with the draft designation and nonrelease language. The displayed wording is preserved without inserting a missing word or selecting an operative status. The October 16 memo's description of working drafts is a separate observation. [000150335 cover](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000150335.pdf#page=1) · [000150256 cover](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000150256.pdf#page=73) · [October 16 memo](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000150329.pdf#page=1)

The PDF hashes differ. To test the apparent ordering, pair page `i` of 000150256 with page `(i % 73) + 1` of 000150335 for one-based `i`. Extract sorted embedded text with PyMuPDF 1.27.2.2, lowercase and remove every character outside `[a-z]`. Python's default `difflib.SequenceMatcher` then gives **65 of 73** pairs at or above **0.95**, and **70 of 73** at or above **0.80**. The three below 0.80 are **34/35**, **39/40**, and **59/60**. The comparison supports the ordering and identifies pages for further review. Character recognition and line order affect the scores. The scores do not prove full content equivalence. The key covers, exclusion-zone text and monitoring passages were checked at their actual locators. Count the matching passages as one draft account.
