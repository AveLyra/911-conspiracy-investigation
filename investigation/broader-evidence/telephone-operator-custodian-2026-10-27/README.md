# Telephone originals: two operator reports and the call-record custodian

October 27, 2026. Existing AUD016-A1 / TC05-B105 / TC06-B110. This is an additive acquisition and source comparison. Earlier reports and conflicting values remain unchanged.

**Seven newly acquired page-image entities provide two released FBI interview summaries and selected pages of American Airlines' February 9, 2004 response.** They advance the operator and custody trail. They do not provide native call events, operator tickets, a field dictionary, calibrated call times or authenticated caller-to-record joins.

## O01 — A report from the operator who says she received the call

[Key-302 reader page 17](https://html.scribdassets.com/4wfmf4jddsfxfj8/images/17-ce884a0772.jpg), OP07, identifies **M-INT-00000059 / 265A-NY-280350-302-22171**, transcription and investigation dated September 11, 2001. Its opening paragraph says AT&T operator Mercy Lorenzo contacted the FBI about an emergency call she received. The following paragraphs attribute to the female passenger a seatback-phone origin, a request to reach her husband in Washington, a hijacking, guns and knives, passengers moving rearward, and uncertainty about the pilots' awareness.

The interview body calls the husband a **sergeant**. It gives no passenger name, flight number, call time, call identifier, destination number, completed transfer or collect-billing confirmation. A separate handwritten **Olson to Operator** notation appears above the form. Its author, date and basis are not established. Do not promote that annotation into an identification made by the interviewee or reconstruct the missing destination from other accounts.

## O02 — The other operator's report is a related, different evidence layer

[Flight-77 reader page 36](https://html.scribdassets.com/7na47jw8009n4uh/images/36-58fb41f51b.jpg), OP01, identifies **M-INT-00000058 / 265A-NY-280350-302-22170**, also dated September 11. Its opening identifies the FBI's interlocutor as **Teresa Gonzalez**. The next paragraph says **Lorenzo** received the passenger's call. This version specifies Flight 77, contains a redacted requested number, and repeats guns/knives and the husband's sergeant description.

This is not a second operator independently claiming to have heard the passenger. Two FBI forms are not two independent passenger-call observations. The forms do document two reported contacts with the FBI; their underlying accounts may share Lorenzo as the source. Notes, recordings, routing and follow-up interviews could test the transmission chain.

The guns/knives wording remains beside the earlier Olson interview's knives/box-cutters account and the later caller attribution. A nonmention of guns is not an explicit denial. Without identifying the same caller and conversation, these differences cannot be treated as established contradictory eyewitness descriptions of one event. Miscommunication, different callers/calls, incomplete summaries and more substantive misdescription remain untested possibilities; none is selected.

## O03 — The airline's response names a different record holder

[Counsel reader page 6](https://html.scribdassets.com/czuxmc1fkakhxb/images/6-d34c11fc42.jpg), OP04, dates the response February 9, 2004 and identifies the January 9 requests. [Reader page 8 / printed page 3, item 7](https://html.scribdassets.com/czuxmc1fkakhxb/images/8-fc7c20e2d1.jpg), OP03, reproduces a request using **GTE** and answers that **AT&T Claircom** provided the service and held the itemized records. Counsel says American received monthly system-wide reliability reports without individual calls, and says Claircom ceased business in 2002.

This pins what counsel represented, not an independently authenticated system configuration, exhaustive custody history or proof the records ceased to exist. The request's GTE label and response's AT&T Claircom label are preserved separately. Generic GTE system rules cannot simply define the Claircom/OSPS printout. The acquired record gives a concrete reason to seek carrier, successor and already-produced investigative copies, not only airline-held records. It does not demonstrate a failed or deliberately misdirected search.

## O04 — Preserve the dates without manufacturing a historical conflict

OP03 cites an email to John Raidt and Bill Johnstone dated **January 20, 2003**. [Reader page 7 / printed page 2, item 4](https://html.scribdassets.com/czuxmc1fkakhxb/images/7-cd5987ce20.jpg), OP02, also prints that date for a load-factor email. The parent letter says 2004. Our earlier retrieval queue used January 20, **2004** for the anticipated airline supplement.

Those are different evidence layers: a date printed in this source, the parent document's date, and our provisional queue label. We have not acquired the cited native email or established that the queue and letter refer to the same message. Do not silently correct the year, infer two historical email versions, or treat our queue as independent corroboration. An editorial error, an earlier message or a mistaken target association are open hypotheses.

[Reader page 7 / printed page 2, item 2](https://html.scribdassets.com/czuxmc1fkakhxb/images/7-cd5987ce20.jpg) says a November 2003 production included American's held audio and transcripts. [Reader page 9 / printed page 4, item 8](https://html.scribdassets.com/czuxmc1fkakhxb/images/9-d1266f0d0c.jpg), OP05, says American could not locate Sweeney/Woodward or Flight-77-person call recordings/transcripts. Page 4 item 12 and [reader page 11 / printed page 6, continuing Response(Item11) and entry18](https://html.scribdassets.com/czuxmc1fkakhxb/images/11-6bf3af6645.jpg), OP06, say the SOC maintained no itemized telephone logs/bills; searches concerning SERO and Logan remained pending.

These are counsel's bounded possession/search representations. Broad production language and a narrower unsuccessful search are not automatically incompatible. An airline SOC is not the Solicitor General's DOJ office: this does not answer whether DOJ incoming, transfer, command-center or voicemail records existed. The November production manifest, underlying searches and later supplements are the discriminating records, not an assumption that all audio was recorded or all logs destroyed.
