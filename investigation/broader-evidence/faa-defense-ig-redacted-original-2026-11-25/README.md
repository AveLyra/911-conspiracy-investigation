## Observations and unresolved wording

On page 3, under “Corrective Actions,” the memorandum reports that the NORAD commanding general wrote the Commission in March 2004 that testimony at a January hearing was inaccurate and that the Commission's developed timeline was correct. It also states: **“DoD officials did not publicize the modified timelines once they became aware of the discrepancies.”** The source does not reproduce the letter, its attachments or receipt; the exact January hearing, corresponding testimony and letter scope remain to be established. The memorandum separately reports on page 2 that officials did not use transcripts, even when available, to prepare press releases and subsequent testimony. These are the IG's reported findings, not direct access to its supporting evidence.

The later [NPS timeline, note 100](https://www.nps.gov/flni/learn/historyculture/september-11-2001-timeline.htm), states: **“Both DoD and the FAA provided the public and the Commission with corrected information”** in the context of multiple identified timeline errors. The NPS account and the DoD IG's non-publication statement are an **unresolved apparent discrepancy**: neither source here identifies the exact public documents, audience and time interval needed for a like-for-like comparison. Preserve the wording without selecting a controlling account. Proposed possibilities about timing, different materials or audiences remain untested; dated releases and source-to-claim citations could discriminate among them.

Page 1 reports that the review found no indication the statements were intentionally false and attributes inaccurate statements to inadequate DoD reconstruction capability and thoroughness. Page 3 also reports no evidence that DoD or DOT officials knowingly misled the Commission. These are the investigation's conclusions; they do not prove a negative or resolve the separate FAA QFR correction and administrative-action trail. The [DOT IG report](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cc2006085.pdf#page=12) separately recommended FAA correct its May 2003 answer and consider action. A NORAD letter is not that FAA answer or its receipt.
