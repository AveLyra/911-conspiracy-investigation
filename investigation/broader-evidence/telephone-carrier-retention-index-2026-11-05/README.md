## Observations

- **The FCC identified a records index at the carrier.** The 2000 and 2001 editions of §42.4 require an operating-company headquarters master index listing records retained, their retention periods and their locations. They also describe a certified entry for loss or destruction before the index period expires. The index is subject to FCC staff review. Section 42.1(a) includes records actually received with acquisitions, including purchases and mergers. This creates a lead to dated index pages, successor records inventories and any applicable early-loss entries. The texts supply neither the contents of either carrier's index nor proof that any particular records transferred. [2000 CFR, printed p. 5](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2000-title47-vol3/pdf/CFR-2000-title47-vol3.pdf#page=15) · [2001 CFR, printed p. 5](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2001-title47-vol3/pdf/CFR-2001-title47-vol3.pdf#page=15)
- **The 18-month provision concerns toll billing.** Section 42.6 specifies retention of enough records to supply caller name, address and telephone number, destination number, date, time and duration for toll calls the carrier bills, including when billing on another carrier's behalf. Section 42.5(b) describes data-description and accuracy certification for machine-readable copies. Section 42.7 separately addresses the periods in the carrier's index and retention connected to complaints or Commission-directed proceedings or inquiries. The September 19 Federal Register notice describes these under **OMB 3060–0166**, with an approval expiration of **August 31, 2004**. The OMB expiration is a different date from any records retention period. The CFR and notice describe the same regulatory framework. [2001 CFR, printed p. 6](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2001-title47-vol3/pdf/CFR-2001-title47-vol3.pdf#page=16) · [FCC notice, 66 FR 48258–48259](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2001-09-19/pdf/FR-2001-09-19.pdf#page=61)
- **Air-to-ground classification and service labels.** The 2000 §20.9(a)(8) lists **800 MHz Air-Ground Radiotelephone Service (part 22, subpart M)** in the common-carriage list. The 2001 edition lists **Air-Ground Radiotelephone Service (part 22, subpart G)** there. DA 01–361, effective February 21, 2001, describes its changes as editorial updates to service names and subpart designations. The May 2001 waiver notice independently names Claircom (**KNKG801**) and GTE Airfone (**KNKG804**) as the commercial air-to-ground licensees in that proceeding. The licensing record identifies the service class. The actual toll biller, calling product and treatment of a specific Flight 77 event require separate proof. [2000 CFR, printed pp. 10–11](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2000-title47-vol2/pdf/CFR-2000-title47-vol2.pdf#page=20) · [2001 CFR, printed pp. 10–11](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2001-title47-vol2/pdf/CFR-2001-title47-vol2.pdf#page=20) · [DA 01–361, 66 FR 10967–10968](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2001-02-21/pdf/FR-2001-02-21.pdf#page=24) · [DA 01–1216, PDF p. 1](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-01-1216A1.pdf#page=1)
- **The two-year-six-month provision concerns a different category.** Both §42.11(b) editions give that period after a carrier ceases to provide service under the relevant terms for covered **price and service** records. The 2000 §42.11(a) describes interstate domestic interexchange offerings of a nondominant IXC. The 2001 §42.11(a) adds international offerings and limits the provision for commercial mobile radio service providers to specified international affiliate routes, with a resale exception. The March 28 rule states an April 27, 2001 effective date and publishes the revised paragraph (a). This amendment concerns the scope of pricing and service documentation. The §42.6 billing language is the same in the two editions reviewed. ## Inference and limits

The dated master index and digital-copy certifications are now specific records to locate. An index entry could identify the system name, retention period and record location. An entry by itself would leave actual disposition and a call-specific match unresolved. The 18-month language supplies no universal coverage for unsuccessful attempts, unbilled legs, switch traces, recordings or operator worksheets. It establishes neither that a relevant item survived nor that it was destroyed. This was a comparison of selected pages and named regulatory sections, without a carrier-specific exception or compliance determination. No new event-level records were recovered.
