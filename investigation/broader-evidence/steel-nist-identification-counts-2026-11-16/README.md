## Observations

The authors describe 236 cataloged structural steel elements. Their categories on printed p. 24 are **90 exterior column panels**, **55 core columns**, **23 floor truss pieces**, **25 channels**, and **43 miscellaneous pieces**, including WTC 5 elements. They report unambiguous identification for **42 of 90 exterior panels** and **12 of 55 core columns**; they report the trusses and channels had unknown original locations. Those are the authors' reported identification outcomes, not a new independent inspection of the artifacts. The arithmetic totals 236; 42/90 ≈ 46.7% and 12/55 ≈ 21.8% are calculated from the reported category counts, not estimates for all recovered steel or any building as a whole.

Keep the separately published [NIST NCSTAR 1-3 inventory category table](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3.pdf#page=60), printed p. 28, as a distinct record: **94 exterior column panel sections**, **2 bow-ties**, **11 rectangular built-up box columns**, **44 wide-flange sections**, **23 floor trusses**, **25 channels**, **7 WTC 5 coupons**, and **30 miscellaneous**, totaling 236. The two records use different labels and component descriptions. The basis for their category-to-category relationship is not established by this check; do not treat the differences as fabrication or silently reclassify either table.

## Limits and next records

The 2007 paper concerns tower material and summarizes prior work; it is not an independent inventory, transfer record or item-level WTC 7 attribution. It does not establish the origin of W-14A or W-14B, the fate of uncollected steel, or any collapse cause. The earlier FEMA WTC 7 field attributions and NIST unidentified entries remain preserved in [FEMA Appendix D](https://www.fema.gov/pdf/library/fema403_apd.pdf#page=16) and [NIST NCSTAR 1-3B](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3b.pdf#page=96), respectively; the underlying attribution rationale remains open.
