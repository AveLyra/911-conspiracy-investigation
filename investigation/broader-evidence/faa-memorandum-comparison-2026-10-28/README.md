# August 19 and August 31 Moussaoui memoranda: distinct purposes, shared information and unresolved court-copy limits

The official court index lists **two August 19 memoranda**, exhibits 688 and 692. Both were acquired and all 12 page images read, then compared with all five images of the unchanged August 31 exhibit 810. These are court-hosted representations of FBI documents, not authenticated native authoring or delivery records. The comparison identifies what these copies say; it does not establish attack foreknowledge, suppression, a coordinated purpose or the full drafting lineage.

In particular, exhibit 692's seven-page length must not be used to identify it as the later seven-page local FAA-warning memorandum described by OIG.

## Concerns already present in the August 19 copies

Exhibit 688 physical page 5 invokes suspected federal terrorism and aircraft-destruction offenses, including 18 U.S.C. sections 2332b and 32, as investigative theories. Exhibit 692 physical page 7 expresses a terrorism concern and a belief in possible UK connections, then requests further information. These are investigators' stated beliefs and requests, not our findings that an identified plan or network actually existed. They prevent describing the August 31 copy as the first appearance of any terrorism concern in these held papers.

The instructor accounts concerning aircraft doors, the mode-control panel, training experience and a proposed Heathrow-to-JFK simulator exercise recur across the copies. Specific matches are in [COMPARISON.json](COMPARISON.json): 688 physical pages 2/4; 692 physical page 2; 810 physical page 2. Repeated accounts in related memoranda are not independent measurements of aircraft capability or independent witness corroboration. A hypothetical simulator route is not an established operational target or attack route.

## What is different in the August 31 copy

Exhibit 810 physical page 4 / printed 6 explicitly states Minneapolis's assessment concerning preparation to seize a 747-400 for a terrorist act. The same passage says the extent of the plan was unknown. The following passages, continuing onto physical page 5 / printed 7, concern uncertainty about detention/property retention in France and possible future training or capability after release. The explicit uncertainty must accompany the warning language.

Exhibit 810 physical page 3 / printed 5 also contains information attributed to a redacted source, including reported extremist beliefs and foreign travel. No matching source-description paragraph was identified in the two fully read August 19 court copies. That bounded textual difference does not establish when the information first reached any agency, whether it existed elsewhere, or why a document included it.

A smaller wording difference also remains: the August 19 opening pages describe typical trainee experience as several thousand flight hours; 810 physical page 1 says at least two thousand. These are document statements, not independently verified aviation requirements. Introductory training-payment estimates of $8,000–$9,000 and later $8,300 statements also recur; both scopes are retained rather than a preferred amount selected. No new financial identity or wrongdoing inference follows from their repetition.

## Relation to the OIG account and missing records

The previously read [DOJ OIG account](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter4.htm), derived paragraphs 327–336, describes a seven-page local assessment, a September 4 instruction to supply an eleven-page headquarters teletype instead, and a September 5 local FAA briefing. The 810 assessment closely corresponds in content; this does not authenticate its full source/version or identify all people behind the OIG's labels. The reported local briefing remains contrary evidence to a claim of total nonwarning. The August 31 document date and September 4 instruction are separate events, not automatically conflicting dates for one act.
