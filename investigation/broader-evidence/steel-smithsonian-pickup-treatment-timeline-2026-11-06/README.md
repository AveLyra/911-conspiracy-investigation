## Side-by-side object descriptions

- The 2002 Smithsonian *Research Reports* article describes a piece selected in a New Jersey scrap yard, **“10 feet high”** and attributed to the **70th floor of the South Tower** from markings. The article's relevant passage gives neither the yard operator's name nor an accession number. [Autumn 2002, no. 110, p. 1, “Giving ‘voice’ to objects”](https://repository.si.edu/bitstreams/cfe7f0e4-a489-49b9-b7fa-eea2fa45bc3a/download#page=1)
- The NMAH catalog for **2002.0205.01 / nmah_1182945** describes a collection including an outside-ring assembly from the **70th floor of the south tower** and **two short column stubs**. It gives **125 × 75 × 36 inches** overall and lists **World Trade Center** as a recovery place. The earlier study separately preserved the two stubs' tentative **“in the 90s”** and **“between the 90th and 99th”** floor descriptions. [NMAH object catalog](https://americanhistory.si.edu/ko/collections/object/nmah_1182945) · [Prior source pins and readings](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/2a46de33b466f4e58ec3b0638ce0830878c7ee53/investigation/broader-evidence/steel-smithsonian-accession-relations-2026-11-04/SOURCE-PINS.json)

The large beam in the chronology is a candidate for the cataloged assembly. Preserve **10 feet high** and **125 inches** as published. The reporting precision, measured axis, handling orientation and physical identity across the descriptions remain open. The catalog recovery-place field, article's New Jersey selection location and chronology's pickup trip in New York remain separately attributed. None supplies the missing yard-to-museum shipping record. The two floor-attribution accounts could share a museum source, so their agreement contributes no independent marking check. The June treatment entry cannot be assigned to this accession on the available evidence. Any claim about the original surface condition of this panel would need object-specific records identifying what happened to it after recovery.
