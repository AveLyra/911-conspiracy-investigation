## Reported requirements and unresolved scope

| Source | Recorded wording or observation |
| --- | --- |
| October 4, 2001 DEP daily report, PDF p. 1, lines 32–40 | DEP reports that DEC Encon Police and DDC described a policy in force. A sentence expressly excepts Pier 25-bound vehicles from its load-wetting provision. The next sentences describe covering loads and washing vehicles. For structural-steel flatbeds, the entry says the load is washed clean of loose material and need not be tarped. It separately describes washing the truck bed after unloading. How broadly the Pier 25 exception applied beyond the sentence that names it is unstated. |
| October 15, 2001 Health Commissioner draft #4, PDF p. 2, lines 9–21 | Proposed spray washing of all departing vehicles and covers for otherwise open debris cargo. It expressly excepts flatbed trailers carrying structural steel and building components from the proposed cover requirement, conditional on thorough wet washing before exit. Pier 25 is absent from the cited clauses. The displayed date and execution area is blank. |
| October 18, 2001 DEP daily report, PDF p. 1, lines 47–51 | A heading identifies the personal and vehicle washing order. The entry says `No comments received.` It describes an attachment for review and comment. The remaining six pages of this release carry an OEM access memorandum. No complete health order was identified in this packet. |
| EPA's written response to Senator Clinton, question 8, in the February 11, 2002 hearing record, source HTML lines 14512–14542 | EPA says “All trucks leaving the World Trade Center site are required by the city to be covered.” It describes fall wetting, short winter suspensions for freezing conditions and foregoing wetting when debris was already sufficiently wet. EPA names its own wash stations, DDC traffic routing, City health monitoring under a commissioner order, and state environmental conservation traffic monitoring. EPA characterizes City violations and fines as its understanding and state citations as information received from NYSDEC. |
| NYSDEC written response, questions 2 and 3, in the same hearing record, source HTML lines 8232–8271 | NYSDEC says its law enforcement personnel monitored tarping and decontamination. It describes wash stations and load wetting, and distinguishes Pier 25 and Pier 6 barges, which lacked the netting systems used at two other transfer stations. The statement on barge netting is separate from the truck policy. |

The October flatbed exception and the later blanket cover wording remain side by side. The written answers appear in the record of the February hearing. Their submission date and the effective date and scope of the City requirement were not established here. A policy change and a later generalization omitting an exception are possible explanations. The issued order, revisions, agency instructions and dated inspection or citation records would distinguish those accounts. EPA's account is evidence that it reported enforcement under a City order. It supplies no executed order text, and it does not identify the unsigned draft as the instrument enforced.
