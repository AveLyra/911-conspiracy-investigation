# New York State Museum: steel descriptions and an inventory lead

**November 3, 2026.** Three newly read museum-authored pages identify a separate route to retained WTC building material and its documentation. A museum release carrying a **January 1, 2003** date field describes a column attributed to **North Tower floors 71–74**, while the exhibition page retrieved in this study describes one attributed to **South Tower floors 7–9**. Their exhibition contexts differ. The same-object relationship is unknown; these statements do not yet establish misidentification or a changed label.

The most actionable record lead is the 2003-dated release’s reference to a **visual inventory of the museum’s WTC collection**. The native inventory/export, object accession records and marking/transfer files could test the floor/tower attributions and the relationship between these descriptions. None was acquired here.

Existing scope: **STL01–STL03, AUD011/AUD016, B188/H005 and SPT01–SPT08**. This unit does not reopen the deferred Beverly Hills certification/attachments, duplicate telephone or technical investigations, or complete the original steel-custody criteria.

## Preserve the different accounts and their context

Retain **North / 71-74 / Recovery / 2003-dated release** and **South / 7–9 / Rescue / exhibition page retrieved November 3, 2026** side by side. Both floor and tower fields were directly read in the captured page bodies. No preferred field was selected and no earlier source was edited.

The strongest objection to a mislabeling interpretation is the different exhibition context: **Recovery** and **Rescue** may describe separate objects or displays. Multiple columns, a replacement/loan, a changed attribution or a descriptive error are untested possibilities. The number and identities of distinct physical pieces cannot be inferred from these descriptions alone. The sources were not joined by accession number, marking, transfer event or authenticated object photograph.

These are **unjoined descriptive differences**, not an established contradiction about a single specimen. An accession/loan/exhibit-installation crosswalk and contemporaneous marking/identification records would distinguish the possibilities. A guide explicitly identifying both objects, if obtained, would also matter; its contents have not been adopted from a search snippet or a browser wrapper.

Museum descriptions of retained structural material weaken a blanket premise that no potentially relevant steel was preserved anywhere. They do not establish present availability for sampling, an intact custody chain, representative scientific sampling, a particular heating/damage mechanism or adequate preservation of the scientific candidates. No link to **W14A/W14B, C12a/b, FEMA Sample 1, I-0137**, a NIST retained coupon or WTC 7 was found in the read passages. No material composition, fracture or temperature analysis was performed.
