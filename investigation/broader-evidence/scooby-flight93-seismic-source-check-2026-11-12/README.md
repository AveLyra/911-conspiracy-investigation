# Scooby report 4: Flight 93 seismic timing source check

November 12, 2026. Selected claims in the recovered original-media report are compared with the authors’ seismic paper, the NTSB study, Commission notes and Lamont’s later account. This is not a raw-waveform reconstruction, an independent clock calibration, or a review of the rest of the report and its attachments.

| Claim | Source check |
| --- | --- |
| The initial estimate was 10:06:05 ± 5 seconds, but the uncertainty did not assess wrong-signal selection. | The [Kim–Baum paper, pp. 3–4](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=bc0e0f6d8ae2b7c2fddc4cea0e9fe7c3672cc51e&repid=rep1&type=pdf), reports four stations 92–218 km away, signal-to-noise ratios around 1:1 at two stations, 2.5:1 at SSPA and 2:1 at MVL, and the estimate 14:06:05 ± 5 UTC. It expressly attributes the stated uncertainty only to upper-crust wave velocity. The paper claims the feature was positively identified; its stated uncertainty therefore does not independently quantify the possibility that another event produced it. The linked copy is a repository-hosted copy of the authors’ paper, not the raw station data. |
| The Commission rejected the seismic interpretation and preferred combined records. | [Commission notes to chapter 1, note 168](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-911REPORT/pdf/GPO-911REPORT-23.pdf#page=15), record the Commission’s assessment of weak signal and uncertain source identification and its reliance on combined recorder, radar, air-traffic and impact data. That is the Commission’s evaluation; this pass did not independently repeat the underlying waveform or clock alignment. |
| NTSB’s original study used radar and the recovered flight-data recorder, and gave 10:03:11. | The [February 19, 2002 NTSB Flight Path Study, pp. 1–2](https://www.ntsb.gov/about/Documents/Flight_Path_Study_UA93.pdf), names the FDR, FAA and Air Force radar as sources and states the 10:03:11 impact time. The study also mentions the cockpit-voice recording, but its summary alone does not provide a fully independent synchronization audit of every clock or the raw native recordings. |
| Lamont later described the 10:06 estimate as unreliable. | [Lamont’s 2021 retrospective](https://lamont.columbia.edu/news/morning-shook-world-seismology-911) reports that the signals used for the estimate were noisy and unreliable, and cites the earlier Commission time. This is an institutional retrospective, not a newly performed waveform analysis or, by itself, a signed retraction by each paper author. |

The authors’ earlier 10:06 claim and later contrary institutional and Commission assessments must remain distinct. The more precisely stated five-second conditional interval does not establish that the selected signal came from Flight 93. Conversely, a weakness in the seismic identification is not an independent reconstruction of the 10:03:11 alignment. Native station waveforms, instrument clocks, processing parameters and FDR/CVR/radar synchronization records are the discriminating inputs. Neither timing dispute supplies a UMS connection.
