# Scooby source checks: Sarasota, visitor card, archives and Able Danger

November 11, 2026. These are bounded checks of selected claims from reports 8, 12, 14, 20 and 23. They do not verify all claims or attachments in those reports. The locally preserved scans are evidence copies; descriptions below distinguish a contemporaneous record, a later assessment and a party's allegation.

## Sarasota — April 2002, 2011 and 2014 records (report 23)

1. **Two overlapping FBI accounts.** The [2019 district court FOIA order, PDF p. 12](https://www.floridabulldog.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Bulldog.pdf#page=12), describes an April 3, 2002 document whose last five paragraphs repeat in the April 16 document. The [released April 16 EC](https://www.floridabulldog.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/April-16-2002-FBI-report1.pdf) records alleged connections but redactions obscure identifying and underlying details. Shared paragraphs cannot be counted as independent corroboration merely because two documents exist; the order does not identify who copied whom or prove the underlying claims.
2. **A negative search, limited to available inputs.** A [government court exhibit, PDF pp. 63–64](https://www.floridabulldog.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/Government-Exhibits.pdf#page=63), contains an analyst's 2011 account of searches of domestic family numbers available to the analyst and no connection found to the hijackers. It is evidence of the analyst's stated search and result, not a carrier-certified exhaustive reconstruction. Inputs, date ranges and underlying query returns remain necessary to measure coverage.
3. **Missing search documentation in the briefing.** The [April 30, 2014 Review Commission briefing, PDF p. 3](https://www.floridabulldog.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/document.pdf#page=3), was downloaded, rendered and visually read. It explicitly says there is no actual documentation of searches and work done to rule out connections. It also says the FBI did not obtain gate records and criticizes the original EC's broad connection statement as speculative; redactions and litigation annotations obscure portions. The 2011 analyst email exists, so do not turn the briefing's undocumented-work statement into a claim that no search occurred or no search-related record exists. The briefing's statement is not the gate logs themselves or proof of their historical availability.
4. **Unresolved causal bridge.** None of these inspected passages establishes a link to UMS, Max Movers or their personnel. Original gate sheets, plate comparisons, subscriber periods and query outputs could materially change the assessment; an authenticated positive match and an adequately scoped negative search would point in opposite directions.

## WTC visitor card and Zim (report 8)

The locally preserved [FBI property receipt, section 2 PDF p. 81](https://www.themoreuknow.com/pdf/fbi%20report%20section%202.pdf#page=81), was rendered and visually read. It records a WTC visitor card in a black fanny pack and has a redacted received-from name. The handwritten WTC line includes further marking and a clipped/redacted date area; this copy alone does not reliably establish the cardholder, issuing office, date of visit, building access or reason for a visit. It is a concrete access lead, not proof of a WTC job or explosives placement. The full police-to-FBI attachment crosswalk and card image remain necessary.

The [April 3, 2001 American Shipper announcement](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/zim-moves-regional-head-office-from-new-york-to-norfolk) reported the planned September relocation from New York to Norfolk. It is contemporaneous trade reporting about the plan, not an original carrier invoice and it identifies no mover. It supports a planned relocation but cannot determine whether UMS or Max worked on any part of it. Vendor payments, Hunter's file and dock/elevator logs are the discrimination targets.

## Flight 93 interview record identifiers (report 12)

The [National Archives MFR index](https://www.archives.gov/research/9-11/commission-memoranda.html) lists Rich Belme as MFR 2610691 and Star-Fix Operator #1 as MFR 2609596, both dated November 21, 2003, with both entries marked redacted. This verifies the catalog identifiers and status shown by that index, not the content, completeness or provenance of associated original interview audio. Playback, authentication and synchronization remain open.

## Able Danger and Odigo (report 14)

The [Senate Intelligence Committee's 2007 activities report, printed pp. 14–15](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-110srpt57/pdf/CRPT-110srpt57.pdf#page=20), identifies four Able Danger allegations, reports interviews with five individuals who said they had seen an Atta chart, and describes committee staff's conclusion that Able Danger did not identify a hijacker before September 11. It also reports staff found no evidence supporting an allegation that team members were prevented from sharing relevant information with the FBI. This documents an institutional investigation and its contrary conclusion; it is not the original chart, a transcript of every witness, or an infallible proof that all records survived. Odigo is a separate chain: none of this verifies or refutes the content, recipient or time of any Odigo message.

## Bayoumi notepad (report 20)

The [plaintiffs' 2024 averment, numbered paragraphs 1889–1893](https://www.motleyrice.com/sites/default/files/documents/AT_HR/9-11/20240802%20Redacted%20public%20averment.pdf), identifies MPS/FBI copy exhibits for a notepad and airplane sketch and alleges the original notepad was returned May 2, 2002, citing a receipt. This verifies what the party filed and which exhibit it cites; the receipt itself was not inspected in this pass. A party averment is not by itself authentication of the return, date of sketch creation, operational purpose or transmission to a hijacker. Obtain the original receipt, complete pad/scan, source-image metadata and underlying examinations, and compare competing explanations before drawing an operational inference.

Read the government court exhibit, 2019 order, NARA index, Senate report and cited publicly filed party averment. Where records were not found or examined, their existence and contents remain unresolved.
