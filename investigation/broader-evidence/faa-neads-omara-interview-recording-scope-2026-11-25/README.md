# NEADS interview: recording coverage and custody leads

B129 / AUD005–006 / AUD016 · November 25, 2026. This bounded review adds a Commission interview account to the existing tape and transcript source chain. It does not test a particular recording or reconstruct a call.

## Source observations

The National Archives-hosted [October 28, 2003 NEADS field-visit memorandum](https://nara-media-001.s3.amazonaws.com/arcmedia/9-11/MFR/t-0148-911MFR-00777.pdf) records a staff interview with communications officer Pete O’Mara. Acquired November 25, 2026; three-page PDF SHA-256 `d847b8bfc208a00e8ba73ac339ea9756e82a9e4a654f9107ee3c6d52138a9a07`. Physical page 2 was visually inspected; page 1's relevant paragraph was read in the extracted text. The document is a staff memorandum of an interview, not a recording, equipment configuration, or independently verified channel map.

- Page 1 attributes to O’Mara a description that radio transmissions were recorded on a 24-track tape and that a contractor operated a Dictaphone system. The memorandum frames the contractor association as his belief, not a verified contract.
- Page 2 distinguishes some unrecorded telephones from recorded radio material. It attributes to O’Mara the belief that the Battle Cab phones were not recorded, describes two phones used to pass situation updates, and says he had no knowledge of conversations when the other parties spoke directly with the commander. The memorandum also says base personnel physically handled digital tapes. These are attributed statements about practices and events, not proof of any particular line's configuration or an item-by-item custody record.
- The [2005 DoD IG report](https://web.archive.org/web/20161222001502id_/http://www.dodig.mil/Foia/ERR/05-INTEL-13.pdf), physical page 13 / printed page 5, separately reports that key NEADS positions were recorded and describes a transcription problem. The [September 2003 NEADS response](https://www.archives.gov/files/declassification/iscap/pdf/2013-002-doc2.pdf), page 1, describes three original tapes and damage. These observations are preserved with their dates and scopes; 24 tracks, recorded positions, phone circuits and three tapes are different units.

## Interpretation and tests

If a particular conversation used an unrecorded circuit, a missing recorder channel would not alone establish that the conversation did not occur. The interview does not establish that a disputed call used such a circuit, that all telephones lacked recording, that a tape was missing, or that recording was intentionally avoided. Its account and the IG's account may address different communications; no conflict is adjudicated or erased. The witness did not claim to know the contents of all direct calls to the commander.
