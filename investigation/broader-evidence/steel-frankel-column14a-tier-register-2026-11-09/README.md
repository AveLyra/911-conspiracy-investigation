# Column 14A shop marks and the W14A field specimen

Public derivative of the November 9, 2026 Eastern source review · STL02–STL03 / AUD011 / B188 / H005

Frankel's E118 erection schedule shows **A2682 / W14 × 211** in the Column 14A strip. W14×211 is the only one of the ten readable section sizes in the two inspected 14A strips whose **modern reference dimensions** are within the earlier illustrative half-inch band on all three recorded W14A field dimensions. No exact A2682 TIFF basename matched in the indexed release mirror. Three newly checked shop sheets corroborate the schedule's marks and stock sizes for other tiers. Their drawn depth or width differs from the W14A field measurements by more than half an inch. This widens the earlier two-piece comparison without establishing the identity of the recovered steel. ## A2682 and what would identify the specimen

The earlier modern screen found W14×211 and W14×193 within half an inch in all three dimensions under both workbook conventions. E118 assigns W14×211 to A2682. W14×193 did not appear in the legible 14A callouts in these two strips. The W14×211 entry gives **15.7, 15.8, 1.56 in.** in tabulated columns and **15.75, 15.75, 1.5625 in.** in detailing columns, in depth, width and flange order. The tabulated values differ from the field record by **−0.3, +0.3, +0.3725 in.** and the detailing values by **−0.25, +0.25, +0.3750 in.** Both are outside the tighter quarter-inch screen on at least one axis. These are modern reference values. The half-inch band is a sensitivity setting. A period fabrication callout, homologous connection details and evidence of physical identification or custody would be needed to connect the specimen to this or another column. [Purdue-hosted AISC v16.0 workbook, Database v16.0 row 199](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~jhunjhu0/Steel%20Design/aisc-shapes-database-v16.0.xlsx) · [Earlier two-workbook comparison](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/commit/5317f50fed4a93b47e52f6cdd50f6223f29cbef9)

Other W14 sections, field measurement conditions and damage, and other installation locations remain possible. The label **W14A** identifies a field specimen in the FEMA table. **14A** identifies a design column in the schedule. **A2682** identifies a fabrication mark. The source set contains no documented join among all three.

## Verification and open records

The review checked five TIFF lengths, CRC-32s and SHA-256 hashes, six shop crop hashes, exact stem matches for 21 marks, both workbook hashes, 38 W14 rows and the two-dimensional shop differences. Scope was the schedule headers and Column 14A strips plus the stock line and title block for each of the three shop drawings. The workbook screen was repeated on the raw XLSX cell strings and the prior reader values. The ledger preserves **20** differing row-level readouts, including `17.399999999999999` and `17.4` for W14×605 width, and the two screens returned the same sole scheduled candidate at half an inch. Both held workbook copies gave identical cell strings in the tested columns. These comparisons used the retained FEMA and NIST transcriptions and their existing source pins.
