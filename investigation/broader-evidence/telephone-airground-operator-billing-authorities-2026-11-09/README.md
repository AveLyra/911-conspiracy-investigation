# Air-to-ground billing records and operator-service rules

Public derivative of the November 9, 2026 Eastern source review. Telephone track TEL02–TEL04 and AUD016-A1/TC05–TC06. This review identifies record series and dated source language relevant to interpreting a zero-duration field or missing charge. It does not assign a service provider, answer state, charge, or duration to a September 11 call.

## Carrier filings identified by date

In *Hardy v. Claircom Communications Group*, 86 Wn. App. 488, 493–94 (May 27, 1997), consolidated appeals 38684-1-I and 38846-1-I, the court describes two declarations. It reports that Airfone had a tariff on file since **February 5, 1992** and had maintained it continuously. It reports that AT&T Wireless filed at service commencement, its tariff was canceled effective **October 6, 1994**, and another tariff was filed **March 3, 1995**. The court describes both schedules as rounding charges up to whole minutes and affirms dismissal. Footnote 3 identifies a complaint attachment with an onboard help-screen path for billing information and separately describes revised AT&T Wireless promotional materials. The carrier declarations, tariff exhibits and advertisement remain unacquired. The two reproductions carry the same judicial account. Their agreement supplies a transcription check of the reported dates. Direct downloads of both reproductions returned HTTP 403 while indexed text was readable. [Justia reproduction, printed pp. 493–94 and n. 3](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1997/86-wash-app-488.html) · [FindLaw reproduction, indexed lines 238–239, 267–269](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/wa-court-of-appeals/1468269.html)

Preserve the court's **February 5, 1992**, **October 6, 1994**, and **March 3, 1995** observations beside the FCC record. The 1994 rule text says commercial mobile radio service providers “shall not file tariffs for interstate service to their customers” and requires cancellation for covered services. The accompanying order gives **July 18, 1994** as the latest filing date for cancellation supplements and specifies effectiveness upon five days' notice. FCC 04-287 ¶51 describes the decision as mandatory detariffing including 800 MHz air-ground licensees. The scope and relationship of these records to the court's reported filings remain unresolved. Possible differences in filing authority, service, or tariff category are untested. Obtain the actual carrier submissions, cover sheets, effective-date and cancellation stamps, and any orders or exceptions before assessing their relationship. [59 FR 18495 and 18499, PDF pp. 3 and 7](https://archives.federalregister.gov/issue_slice/1994/4/19/18493-18502.pdf) · [FCC 04-287 ¶51 and nn. 191–193](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-04-287A1.pdf#page=28)

## Separate records govern tariff filing and call charging

- **1996 statute.** Public Law 104-104 §705 added 47 U.S.C. §332(c)(8). It provides that a person engaged in commercial mobile service, in that capacity, need not provide equal access to common carriers for toll service. It separately sets a conditional route for FCC unblocked-access rules. The text addresses access. An answer-state definition or a rate schedule for the carrier must be sourced separately. [110 Stat. 153, PDF p. 99, extracted lines 3–21](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ104/pdf/PLAW-104publ104.pdf#page=99)
- **1998 FCC order.** Paragraph 72 describes air-to-ground service within the telephone operator services regime. Paragraph 81 expressly distinguishes the earlier FCC treatment of §226 informational tariffs from §203 tariffs. Paragraph 85 forbids CMRS operator-service providers from filing §226 informational tariffs and gives existing filers 90 days from Federal Register publication to cancel. The agency distinction identifies two record series to search. Which series contains the carrier filings reported in the opinion remains open. Paragraphs 88–89 affirm the underlying GTE ruling while describing limited relief covering informational tariff filing and unblocked access. The order identifies the earlier 8 FCC Rcd 6171 ruling and GTE reconsideration petition (MSD-92-14, filed September 27, 1993, p. 4 nn. 9–10), as well as AT&T's reply comments at pp. 12–13 (n. 255). The order's descriptions and the underlying filings are separate evidence. [FCC 98-134, PDF pp. 35 and 39–43](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-98-134A1.pdf#page=35)
- **2001 printed rule.** Section 64.705(a)(1) says “Not bill for unanswered telephone calls in areas where equal access is available”; §64.705(a)(2) says “Not knowingly bill for unanswered telephone calls where equal access is not available”. The visible exemption in paragraph (c) identifies “(a)(5) and (b)”. The historical print record preserves the language that a later agency discussion must be compared against. [2001 CFR Title 47, vol. 3, printed p. 249, PDF p. 259, extracted lines 49–93](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2001-title47-vol3/pdf/CFR-2001-title47-vol3.pdf#page=259)
- **2003 FCC order.** Footnote 56 says “the billing for unanswered calls provision of TOCSIA does not apply to CMRS carriers”. Paragraph 38 says “GTE’s Airfone and Railfone services must comply with TOCSIA provisions fully”. Preserve both statements and the 2001 rule text. The order cites §226(b)(1)(F–G) and §332(c)(8) for the footnote. The relationship between the general and service-specific wording, and its application to any 2001 call leg or entity, remains unresolved in this review. The order cites GTE's **August 3, 1998** comments and AT&T Wireless's **December 11, 2002** ex parte, which are additional sources to seek. [FCC 03-203, p. 9 n. 56, p. 13 ¶38 and p. 4 nn. 31, 34](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-03-203A1.pdf)

The sources distinguish rounded charges, recorded seconds, answer supervision, and an operator or carrier billing leg. Testing an actual row requires the source export and dictionary, its event or status code, applicable historical contract or tariff, the carrier and billing entity for each leg, and the linked destination/operator record. A generic billing rule leaves the meaning of a particular zero-duration field open. A later characterization cannot stand in for the call-level data.
