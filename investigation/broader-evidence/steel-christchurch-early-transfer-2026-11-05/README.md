The two **five** statements use different populations and actions: *countries gifted steel* and *places given permission to use it*. Preserve both. Their denominators, recipient lists and overlap remain unresolved. The **five girders** describe the reported contents of one gift. The May gift wording, September transit account and October unveiling refer to separately reported stages. The source record leaves exact dates for departure, landing and receipt open.

## Competing descriptions of the suspended member

The 2021 *Places Journal* account gives **“20-foot-long”** for the spear and places pre-shipment photographs of the steel at **Hangar 17**. Its notes cite Rosa Shiels’s 2020 chapter, p. 132, and a January 6, 2021 interview with Bennett. The public podcast transcript preview, at **04:31**, attributes **“sort of eight meters long”** to Bennett and describes photos requested during crane loading, requested dimensions and cardboard models made during transit. Those are approximate descriptions and may use different measurement endpoints. The preview has not been checked against audio. Retain both reported dimensions without choosing one. The loading-photo description does not identify a hangar. The article’s Hangar 17 attribution remains a lead to test against item-level dispatch and photo records. The artist’s accounts and an article relying in part on his interview do not provide independent engineering identifications. [Article, opening paragraphs and notes 1–2](https://placesjournal.org/article/relic-steel-making-9-11-memorials/?cn-reloaded=1) · [Transcript preview, 04:31 and 07:16](https://podcastrex.com/shows/nights/how-steel-from-the-world-trade-centre-made-its-way-to-christchurch/transcript)

The Christchurch chronology predates or overlaps the **October 24, 2002** Port Authority vote, which authorized a particular preservation program and described collection and cataloging already underway. The chronology supports looking in both New York City and Port Authority files. The date of that vote alone cannot determine where the Christchurch steel was held, who released it, whether this was an exception to any earlier practice, or which later conditions covered it. The reviewed sources also give no member-level link to WTC 7 or a NIST specimen. Use the freight sponsor and consignee in the municipal account to narrow the search. Match the piece count, weights and member identifiers across shipment and custody stages.
