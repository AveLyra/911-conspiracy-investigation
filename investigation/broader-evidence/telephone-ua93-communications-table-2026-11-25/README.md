## Accounts preserved

Page 6 attributes an emergency-call start of **9:57** to a county public-safety source, and says the call was taped and a dispatcher had notes. The [National Park Service chronology](https://www.nps.gov/flni/learn/historyculture/phone-calls-from-flight-93.htm) places the call under **9:58** and states it was connected for **70 seconds**. These descriptions are retained as printed; neither source here establishes clock calibration, precise boundaries or a direct join to native dispatch/switch logs.

The table cites FBI interview **265A-NY280350-302-17823** for a report of an explosion and white smoke. In a separate note, its unidentified compiler states that the transcript they reviewed did not say those things. The table's to-do column asks for the call's time and duration, dispatcher's notes, the tape, and investigation of the difference and any portions not recorded. Those are **questions recorded in a working table**, not proof they were answered or that a recording was incomplete.

The [previously examined FBI-released interview and transcript packet](https://archive.org/download/911datasets/NARA_9-11_Commission_Records_Released_Jan_14_2009.zip/NARA_9-11_Commission_Records_Released_Jan_14_2009/911%20NARA/Team%207%20Files/Team%207%20Box%2012/T7%20B12%20DOJ%20Doc%20Req%2035-13%20Packet%204%20Fdr-%20Entire%20Contents-%20Response%20Letters-%20Reports%20403.pdf#page=44) records an interviewee's account and, separately, a transcript with unintelligible portions; the prior comparison found no intelligible explosion or smoke words in the inspected transcript. The table's summaries may derive from these same records and are not independent confirmation. No original recording was authenticated or heard in this step. A transcript omission does not establish the audio's complete contents; the interview account does not establish that an explosion occurred. Neither discrepancy establishes editing, intent or a cause of the crash.
