# Scooby claim checks: warnings, the CIA hold and interrogation dependencies

November 11, 2026. These are additional **claim-level checks**, not a completed audit of the 30 reports. The distinction between a historical warning, the source's reliability, actual receipt, and what a particular person knew is material throughout.

## Warning products — report 1

The [CIA's release catalog](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/cia-releases-presidents-daily-briefs-in-commemoration-of-the-25th-anniversary-of-9-11/seventy-one-declassified-presidents-daily-brief-products/) was inspected and its publication-date entries counted: 71. The catalog describes a 101-page combined download; that is the agency's stated collection size, not a page-by-page audit of the combined PDF. Four individual products were downloaded, rendered, OCR-screened and the specified pages visually reviewed:

- [September 10, 1998](https://www.cia.gov/static/09-10-1998-Bin-Ladins-Location-and-Intentions.pdf), pp. 1–2: CIA said intelligence did not conclusively establish bin Laden's future intentions, and included a reported possibility of an explosives-packed aircraft being flown into a U.S. city. The source identifying information is redacted in the visible passage. This demonstrates that the product contained the warning and qualification; it does not establish that a named official read or believed it, or that it identified the 2001 operation.
- [December 4, 1998](https://www.cia.gov/static/12-04-1998-Bin-Ladin-Preparing-To-Hijack-US-Aircraft-and-Other-Attacks.pdf), p. 1: the product discussed reporting of possible U.S. hijacking to seek release of prisoners, alongside other reported aviation threats. This is a different reported scenario from the September 10 aircraft warning, not proof of an identified 2001 attack.
- [July 24, 2001](https://www.cia.gov/static/07-24-2001-One-Bin-Ladin-Operation-Delayed-Others-Continue.pdf), p. 1: it reported an imminent operation had been postponed while preparations for other attacks might continue, and described reporting concerning targets including Israel. Redactions and lack of a specific date/target make attribution to a particular later attack an unsupported inference.
- [August 6, 2001](https://www.cia.gov/static/08-06-2001-Bin-Ladin-Determined-To-Strike-in-US.pdf), pp. 1–2: the product discussed U.S. attack intent, suspicious activity potentially consistent with hijackings or other attacks, about 70 FBI investigations, and a reported embassy call alleging explosives planning. It expressly said some 1998 hijacking reporting was uncorroborated. The document is evidence of a warning presented in a presidential brief; this inspection does not prove a particular reader's recollection or establish identification of the 19 hijackers, flight numbers or September 11 date.

The remaining 67 products, claimed product-by-product classifications, 22%/82% overlap calculation, recipient records and full source-chain evidence were not independently audited in this pass. The four checked products are not a complete reconstruction of all warnings or responses.

## CIA draft CIR and later preservation — report 7

The [CIA inspector general's 2005 report, printed p. 53 and footnote 13](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/DOC_0006184107.pdf), says an FBI detailee drafted a January 5, 2000 CIR, named FBI headquarters and New York as intended recipients, and received a hold instruction. It reports that the drafter sent a copy to the CIA deputy on January 13 asking whether to revise it, and that the team found no reply or formal dissemination. The OIG says a February instruction asked that this draft be retained, and that it obtained the January 13 Lotus Note version in January 2004. Its footnote says it did **not** know when the particular draft was deleted; CTC deleted its DUCS database in late 2002 during system decommissioning.

These are findings of an oversight investigation based on available records, not native system audit logs examined here. Deleting a database after decommissioning is documented; deliberate destruction of this specific draft to conceal the hold is not established. The OIG account also does not determine why the hold was imposed or exclude an unrecorded transmission outside the formal channel. The original cable, email attachments, audit trail and recipient records remain discriminating evidence.

## Moussaoui information routing — report 19

The [DOJ OIG report, chapter 4](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter4.htm) says the Minneapolis 26-page communication was read by headquarters personnel Martin and Robin. It describes August 22 oral consultations with lawyer “Howard” and says neither Jack nor Martin provided him the 26-page EC; it also reports later oral consultations with other lawyers. The OIG criticized the incompleteness of the briefings and dissemination and recorded differing witness recollections. This supports a distinction between an agency's possession of a document and a particular decision-maker's actual review. It does not itself prove an intentional attack-facilitation plan, nor does the OIG reconstruction replace original routing and attachment records.

## Detainee-reporting dependency — report 22

The [Senate Intelligence Committee study, printed pp. 75–81](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/press/executive-summary_0.pdf), describes coercive CIA interrogation of Ramzi bin al-Shibh and quotes a later CIA assessment that much of his September 11 reporting was speculative or not independently verifiable. The same Senate text also records that some CIA officers considered information supplied in prior foreign custody generally accurate and that CIA assessed some earlier reporting useful. These are assessments of particular reporting and periods: neither the critical assessment nor the contrasting assessment automatically validates or falsifies every statement. Do not treat multiple derivative summaries of the same source as independent corroboration; compare the original interrogation reports with independent travel, financial and communication evidence for each proposition.

## Method and remaining work

Counted catalog entries; inspected four CIA products and the CIA/DOJ inspector-general and Senate texts. Used PyMuPDF rendering and Tesseract for the scanned CIA products and visually checked their relevant pages. A locally saved, machine-segmented register contains **735 candidate statements from the visible research-card summaries** across 30 reports. Segmentation can split or merge claims, includes nonclaims and does not capture all tables or attachments; it is a work queue, not a verified or exhaustive claim count. It remains private. The source-to-claim audit, original attachments, individual warning corpus and disputed calculations remain incomplete.
