# Recovered warning-corpus claims: June–August 2001 source checks

November 12, 2026. This checks selected claims and four entries in the recovered table of Scooby report 1 against individual CIA-released documents and the 9/11 Commission. It does not verify all 71 products, the report's complete table or its attachments. Each CIA warning is reporting and analysis as presented to the recipient; it is not proof that every source claim was accurate.

The [9/11 Commission report, chapter 8](https://www.9-11commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch8.htm), reports that the President had asked about U.S.-directed threats and describes his subsequent account of the August 6 briefing. This is evidence of presidential receipt and the Commission's account of recollection; it is not a verbatim contemporaneous transcript or proof that a particular operational response followed. The Commission also says the following day's more broadly disseminated SEIB omitted several details in the PDB. A warning in one product cannot automatically be attributed to every government decision-maker.

The September 10, 1998 item previously [visually checked](../scooby-warning-routing-dependency-checks-2026-11-11/README.md) reports the possibility of an explosives-packed aircraft being flown into a U.S. city, in a larger uncertain warning context. This is evidence of a warning concept years before the attack, not a demonstrated identification of the 2001 plan. The research report's proposed 22%/82% dissemination comparison, the rest of its product-by-product table, and any underlying distribution/receipt records were not independently recalculated in this pass.

## Commission review: overlap counts and release inventory

The [Commission PDB Review Team memorandum, February 9, 2004](https://www.archives.gov/files/declassification/iscap/pdf/2022-008-document-release-508.pdf), printed page 1, provides the underlying counts for the recovered report’s percentages. Its team counted 353 relevant articles from 1998 through September 20, 2001, with 362 an alternative count depending on treatment of sets. For 1998–2000, 55 of 255 selected PDB articles overlapped wholly or substantially with 188 selected NID/SEIB articles: 55/255 = 21.57%, rounded to 22%. For January 20–September 20, 2001, the memorandum separately states that **before September 11**, 41 of 50 selected PDB articles overlapped with 142 selected SEIB pieces: 41/50 = 82%. The 48 post-attack articles described on the following page are not the denominator of that pre-attack calculation.

The search covered articles touching al-Qaeda, bin Laden, Afghanistan, or terrorism issues involving Pakistan, Saudi Arabia or Sudan. The percentages describe article-content overlap in that selected corpus; they do not measure distribution to, receipt by, or actual reading by any named official, or cover all intelligence products. The review team’s classifications and counts are the source for this calculation; individual matching PDB and SEIB articles and routing receipts have not been independently reconstructed here. A change in article overlap alone does not establish intentional suppression or coordination.

The [National Archives release index](https://www.archives.gov/declassification/iscap/pdf/2026-201) lists **11** Commission interview memoranda released under 2026-201: Berger (1), Clarke (3), Rice (1), Scheuer (3), and Tenet (3). This checks the inventory count, not every document’s content or the completeness of all Commission interviews. The memo itself summarizes restricted underlying briefing articles; its 24-item core group and its broader discussion are not substitutes for the originals.
