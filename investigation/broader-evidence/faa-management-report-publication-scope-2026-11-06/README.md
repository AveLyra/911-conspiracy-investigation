# DOT management report identifiers and annual reporting scope

November 6, 2026 · B129 / PD01–PD05 · Source acquisition and publication comparison

Two House referral entries for the DOT Secretary's audit follow-up reports cite **31 U.S.C. 9106**. The provision concerns annual reports of government corporations. The reports named by the two referrals cover six-month periods and use a title about OIG audit recommendations. I acquired the 2006 and 2007 OMB circulars and checked the citation against the reporting categories they distinguish. The issuing office's own citation and the exact enclosures are still needed. ## Annual report check

The 2000 Reports Consolidation Act allows specified reports to be combined, subject to the stated conditions. It also requires an Inspector General management-challenges statement in the consolidated report. That statement has a distinct purpose from a report on final actions. The 2006 and 2007 OMB editions describe the consolidated report route in their introductions. This gives a reason to check the annual publications separately from the House referrals. [Pub. L. 106-531 §3, PDF pp. 1–2](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-106publ531/pdf/PLAW-106publ531.pdf#page=1) · [2006 circular, PDF p. 17](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/default/files/omb/assets/omb/circulars/a136/a136_revised_2006.pdf#page=17) · [2007 circular, PDF p. 16](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/default/files/omb/assets/omb/circulars/a136/a136_revised_2007.pdf#page=16)

I reviewed the contents lists in the published FY 2006 and FY 2007 DOT annual reports and ran indexed exact-phrase searches for **CC-2006-085**, **Audit Resolution Reports** and **Final Actions**. The contents and scoped searches yielded no identified copy of the Secretary's target semiannual reports. Both annual editions contain separate Inspector General material about management challenges. The annual searches used the online indexed text because direct downloads returned HTTP 403. Text indexing can miss a scan, alternate title, or attachment. These checks leave the contents of the congressional enclosures and the FAA's case-specific disposition open.
