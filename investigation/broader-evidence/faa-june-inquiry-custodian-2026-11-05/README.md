# FAA production accounts and the June inquiry record trail

November 5, 2026 · PD01 / PD07. This review follows the June 13, 2003 inquiry indexed at item 26 of the [Eastern Region chronology inventory](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/106eeae1f139b9875c43a2063b332ad8251cbe63/investigation/broader-evidence/faa-chronology-transmittal-2026-11-05/README.md). It pins the contemporary FAA and Commission production accounts, the subpoena itself, and a separate internal fax route.

## What each record establishes

| Record | Observation and limit |
| --- | --- |
| **July 9, 2003 DOT reply, AWA-818** | Responds to the Commission's **July 2** transmission of **Document Request No. 2**. DOT commits to rolling production and identifies certain material it regards as TSA's responsibility, with FAA retaining part of the response for certain items. This is a dated routing and scope record. Its stated request date and number leave its relationship to the June 13 inquiry and the May 22 questions for the record unverified. [NARA 7602308, p. 1](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7602308) |
| **October 15, 2003 Commission statement** | Gives **May 7** as the date of its request for FAA aircraft-tracking and NORAD records. It says it had been assured of complete FAA production as of **early September**, then learned through field interviews that material was missing, including tapes, statements, interview reports and agency self-assessments. It reports receiving dozens of boxes, with staff review incomplete, and says the delay impeded its work. [Archived Commission statement, p. 1](https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/911/press/pr_2003-10-15.pdf) |
| **October 16, 2003 subpoena, AWA-810** | Attachment A seeks aircraft-tracking and NORAD records, draft and final evaluations, specified FAA interview records and logs, tapes and transcripts of FAA–NORAD and internal FAA communications. Its definition includes email, computer files, drafts and non-identical copies. The requested scope does not establish which items were delivered. [NARA 7602301, pp. 1 and 3](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7602301) |
| **FAA response, AWA-754** | The response acknowledges gaps in the initial production and says a **majority** of the information had been delivered by the time the subpoena was issued. It reports **over 6,000 documents** during the summer, **over 70 boxes and packages** over the preceding two weeks, and **approximately 300 voice tapes** over the preceding week, including some already provided. It gives **July 18** for the earlier NTSB/FBI-related packages and says the balance of the tapes was expected the following week. It also reports that Commission staff asked it to concentrate on specific lines. Relative time wording is preserved. The reviewed text supplied no absolute date for the response itself. [NARA 7602277, pp. 1–2](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7602277) |

The FAA's description of the quantity delivered, the Commission's account of omissions and delay, and the subpoena's scope are separate observations. Counts of boxes, documents and tapes have different units, and the FAA says some tapes were repeated. The reported staff focus on certain lines leaves the formal request and any later instructions to be checked separately. The Commission's later FAQ says the subpoena issues were resolved without litigation and that it received access necessary for its mandate. That statement provides no itemized receipt or version history. [Commission FAQ, subpoena question](https://www.9-11commission.gov/about/faq.htm)
