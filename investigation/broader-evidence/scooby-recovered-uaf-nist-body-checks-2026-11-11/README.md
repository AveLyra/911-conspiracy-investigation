# Recovered UAF and NIST report-body claims: source checks

11 November 2026. This review checks additional material claims from the saved rendered bodies and tables of Scooby reports 24 and 27. It is not an exhaustive audit of either report, their attachments or all 30 reports. The captured bodies are preserved privately; the public record below identifies the source claims without republishing private project conversations.

## UAF: prescribed failure and a time-dependent function

The authors' [March 2020 report](https://www.novarepublika.cz/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/A-Structural-Reevaluation-of-the-Collapse-of-World-Trade-Center-7-March-2020.pdf) (third-party-hosted copy; [UAF project page](https://ine.uaf.edu/wtc7) links the final report and archive) provides several checks:

| Recovered claim | Source check and limits |
|---|---|
| A global scenario failed core columns across eight stories, then exterior columns 1.3 seconds later. | **Supported as the authors' model description:** report printed pp. 93–94 and 105 identify the prescribed scenario and Figure 4.17. A prescribed failure pattern is a modeling condition, not evidence identifying a physical cause or a person. |
| A time-dependent acceleration function was used to account for impact resistance. | **Supported as report prose:** printed p. 106 says this and reports a close match to a selected motion analysis. It does not disclose enough in that passage to establish the function's numerical values, units, physical derivation, assignment, or actual contribution to the trajectory. Its mere presence does not show deliberate manipulation or establish that the result was fitted to the target. |
| A no-imposed-function run would test the contribution of that function. | **Proposed experiment, not a result:** it must hold the identified model, mass, connections, boundary conditions and failure scenario fixed; document damping; and compare the same output/observation under a preregistered protocol. Whether it converges or matches the observations is unknown. |
| Penthouse and global-collapse cases can be identified with numbered archive runs 37 or 49. | **Not established:** the report and a video or file label do not alone join archive revision, active input, solver output and the published figure. The full archive, native cases and figure-to-run manifest were not examined here. A reported external review is a lead, not an author-certified mapping. |
| UAF's penthouse model indicated upper-story failures with limited exterior movement. | **Supported as the authors' interpretation:** report pp. 97–98 describe their simulated scenarios and resulting interpretation. Its accuracy depends on the model and assumptions, and no independent rerun has been performed here. |

## NIST: calibration, instability timing and a corrective qualification

In [NCSTAR 1-6, volume 2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-6v2.pdf), NIST states at printed p. 226 that exterior-wall models were used to estimate inward pull magnitudes and locations to reproduce observed bowing. Consequently, matching those *same* bowing observations is a calibration check rather than independent validation. That does not itself show the modeled mechanism is false. At p. 245, the WTC 1 Case B comparisons report 14.5 inches at 4 kip and 42.8 inches at 5 kip after 100 minutes. Their ratio is about 2.95; the comparison is model sensitivity under those settings, not a measured force in the building.

For WTC 2, the report says the modeled east wall became unstable at about **43 minutes** and gives the observed collapse onset at about **56 minutes** after impact (printed pp. 263–265 and 296–297). The approximately 13-minute difference is a numerical mismatch between that modeled scenario and the observed event; possible error sources include uncertain damage, fire histories, force assumptions and simplifications. It is not, without a documented blinded analysis, automatically an independent out-of-sample validation test. Nor does the mismatch alone rule out an impact-and-fire explanation or establish explosives.

A qualification to the recovered Scooby table: NIST did present a **modeled southeast tilt and numerical displacements**, including vertical and lateral displacements in the 43-minute case, and Figures 8-37 and 8-45 depict model tilt. The fact that a particular summary comparison does not report a tilt angle is not evidence that NIST supplied no tilt prediction at all. Whether the tilt direction or specific measurements were used in input selection must be traced before calling them held-out. Comparison against measured angle/time histories and sensitivity to inputs remains open.

## Boundaries and remaining tests

- Retrieve the exact UAF model, active acceleration function, assignments, logs, outputs, and figure crosswalk; check units and compare a controlled baseline without assuming its result.
- Trace NIST's choices and photographs used for adjustment, including disconnections and timing, then compare independently specified observations with the matching model outputs. Retain the 43-versus-56-minute mismatch and the fit of deformation as distinct findings.
- Neither review identifies Urban Moving Systems, an explosives installation, or a coordinated operation. Support for a structural mechanism and attribution of an actor are separate questions.
