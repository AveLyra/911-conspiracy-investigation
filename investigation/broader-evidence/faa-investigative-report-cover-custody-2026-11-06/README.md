# FAA investigative report covers and custody by order revision

November 6, 2026 UTC · B129 / PD02–PD04. FAA orders identify the front cover and its reverse as a place to look for report review and disclosure instructions. The agency's forms index supplies separate front- and back-cover identifiers. Two later order revisions give different instructions for custody of the original report and handling of recipient copies. This adds records to seek alongside the existing [FAA investigative-directive lineage study](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/e993bc5647918f6bb014f10d0648b232e42bae6c/investigation/broader-evidence/faa-investigative-directive-lineage-2026-11-05/README.md) and the [DOT OIG report, recommendations 1–2](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cc2006085.pdf#page=12).

## What this adds

The cover record, evidence of the report's return or transfer, a notice to the investigating office, and the signed decision are separate targets. The **2010 return-to-SSE** and **2022 recipient-copy destruction** instructions belong to their respective versions. These passages leave open how legacy paper originals and recipient copies were handled during any transition. They provide no case-specific evidence of destruction, transfer, retention or a hold.

The first located FAA provisions above postdate **CC-2006-085 / 042R0122001**. They describe FAA investigative reports. The 2006 document at issue is a **DOT OIG** report. DOT 8000.1C separately describes disclosure records on the reverse of OIG report covers. Establish first whether FAA opened its own investigation or generated an ROI. The existence of either blank FAA form would supply no proof of an FAA report in this case.
