November 8, 2026 · **B129 / PD01–PD04**. A newly acquired archival email copy attributed to Dan Marcus reports a referral to both the Defense and Transportation inspectors general and proposes avoiding public confirmation. It adds an underlying source to the circulation/decision trail. It does not supply the actual referral package, a delivery acknowledgment or the later FAA correction and administrative outcome. ## What the single page records

The displayed header gives **Monday, August 2, 2004, 10:36 AM**, from Marcus to Commissioners. The opening says he sent a letter to **Joseph Schmitz at DoD and Kenneth Mead at DOT** on **“last Thursday”**, enclosed earlier official testimony and Commission findings, and called both recipients. It describes the inconsistency between the Commission's findings and earlier NORAD/FAA accounts as the reason for referral. **Page 1, 1200-pixel OCR lines 5–13**, checked against the complete image.

Marcus says he told the recipients the Commission did not intend to publicize the referral. He then attributes possible press attention to Senator Dayton's questions at the preceding Friday's hearing, while qualifying his belief about Dayton's knowledge. The last paragraph proposes directing questions to the published report and Staff Statement 17 and declining further comment. Marcus says he sees no legal obstacle to announcing the referral but prefers avoiding confirmation. **Page 1, OCR lines 13–28.** These are his recorded assertions and recommendation; the page does not establish what recipients knew, whether commissioners followed the advice, or whether an actual public answer was false.

This is evidence of an expressed public-response preference alongside a reported investigative referral. It is contrary evidence for a hypothesis that no referral was made, without independently proving dispatch or receipt. It does not establish a common motive, unlawful concealment or the outcome of either investigation. The reference to published findings also matters: the proposed nonconfirmation concerns the referral, while the underlying discrepancy is described as already covered in published material.
