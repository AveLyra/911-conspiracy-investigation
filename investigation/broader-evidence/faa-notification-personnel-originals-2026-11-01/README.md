# FAA notification form and personnel statements: what was recorded, and what remains unverified

November 1, 2026. Supplement to **B129 / PD01–PD04 / FAA01–FAA04**, not a replacement claim register or a completed correction/disposition inquiry.

The held official accident packet contains a **military-authority notification entry at 1239**, alongside personnel accounts that identify concrete source-matching targets. It also contains uncertainty, relayed information, differing call-sign readings and repeated narrative material. These records help test who reported knowing what; they do not establish the call's contents, a calibrated notification timeline, deliberate deception or an executed intercept.

## 1. A recorded notification is not a content-authenticated call

On **physical192**, the military-authority row has **1239** in the time column and **Huntress ID** in the recipient field. The Washington Operations Center row also records **1239**, with **Via ROC** in its routing field. Several other rows contain **N/A**. Preserve these entries individually: N/A in one row does not erase a recorded entry in another.

The military row cites **FAA Order8020.11, paragraph66**; the form footer identifies **8020-3 (1-86)** and **Updated June13,2001**. The date field reads **9-11-01**. Those are different fields, not an authenticated time of form creation or a demonstrated policy violation. This page's time column does not itself specify a time zone. No UTC conversion, elapsed delay or first-ever military-notification time is computed here. [Form, physical192](https://catalog.archives.gov/medialz/dc-metro/rg-237/7419198-9-11-faa/4-UAL-175/e-Other/4-AEA-1130-COMPLETED-ACCID-PCKG--FORMAL-ACCID-PCKG-ZNY-ARTCC-148-UAL175-REDACT-SEALED.pdf#page=192).

The surrounding UAL175 packet contains explicit **AAL11** accounts. Filing under UAL175 does not establish that the1239 entry conveyed knowledge of UAL175's hijacking, rather than AAL11 or another content scope. The entry qualifies a blanket claim that no military notification was recorded. It does not prove completed receipt, who actually heard the call, what was said, or a resulting operational action. The source recording, counterpart log, completion/receipt information and record-preparation history are the next discriminating inputs.

## 2. The later Huntress exchanges remain separate records

The [previously published OMIC review](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/dcdc9f647130139c60128ae3c131d40955536b34/investigation/broader-evidence/faa-omic-body-completion-2026-10-31/README.md) pins actual Huntress-labelled dialogue at physical164–169, printed17–22. Its **1326** and **1329** exchanges discuss qualified, changing reports about American11. Both the new form entry and those later transcript labels remain preserved.

They are not automatically one contradictory call or one continuous knowledge state. Different events, content, channels and record conventions may be involved; that possibility remains untested. Conversely, the form cannot be used to explain away the later disagreement. A native source-to-call join is required before calculating delay, attributing a false statement or deciding that these reports conflict about the same event. This unit read191–206; it references the earlier verified reading of164–169 rather than claiming a new independent reading or witness.

## 3. Primary-target labels and visual reports have distinct origins

The R42 statement block on **194–196 and198** says another controller pointed out a primary target attributed through Boston to AAL11. It describes starting a track using **AAL11A** to follow the target. A tracking label assigned in that account is not, by itself, an independent aircraft identification.

On195, a reported visual observation associated with **USA583** suggests **FL290**; a later passage says the aircraft was not then seen, including on TCAS. On196, the writer reports another aircraft not seeing the primary target, then recounts loss of radio contact with UAL175, a changed code, and a later Delta-pilot report of United colors and **B767200**. On198, the account describes observing a descent from **28,400 to2,200 feet**, the latter being its last observed target/altitude. These are statements about display and relayed observations, not an independently decoded trajectory, authenticated aircraft identity or measured impact altitude. The distinctions matter in both directions. A failed visual search does not prove the aircraft was absent; a later identification report does not retrospectively make every earlier primary label certain. Underlying position recordings, pilot reports, radar/track data and their source/time associations are required. This does not perform or duplicate the separate recorder-decoding work.

## 4. Preserve the Delta call signs without choosing one
