# Ground Zero cleanup monitors: contemporaneous firm assignments

**Status:** public-source text review and records lead, not recovery of monitoring records. Steel preservation track AUD011/AUD016, B188/H005. Review date: 2026-11-25.

## Source and scope

The NYC Department of Investigation (DOI), [2002–2003 annual report, printed p. 12](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doi/downloads/pdf/2002-2003_doi_annual_report.pdf#page=14), describes the World Trade Center Independent Project Integrity Compliance Monitor Program. The web research service supplied an extracted text rendition of lines 692–716 and identifies this passage as printed page 12 (PDF page 14). The direct download had returned HTTP 403 during the preceding access check; **the PDF bytes have not been acquired or hashed**, and the page image was not visually verified in this review. The source link and textual rendition are preserved as locators, not a claim of native-file authentication. The [earlier hearing review](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/942a18051480bb6ad2b3095b55e7078c18bc1181/investigation/broader-evidence/steel-doi-ddc-monitor-reports-2026-11-25/README.md) records its own source and limitations.

## What DOI reported

DOI reports that in the first week of October 2001 it appointed four monitors, one for each quadrant, and identifies these monitor–construction manager pairings (lines 702–705):

| Monitor named in DOI report | Construction manager named alongside it |
| --- | --- |
| Getnick & Getnick | Turner Construction |
| Decision Strategies/Fairfax Int’l | Tully Construction |
| Thacher Associates | Bovis Lend Lease |
| Stier, Anderson & Malone | AMEC |

The report describes round-the-clock monitoring, an integrity hotline, review of contractor wages and invoices, and work with the city's Department of Design & Construction (DDC) and KPMG (lines 706–716). These are DOI's reported program descriptions; they do not independently establish that every load, specimen, or transaction was individually monitored. The report does not identify the specific quadrant or building origin of each piece of material. The firm–manager assignments supply distinct names to use when locating the monitoring agreements, reports, supporting records and DDC transmittals; they do not prove which records survive or who currently holds them.

## Preserved earlier observations and limits

The report states that there were **allegations** of stolen steel and debris in the two weeks after the attacks (lines 697–701); this is not itself a finding of theft, an account of a particular load, or evidence of concealed building-failure evidence. The [earlier diverted-steel comparison](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/f50419a604fb6fa6636f1bebd31802486d98ede2/investigation/broader-evidence/steel-diversion-contemporary-quantity-2026-11-25/README.md) preserves contemporaneous descriptions of **255 tons**, **250 tonnes**, and the later **few loads** formulation. They remain separate accounts with unresolved scope and units; this annual-report passage provides no basis to equate or reconcile them. The DOI program began in October according to this account, so its stated monitoring cannot on its own establish the handling of the earlier two-week period. Nor can absence of a report establish destruction, improper diversion, conspiracy or motive.
