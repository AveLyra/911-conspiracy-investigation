## Source and observations

The [National Archives-released Commission interview memorandum](https://www.archives.gov/files/declassification/iscap/pdf/2012-042-doc29.pdf), nine PDF pages, identifies a March 1, 2004 interview of NORAD commander Ralph Eberhart, prepared by Commission staff. Acquired November 25, 2026; SHA-256 `7aaa3a25f5a800e70cb6851a3b70172480c8b14551308c64aa39e1838343a445`. Physical and printed page 8 was visually inspected; the nine-page source is retained intact, including its original historical markings and any redactions.

- The memorandum reports that, after staff presented research findings, Eberhart said “no one has ever discussed that with me before” concerning differences between the official NORAD timeline and staff findings.
- A bracketed staff note says further interviews indicated Eberhart had been briefed before Commission interviews using the timeline delivered to staff on February 23, 2004; it says he denied having been exposed to that timeline. The original wording appears in the source's page 8 and is preserved in the acquired PDF.
- The same page records Eberhart's statement that the newer timeline was likely the result of his standing order to correct the record when possible, and his statement that he had not created the timeline presented in May 2003. The staff memorandum also reports his review of a prepared statement and hearing transcript.

These are attributed interview remarks and a staff note about other interviews, not sworn testimony or independently authenticated recordings. The memo's bracketed note does not date the further interviews or provide their individual sources on this page. The reported statements address related but not necessarily identical propositions: exposure to a specific February timeline and prior discussion of differences with staff findings. Preserve both the staff's reported inconsistency and the interviewee's accounts without choosing a controlling value or inferring intent.

## Tests and limitations

The memo is evidence of what its author recorded; it does not independently establish what briefing occurred, the precise content seen by the interviewee, whether he knew any particular entry was inaccurate, or whether any correction was delivered. Its statements about a standing order and a May 2003 prepared statement are not independent proof that the order was issued or applied. The [2006 DoD IG memorandum](https://web.archive.org/web/20161222015721id_/http://www.dodig.mil/Ir/reports/06-intel-12.pdf), page 3, reports a later March letter and no evidence of knowing deception; that remains a separate source account with its own scope and limits.
