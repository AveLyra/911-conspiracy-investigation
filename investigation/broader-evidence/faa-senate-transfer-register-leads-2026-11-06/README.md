# Senate committee accession reports and transfer totals

November 6, 2026 UTC · B129 / PD03–PD05. Contemporary National Archives records identify **accession reports for individual Senate committees** used during the budget process. They also describe further transfers involving the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs during the period when three DOT management reports were referred to it. This narrows the recipient-side search to the committee's budget-cycle accession reports and underlying transfer lists. The report enclosures and their case coverage remain open. [September 2008 advisory minutes, p. 7](https://www.archives.gov/files/legislative/cla/advisory-committee/minutes/2008-8-9.pdf#page=7) · [April 2009 advisory minutes, pp. 22–24](https://www.archives.gov/files/legislative/cla/advisory-committee/minutes/2009-27-04.pdf#page=22)

## Locators for the underlying records

- **Budget-cycle accession reports.** The Fourth Report says Rules and Administration asked each committee to report its 2004 archiving activities in its 2005 appropriations request. It describes separate reports prepared by the Senate Archivist with the Center for Legislative Archives that detail **1998–2004 accessions** and says the plan was to repeat the requirement each budget cycle. In September 2008 the Senate Archivist described the Center's committee-by-committee reports as used by the Senate Rules Committee during budgeting. The earlier account establishes the report type and an example period. The 2008 account supplies the contemporaneous usage and recipient. The actual budget-cycle reports covering 2007–08 were unacquired. [Fourth Report, GovInfo PDF p. 14, printed p. 4](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CDOC-109hdoc156/pdf/CDOC-109hdoc156.pdf#page=14) · [September 2008 minutes, p. 7](https://www.archives.gov/files/legislative/cla/advisory-committee/minutes/2008-8-9.pdf#page=7)
- **Further transfers.** The April 2009 minutes record additional electronic transfers from the Homeland Security committee and other Senate committees before the discussion of holdings at August 2008. They also report that Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs led in frequency and number of transfers. This is a dated lead to the transfer lists. The minutes leave the individual accessions, media, dates covered and file contents open. [April 2009 minutes, pp. 22–24](https://www.archives.gov/files/legislative/cla/advisory-committee/minutes/2009-27-04.pdf#page=22)
- **Referral population.** S. Rept. **111–360** bears a 111th Congress printing header and covers the **110th Congress** committee's activities. It reports **1,172** official communications, including **1,150** executive communications and **22** petitions or memorials. The categories add to the stated total. This is a referral count and leaves custody and completeness open. The three DOT executive communications retain their item-level anchors in the committee's calendar. [Activity report cover and section VI](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-111srpt360/pdf/CRPT-111srpt360.pdf#page=55) · [Committee calendar, PDF pp. 161, 172 and 183](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CPRT-110SPRT46628/pdf/CPRT-110SPRT46628.pdf#page=172)

The overlapping 2006 descriptions attach **73 GB** and **about 1.46 GB** to differently described populations. Their exact relationship is unresolved. Different inclusion rules or snapshot definitions are possible. The source presentations, tabulations and itemized accession lists could test that. No volume is assigned to the DOT reports, and the two appearances of 450 are retained in their separate contexts.
