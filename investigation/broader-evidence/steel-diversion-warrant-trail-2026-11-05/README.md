# Ground Zero steel diversion investigation and warrant records


November 5, 2026. This STL01 follow-up examines a specific reported route from Ground Zero to scrap yards and back to Fresh Kills. The earlier load review identified manifests, GPS records and field logs in testimony. The additional prepared statement identifies a **warrant, inventory and municipal return-haul chain**. It supplies no individual load identifier. [Prior load-record review](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/699ac9c0a038b6bd4ed0515ac2898f2676af8712/investigation/broader-evidence/steel-fresh-kills-screening-and-load-records-2026-11-05/README.md)

## What the records say

- **Recovery and return.** Thomas McCormack, chair of the New York City Business Integrity Commission (BIC), describes its predecessor Trade Waste Commission (TWC) working with NYPD, the New Jersey attorney general's office and NYC DOI on a scrap-steel investigation. His July 13, 2006 prepared statement says investigators located “a few loads” in scrap yards in New Jersey and Long Island. It reports working with the New York County DA's Rackets Bureau to obtain search warrants, inventory and reclaim the steel, then arranging for DSNY trucks to return recovered material to Fresh Kills. It gives no yard names, dates, weights, return numbers or truck IDs. [House hearing 109-76, McCormack prepared statement, preserved HTML lines 12288–12303 and 12408–12435](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-109hhrg35501/html/CHRG-109hhrg35501.htm)
- **Different accounts of prosecution.** McCormack says investigators handed evidence to the DA and qualifies the outcome with “My understanding is that this Grand Jury returned no indictment.” DOI's prepared statement elsewhere in the same hearing reports indictments of **two principals of a Yonkers carting firm** over allegedly false statements in City filings. The reports concern different described conduct. The hearing provides no docket or warrant identifiers linking the two accounts. Preserve both outcomes and their attribution, with the relationship open. [House hearing 109-76, preserved HTML lines 12432–12435 and 4072–4078](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-109hhrg35501/html/CHRG-109hhrg35501.htm)
- **Contemporary tracking directive.** DOI Release **042-2001**, dated October 4 and marked **Revised Nov. 15, 2001**, says monitoring would include “charting of all pick ups and drop offs of debris.” It names AMEC → Stier, Anderson & Malone, Bovis Lend Lease → Thacher Associates, Tully → Decision Strategies/Fairfax International, and Turner → Getnick & Getnick. The four pairings also appear in DOI's 2006 testimony. The directive and retrospective account identify plausible holders of the records and leave actual coverage to be tested. [DOI release, pp. 1–2](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doi/downloads/pdf/pr100401.pdf) · [House hearing 109-76, preserved HTML lines 3938–3942](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-109hhrg35501/html/CHRG-109hhrg35501.htm)
- **Amount unverified.** A September 28, 2001 UPI report attributes to the police commissioner an investigation involving **255 tons** and **three scrap yards**. McCormack gives only “a few loads” in the two regions, with no tonnage or yard count. These statements could overlap, but no warrant, inventory or matching identifier was found to test that. Keep the press figures separate from the hearing and out of the mass balance. [UPI, September 28, 2001, opening paragraph](https://www.upi.com/Archives/2001/09/28/World-Trade-Center-scrap-steel-stolen/9411001649600/) · [House hearing 109-76, preserved HTML lines 12424–12435](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-109hhrg35501/html/CHRG-109hhrg35501.htm)

The same McCormack statement describes Fresh Kills as the intended destination for all debris and steel. The earlier Army Corps account describes structural steel going to two salvage yards for recycling. Keep intended destination, suspect routes, recovered loads, and later authorized recycling separate. A yard name by itself would leave those paths ambiguous. The warrant inventories and dated transport records could establish route and recovery for individual loads. They would still require a separate specimen or building identifier for attribution. [House hearing 109-76, preserved HTML lines 12418–12435](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-109hhrg35501/html/CHRG-109hhrg35501.htm) · [USACE printed p. 9, landfill operations](https://corpslakes.erdc.dren.mil/employees/engineerupdate/Engineer_update_092002.pdf#page=8)

Confirm dates, yard identities, item descriptions and case identifiers before joining accounts.
- [ ] **Return haul.** Locate DSNY dispatch tickets and Fresh Kills receiving or weigh records for the reclaimed loads. Join on a shared load, warrant or ticket identifier with matching date and material. Keep duplicate reports and any residual unrecovered loads separately counted.
- [ ] **Routine hauling.** Locate the four monitoring files and DOI charting directives by construction manager, including the original October release if available. Compare monitored periods and authorized destinations with the warrant/load records. Read the City PDF through indexed public text. - [ ] **Disposition.** Seek any public administrative or court record that identifies the recovery matter's disposition and any docket that identifies the distinct reported filing charges. The grand-jury account remains attributed and unverified independently.
- [ ] **Item provenance.** Obtain building or member marks and transfer records before linking any recovered steel to a NIST or museum specimen.
