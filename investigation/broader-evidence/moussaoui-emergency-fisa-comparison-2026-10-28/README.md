# Emergency FISA request, reported headquarters opposition and the separate criminal-warrant trail

**Exhibit 334 adds a contemporaneous account of an adverse headquarters assessment and internal disagreement.** It does not contain a signed headquarters decision, DOJ/OIPR disposition or court ruling. The available August 24 control, exhibit 347, supplies an explicit emergency search request and a supporting memorandum. Reading them together advances the request/response trail while leaving the actual decision basis, routing and outcome unresolved.

## What the September 10 email actually supplies

[Exhibit 334](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/334.pdf) is one court-hosted page with a top-level Kiser reply, a quoted Samit update and an earlier quoted Kiser message. Physical page 1, header, displays **9/10/01 3:45PM**; the quoted Samit header displays **09/10 3:38 PM**. No time zone is visible. These are rendered message-header statements, not native headers, transport logs or independently synchronized send/receipt times. The earlier quoted message is faint; no uncertain words are reconstructed.

In the quoted Samit update, first paragraph, Samit attributes to ITOS/RFU SSA Maltbie the assessment that **“we do not have enough for either a criminal search warrant or a FISA search warrant”** of Moussaoui's computer. He reports that the matter was not pursued further because of direction treating it as an INS matter, including the quoted characterization **“FBI does not have a dog in this fight”**. He immediately adds **“Of course, I strongly disagree.”** This directly documents Samit's contemporaneous report of opposition and disagreement; it is not Maltbie's original assessment, an OIPR response, a prosecutor's decision or a judicial denial. The material actually reviewed and the standards applied are not supplied here.

The top-level reply expresses regret about how the matter was handled and a conditional fear about a future incident involving the same type of aircraft. That is evidence of the writer's stated concern and sympathy. It is not independent corroboration of every quoted factual assertion, a statement identifying a particular future attack, or proof of advance knowledge of September 11.

The call recording/notes, the complete native transmittal and any returned decision are separate retrieval targets. Pages **2–3 and 9** also explicitly retain uncertainty about how far a possible plan had advanced or how many unidentified associates existed. These are investigators' stated reasons and uncertainties, not our findings that an identified attack plan or network existed. The request and imperative lead wording are not proof that a court application was filed, heard, granted or denied. “Emergency” in a request is not proof that a particular statutory emergency-authorization procedure was invoked.

## What this supports, and what could change the assessment

The source-supported inference is narrower than a conspiracy conclusion: a field investigator contemporaneously reported headquarters opposition to two warrant routes, disagreed, and described trying a foreign-search alternative. This is a concrete lead about decision authority, review quality and unperformed/blocked investigative steps. It does not establish an unlawful refusal, deliberate protection, coordination, concealment or foreknowledge.

An incomplete or inaccurate summary of the headquarters exchange, materially different evidentiary versions, documented legal advice, a later reconsideration, or records showing that an alternative search actually proceeded could change that assessment. Those remain competing possibilities, not accepted explanations. Obtain the original exchange and decision basis rather than count Kiser's sympathetic reply or repeated summaries as independent proof.
