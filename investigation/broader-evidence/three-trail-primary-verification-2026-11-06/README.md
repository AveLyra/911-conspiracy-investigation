## K-13: sample photograph independently linked to published figure

- I visually compared the public archive photograph named [Mount of K1-C1M-FL-1.jpg](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1p8jCwTYiGjBPcAV6c__GKPBxBqiO2XjY/view) with **Figure 6-16(a), printed p. 255**, in [NIST NCSTAR 1-3C volume 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3cv1.pdf). The specimen silhouette, mount, interior speckling and one-inch scale show the same photograph, with labels added in the report. The caption identifies material from the flange of column 210 of panel K-1 (A209:97–100), above the 98th-floor spandrel. The direct NIST `ncstar1-3cv1.pdf` downloaded in this pass has that figure at **PDF p. 34**, despite a reported PDF-p. 305 locator for a different NIST-hosted copy. Use the printed figure number and page to distinguish the PDF versions; this does not imply 305 is wrong for the other copy.
- I visually examined the archive folder's [precut photograph](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1aQoGrRYavK0NU4jCayJWdt7-JRPp2mF4/view) and [cut photograph](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1AacVuIU_PVHpYqghKfAcujMO74JWPfcG/view). Both show a marked bent member labeled K1-C1M-FL-1; the latter shows a cut at the marked region. The shared archive name and photographed geometry support a sample trail but do not replace signed sample transfer or laboratory intake records. The folder name's August 29, 2003 is a locator, not a verified capture timestamp.
- I visually examined [NIST NCSTAR 1-3B Table A-1, printed p. 62 / PDF p. 90](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=101430#page=90): it lists `K-1 or K-13` panel and a distinct `K-13` flange coupon reportedly received from Gross July 29, 2002. The same label does not establish their relationship.
- I visually examined [FEMA 403 Appendix D, PDF pp. 276–277](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-FEM1-PURL-LPS18973/pdf/GOVPUB-FEM1-PURL-LPS18973.pdf#page=276). Row 101, K-1, records a 93–96 marking; row 106, K13, records different flange measurements and a coupon taken to GMS. These are contemporaneous field inventory descriptions, not a demonstrated linkage to the later Gross coupon or a calibrated comparison of identical component surfaces. Original photos, measurement orientation, reconciliation and GMS–Gross–NIST handoffs remain missing.
- The previous [K-13 source crosswalk](../steel-k13-panel-coupon-crosswalk-2026-11-06/README.md) independently compared the museum photograph with the archived spandrel image; this pass independently checks the formerly unverified mount-to-figure relationship. It does not establish an individual specimen's entire chain of custody, timing of collapse damage, explosive residue or a UMS connection.

## Flight 93: limits of primary-source access

The [public reader reproduction of FBI March 28, 2002 Rugg/Phillips interview, typed April 23](https://www.scribd.com/document/18886049/T7-B12-DOJ-Doc-Req-35-13-Packet-5-Fdr-Entire-Contents-Response-Letters-Reports-404), states that Oracle-to-Excel conversion altered the final digits of credit-card numbers; it reports receipt of a corrected spreadsheet March 29 by Airborne Express **50588655-972**. Its continuation says Todd Beamer's name appeared on the initial spreadsheet next to certain calls, while the witnesses offered a belief as to why. Those words support the initial-name qualification; they do not prove who made each call, eliminate other attribution changes or supply the native versions and waybill. This pass checked accessible **third-party transcribed text**, not the FBI page images or native originals; do not label it an authenticated original.

A separate research report identifies [FBI Vault September interviews 01 of 08, PDF pp. 31–34](https://vault.fbi.gov/9-11%20Commission%20Report/9-11-interviews-2001-09-sep-01-of-08/) as typed reports quoting the Star-Fix manager's and operator's handwritten statements. The agency PDF text was accessible through the web reader but the relevant OCR did not identify the names, screenshot requests failed and a direct download returned an access page. Thus the particular page-image claim and reported Commission audio remain unverified in this pass. Earlier third-party Box 19 transcriptions remain separate leads, not original handwriting or a verified note-set crosswalk.

## Debris: primary institutional sources

The original [July 2006 congressional hearing, David Varoli's prepared statement](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-109hhrg35501/html/CHRG-109hhrg35501.htm) reports KPMG certification of marine debris transport by examining vessel logs. This supports a genuine records route, not proof of particular fields, complete coverage, present retention or a specific shipment. The same testimony's project-material receiving slips are not established as outbound debris receipts.

The [DSNY 2002–03 Annual Report, printed p. 15 / PDF p. 17](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dsny/downloads/pdf/studies-and-reports/2002-2003_Annual_Report.pdf#page=17), describes Fresh Kills handling and reports that the last load of steel was removed for recycling August 21, 2002. It also lists piers 6 and 25; it does not identify a specific ticket, recipient address or specimen. A [1993 EPA draft](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=9100JJSA.TXT) documents earlier barge-cleanliness form names; use during WTC operations and survival are unverified.

The [third-party reproduction of the 2006 federal opinion](https://aele.org/law/2006FPDEC/wtc.html) describes Load Debris Tickets and cites Plaintiffs' Joint Appendix vol. 1 ex. 7 for DDC operations; the exhibit and completed ticket were not obtained. Contradictory pier pairs may have different scopes or dates, or involve error; none is resolved without operational records. The reported negative search is bounded, not an exhaustive government holdings search.

## What would change the assessment

Find the corrected native workbook and contemporaneous version-to-version call keys; authenticate Star-Fix original notes and intake; locate itemized K-13 custody/cutting records and original FEMA field photographs; and match a particular debris ticket to vessel logs and an actual receiving record. No new connection among UMS, explosives, a specific shipment and attack planning follows from these results. The original underlying records remain the objects of the follow-up.
