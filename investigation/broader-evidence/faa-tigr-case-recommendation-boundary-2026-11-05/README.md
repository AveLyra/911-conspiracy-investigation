## What the system records show

- **TIGR's system notice distinguishes referral and action data.** DOT/OST 101 identifies the Transportation Inspector General Reporting System. The April 2000 notice names referral source and action taken among the investigative data. The December 2005 revision preserves those categories and adds investigation information as an express purpose and public posting of some results as a routine use. The notice specifies January 30, 2006 as the effective date unless a later notice modifies it. The public-posting provision gives no requirement to publish every outcome and establishes no particular posting. [2000 notice, 65 FR 19558, physical p. 83](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2000-04-11/pdf/00-8505.pdf#page=83) · [2005 notice, 70 FR 75856–57](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2005-12-21/pdf/E5-7599.pdf)
- **Recommendation counts and investigation counts came from different subsystems.** In its FY 2009 performance plan, OIG describes the TIGR audit subsystem as tracking, among other things, recommendations issued and resolved. Its investigative examples include hotline complaints, indictments, convictions and recoveries. These are examples of subsystem data used to compile performance statistics. They do not establish that the FAA report's recommendations entered the audit subsystem. A report-number cross-reference, recommendation IDs or a different tracking record are needed. [FY 2009 Performance Plan, § VII, printed p. 9](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/docs/oig_fy_2009_budget_estimate.pdf)
- **A later system description separates steps, outcomes and supporting documents.** OIG's October 2022–March 2023 semiannual report describes an **Investigative Case Management System** capturing dates, referrals and administrative as well as civil and criminal outcomes. It also identifies reports of investigation, stakeholder communications and management implication reports as repository contents. The report describes its own reporting process. Migration from TIGR and coverage of the 2006 case are unverified. [Semiannual report, printed p. 8, physical p. 10, lines 20–31](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/DOT%20OIG%20Semiannual%20Report%20to%20Congress%20-%20October%202022%20-%20March%202023.pdf#page=10)

## Separate retention descriptions

The April 2000 and December 2005 DOT/OST 101 notices say machine-resident TIGR records are kept for **two years after becoming inactive**, with inactive records on tape. They separately describe retaining hard copy until replacement or obsolescence. The 2004 TIGR privacy assessment also describes a two-year period for inactive records. These descriptions concern the named system. The [separate DOT/OST 100 case-folder notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2000-04-11/pdf/00-8505.pdf#page=83) describes **ten years from Federal Records Center receipt** for official investigative case folders. Both descriptions are retained under their respective scopes, together with the later schedule variants in the predecessor review. Neither the system's inactive date nor the actual file and printout disposition is established for this case. [2000 notice, 65 FR 19559, physical p. 84 lines 16–26](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2000-04-11/pdf/00-8505.pdf#page=84) · [2005 notice, 70 FR 75857, physical p. 2 lines 111–121](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2005-12-21/pdf/E5-7599.pdf#page=2) · [2004 assessment, data accuracy section](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/privacy/pia-transportation-inspector-general-reporting-system)

The local check compares those two Federal Register retention paragraphs after joining line wraps and removing the wrap in the two-year expression. The wording matched. Separate documents and schedules may cover printouts, official files, recommendation records or later case-system copies. No historical disposal date was calculated from the investigation closure entry.
