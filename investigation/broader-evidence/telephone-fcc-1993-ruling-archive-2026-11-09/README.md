## Source observations and billing implications

| Original passage | Scope of the evidence |
|---|---|
| **¶9**, printed p. 6173, PDF p. 3 extracted lines 27–40 | The Bureau describes Airfone as owning and operating the handsets and radio equipment on aircraft and nearly 100 ground stations. It says calls went through the carrier's private network or the public switched network. It describes an activation charge and time-sensitive use charge and says the land portion of long-distance calls was included. This describes the product then. The passage supplies neither a carrier export nor a billable-duration definition. |
| **¶18** and **n. 30**, p. 6174, PDF p. 4 lines 82–95 and 128–129 | The ruling classifies Airfone as a provider of operator services (OSP) based on direct switched-network interconnection and automatic billing assistance from credit-card activation. The note says Airfone also provided its own live operators. Automated processing and live assistance were distinct capabilities. An operator label alone leaves the carrier, person and specific leg open. |
| **¶¶19–20**, p. 6174, PDF p. 4 lines 96–123 | In discussing transfer to a caller-selected long-distance carrier, the Bureau says the origin on a bill would reflect the receiving ground station under the then-current arrangement. It describes Airfone as converting to a system that would use one of two switching-center locations. Both statements concern the indicated billing origin in the described scenario. Whether the conversion was completed, the chosen carrier actually handled a disputed call, and what a particular record field represents are separate questions. |
| **¶20 n. 32**, pp. 6174–6175, PDF p. 4 lines 133–139 and p. 5 lines 45–49 | The ruling describes Airfone's bundled airtime and long-distance rate and raises the possibility of a separate charge from the caller-selected carrier. The Bureau expresses concern and places adjudication of the practice outside the order's scope. A reproducible comparison would first match a carrier invoice and original switch events to their respective legs and tariff or contract terms. |
| **¶¶21–22**, p. 6175, PDF p. 5 lines 4–30 | The Bureau classifies Railfone and the described Mobilnet service as aggregators. It describes the underlying cellular carrier as the operator-service provider. In the next paragraph it extends the Airfone holding to air-to-ground licensees. Footnote 1 expressly confines Mobilnet to its credit-card rental-car service and excludes GTE's cellular licenses as such. Keep the service-specific roles separate. [Scope note, p. 6171](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-93-1022A1_Rcd.pdf#page=1) |

The original's **¶6** describes restrictions on charging for unanswered calls (p. 6172, lines 69–80). Preserve that language with the **2001 CFR §64.705** and the later **FCC 03-203** wording. Its **p. 9 n. 56** says the provision “does not apply to CMRS carriers” and **p. 13 ¶38** says Airfone and Railfone “must comply with TOCSIA provisions fully”. The ruling's service classifications and the later language are observations from different dates. Applying them to a 2001 record still requires the contemporaneous configuration, actual service and billing entity, and the scope of the relevant rules. An unanswered billing restriction alone cannot assign an answer state to a zero-duration record.
