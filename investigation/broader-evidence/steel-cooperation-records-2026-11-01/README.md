# Port Authority support letter, report use and the missing confirmation

November 1, 2026. Existing B188/H005, AUD011/AUD016, SPT01–SPT08 and SR01. This adds an underlying published letter reproduction to the steel preservation/access inquiry; it does not supply the executed agreement described in earlier testimony.

## The newly read record

The Port Authority's letter to NIST director Arden Bement is dated **January 24, 2002**. Its reproduced office stamp reads **2002 JAN 28 P 2:13**. These are separate fields; neither authenticates historical dispatch, receipt or a calibrated clock. The full image identifies Francis J. Lombard as chief engineer. [Hearing, printed 176](https://books.google.com/books?id=R1GrCiT1US8C&pg=PA176); source PA176 in [SOURCES.json](SOURCES.json).

The letter supports the proposed investigation, describes staff attending the January 10 briefing, and states an understanding restricting report use in damages suits and investigators' expert testimony. It asks NIST to confirm that stated understanding. This is a specific confirmation-request lead, not a recovered NIST acceptance or completed bilateral agreement.

The underlying image and public text both use **submitted** in the report-use clause. The historical statute below uses **admitted**. Both records remain unchanged; no legal effect or cause of that wording difference is decided here.

## Necessary statutory context

The **2000 edition of 15 U.S.C. §281a** already limited use of resulting investigation reports in damages suits. That matters when assessing whether the report-use language was exceptional. The provision's inspected paragraph contains no separate investigator expert-testimony prohibition; this limited textual observation does not establish that no other rule, agreement or advice addressed testimony. [Official historical text, physical 1 / printed 546](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title15/pdf/USCODE-2000-title15-chap7-sec281a.pdf); source USC2000, extraction lines 111–124.

The letter, statute and earlier witness descriptions must be kept separate. No conclusion about enforceability, unlawful restrictions, deliberate concealment or what NIST actually accepted follows from this comparison. The strongest qualification is that the language may reflect existing law or additional guidance not yet acquired.
