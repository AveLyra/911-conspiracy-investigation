# FAA investigative tracking and retention authorities

November 5, 2026 · B129 / PD02–PD04. This follows the [FAA liaison routing comparison](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/c3a52736897e8a6535e5ef4f3537120a94242a2b/investigation/broader-evidence/faa-liaison-office-routing-2026-11-05/README.md). It identifies an FAA correspondence-assignment module and compares what the FAA and NARA say about investigative files, correspondence and index records. The inquiry remains whether the August 2006 DOT OIG report **CC-2006-085**, investigation **042R0122001**, led to a documented FAA correction or action decision. [OIG recommendations 1–2, p. 12](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cc2006085.pdf#page=12)

## Where a referral or action record might be indexed

- **2000 system notice.** **DOT/FAA 815**, *Investigative Record System*, describes investigative reports, agency-originated and received materials, and retrieval by name or other identifying symbols. It names Civil Aviation Security headquarters and field offices and Federal Records Centers as locations and refers retention to the applicable FAA Order **1350.15** by record type, investigation category and significance. [65 FR 19520–19521, physical pp. 45–46](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2000-04-11/pdf/00-8505.pdf#page=45)
- **2009 FAA assessment.** Its section on use says the **Investigative Tracking System (ITS)** tracks internal administrative and misconduct investigations and describes a **“control assignments”** module for correspondence routing, including complaints received from DOT OIG. The stated examples supply a concrete module name. This document postdates the target referral and does not establish whether the referral entered that module. The assessment separately describes about **five years** for paper records, a **proposed** period of about **five years** for electronic records, and indefinite maintenance of those electronic records while unscheduled. [January 5, 2009 assessment, System Overview, How ITS Uses Information and How Long ITS Retains Information](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/privacy/pia-investigative-tracking-system-its)
- **Later routing.** The 2022 notice explicitly lists **case number** among possible retrieval keys. The 2023 and 2026 FAA assessments describe the **National Case Intake Portal** and **Busser Investigation Management System** within ITS, with intake, review, referral or assignment, investigation approval, briefing and a report to the requester. The OIG's case number and any separately assigned FAA intake number require a cross-reference. The later workflow cannot establish what system or identifiers staff used in 2006. [87 FR 51486, physical p. 5, lines 133–139](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-08-22/pdf/2022-17943.pdf#page=5) · [2023 assessment pp. 6–7](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2023-10/Privacy%20-%20FAA%20-%20ITS%20-%20PIA%20-%202023.pdf#page=6) · [2026 assessment pp. 7–8](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2026-10/Privacy%20-%20FAA%20-%20ITS%20-%20PIA%20-%202026.pdf#page=7)

These differences concern what each document calls the series and how it states retention. They do not establish which series held a particular case, whether the file was subject to a hold or later migration, or when any actual disposition occurred. The **2006 OIG investigation closure**, FAA action decision, correction delivery and any later recommendation closure require separate records.

Seek the 2006 file plan and system migration documentation to test whether a legacy index survived apart from the case file. A retention rule or index entry alone cannot establish the underlying decision.
