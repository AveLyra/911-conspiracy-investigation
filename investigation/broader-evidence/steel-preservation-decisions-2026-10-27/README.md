## What the records add

**1. An original custody commitment and specific inventory targets.** The April22,2004 agreement’s ExhibitH reproduces the Port Authority’s April21 letter. It says a current inventory had been supplied and promises a complete listing of holdings **as of July1,2004**, plus updates. Stipulation4 requires related consultation, safeguarding and inventory work, subject to ownership/disposition terms. The as-of date is not proof of a missed delivery deadline. We now have the commitment itself; the actual lists, delivery proof and item-level implementation are still missing. [Agreement, physicalpp5–6,37](https://www.renewnyc.com/attachments/content/pdfs/programmatic_agreement_5704.pdf#page=37), pinsS01–S02.

**2. A documented preservation risk with important contrary context.** May26,2005 minutes report that specialized-maintenance agreements expired in late2004 and describe December2004 roof leaks threatening artifacts. They also describe existing **basic** maintenance. These statements must not become a claim that all maintenance stopped or a particular specimen suffered water damage. The board approved additional preservation/maintenance authority; authorization is not proof the supplemental contract was executed or the risk remedied. Obtain the contract, work orders, inspection/condition records and invoices. [Minutes, physicalpp6–8/printed193–195](https://dspace.njstatelib.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/c960df66-1aba-40cd-9eb0-140585e3be89/content#page=6), S03–S04.

**3. Different reported inventory-completion dates remain side by side.** The minutes put completion of a two-volume *WTC Archive: Artifacts Inventory Report* in **October2004**. January2011 and January2012 LMDC reports give **2006** for a similarly titled two-volume inventory. Both say updates were made. A later edition, different completion milestone or a reporting error are untested possibilities, not a selected explanation. Obtain the actual2004/2006volumes, revision history and transmittals. Neither year proves fabrication or missing artifacts. The separately cited November2002Draft2 report also remains a distinct unacquired edition. [2005minutes, p6](https://dspace.njstatelib.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/c960df66-1aba-40cd-9eb0-140585e3be89/content#page=6); [January2011,p5](https://renewnyc.com/attachments/content/pdfs/WTCHistoricResourcesReport_January2011l.pdf#page=5); [January2012,p5](https://www.renewnyc.org/attachments/content/pdfs/WTC_Historic_Resources_Report_January_2012.pdf#page=5), S03/S05/S06.

NIST’s current page, labeledOctober6,2009 and updatedFebruary3,2025, reports a September29convoy, more than200pieces, extracted examined samples retained for possible retesting, and a few whole pieces retained with permission. ## What this means for the investigation

The strongest new evidence is a traceable organizational commitment and an adopted response to a recorded preservation risk. It contradicts an assumption that no preservation program existed. It does not establish adequate execution, complete forensic custody, candidate-specific origin, intentional loss or concealment. Funding language cannot replace condition logs; a possible mundane version change cannot erase different reported dates.

Nothing acquired joins W14A/B, C12a/b or FEMA Sample1 to Hangar17. Preserve the earlier FEMA WTC#7 labels and NIST unidentified classifications without selecting a winner. [Earlier attribution comparison](../steel-attribution-roots-2026-10-27/README.md) and [C12 source limits](../steel-c12-source-controls-2026-10-27/README.md) remain intact. A complete transfer/alias/specimen chain could strengthen or weaken an alleged preservation failure; these general records alone cannot decide it.

The agreement includes a ten-year/earlier-termination provision and amendment procedures. Its scanned signature page is visible, but signatures, execution and subsequent amendments were not independently authenticated. This report does not treat the2004terms as present law, establish legal breach or allege a violation of an evidence-retention order.
