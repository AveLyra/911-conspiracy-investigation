## Source observations

The PDF carries an ISCAP declassification stamp dated July 8, 2015. This is a released scan, not an authenticated native production history or the tape itself.

- The question says the transcript cuts off around **10:15 a.m.**, asks whether tapes and/or transcription continued, and requests later material if available.
- The response attributed to NEADS commander Bob Marr says “The tapes end at 10:15 am” and separately says he decided “to terminate the transcription at that time.” It reports that old transcription equipment damaged “some of the tapes” and that an outside transcriber worked full-time for two weeks. Preserve these descriptions together without deciding whether the tape and transcription boundaries were identical.
- A separately attributed response from NEADS operations says two of the original three tapes were in the archive and that “One of the tapes was damaged” during transcription. It says NEADS would not copy remaining originals on local equipment and describes a condition for specialist copying. The two damage-count descriptions are preserved as an unresolved apparent discrepancy; scope and exact affected media are not established.

## Comparison and limits

The [June 2004 hearing transcript](https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/911/archive/hearing12/9-11Commission_Hearing_2004-06-17.htm) records Arnold's account that he had been unaware of a tape and understood a contractor later made it playable. The [2005 DoD IG report](https://web.archive.org/web/20161222001502id_/http://www.dodig.mil/Foia/ERR/05-INTEL-13.pdf), physical page 13, reports a transcription problem and says tapes were not used for the press release or event reporting until Commission staff reviewed them. These are separate observations with different authors and dates. No item-level source here establishes that the hearing tape is the damaged tape or a particular one of the three; do not combine the descriptions into a single verified custody history.

The response does not prove whether audio after 10:15 exists, what was lost, the fate of any original or copy, the extent of recoverability, or why a particular person did or did not use it. An asserted transcription decision is not an independent finding of missing recording; the reported damage is not proof of intent. No contractor work order, audio playback or native transcript comparison was performed.
