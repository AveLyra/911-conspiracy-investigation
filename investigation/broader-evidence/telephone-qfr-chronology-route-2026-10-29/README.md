## What the held report actually adds

The [Commission staff report, PDF p96/note285](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/9-11/staff-report-sept2005.pdf#page=96), preserves several different accounts:

- Operations-center interviewees said management did not request that dispatchers warn airborne crews about hijackings/cockpit intrusions.
- The airline reportedly said management did instruct dispatchers to contact aircraft with diversion instructions for the airspace shutdown.
- The report cites an **August9,2004 AAL letter** saying individual dispatchers improvised warnings to certain aircraft. It describes a **9:22** vigilance message referring to WTC reports and unconfirmed AA-aircraft information, and a more explicit **9:36a.m.** hijacking/high-alert message.

These statements concern different decisions, people, message contents and scopes. Preserve them together. An assertion that no pilots were warned would go beyond this record. Conversely, two reported dispatcher messages do not establish a management-wide warning program, complete coverage, operational receipt by a specific crew or timely delivery. The original letter, message exports, recipient/distribution records and clocks remain missing.

This is a dependent source chain: **staff report → reported later airline letter → quoted earlier messages**. We read the staff-report scan, not the underlying letter or native messages. Neither the report's time labels nor its account of sending calibrates a switching, ACARS or cockpit-receipt clock. Note285 must not be expanded into evidence of what every aircraft received or what any warning could have prevented.

[The predecessor TI05 is preserved exactly](PREVIOUS-TI05.json). It already distinguished no received Herndon request from no management decision and required timely-warning evidence. This supplement adds the specific contrary/contextual source passage; it does not attribute an absolute no-warnings claim to the predecessor or overwrite it.

## Lockout context is also present

[Report pp12–13](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/9-11/staff-report-sept2005.pdf#page=12) describes the Flight11 lockout as a standard safety/security procedure, intended to isolate information for senior management, prevent alteration or release, and protect passenger/crew identities. Page96/note285 reports the airline invoking ACSSP need-to-know requirements.

Those are the report's description and the airline's reported policy account. We have not acquired the controlling policy version or tested actual compliance. This context weighs against treating the reported containment instruction or manifest restriction, by themselves, as unexplained suppression. It does not establish proper application, appropriate scope, timely warnings or the absence of obstruction elsewhere. Policy, access/instruction logs and actual dispatch distribution are the discriminating records.
