# World Trade Center cleanup invoices and the AMEC audit trail

November 6, 2026 UTC · STL01–STL03 / B188 / H005

A public New York court decision reproduces part of a **September 20, 2001** City letter requiring AMEC to keep records for time-and-materials invoices subject to later audit. The related decisions identify an **October 4, 2001** subcontract exhibit, a reported post-performance audit and an order requiring the City to match produced documents to individual requests. These give the investigation a route to contemporaneous work and payment records through two specific dockets. The reviewed decisions do not link any invoice, shipment or yard release to **W14A** or **W14B**. [2008 decision, PDF pp. 9–10](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/pdfs/2008/2008_32797.pdf#page=9) · [2013 decision, PDF p. 5](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/pdfs/2013/2013_30104.pdf#page=5) · [2012 order, PDF p. 7](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/pdfs/2012/2012_30072.pdf#page=7)

## Keep the accounts separate

- **City contract timing.** Preserve the 2008 decision's account of AMEC’s position that no formal agreement was reached alongside the 2015 factual background’s statement that the City entered into contracts with construction managers months after the attack. Whether the accounts concern the same phase, parties and instrument remains unverified. The September 20 advance letter and October 4 subcontract describe different transactions. [2008 decision, pp. 9–10](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/pdfs/2008/2008_32797.pdf#page=9) · [2015 mirror, p. 4](https://cases.justia.com/new-york/other-courts/2015-2015-ny-slip-op-30444-u.pdf?ts=1427922715#page=4)
- **Evergreen’s role.** The March 2015 background includes **EROC** in its four-construction-manager list. The October 2015 appellate account describes **Tully Environmental, Inc. doing business as Evergreen Recycling of Corona** as overseeing certain subcontractors on behalf of **Bovis and AMEC**, passing invoices to them and remitting payments. The 2013 background uses **Tully** in its construction-manager list. Keep the corporate names, roles and procedural scopes as stated. Which entity made or retained a given record requires the actual contract, MOU or routing documents. [March 2015 mirror, p. 4](https://cases.justia.com/new-york/other-courts/2015-2015-ny-slip-op-30444-u.pdf?ts=1427922715#page=4) · [October 2015 appellate decision, pp. *1–*2](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2015/2015_07741.htm) · [2013 decision, p. 4](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/pdfs/2013/2013_30104.pdf#page=4)

The City’s DDC counsel told Congress in 2006 that KPMG entered findings in a central database, DDC cross-checked requisitions against daily field logs and FEMA reviewed source documents. The hearing describes a general workflow. The decisions identify a specific audit dispute and a request-to-production mapping to seek. They supply no case-specific join from that audit to the KPMG database. A date-only check puts the **December 19, 2001** Claremont visit inside the 2015 decision's stated **Phase I** window. The field table dates a yard visit. It supplies no matching departure, carrier or invoice record.
