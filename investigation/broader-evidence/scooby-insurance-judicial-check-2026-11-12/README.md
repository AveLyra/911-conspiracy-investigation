# Scooby insurance report: judicial-record checks

November 12, 2026. This check addresses selected claims from the recovered body of report 2, not its complete claim inventory or its unavailable attachments. Judicial opinions are original judicial records of findings and proceedings; they are not the underlying contemporaneous insurance exhibits, payment instruments or full financial statements.

| Recovered claim | Original judicial record and assessment |
| --- | --- |
| St. Paul bound coverage before closing, while final wording remained open. | The [Second Circuit’s 2003 opinion, 345 F.3d 154](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/345/345.F3d.154.02-9351.02-9431.02-9440.02-9350.02-9349.html), describes the July 18 exchange, a policy number confirming coverage and a July 23 confirmation stating that the manuscript form was to be agreed. The court affirmed that St. Paul was bound to the WilProp occurrence definition in the circumstances. This is insurer-specific; the opinion does not establish that every insurer agreed to an identical form. The underlying confirmation and complete attachments remain to be inspected. |
| The principal insurance recovery and court-defined net are not the owner’s personal profit. | The [2013 district court decision, 957 F. Supp. 2d 501](https://hallapproved.com/ny/cases/federal/district/2013/8726734/), findings 19–24, reports WTCP’s net recovery of $4,044,082,315 after three specified deductions: $10,352,540 claims-preparation fees, $31,030,471 appraisal expenses, and $5,898,714 for two years’ premiums. Adding them gives $4,091,364,040 before those deductions. The court separately describes 7WTCo. recoveries; avoid silently adding different insured entities or presenting the principal entity’s figure as a personal distribution. The calculation is the court’s defined insurance net, not a complete owner-level return. The decision was affirmed in part, vacated in part and remanded; check the appellate mandate before making a broader claim about its legal effect. |
| A WTC investor received capital plus interest, but the source of funds is not established. | The New York Supreme Court’s [2006 Ritorto decision](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/pdfs/2006/2006_30126.pdf), printed pp. 3–4 and 8, says Ritorto invested $500,000 and in January 2004 received his capital back with 12% interest through December 23, 2003. The decision does not identify the check’s funding source or establish a distribution of insurance proceeds. Its later account of a disputed 7 WTC cash call is separate from the investor repayment. |

These records support both ordinary preattack insurance placement and the need for a real accounting of economic benefit. Neither recovery nor an investor payment by itself establishes advance knowledge. Rebuilding expenses also do not prove that the owners received no economic benefit: retained rights and distributions must be included in a full account.
