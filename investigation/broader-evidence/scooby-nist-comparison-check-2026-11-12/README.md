# Scooby report 27: tower comparison and calibration check

November 12, 2026. This checks selected recovered claims against [NIST NCSTAR 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1.pdf), including the observation/simulation comparison tables. It is a documentary check, not a numerical rerun, independent photographic measurement or complete audit of report 27 and attachments.

| Claim | Source check and qualification |
| --- | --- |
| Observations influenced model setup and case selection. | NCSTAR 1, printed pp. 102–104, says exterior wall sections were selected based on photographic bowing, compares floor-sagging cases to observed locations, and reports choosing final global cases B and D based on subsystem results. It also says floor-sagging extent was greater in observations than full-floor predictions and discusses omitted possible sources of insulation/floor damage. Agreement with an observation used in selecting or adjusting inputs is not a fully independent validation of that same observation. This methodological limit does not by itself establish the model is false or identify another mechanism. |
| WTC 2 timing showed a 13-minute difference. | NCSTAR 1, printed p. 153, table 6-11, reports observed collapse initiation at 56 minutes and modeled global instability at 43 minutes after impact. The arithmetic difference is 13 minutes. NIST characterizes overall agreement as reasonably good, says exact timing was sensitive to factors controlling bowing, and says the event sequence was not sensitive to those factors. The table itself does not provide a quantitative uncertainty interval for the 13-minute difference. |
| The comparison table shows WTC 2 tilt direction but not numerical simulated tilt. | The same table reports observed pre-downward-motion angles of approximately 7–8° east and 3–4° south and describes simulated tilt as south and east at instability. That is a qualitative directional comparison in this table, not a numerical simulated-angle match. Other model outputs may contain quantitative information; do not generalize this table’s omission to all NIST publications. |
| NIST’s late eastward tilt descriptions agree exactly. | **Unresolved textual difference:** NCSTAR 1, printed p. 152, describes eastward tilt increasing to approximately 20° before obscuration; table 6-11 on p. 153 describes approximately 25°. Both describe the same broad direction and distinguish the earlier 7–8° estimate before significant downward movement. This pass did not inspect the underlying measurements or determine whether different image frames, methods or rounding explain the difference. It is not evidence by itself of manipulation. |

The published comparison supports investigating whether different stages were calibrated or independently predicted. A defensible held-out test requires the actual selected inputs, timing and image measurement uncertainties, a predeclared target set and sensitivity analysis. This source check neither independently confirms collapse mechanics nor establishes explosives or an installer. Native model files, the visual measurement history and the remaining report-27 claims and attachments remain open.
