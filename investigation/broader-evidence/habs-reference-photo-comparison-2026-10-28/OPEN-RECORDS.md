# Inputs and discriminating outcomes

1. Original negative/exposure numbers, dated contact sheets and reproduction/crop/scan/caption history for both proposed pairs. A documented common negative could authenticate shared source; distinct exposure records could weaken that inference. Matching scene content alone does not choose between them.
2. Actual catalogue-listed N1200 field material and its accession/manifest. A successful catalogue response now confirms the locator, not the contents or a negative/object join.
3. Native marking/cut/removal/transport/Hangar17 intake/object records for the reported paint- and smoke-affected remnants, plus current museum/sample custody where recorded. A confirmed mismatch can exclude the proposed identity; similar descriptions cannot establish it.
4. Construction drawings and component/material/coating/region identification for J3/10 and J4/12, plus any source-specific calibration and later sampling. Do not assume these are the exposed-steel specimens or paint calibration used in a different NIST study.
5. Actual April2005 agreement and August2005 submission/receipt/version manifest, distinct from the earlier April2004 programmatic agreement and the inventory editions. The new catalogue objects do not authenticate that delivery.
6. Native inventory/alias/coupon/intake/building-attribution records for the existing selected NIST candidates remain indispensable. All earlier conflicts and open FAA, telephone, recorder and materials tests remain open.

No request or contact is sent. A failure to find an original is not proof it was destroyed, withheld, false or deliberately altered.
