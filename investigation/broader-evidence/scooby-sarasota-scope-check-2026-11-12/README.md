# Scooby report 23: Sarasota records and search-scope check

November 12, 2026. This compares selected recovered report claims with released litigation exhibits, a court order and the 2014 briefing. The cited FBI and court records are primary records of their authors’ statements and decisions; their assertions about earlier investigative facts are not independently authenticated gate logs or carrier records.

| Claim | Source and limitation |
| --- | --- |
| The April 3 and April 16 FBI communications share five paragraphs. | The [August 22, 2019 district-court order, PDF p. 12](https://www.floridabulldog.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Bulldog.pdf#page=12), describes the April 3 document’s last five paragraphs as repeated in the April 16 document. The court’s observation supports treating the overlapping passages as dependent, while the unseen drafting history cannot establish which document was copied or whether both used a shared source. |
| A later analyst reported a negative telephone search. | The [released analyst email, PDF pp. 63–64 / SARASOTA-34–35](https://www.floridabulldog.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/Government-Exhibits.pdf#page=63), quotes the earlier assertion and says the analyst ran the domestic family numbers available to them without finding a hijacker connection. This is evidence of a reported search and a negative result within its scope, not proof that every historical number or every carrier record was checked. The inputs, date ranges and underlying outputs were not produced here. |
| The 2014 briefing said the FBI lacked gate records and complained of missing search documentation. | The [redacted briefing, PDF pp. 1 and 3](https://www.floridabulldog.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/document.pdf), records that the FBI did not have gate records and says documentation of searches to rule out connections was lacking. The released 2011 analyst email shows why a lack-of-documentation statement should not be restated as proof that no search ever occurred. The briefing presents the FBI’s negative finding; its evidentiary basis and redacted material need further examination. |
| The April 2002 positive wording itself proves a named person’s visit. | The [released FBI material, PDF pp. 32–35](https://www.floridabulldog.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/DE-97-2-Universe-of-Documents.pdf#page=32), includes a redacted quotation of the positive assertion and a later case narrative. The visible text does not supply the actual visitor log, plate image, user identification, phone comparison or source of the assertion. It therefore supports the existence of an investigative claim, not a named person’s confirmed visit. |
