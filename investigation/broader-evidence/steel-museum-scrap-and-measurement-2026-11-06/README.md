The two-inch material thickness is a separate field from the envelope dimensions.

## What the records support

- **There are concrete routes to test.** The anonymous gift identifies an accession file to check for the original account of recovery. The skyline-cut object identifies a Port Authority accession whose collection and treatment records could help distinguish later alteration from damage present at recovery. A donor credit supplies one custody link. The earlier links remain open. [Anonymous gift](https://collection.911memorial.org/Detail/objects/120364/rel/1) · [Skyline-cut fragment](https://collection.911memorial.org/Detail/objects/117624/rel/1)
- **Keep the recycling account and selected recovery record side by side.** The museum's historical note uses the phrase “flagged for recycling instead of forensic analysis.” NIST §5.1 reports that engineers searched debris yards and selectively marked specimens `SAVE` for study, including a search for WTC 7 material and connections. It also describes a separate Hangar 17 preservation effort and transfers from that collection into the NIST study. These accounts have different stated scopes. Which route, if any, applies to C.2020.39.3 remains open. [Museum catalog](https://collection.911memorial.org/Detail/objects/120364/rel/1) · [NCSTAR 1-3, printed pp. 27–28](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3.pdf#page=75)
- **The unit mismatch is a catalog finding.** A data-entry or conversion issue is one untested explanation. A dated measuring sheet, scale photographs or an earlier catalog version could distinguish the published fields. No original measurements, object marks, material samples or transport records were tested. The pages reviewed do not establish a WTC 7 attribution or a forensic specimen match for these three objects. [Catalog dimensions](https://collection.911memorial.org/Detail/objects/6102/rel/1) · [NIST identification method, printed p. 29](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3.pdf#page=77)
