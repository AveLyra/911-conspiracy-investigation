# What Marr told Commission staff about the Langley scramble

November 8, 2026 · **B129 / FAA01 / PD01–PD04**. The National Archives scan of the January 23, 2004 interview gives a dated record of what Commission staff asked the NEADS commander and what they attributed to him. It records a qualification of the earlier United 93 account and a direct lead to the evidence behind the public NORAD timeline. The memorandum identifies Geoffrey Brown as preparer and refers readers to a recorded interview for details. The recording remains unacquired. ## The public timeline and its source records

Physical p. 8 lines 14–31 attributes to Marr a concern about Dictaphone DAT times, an account of tape damage during transcription and speculation about inaccurate computer timing. The same passage records staff presenting evidence for an accurate NEADS transcript and identifying the **8:43 AM** call as American 11, while describing NORAD's time frame for United 175 as inaccurate. The printed **NORAD** and surrounding phrase at p. 8 lines 15–16 have pen marks over them. The existing text layer drops letters and words in that passage. Preserve those representations and use the page image for the visual locator. [Interview, physical p. 8](https://catalog.archives.gov/medialive/66/6103/2610366/content/arcmedia/9-11/MFR/t-0148-911MFR-00767.pdf#page=8)

The memorandum describes Marr's stated purpose for entering times as explaining the scenarios to the public (p. 8 lines 44–45). Its preparer then records this statement in indirect speech on p. 9 lines 7–8.

> Marr was emphatic that the mistakes in the data points were specifically made to show that they did not shoot down flight 93.

The next sentence records that Marr considered the public presentation less accurate than the Commission's investigation. The wording about mistakes and purpose is consequential. It leaves the specific data points, who entered or approved them, what those people knew, and whether the preparer captured Marr's exact meaning open. The timing and tape explanations on p. 8 and the assertion on p. 9 remain side by side. The memorandum supplies neither a finding that Flight 93 was shot down nor a determination of why the timeline was wrong. [Interview, physical pp. 8–9](https://catalog.archives.gov/medialive/66/6103/2610366/content/arcmedia/9-11/MFR/t-0148-911MFR-00767.pdf#page=9)

The September 12, 2006 Defense Inspector General memorandum reports finding no evidence of knowing deception and no indication of further erroneous statements after officials became aware of the discrepancies. Preserve that assessment beside the interview memorandum's narrower account of the purpose of the data points. Their scopes differ. No record examined here identifies which data points the speaker meant or proves who knowingly used them. The two June printouts reproduce the same exchange. The Commission's final narrative draws on Commission work and shares its source chain with the interview memo.
