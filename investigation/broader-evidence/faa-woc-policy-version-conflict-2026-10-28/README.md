Reviewed October 28, 2026. This is a supplement to the [preceding recording/retention review](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/df96947a51e984f1fb2e7afad1925e63a67a926d/investigation/broader-evidence/faa-recording-retention-source-joins-2026-10-27/README.md), not a replacement or a resolution of its findings. Existing keys: B129; FAA PD01–PD04 remain open. These source observations do not establish a conspiracy, historical intent, concealment or a completed administrative action.

## What this pass adds

A three-page DOT OIG meeting summary records both a claim that the FAA Washington Operations Center (WOC) lacked recording capability on September 11, 2001, and an agent's concern that the handbook supplied as the applicable version said emergency nets were recorded. The same summary identifies a completed contact-and-time checklist reported no longer available. Those are specific version, capability and preservation questions. The original handbook, version-check result, checklist and reorganization memo have not been obtained in this pass.

The original sources remain unchanged. Earlier notes remain published at their original paths. Repeated references and derivative inventories are not independent corroboration.

## 1. The recording statements conflict; the handbook version was questioned

The meeting record covers February 22 and 23, 2005 and bears a February 24, 2005 preparation date, under investigation 042R0122001. Its first page says the reporting agents received WOC hijacking/bomb-threat policies described as in place on September 11, 2001 and then-current policies. This is an agent report of what was supplied; it is not an independently authenticated version history. [DOT release, PDF p. 2285, document p. 1/3](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2285); anchor R01.

On the last page the report says:

> The WOC did not have the ability to record any of the lines on September 11, 2001. Following 9/11 that capability was added. However, there are some secure communication lines that are not recorded.

Its agent's note separately records an agreement to check whether Chapter 4, Air Piracy of the Crisis Management Handbook supplied as the September 11 version was the correct version. It says that version was questioned because tab 4-2, Crisis Management Telephone Net, indicated that “...all emergency nets are recorded.” [Same release, PDF p. 2287, document p. 3/3](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2287); R03 and R06.

**Preserved distinction:** one is a reported historical capability statement; the other is an agent's quotation of a supplied policy whose applicability was expressly questioned. We have not read that original handbook in this pass. Neither statement is selected as controlling. This does not establish that a recording was made, that a legally required recording was omitted, or that anyone destroyed one.

**Tests that could change the assessment:** obtain the supplied handbook files and tabs, effective dates, revision/transmittal history, the promised verification and response, contemporaneous equipment/configuration and routing records, and any preserved recordings or exports. An inapplicable edition, a narrower net definition, a mistaken recollection, a policy/capability mismatch or a genuinely missing recording are separate, untested possibilities. A policy sentence alone cannot distinguish them.

## 2. A particular completed notification checklist is reported missing

The same last page says the WOC “Guidelist” followed and completed on September 11, 2001 was no longer available. It describes a list of people to contact and blanks for the time each was contacted. It attributes a belief that it was lost during WOC renovations to a person whose identity is redacted. Preserve that account as a reported belief, not a documented finding about when, how or why the record disappeared. No identity was reconstructed. [PDF p. 2287](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2287); R04–R05.

This identifies a potentially useful primary timing record; it does not reveal any entries. Seek surviving copies/scans, the blank form and governing version, search descriptions and results, renovation/transfer records and actual retention/disposition decisions. An unavailable copy in a 2005 account does not prove that every copy was lost or that the loss was unlawful or deliberate.

## 3. The reorganization memo is still a referenced original, not an acquired one

The newly inspected meeting summary names an October 18, 2001 “Restructuring Emergency Operations Personnel” memorandum describing recommendations. [PDF p. 2286, document p. 2/3](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2286); R02. The preceding review already recorded another interview's reference to “ACTION: Restructuring Emergency Operations Personnel” on PDF p. 2299, which also said a separate findings report and equipment recommendations were not memorialized. [PDF p. 2299, document p. 2/7](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2299); R07.

The title forms are preserved separately, including the ACTION prefix. Neither supplies the memo's actual text, approval, routing, enclosures or implementation. Matching titles/dates support a candidate association, not proof that both accounts describe an identical version, nor independent evidence of misconduct. Six bounded public search formulations returned no original candidate in the supplied results; several results were plainly off topic. Search-engine coverage and enforcement of quoted/site terms were not verified. This is not a finding of withholding, deletion or nonexistence.

Seek the actual memo, its attachments and sign-off/routing, the underlying review and any implementation records. A 2001 reorganization memo would still be distinct from the later corrected congressional answer, delivery/receipt, signed personnel action/no-action and OIG recommendation dispositions in PD01–PD04.

## 4. Net terminology and a separate Boston recording trail must stay separate

The held-text locator for Chapter 4 led to three pages of an “Unofficial, uncorrected” transcript as labeled by the release itself: PDF pp. 260–262, printed pp. 44–46. The exchange discusses an initially internal FAA tactical net and a later primary net involving outside participants. The witness acknowledges not having previously understood the two-net distinction. These are transcript statements and questions, not the handbook itself, an authenticated participation log or measured activation times. Only these three fragment pages were read here, not the complete interview. They caution against assuming every reference to “the net” identifies the same communication path. [PDF pp. 260–262](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=260).

Three October 18 date hits in the separate FAA/NEADS release concern a Boston Center/FBI recording-transfer trail, not the reorganization memo:

- PDF p. 149, bearing visible page 147: a copied communication headed “Information Release to FBI” reports a Sector 46R transcript covering 1220–1240 UTC and three DAT voice-recording selections: Sector 46/Channel 27, 1220–1226 UTC; Sector 32/Channel 07, 1223:50–1226 UTC; Sector 46/Channel 07, 1230:30–1234:30 UTC. The heading date is October 18, 2001. This is a transfer representation, not independent receipt or validation of the audio. [Source p. 149](https://www.governmentattic.org/6docs/9-11FAA-NEADS-NORAD_2001-2005.pdf#page=149); R08.
- PDF pp. 173 and 188, visible pp. 171 and 186: two lists name an October 18, 2001 “ZBW/FBI Chain of Custody Document” as items 31 and 29. These are two inventory references, not two recovered custody forms or two independent transfers. The specific underlying form remains a target in this pass. [p. 173](https://www.governmentattic.org/6docs/9-11FAA-NEADS-NORAD_2001-2005.pdf#page=173) and [p. 188](https://www.governmentattic.org/6docs/9-11FAA-NEADS-NORAD_2001-2005.pdf#page=188); R09–R10.

Boston Center recordings do not establish recording capability at the Washington Operations Center. No audio was acquired, decoded or aligned here. The text derivative spells the visible DAT label “OAT”; this is a text-extraction reading difference, preserved in R08, not an inconsistency asserted in the source's underlying recording format. No time-zone conversion or continuity/custody completion was performed.

## Reading, verification and limits
