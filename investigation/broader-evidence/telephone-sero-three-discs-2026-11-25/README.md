- **Physical p. 55:** an FD-302 records a September 12, 2001 investigation and dictation, with September 13 transcription, concerning lead controls **DL267 and CE66**. The report says a reservation-center systems analyst supplied three CD-ROMs: one WAV copy; a second containing both WAV and a proprietary Rockwell-format recording; and a third described as an exact duplicate of the second. It says the first two were placed into evidence and the third in a 1A envelope as an investigative work copy. These are report assertions about supplied and placed media, not an inspection or independent bitwise comparison of the CDs.
- **Physical p. 57:** a continuation of a separately dated interview, headed September 11, describes an emergency-button recording system with an approximately four-minute limit. It reports that a WAV conversion was emailed to corporate security and a Rockwell-format version was routed to a standalone computer for protection. These are the interviewee's reported sequence and technical descriptions; the software, original configuration, message, computer and files were not examined.
- **Prior inventory:** the September 13 Charlotte communication separately lists two original-labeled CD items and says a work copy in a 1A envelope would be sent under separate cover. Keep the interview's reported placement and the inventory's later future-tense dispatch distinct; neither establishes that a recipient actually received that envelope. ## Uncertainties and tests

The p. 55 report calls the three CDs “distinct” and also calls the third an exact duplicate of the second. Preserve both formulations: the wording alone does not establish whether “distinct” refers to physical discs, content or some other distinction. Do not count three discs as three independent calls, original masters, or independently corroborating witnesses. The same p. 57 account asserts that only the first four minutes of a longer call were captured; the known emergency-button and timer descriptions must be examined with original system timing and settings before treating this as a calibrated start-of-call measurement. It does not establish intentional truncation.
