# Handwritten SOC interview notes: source trail, time labels and packet boundary

**November 7, 2026 — TEL02/TEL04; AUD016-A1; TC05–TC06; TI03–TI05.** Two newly acquired archival-mirror PDFs add handwritten working material to the source comparison with the released April 26, 2004 American Airlines SOC memorandum. They do not supply carrier events, recordings, clock calibration or an authenticated complete interview history.

## Newly acquired records and reading scope

The preserved public ZIP index identifies **Team 7 Box 1, AAL SOC Notes, items 315 and 313**. Exact member names, archive offsets, sizes, CRC32 and SHA-256 are in [SOURCE-PINS.json](SOURCE-PINS.json). Only two bounded member ranges were retrieved from the [public Commission-records ZIP mirror](https://archive.org/download/911datasets/NARA_9-11_Commission_Records_Released_Jan_14_2009.zip); no complete multi-gigabyte archive was downloaded.

- **315:** two handwritten pages, **112,016 bytes**, SHA-256 **c3199af258b18ea5a7b6f57ed11ff29a9a071dfd011b970a6a71bef495936c01**. Page 1 labels a telephone interview with SOC personnel and gives **4/26/04**.
- **313:** eleven handwritten pages, **381,711 bytes**, SHA-256 **930750e419d3e078789c15d2f4232cde56b873611cc994a9b2eb31f6a959be4b**. Its opening gives an SOC heading and overlapping participants, but no confidently identified meeting date or note author. Relationship to the dated packet and typed memorandum is a supported source association, not an authenticated author/event join.

Both opening pages carry a **01-23-2006 screening stamp**. That date does not establish when the notes were written or publicly released. The acquisition authenticates these current mirror bytes and ZIP-member relationships, not an unbroken agency delivery chain. Existing redactions remain intact.

All **13 page images** were inspected on the verified Intel store. The detailed comparison covers the time-label and information-control passages below. Remaining pages were inspected for context, handling and assembly boundaries; no complete certified transcription, independent handwriting assessment, audio review or historical event test is claimed. OCR was inadequate for substantive handwriting, so reported readings come from the images.

## Information-control account and its limits

**313, p. 2**, lower block on the sideways page, records the direction concerning Peggy and containing/controlling information. **313, p. 4**, middle manifest block, records restricted internal manifest access and describes a computer entry. The official **MFR p. 2** separately reports the information-control instruction and manifest restriction.

The newly recovered notes therefore provide a working-document comparison for those already reported accounts. They do **not** add a second independent witness, establish an instruction's exact contemporaneous wording, prove an access change was executed, or identify deletion. Authorized incident controls, an improper restriction and incomplete recollection remain alternatives. Relevant policy, the actual instruction, permission changes and audit logs remain missing.

Conversely, **315 p. 1**, Herndon/in-flight-center block, and **313 p. 2**, corresponding conditional discussion, record a reported willingness to act on a warning request. **313 p. 6** also retains confusion/conflicting-information and landing-priority context. These passages must accompany any use of the information-control account. They neither validate compliance nor resolve whether a warning request arrived.

## Eleven pages do not equal eleven pages of one SOC interview

**313 p. 8** visibly starts a **post-interview** section; pp. **8–11** have a separate handwritten **(1)–(4)** sequence and discuss other operational records. Page **10** contains a **10874–10913** chronology locator. The named underlying chronology and its exact relationship to those notes remain unacquired.

The appended material contains additional **unverified record-handling claims** that should not be lost merely because the context is uncertain. On **p. 9**, lower block, a passage describes notes being integrated into another record and earlier notes being **tossed**. On **p. 10**, lower block, a passage describes side-bar calls on a multi-line phone as not recorded. These are handwritten source reports, not a destruction authorization, custody receipt or measured recording-completeness result. Neither is assigned here to the SOC, FAA, a named note-taker or a particular recording.

Page **9** also begins with a question about gaps and includes both a no-gap statement and a stopped-tape/unknown-time description. Their referents and sequence remain unclear; they are preserved without turning them into a numerical gap or an intentional-erasure finding. The original notes, consolidation history, recording/session logs, referenced chronology and full interview/context records would discriminate between routine consolidation or recording limitations, mistaken notes, and improper handling. No explanation is selected.

Do not attribute every appended statement to SOC personnel, assume all pages share one meeting/date/author, or convert questions and fragmentary tape-handling notes into verified events. This pass identifies the assembly boundary and locator; it does not adopt an independent tape-gap, destruction, military-notification or recorder finding from the appended pages. Their provenance and referenced originals require separate examination.
