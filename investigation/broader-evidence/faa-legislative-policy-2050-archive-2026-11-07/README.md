# Recovered DOT policy separates QFR preparation, clearance and return

November 7, 2026. **B129 / PD01–PD04.** The previously unacquired body of **DOT Order 2050.3A** has now been recovered from a public archive capture and all six page images read. It identifies distinct procedural roles and record targets. It is a scanned policy copy, not the FAA's actual corrected answer, clearance file, signed disposition or delivery receipt.

The order's **§7(e), p. 6**, assigns the Operating Administrations primary responsibility for editing their witnesses' transcripts and preparing answers to questions for the record. It assigns clearance to the General Counsel's Office of Legislation, and returning corrected transcripts and QFR answers to the Assistant Secretary for Governmental Affairs / Office of Congressional Affairs. These are different functions. Evidence of a draft or internal clearance would not, alone, establish return or receipt. [Recovered order, p. 6](https://web.archive.org/web/20250205000544id_/https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/docs/dotorders/DOT2050.3A_Preparation%20of%20Legislation%2C%20Legislative%20Reports%20and%20Legislative%20Testimony_22-Apr-86.pdf#page=6).

## Scope limits that could change the interpretation

**This is not yet a finding that those offices controlled the target answer.** Section 2, p. 1, addresses DOT legislation/reports and testimony before congressional committees, with an appropriations exception. Section 6(a), p. 3, defines a legislative report around pending or proposed legislation. Its mention of a commission is within that definition; it does not identify the 9/11 Commission or automatically cover every response to it. The May 22, 2003 Commission QFR and its post-sunset correction need their own classification, delegation, operative-version and actual-routing evidence. [Scope, p. 1](https://web.archive.org/web/20250205000544id_/https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/docs/dotorders/DOT2050.3A_Preparation%20of%20Legislation%2C%20Legislative%20Reports%20and%20Legislative%20Testimony_22-Apr-86.pdf#page=1).

Section 7(e) allocates returning corrected transcripts and QFR responses. It does not, by itself, identify a corrected May 2003 answer, its later recipient, a new correction after an error was discovered, or a case-specific post-investigation deadline. Section 6(c), p. 4, also preserves the Inspector General's legislative authority. Do not substitute ordinary testimony clearance for OIG investigation follow-up or personnel authority.

The earlier FAA AOA-3 and DOT Secretariat correspondence routes remain separate leads. This order does not select one as the controlling historical route or erase conflicting preparation, awareness and correction accounts. The policy could prove inapplicable, be supplemented, or describe a route that was followed; those alternatives require actual records. It supplies no new evidence of deliberate noncorrection or coordination.
