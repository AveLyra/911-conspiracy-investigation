# FAA correction and administrative-action follow-up

Review date: October 27, 2026. This is an additive public-source research supplement to [the prior FAA study](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/2e8a072977f1fd387a2cb9e3e0336e5268a7d895/investigation/broader-evidence/faa-knowledge-correction-trail-2026-10-26/README.md). Earlier sources, conflicting accounts and published assessments remain unchanged. The new progress is a more specific route to missing records, **not recovery of the final FAA correction or personnel decision**.

## In plain English

In 2006, DOT's inspector general said FAA should correct a misleading answer given to the 9/11 Commission and consider administrative action against two executives. An investigation status screen later says the investigation closed. Those are three different events: identifying an error, deciding what to do about it, and closing an investigator's case. The closing screen does not tell us that the answer was corrected or that anyone was disciplined. [Original report, p12](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cc2006085.pdf#page=12); [released case records, pp6–7, 15–16](https://www.governmentattic.org/6docs/DOT-OIG-FAA9-11_2001-2006.pdf#page=6). Pins P11–P13 and V01–V02 in [SOURCE-PINS.json](SOURCE-PINS.json).

The new documents name files that could answer the follow-up question. Finding those file categories helps focus acquisition. It does not show that these particular records existed, survived, were searched, were withheld or were destroyed.

## Newly pinned retrieval leads

1. **FAA's historical records schedule distinguishes the agency's OIG reply from follow-up verification.** Item 2970(1) covers OIG audit reports, coordination papers and documents used to formulate an agency reply; 2970(2) covers independent reviews of whether FAA's commitments were implemented. The former gives ten years for closed cases, conditional on completed action; the latter gives four years after final action. These are different descriptions and triggers, not proof of actual disposal. The schedule concerns audits; applicability to this correspondence/investigation remains open. [Order 1350.15C, chapter 5, printed p95/physical p27](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/order/finance/1350-15C/media/chapter5.pdf#page=27), P01.

2. **A historical audit order describes a recommendation-by-recommendation trail.** Paragraph 9(d) names each recommendation, response, planned action/date or nonconcurrence, higher-management decisions, and action taken. Paragraphs 14(d) and 15 describe coordinated headquarters replies. This gives concrete record types and historical custody leads, not verified 2006 custodians or an established deadline violation. The order is dated July 17, 1984, concerns audits, and expressly separates audit follow-up procedures. Its 2006 validity and applicability to CC-2006-085 must be checked. [Order 2970.3, pp1–3, 6–7](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/FAA_Order_2970.3.pdf#page=3), P03–P07, V03/V05. The 2001 checklist separately lists Order 2900.1, Audit Followup; its text was not recovered. [Checklist, printed p22/physical p28](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/0000.5E.pdf#page=28), V04. DOT 8000.1A's applicable investigation provisions remain another missing policy source.

3. **Personnel disposition has a separate possible record route.** Item 3770(2) describes proposed adverse actions, supporting materials, replies, decisions, reversals and appeals, and excludes reprimands filed in the Official Personnel Folder. Its stated retention is five years after case closure or court-action conclusion. This does not establish that the recommended consideration produced any covered action, that FAA executives fell under the cited provisions, or that these files are publicly releasable. A considered-and-declined decision could be elsewhere. [Order 1350.15C, chapter 6, printed p118/physical p22](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/order/finance/1350-15C/media/chapter6.pdf#page=22), P02.

4. **Contemporary reporting points to a separate FAA public statement.** A September 2, 2006 New York Times article, reproduced in the release, quotes FAA welcoming the report's finding on knowing falsehoods. It reports no disclosure of whether disciplinary consideration would follow. That is a reported press position at the time, not a final management decision or proof FAA subsequently did nothing. The native statement and its clearance trail remain missing. [Article locator](https://www.nytimes.com/2006/09/02/washington/02faa.html); [reproduction, physical p20](https://www.governmentattic.org/6docs/DOT-OIG-FAA9-11_2001-2006.pdf#page=20). The full copyrighted article is not republished in this supplement.

The schedule's age matters: FAA's index marks 1350.15C cancelled on December 24, 2013; the replacement order expressly cancels it. Do not apply its historic periods as present disposal instructions. [Official index](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/orders_notices/index.cfm/go/document.information/documentid/13134); [1350.14B, p1, paragraph 4](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/FAA_1350.14B.pdf#page=1), P08/V06. Item-by-item successor authority, holds, transfers and actual disposition are unverified.

## What remains unresolved

This pass did **not** locate the corrected QFR answer and delivery acknowledgment, a signed action/no-action determination, or an OIG recommendation-implementation/closure record. Searches of newly downloaded FAA FY2006/FY2007 annual-report text did not locate the case-specific disposition. Those reports are not complete case registers; this negative locator result cannot establish nonexistence. [Locator results](TEXT-LOCATOR-RESULTS.json); [method and access limits](METHOD.md).

[CONFLICTS.md](CONFLICTS.md) carries the differing awareness accounts, expected-correction versus liaison-denial accounts, the pending/issued/closed entries, and the distinction between notification-intent findings and the specific known-error finding forward without adjudication. It retains links to the prior 45-pin study and its ten open-record groups; this supplement does not replace them or add new master-register IDs.

An institutional self-protection or deliberate noncorrection hypothesis remains testable, but the new policies do not substantiate it. The strongest objection is that a correction or reasoned decision may exist outside the indexed/released material, under another system or appropriate privacy restrictions. Evidence of deliberate suppression would require an authenticated decision, instruction or comparable affirmative link. Evidence of a delivered correction and documented consideration could weaken that interpretation, while leaving the earlier historical failure intact.
