# Scooby report 24: UAF model-lineage source check

November 12, 2026. Selected recovered desk-audit claims are checked against the [UAF final report](https://files.wtc7report.org/file/public-download/A-Structural-Reevaluation-of-the-Collapse-of-World-Trade-Center-7-March2020.pdf) and [NIST’s WTC 7 FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation). This is a documentary comparison, not inspection or execution of the corresponding native solver jobs.

| Claim | Source and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| UAF increased the thermal-expansion coefficient in a particular counterfactual. | UAF printed pp. 75 and 80 says it increased the coefficient for four specified beams by approximately 40% to 11.5 × 10⁻⁶ per °F, and removed the side-plate protrusion in the separate test. This is a reported test input, not a measurement of the actual steel or evidence that NIST used the same value. The alleged 8.2 × 10⁻⁶ input and its relationship to the published figure require the matching native file, includes and solver log. |
| UAF’s Figure 3.14 is visibly labeled W21×44. | UAF printed p. 86 captions and labels the rendered figure W21×44 G3005 without bracing. Its p. 84 attributes W21×44 to a cited drawing and claims NIST used W24×55. This verifies what UAF published, not the drawing’s complete revision chain or the actual section in the executed NIST and UAF jobs. The [prior image-layer check](../../wtc7/uaf-g3005-native-section-2026-10-21/README.md) records a different label in an underlying raster; a visible label and a native section assignment are separate evidence. |
| The visually matched whole-building scenario imposed failure timing and a time-dependent function. | UAF printed pp. 105–106 describes simultaneous failure of core columns over eight stories followed 1.3 seconds later by exterior-column failure and says a time-dependent acceleration function represented impact resistance. Matching a trajectory under those prescribed conditions demonstrates the model’s reported behavior under those inputs; it does not by itself identify the physical cause of the failures or establish that the same trajectory would arise under an unforced model. The report does not by itself establish the specific F1–F4 file lineage or whether any function was tuned. |
| NIST’s partial-floor and 16-story models were identical in boundary conditions. | **Contrary source:** NIST’s FAQ, question 28, distinguishes the partial-floor model’s uniform monotonic thermal loading and fixed lateral column restraints from the 16-story model’s computed thermal histories and laterally movable columns. A criticism of one model must be mapped to the exact analysis before assuming it applies to the other; the FAQ itself is NIST’s account, not a completed native-model verification. |

**Open:** final figure-to-input/log/output linkage; authenticated Model 16 material tables and overrides; native G3005 section assignment; time-function definitions and derivation; calibrated observation and independent baseline tests; and the remaining report-24 claims and attachments. The current documents do not establish how the columns failed, explosives placement, or a UMS role.
