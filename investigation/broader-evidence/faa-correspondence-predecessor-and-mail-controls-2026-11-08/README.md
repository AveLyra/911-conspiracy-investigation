## Dispatch and intake

FAA Order **1770.11C**, December 30, 1987, describes separate artifacts for registered or certified mail. Section 2-25 places an office routing symbol on the envelope and on a sender’s return-receipt card when requested. Table 2-1 distinguishes a sender’s optional return receipt from a postal delivery receipt and states a two-year retention at the post office for the latter. Sections 3-1 and 3-3–3-6 describe selective controls and time stamping, direct routing, and follow-up at the action office. The May 2001 checklist lists `APF-100` for this order. The current FAA order entry lists `Active` and `APM-400`. The office identifiers belong to different source dates. [Mail order, physical pp. 13–14, §§2-25 and 3-1–3-6](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/1770.11C.pdf#page=13) · [2001 checklist, p. 21](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/0000.5E.pdf#page=21) · [Current order entry](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/orders_notices/index.cfm/go/document.information/documentID/7184)

If an actual package identifies registered or certified mail, compare the sender’s retained envelope, mailing receipt or tracking identifier with the recipient’s intake and the controlled correspondence record. A sender-held receipt and a postal retention table answer different questions. If the package identifies electronic dispatch, seek the native transmittal and acknowledgment instead. The orders supply conditional retrieval routes. The actual dispatch medium, delivery, and retention of any case-specific artifact remain open.
