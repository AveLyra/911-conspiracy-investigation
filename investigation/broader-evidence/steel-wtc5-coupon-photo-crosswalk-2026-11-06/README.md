# Candidate link between FEMA’s WTC 5 field sample and NIST coupon GZ-1

November 6, 2026 · STL02–STL03 / AUD011 / AUD016 / B188 / H005

FEMA’s photograph of a steel connection labeled **WTC 5, 8th Floor** shows a close visual match to the front of NIST’s **GZ-1** coupon. The painted pattern, diagonal surface mark, plate outlines and fasteners match in position. A different eighth-floor coupon in the same FEMA figure also has green paint, and a sixth-floor coupon has the same eight-fastener layout. Both differ in additional features. This is a candidate link between two publication records and a practical comparison for testing WTC 7 specimen attribution. The original specimen tags, field-to-lab transfer and any testing remain separate questions. ## Candidate comparison and controls

The top photo on FEMA p. 19 and the NIST draft front show two columns of four fasteners on a smaller rectangular plate at left. The main plate extends above, right and below it. Both show green paint extending across the inside bolt column onto the main plate, a separate green band toward the right edge, and a short diagonal brown mark above the smaller plate. The published FEMA photo has no visible GZ-1 label. The NIST figure supplies that label but gives no floor.

I reviewed all seven FEMA photo panels against the front image. On p. 19 the middle panel has a scalloped torn edge without the bolted plate, and the bottom panel has eight fasteners but a large curved margin and a separate line of holes. On p. 20 the top panel has six visible fasteners on the right, the middle has three on the left and open tear-outs on the right, and the bottom is a curved fragment without the eight-fastener plate. The p. 21 sample is a bent fragment. The p. 20 middle panel is a useful control for green paint and the eighth-floor label. The p. 19 bottom panel is a control for the eight-fastener layout. Only p. 19 top matches both features together with the front plate outline and diagonal mark. Similar standard connections and the limited published viewpoints leave room for a mistaken photo match.

As a geometry check, I manually marked eight fastener centers in each embedded photograph and fit an affine mapping from FEMA to the NIST draft. The fitted points differ by **2.3 px RMS**. Six separately marked plate-corner and surface-mark points differ by **15.1 px RMS**, with a maximum of **28.6 px**, in the **433 × 577** NIST image. These are approximate image measurements from a fit on the selected pair. They provide no physical dimensions, identity probability or calibrated error rate. The control comparison was visual.
