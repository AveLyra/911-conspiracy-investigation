# Archival photograph: steel prepared for transport to the State Museum

**November 3, 2026.** A specific New York State Archives photograph and its catalog record provide a new input to the steel-preservation search. The archive dates it **June 2002**, attributes the image to **Mark Schaming**, and captions the load as **North Tower steel ready to move to the New York State Museum**. The preserved, actually viewed image shows a large folded metal assembly resting on a flatbed trailer. Shipment, receipt, original floor and accession identity remain unverified.

This is partial operational-record progress within **STL01–STL03, AUD011/AUD016 and B188/H005**. It does not complete the steel provenance chain or any of the original eighteen acquisition/testing criteria. The FAA correction/disposition originals, Beverly Hills certification and approved attachments, and the other tracks' outstanding native inputs remain TODOs.

## What was actually visible

The acquired display rendition is a **700×457 JPEG**. It shows a folded structural-looking metal assembly on a flatbed. No reliable specimen number, accession label, original member marking or floor identification was read. No scale measurement, alloy identification, temperature estimate or damage-mechanism analysis was performed. Apparent shape alone does not establish tower/floor provenance, when deformation occurred or which later exhibit contains the piece.

The caption establishes that the archive describes the scene as preparation for museum transport. It does not itself establish departure, completed delivery, title transfer, museum accession or present availability. A dispatch/receipt/loan/intake crosswalk would test those later events. A museum destination in a caption also does not establish that the object was a NIST scientific sample or was available for the specific tests previously discussed.

The strongest objection to treating this as a recovered custody chain is the missing item-level join: a photograph with an attributed destination is not a signed transfer or receipt. Conversely, the image is pertinent contrary evidence to a blanket claim that no structural material was retained for potential preservation. It does not answer whether the scientific candidates were adequately preserved or representative.
