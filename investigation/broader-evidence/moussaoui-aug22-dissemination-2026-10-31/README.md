Research date: October 31, 2026. **The court copy supplies an eight-page FBI communication with explicit investigative leads and a stated aircraft-attack suspicion before September 11. ## What the communication actually records

**Distribution and task setting.** Page 1 is marked PRIORITY, dated 08/22/2001, and originates in Minneapolis. It lists Counterterrorism, Oklahoma City and Memphis, references case 199M-MP-60130, Serial 3, and says the information was discussed in August 21 telephone calls. The full-field-investigation field says 08/15/2001. Page 8 sets three leads: headquarters at ITOS/RFU receives an information lead; Oklahoma City receives an identity-coordination task plus investigation into possible terrorist or criminal activity; Memphis receives an identity-coordination task. These are visible destinations and requested work, not dispatch logs, acknowledged receipt, the exact package a legal reviewer examined, or proof of completed work. [Pages 1 and 8](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/327.pdf#page=8), AD01 and AD07.

**Suspicion is explicit; the directing group is not established.** Page 2 reports Minneapolis's belief that Moussaoui was preparing for international terrorism. Page 7 says Minneapolis opines that Moussaoui and Al-Attas were preparing an attack involving a Boeing 747-400, directed by an as-yet-unknown international group. Those are contemporaneously dated investigator assessments in this court representation. They are not an independently verified operational plan, a demonstrated al-Qaeda link, proof of a specific target/date, or evidence that every recipient knew an attack would occur. Page 7 also alleges that associates deliberately downplayed the threat. Preserve that allegation as the author's interpretation, not a finding independently established here. [Pages 2 and 7](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/327.pdf#page=7), AD02.

**The supporting accounts are disputed and qualified within the memo.** Pages 4–5 report an interviewee's account of ordinary flight training, peaceful study, denial of violent discussions, and a practical explanation for arranging bail. Pages 6–7 argue that inconsistencies and other information suggest deception and involvement. Page 4 says investigators are trying to establish an identity match; page 8 repeats that task. Part of the comparison is masked. Page 6's criminal-history argument itself includes a probability qualification. The investigator's interpretation, the reported contrary account and the unresolved identity question all remain visible. Neither this reading nor similarity of names resolves them. Underlying interviews, database returns and identity-resolution records would be needed. [Pages 4–8](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/327.pdf#page=4), AD03–AD04.

**A telephone-record lead has a specific source chain.** Page 3 reports that a detention-unit sergeant supplied a call log: 13 attempts between 1:07 p.m. and 4:14 p.m., with the first 12 refused, busy or unanswered and a final conversation described as heated. The language and tone are reported characterizations; the memo says the officer presumed the language. Pages 6–7 refer back to the same calling pattern. This is not 13 demonstrated completed conversations, a translated recording, an independent carrier return or several independent corroborations. No telephone number, address, birth date or other unnecessary personal identifier is reproduced in these notes. The original log, event definitions, any recording and the underlying interview are concrete TODOs. [Page 3](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/327.pdf#page=3), AD05.

**Training and custody descriptions need their source records.** Page 2 reports a request for 747-400 instruction sufficient to take off and land. That wording is preserved; no contradictory source is invented or selected to replace it. Original school records, instructor interviews and actual requested lessons would test it. Page 3 reports a conditional estimate of deportation and return of property unless investigative progress was made. An estimate is not an executed deportation, an authorization to search, a completed property return or the later France proposal's disposition. [Pages 2–3](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/327.pdf#page=2), AD06–AD07.
