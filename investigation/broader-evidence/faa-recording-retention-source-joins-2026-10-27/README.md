## J02 — Retention practice and documented authority remain separate

[Handwritten2210](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2210) records DEN retention for30days, DVD export, a recorder description and a **6/1/05** follow-up. In the final handwritten sentence, **not** is inserted above the line in the statement about a documented30-day procedure. This reading is retained explicitly, rather than silently dropping the small insertion.

[Typed2276](https://www.governmentattic.org/42docs/DOToigFAA9-11interviews_2002-2006.pdf#page=2276),lines20–24, says recordings were held30days, calls the equipment THALES, describes a30-day maximum recording capacity and computer-readable DVD export. Its June1 addendum,lines30–31, states: **“There is currently no documented procedure for maintaining the tape-recordings of the DEN.”**

The formulations differ in breadth: the handwritten follow-up addresses documentation of keeping recordings30days; the typed addendum addresses maintenance of DEN recordings generally. Preserve both. Neither supplies an actual retention schedule, approved instruction, automatic-overwrite configuration, disposition log, hold record or proof that specific audio was lost. Hardware capacity is not itself legal disposition authority, and export capability is not proof an export occurred. No legal violation or actual destruction date is determined here.

The strongest contrary evidence to a blanket recording-loss claim is the same account's statement that DEN was always recorded and could be exported. Conversely, capability and a witness's claimed practice do not establish complete preservation. Actual equipment configuration, storage/overwrite/export logs, governing procedures and preservation instructions would distinguish these possibilities. The THALES reference concerns WOCC telephone recording; its model/firmware and installed configuration remain unverified.
