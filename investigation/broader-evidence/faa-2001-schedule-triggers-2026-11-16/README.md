# FAA records schedule: two five-year starting points

November 16, 2026 · B129 / FAA PD04. The applicable category and disposition for any particular 9/11 record remain unverified.

## Source observations

- [FAA Order 1350.15C, chapter 11, item 8020(1)](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/order/finance/1350-15C/media/chapter11.pdf#page=3), dated August 29, 2001, printed page 197 / physical page 3, describes aircraft accident/incident report packages prepared and maintained by air traffic field facilities. Its disposition reads **“Destroy 5 years after completion of investigation.”** It separately provides an exception where specific retention instructions are received and a conditional 90-day return-to-service rule for a removed tape where no further data is requested or provided. Computer readout data is separately described as remaining at the originating facility under appropriate orders. The row cites **II-NNA-1102 Item 23, approved July 12, 1954**. Adjacent items 8020(2) and (3) describe distinct categories; they are not silently combined here.
- [FAA Order 8020.11B, 2000 library copy](https://libraryonline.erau.edu/online-full-text/books-online/1177.pdf#page=62), paragraph 79a, printed page 326 / physical page 62, says a formal accident file **“shall be destroyed 5 years after the accident date”**, with a litigation exception, and refers to the latest edition of Order 1350.15. Prior research visually inspected this page; that preserved PDF has SHA-256 `4a079eef3618704785a50283ca6a28fb26be694191570a7f0b8549893ef8e3cf`.

## Limits and tests

Preserve **five years after investigation completion** and **five years after the accident date** as separate source descriptions. The records may have different scope, dates, authority, exceptions or implementation; none has been established as the explanation and no rule is selected as controlling. No specific file has been classified under both texts. Neither instruction proves any particular record was destroyed, the presence or absence of a hold, or misconduct.

To test applicability, obtain the approved schedule authority and changes, the specific file's classification and custodians, applicable written instructions or litigation holds, relevant investigation-completion date, and actual disposition or transfer records. Keep recommendation resolution, investigative closure and individual record disposition separate.
