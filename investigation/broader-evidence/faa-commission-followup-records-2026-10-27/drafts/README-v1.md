## O01 — The FAA call record reports production and restrictions

The [AWA661 PDF](https://catalog.archives.gov/medialz/dc-metro/rg-237/7419198-9-11-faa/5-Miscellaneous/e-Other/5-AWA-661-Record-of-Telephone-Calls-9-11-Commission-Followup-.pdf#page=1) is headed Record of Telephone Calls /9/11Commission Follow-up /AEA/ANE/AGL Regions and dated **10-03-03,1500–1600EDT**. Reading that printed date as October3,2003 is an interpretation of its format; the original string is preserved here. It records regional officials’ accounts, not an audio recording of those calls or the underlying legal instructions.

- **AEA / New York:** the note says the Commission received a complete accident-file index, copies of all requested information and controller statements; the facility felt all requests had been met. This is affirmative production evidence in the record and must not be omitted. F02,p1,extracted lines7–12.
- **AGL:** the note says the Commission did not receive the entire accident file; approximately40 requested pieces were copied and sent for release. It says the facility received guidance from **AAT-200 litigation personnel to release all data concerning UAL93 but nothing else**. It separately reports that the Commission sought field copies of headquarters telecons because headquarters was not releasing them. That latter claim has additional attribution layers: the note-taker records a regional official relaying the Commission’s position. F02,p1,lines13–26.
- **ANE / Boston:** the note says interviewers learned about material in the accident file absent from the formal accident package. It lists QA bulletins, lessons learned, sector maps and frequencies supplied, while saying some requested material was not released because of **ANE legal guidance about information on hold by the FBI**. F02,p1,lines30–35.

**What this establishes:** a publicly archived contemporaneous-looking FAA record reports particular release restrictions, partial production and claimed compliance. **What it does not establish:** the exact original instruction, its legal basis, what each item contained, whether another channel supplied it, final completeness, unlawful withholding or motive. No signed action/no-action decision is attached. Obtain the actual instructions, itemized inventories and receiving records before inferring their effect.

## O02 — A separate note disputes production completeness and request scope

The [AWA660 PDF](https://catalog.archives.gov/medialz/dc-metro/rg-237/7419198-9-11-faa/1-AAL-77/e-Other/1-AWA-660-Note-9-11-Commission-File.pdf#page=1) is addressed to **Shirley** and ends **Dana** onp3. It has no verified complete sender header, native email metadata or exact date. The phrase **middle of October** appears onp2. October2003 is contextual inference from the2003 request discussion, not an authenticated exact sending date. Do not silently assign a full author identity or treat this scan as proof of email delivery.

The note quotes a May7,2003 request, due May14, for all FAA tracking documents including NORAD communications. It cites DOTRequest2,items3,7,16 and19 as additional examples, while expressly retaining the broader original request. It says managers/QA officials at **ZBW,ZNY,ZID,ZOB** reported never having been asked to gather these materials or to produce them from headquarters. These are statements in one note, not four independently acquired center affidavits. F03,p1,lines5–37;p2,lines1–9;p3,lines2–4.

Its next steps insist on the complete **accident file**, rather than the **accident package**; all three Boston boxes if those contain relevant material; and all FAA facilities, not merely the four visited. It specifically identifies a Cleveland management memo, the Indianapolis outside-contractor review of the Flight77 radar-coverage lapse, and centers’ SATORI reconstructions. F03,p2,lines11–32. Page4 is a handwritten locator sheet; authorship, date and unclear characters remain unverified. A handwritten recording lead is not proof the recording was made, preserved or supplied.

**Preserved difference:** AWA661 records facility-level claimed compliance and some productions; AWA660 describes unproduced materials and rejects a narrower file/package reading. Their dates, speakers, request scopes and later production sequence are not established as identical. We do not smooth them into one account, label one false, or infer concealment. Original requests and amendments, HQ-to-center tasking, actual legal guidance, center inventories, release manifests and receiving acknowledgments would test the relationship.

## What changes, what remains open

This pass moves from search-result titles to five preserved official PDF entities and fully reads three short records. It identifies specific release-instruction, tasking, interview and source-envelope targets. It does **not** close **PD01–PD04**, establish a deliberate cover-up, authenticate an FAA correction’s actual delivery, resolve conflicting accounts, complete a recorder reconstruction or validate a materials/steel hypothesis. A complete enlarged review remains pending77pages. Other tracks retain their previously documented dependencies; [OPEN-RECORDS.md](OPEN-RECORDS.md) carries them forward.

A cover-up hypothesis would gain support from authenticated instructions or communications showing knowing suppression or alteration and actual withheld material inconsistent with representations. It would be weakened by contemporaneous complete transmissions, coherent lawful handling records, corrected answers and receiving acknowledgments. Neither account can be chosen from these scans alone. The strongest objection to treating the new material as proof of wrongdoing is that it records **disputed scope and channel-specific restrictions as well as production**, without the actual instructions, final disposition or complete receipt trail.
