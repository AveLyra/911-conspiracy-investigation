## Impact placement and condition descriptions

- **Museum installation note.** Refers to both pieces as impact steel and says they were **“directly above the point of impact of Flight 11.”** The two accession records separately attribute damage to the impact. [Installation note](https://www.911memorial.org/connect/blog/museum-construction-progressing-large-artifacts-installation) · [M-27](https://collection.911memorial.org/Detail/objects/1932/rel/1) · [M-2](https://collection.911memorial.org/Detail/objects/1935/rel/1)
- **NIST chapter 3.** Describes **M-27** as hit directly by the nose and fuselage. Describes **M-2** as immediately above M-27 and struck by the upper fuselage and vertical stabilizer. Figure 3–1 depicts their positions within the annotated impact region. The conclusions state that pre-collapse imagery supported comparison of M-2 with its recovered condition, and that insufficient detail prevented the same comparison for M-27. These passages describe different claims about placement and the extent to which damage could be compared. [Printed p. 98](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3cv1.pdf#page=191) · [Figure 3–1, p. 120](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3cv1.pdf#page=169) · [Conclusions, p. 279](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3cv1.pdf#page=10)

The difference is material when joining photographs or test coupons to a facade position. No physical remeasurement, engineering recalculation or photo-to-object authentication was performed. These are North Tower records. The separate WTC 7 attribution question still needs item-specific evidence.
