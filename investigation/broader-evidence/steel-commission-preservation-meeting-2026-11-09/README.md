# Steel preservation concerns recorded by Commission staff in September 2003

Reviewed November 9, 2026 Eastern. STL01–STL03 / B188 / H005. Two separately filed Commission records document a preservation concern raised in the same meeting. The handwritten notes also retain a decision-routing question that is less explicit in the formal memorandum.

## What the records say

| Record | Observation | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| September 22, 2003 meeting memorandum, archive item 01034, PDF p. 1 lines 5–11 and p. 3 lines 22–29, item 10 | The memo identifies Emily Walker as preparer and Team 9. It reports that participants were concerned that “the steel was shipped out so quickly,” felt “that no one was in control,” and said “that NIST was not looking into this issue.” It records their description of NIST as “fact-finding, not fault-finding.” | A staff account of the participants’ concerns. The quoted statement refers to “this issue” in a paragraph about steel shipment, control and preservation. |
| Handwritten notes, NYC Box 7 item 771, PDF p. 1 header and p. 4 middle block | The header visibly reads “9/22/03” and “Emily Walker.” The steel block reads “Steel shipped out,” “why did this get out,” and “left w/ steel decision.” The following handwriting asks about steel being “destroyed?” and records “NIST not looking at / the issue” and “NIST fact-finding not fault finding.” The slash between “at” and “the” marks a visible line break. | These are shorthand notes. The routing fragment leaves the decision-maker unspecified. The question mark belongs to the handwritten record. |
| NIST May 2003 progress report, §3.6, printed p. 30 / PDF p. 36, lines 3–29 | NIST reported **nearly 250 pieces in its possession**, **235 cataloged as of March 28, 2003**, examination at salvage yards and JFK, and **12 specimens transported to NIST**. It described metallurgical and mechanical work on recovered steel. | The surviving-steel collection and analysis are documented. The cited section does not trace who authorized removal or processing, what objections reached them, or the scope of any review of those decisions. |
| Press item forwarded September 22, included in item 01034, PDF pp. 35–36, p. 36 lines 1–2 | The article attributes to Sunder a report of **37 pieces of steel from near the airplanes’ impact zones** recovered from scrapyards and other sources. | A forwarded newspaper account with a stated population. No item list or WTC 7 specimen identification accompanies the count in this passage. |

The memo and notes trace the issue to a dated Commission briefing. They are two records of the same meeting and identify the same staff preparer. The notes’ routing question and the memo’s report about perceived control remain separately attributed. NIST’s documented salvage-yard and lab work directly bears on a broad reading that NIST ignored the steel. The narrower issue of whether preservation decisions were examined, and by whom, remains unresolved. The published quantities above describe their respective populations as reported. An item-level comparison would require the underlying lists and selection rules.
