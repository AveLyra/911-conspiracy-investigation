# Flight 93: FAA phone-record leads and duration checks

**Scope.** A Commission staff note describes two 2001 FAA operational-line reports. The National Archives finding aid identifies digitized recordings for those lines. We retrieved the indicated first files and measured their reported durations. We have **not verified the words on either recording**, matched the cited passages to them, identified the passenger, or verified the clock alignment. This is an acquisition and source-comparison lead, not proof of a particular call, alteration, or conspiracy.

## Records and observations

1. A March 21, 2004 Commission staff email, in the January 2009 Commission records release, identifies **severe weather position 4, line 5148 at 10:06 AM** and **line 5134 at 10:14 AM**. For the first, its author reports a regional office relaying a passenger's supposed 911 airphone call and describes knives, a bomb threat, and disconnected contact. For the second, the author reports a Newark Tower inquiry relaying an FBI request for confirmation, followed by a Command Center answer. The note is the author's later description of two operational recordings, not the passenger's original 911 call or carrier log. The note does not specify a time zone or identify the passenger. Its discussion of weapons remains attributed to a chain of relays, not independently verified here. The [National Archives finding aid, box 28](https://www.archives.gov/research/foia/faa-finding-aid-24-35), lists **DCC-1935**, operational phone 5148, position 4 severe weather, **1215–1515 UTC**; its first digitized file is labeled **1215–1415 UTC**, catalog [7598789](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7598789). It separately lists **DCC-1927**, operational phone 5134, position 34 national operations manager, **1215–1515 UTC**; its first file is labeled **1215–1415 UTC**, catalog [7598772](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7598772). These matching line labels nominate candidate recordings; they do not prove the cited exchanges are present in them. The file range labels do not establish a calibrated mapping between elapsed file position and wall-clock time.
3. The acquired, complete HTTP response bodies were independently read back and hashed. Their source URLs, sizes, hashes and catalog metadata are in [SOURCE-PINS.json](SOURCE-PINS.json). The originals are preserved in the operational media store; they are not republished here. Each two-hour range label implies 7,200 seconds if treated as an exact elapsed span. `ffprobe` 7.1.1 instead reported **7,366.508469 seconds** for 7598789 and **7,333.071750 seconds** for 7598772, respectively **166.508469** and **133.071750 seconds** longer than that span. These are format-duration reports and arithmetic comparisons, not independently calibrated recording times. Preserve the labels and measured values as separate observations. Lead-in, trailing material, timestamp convention, encoder or duration estimation are untested possibilities; the reason has not been determined. Neither excess duration establishes a missing segment, duplication, manipulation or inaccurate historic event time.
4. NARA separately labels [DCC-1291](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7599168) a full national-operations-manager-position transcript. The acquired PDF is dated October 14, 2003 and states coverage **1241:20 UTC to 1515 UTC** on physical page 1; it identifies the position but the inspected title page does not identify line 5134. This transcript is a separate derivative; its identity and completeness relative to either audio file and to the email's 10:14 passage have not been established.
