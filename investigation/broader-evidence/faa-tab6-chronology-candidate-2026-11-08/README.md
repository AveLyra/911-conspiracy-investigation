# FAA Tab 6 chronology candidates

Public derivative of the FAA source identification study. Exact retrieval and comparison locators are in the accompanying source pins.

November 8, 2026 Eastern. B129 / PD01 / PD07.

Two chronology scans were acquired while tracing the June 2003 FAA headquarters compilation named in the November 17 subpoena response. One is a newly examined, annotated copy of the already known FAA teleconference chronology. The other is a 14-page chronology using UTC and identifying Command Center positions in its entries. The checked sources leave the identity of the June compilation and the delivery of its source chronologies open. ## AWA-897 and the teleconference chronology

The National Archives lists **AWA-897** in Box 98 as a chronology. The seven-page scan begins with the typed title *Chronology of Events, September 11, 2001*. The upper-right handwriting calls it an AAT log of the September 11 operations teleconference and says **“From AAT-1.”** The annotation supplies an office lead. Its writer, date and basis are open. The visible event date supplies no date for the handwriting or the assembly of this copy. [AWA-897, physical p. 1](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/34913707)

Six distinctive anchors appear on four corresponding pages of **AWA-897** and the held **AWA-610 UAL93** copy. They include the two WTC labels on p. 1, a **1026** UAL93 military position report and the **1041** AA77 qualification on p. 2, the **1526** final timed entry on p. 6, and the p. 7 statement that specific notes are no longer being kept. The separately held six-page **Canoles 212** archive member shares four scoped wording anchors, including the p. 2 rows and the **1526** end entry. The copies contribute a version and source lead. They are dependent evidence for the events described. A common underlying file and direct copying are untested possibilities. Keep the entries, source zones and event descriptions separate. In particular, the first untitled entry describes an observation after an aircraft had already crashed. An event, an observation, a call and a later log entry can have separate time labels. Which convention generated each disputed label remains unresolved.

On p. 1, lines 3–8, the untitled chronology places a Boston Center call to the Command Center at **12:28** and an FAA Command Center manager's relay to the **Department of Defense** at **12:29**. The named recipient is department-level. The underlying line, receiver and exact communication would be needed before joining this entry to a named military command's notification record. ## What would identify Tab 6 item (13)

The November 17 fax distinguishes the **June 2003** compilation, the FAA's reported early-June disclosure that it was being developed, and its reported **July 18** delivery of source chronologies. The checked candidate text and handwriting supply no matching June creation field, July 18 intake field or Tab 6 enclosure mark. The separate **July 16** item 21 claim remains a distinct source statement. The April 16, 2004 reply also describes an already-provided chronology, most recently supplied on November 17. These are leads to check against the actual attachment manifest and item-level receiving file. The documents leave open whether either newly examined scan was a source for the compilation or part of the November production. [August 29 reply](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/34913667) · [April 16 reply](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7601904)

- [ ] Obtain the Tab 6 manifest or packet with item (13) and the contemporaneous June development reference. Compare version, page count and distinctive rows.
- [ ] Obtain the July 18 cover, attachment list and Commission intake. Keep the July 16 item 21 chronology and its receipt separate pending an item-level match.
- [ ] Locate the AAT-1 teleconference log and the underlying Position 15 source records, including the time and sender/recipient fields.
- [ ] Preserve the actual corrected May 2003 answer and its receipt, signed administrative action or no-action decision, and OIG recommendation outcomes as separate outstanding records.
