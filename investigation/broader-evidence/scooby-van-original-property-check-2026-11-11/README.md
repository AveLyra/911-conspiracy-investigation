# Van samples: recovered claim checks against FBI property pages

11 November 2026. This checks selected claims and the barcode table from the captured body of Scooby report 16. Its researcher could not access the relevant property pages; those pages are now available and were visually inspected in the [three-page FBI scan](../../urban-moving-systems/fbi-notebook-custody-2026-11-06/raw/fbi-selected-disposition-and-inventory-pages.pdf). This does not complete review of that report, its attachments or the wider 30-report set.

## What the original pages say

The July 29, 2003 FD-192 package-copy page (PDF p. 3) records acquisition on September 12, 2001 and groups the following descriptions with barcodes:

| Recorded description | Barcode | What this establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Item 15, fabric sample, with the parenthetical “explosive residue” | E01889035 | The words are part of the property description. The page supplies no analytical method, signed result or sample-to-laboratory accession; its wording alone does not establish a positive result. |
| Items 16 and 17, control swabs, respectively associated with gloves and bomb suits | E01889036 | Both descriptions occur under the same barcode. This does not establish that either control was run, its outcome or its relationship to a questioned specimen. |
| Item 34, blanket samples for explosive residue | E01889037 | The inventory identifies the samples and apparent purpose; it does not report a chemical test outcome. |

The same page identifies a larger 1B4 group and E01889578 for multiple non-sample items. Its handwritten note refers to a September 12, 2002 electronic communication. The note is a locator, not by itself a complete original-to-consolidated mapping or proof of laboratory submission. The inventory's 2001 acquisition date and 2003 print date should not be confused with a laboratory test date.

The January 27, 2014 electronic communication (PDF pp. 1–2), titled *Destruction of Evidence*, says the writer destroyed or disposed of case evidence and lists the three sample barcodes. It says the writer sought advice about disposal because no assistant U.S. attorney was assigned and was advised that a declination letter was not required. The communication is evidence of the writer's reported action and the listed barcode groups; it does not specify a separate execution date for each component, what happened to any derivatives, a chemical examination or why the samples were chosen for disposition. A crossed-out classification marking and declassification notation do not independently establish an effort to hide a positive result.

## Corrections and unresolved tests

The recovered chat's statement that disposition was undetermined reflected its failure to retrieve these pages. It must now be qualified: the 2014 communication **reports destruction/disposal of each listed barcode group**. It does not settle the item-specific execution chronology or whether any retained subsamples exist. Its broader caution remains sound: property descriptions and destruction are not laboratory reports, and neither a canine alert nor a sample's label establishes the presence or absence of explosives.

A laboratory receipt or submission, accession-to-barcode and specimen mapping, signed examination or nonexamination/cancellation record, method and controls, and item-level custody/derivative records remain the decisive comparisons. For E01889036, both control descriptions must be tracked separately within the shared barcode. This check did not inspect or update the live status of any existing FBI request, and it makes no claim about what a future production will contain.
