# Online audio, transcript, and radar record check

**Checked:** October 27, 2026
**Scope:** public NARA/Commission/NTSB sources relevant to the existing audio and radar work; record-location review, not a new clock reconstruction or audio interpretation.

## Results

### Audio and transcript

The official May 23, 2003 Commission hearing transcript is online. It records Mineta's recollection, including his express uncertainty about what order was being discussed and his statement that the aircraft was the one that struck the Pentagon. It is testimony about a remembered conversation; it is not a transcript synchronized to the selected mission-crew-commander channel recording. (Commission hearing transcript: https://www.911commission.gov/archive/hearing2/9-11Commission_Hearing_2003-05-23.htm.)

NARA's FAA finding aid lists a “9/11 Voice Tape Index” (A90-102), original Boston ATCT digital-audio tapes A90-138 through A90-141, and several digitized FAA voice-recording derivatives. The A90-140/141 entries describe physical DATs and have no linked digitized file in the finding aid. The finding aid also links some other audio derivatives, so an online audio file must be matched by facility, position, time range, source generation, and channel before treating it as the same recording. NARA says not every FAA record has been digitized. These catalog entries establish locations and online availability distinctions; they do not prove that a physical tape survives or that a derivative is complete.

The selected MCC MP3 archive remains a public derivative whose identity has not been joined to the WAV CDs described in the release letter, the continuous NEADS recordings, or the manufacturer-recovered segments discussed in contemporaneous/retrospective accounts. The earlier ASR silence-control failure remains disqualifying for that pilot's words. No new listening result, valid transcript, clock anchor, loss, or tampering finding is claimed here.

### Radar

The official NTSB radar study and NARA's digitized NTAP printout for ZID-48 (item 7596008) are already publicly available and already in the research record. NARA's FAA finding aid additionally identifies:

- A90-142, described as a list of 50 seconds of radar data, with linked digitized text records for several aircraft;
- A90-143, a physical compact disk described as containing the same 50-second radar interval, with only a subset of files linked in the online finding aid;
- DCC-1739, the AAL77 radar-track report;
- ZID-49 NTAP records, including four digitized text files; and
- ZDC-41 through ZDC-60 computer printouts, plus ZDC-61 voice-tape chain-of-custody tracking and ZDC-100 through ZDC-106 NTAP/DART records.

The A90-142/A90-143 interval is 12:24:47–12:25:37 UTC (08:24:47–08:25:37 EDT), before the hijacking interval relevant to the AA77 return track. I therefore exclude those 50-second files from the narrowed request rather than treating their catalog status as a gap in the later-flight radar record. The official finding aid's linked-file list is not a complete inventory of A90-143's original contents; that remains a catalog completeness question, not evidence of removal. The public NTSB report describes selected clock adjustments and correlation methods; the source-specific worksheets, fit residuals, exact separately cited “Time Correlation Report,” original RAPS project/CD and complete input/output lineage remain unjoined or unacquired in this workstream.

## Revised remaining-record targets

1. **NARA, RG 237 / FAA 9/11 records:** inspect and, if not already digitized/released, request the actual A90-138–A90-141 DATs or preservation copies; A90-102 voice-tape index and associated channel/position map; and contemporaneous transfer, rerecording, conversion, quality-control, timecode/reference, and custody records for the selected positions. Cite the listed identifiers and request only existing records. Ask NARA to identify records already online by item identifier rather than reproduce them.
2. **NARA/FAA radar records:** for the AA77 radar/FDR source items already located in the finding aids (including DCC-1739, ZID-48/ZID-49, the relevant ZDC printouts and DART records), request only nonpublic source versions and existing item manifests, definitions/control cards, processing settings, time/clock calibration worksheets, source-to-derivative mappings, and custody/transfer records needed to establish which source produced each released table or plot. Do not re-request the released NTSB study, NARA item 7596008, or already-linked printouts. Exclude A90-142/A90-143's 08:24 EDT 50-second interval from this AA77 return-track request.
3. **NTSB, AA77 radar-study source file:** request the existing RAPS project/CD and its native outputs, the per-recorder clock/anchor worksheet and residual/error tables, the exact separately referenced “Time Correlation Report” if held, and source/version/settings records that identify the inputs and transformations used for the published study. Request existing records, not a new analysis.

These are retrieval targets, not claims that any agency currently possesses each item. If an item was transferred, ask for the transfer record and current custodian; if it was not retained, ask for existing disposition/scheduling documentation. Do not characterize nonavailability as destruction without records supporting that conclusion.

## What this does and does not indicate

The newly verified online routes make the request scope more specific and reduce duplication. They reveal that some primary or source-generation materials are indexed but not linked as downloadable files, while selected derivative data are online. This can support a targeted record-provenance investigation. It does not by itself establish an unexplained substitution, missing flight, false time, deliberate omission, or conspiracy. The decisive tests require source identity, complete manifests, clock references, and reproducible joins.
