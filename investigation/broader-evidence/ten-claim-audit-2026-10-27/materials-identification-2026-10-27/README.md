# Materials identification: what the competing measurements can establish

This continuation of AUD011-A2 and AUD016-A5 examines frozen claims B160 and B161. It strengthens the explanation of both studies' positive evidence while retaining the sample mismatch. It does not establish either a thermitic installation or a universal paint explanation.

## The strongest case for taking Harrit's result seriously

The important argument is not simply that aluminum, iron and oxygen appear together. Harrit's MEK-treated region reportedly has approximately three times as much aluminum as oxygen, accompanied by spatial segregation from silicon. The authors also describe repeated measurements of known iron oxide to check quantification. If the composition is accurate, an explanation assigning all that region's aluminum to ordinary oxygen-bearing material has a substantive problem. The iron-oxide check is positive methodological evidence and must not disappear from our criticism. [Harrit et al., physical11–13 / printed17–19, figures12–18](https://benthamopenarchives.com/contents/pdf/TOCPJ/TOCPJ-2-7.pdf).

The new calculation makes that claim's assumptions explicit. Assigning **all measured oxygen** to Al2O3 gives the largest possible amount of aluminum that this oxygen can explain as that compound:

| Interpretation of reported Al:O = 3:1 | Maximum reported Al explainable as Al2O3 | Reported Al left unexplained by that allocation | Increase in O/Al needed to allow all Al as Al2O3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Atom ratio | 2/9 = 22.2% | 7/9 = 77.8% | 4.5 times |
| Mass ratio, rounded atomic masses Al27/O16 | 3/8 = 37.5% | 5/8 = 62.5% | 8/3 = 2.67 times |

These are conditional fractions **of the reported aluminum**, not measured metal fractions of a chip or the dust. They do not independently verify the approximate ratio. The aluminum passage does not explicitly specify its atom/mass basis or supply a complete quantification table. Its adjacent iron-oxide discussion uses Fe:O2:3 and O/Fe1.5, which are atomic ratios for Fe2O3. That context favors an atomic reading; the mass branch is a sensitivity check, not an equally supported interpretation. If it describes raw peak counts rather than corrected composition, neither calculation applies without detector/matrix response. Unaccounted aluminum is not uniquely metallic aluminum: a wrong composition estimate, substrate contribution or other chemical species could break that inference. No such explanation has been demonstrated here.

STOICHIOMETRY-RESULT.json and stoichiometry.py preserve exact rational arithmetic, explicit assumptions and controls. A correctly quantified Al2O3 control yields zero remainder. Twelve checks pass; they check algebra, not the experiment. No uncertainty interval can be assigned from the word “approximately” alone.

## Why calibration and geometry remain decisive

Quantitative EDS can work well. Treating it as incapable of useful composition measurement would be an unfair dismissal. A primary methods paper demonstrates accurate analysis under appropriate conditions, while documenting severe geometry effects for rough specimens. It explains the role of standards, matrix corrections and unnormalized analytical totals. Standards do **not** need to have the same composition as the unknown if the method accounts for the relevant effects. [Newbury and Ritchie, physical2–3,19–20,25 / printed494–495,511–512,517](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10853-014-8685-2).

That general evidence neither proves an error in Harrit's region nor supplies its error bar. The known-iron-oxide check does not, by itself, bound response/geometry error for a swollen, porous aluminum-rich region. Conversely, calling a possible geometry error the explanation would be speculation. Native counts, quantification settings, standards, raw totals, sample/support geometry and repeated same-region measurements could decide this. We have not established that the study used standardless analysis; the paper's “conventional” routine is insufficient to identify that protocol.

## What Millette contributes beyond a competing assertion

Millette's published evidence includes FTIR comparisons, electron-diffraction and thin-section microscopy, and post-MEK phase maps. Those are more specific support for his tested coating interpretation than elemental overlap or appearance alone. The appendices contain substantial published measurement output; they should not be described as offering only a bare conclusion. They still are not a same-specimen test of Harrit's region. [Millette, physical14,19,32,48,54,57–59](https://www.911facts.dk/resources/Millette/9119ProgressReport022912_rev1_030112web.pdf).

We inspected the actual plots but did not independently fit FTIR spectra or index diffraction spacings. The MEK mapping uses 20kV and a gold coating, while Harrit's key map/Al-rich measurement uses 10kV. Differences in preparation, beam conditions and specimen population remain variables to test, not established explanations of the disagreement. “No aluminum-only phase detected” is an outcome of the reported map analysis; without its sensitivity and classification settings, it is not a universal zero-aluminum-metal bound.

## A concrete sample-to-test limitation

Figure19 has four heating traces, labeled MacKinlay1, MacKinlay2, Intermont and White. Under the paper's collection mapping, those labels cover three donor groups. Neither Delessio nor Breidenbach appears in that legend. The key MEK aluminum-rich experiment is explicitly attributed to sample2, the earliest Delessio/Breidenbach collection. Therefore the published four curves cannot be counted as a displayed calorimetry test of every one of the four collections, or as a demonstrated heating result from that particular early collection. [Physical3,11,14 / printed9,17,20](https://benthamopenarchives.com/contents/pdf/TOCPJ/TOCPJ-2-7.pdf).

This does not prove the earliest sample was never heated, or that its chips are chemically different. It identifies a missing published specimen/test join. Evidence of pre-cleanup collection and the plotted exotherms are different parts of the evidence chain until their specimen records are joined. Figure21's post-DSC sphere and spectrum add reported product evidence; viewing them is not an independent chemical-state determination. A claim that every analyzed chip shares every reported property needs the chip/run crosswalk, not similarity alone.

## Revised evidentiary weight

| Proposition | Assessment after this pass |
|---|---|
| At least some selected red/gray WTC-dust specimens have a coating/mineral interpretation supported by multiple analytical methods | More substantial published support than our earlier brief summary conveyed; still a laboratory report whose raw analyses we have not replicated. |
| Harrit's selected region contains oxygen-deficient aluminum inconsistent with an all-Al2O3 allocation | A specific, quantitatively meaningful conditional claim. Its measurement and ratio basis require verification; it must not be dismissed as merely noticing an aluminum peak. |
| The two studies tested identical material or mutually excluded every other chip type | Not established. Different specimens and possible mixed populations limit that conclusion. |
| Reactive chips demonstrate the required demolition performance | Not established. The source discusses further burn-rate work and alternatives for the material's role; a composition or calorimetric result is not an installed system demonstration. |
| The chemistry identifies WTC7-specific material, deliberate placement, an authorizer or the historical initiating mechanism | Not established by the measurements reviewed. No historical-cause probability is derived from the algebra. |

The strongest objection to this assessment is that Harrit's other reported observations, including reaction products, might supply corroboration beyond the aluminum ratio. They must be evaluated together, rather than assuming that an air atmosphere alone disposes of the result. The contrary objection is that Millette's phase evidence may identify a common coating population. Resolving which original specimens and regions each result covers is essential before either can answer the other's full claim.
