The documents' statements that attachments existed do not prove that the scans include the attachments or that the Commission received them. The scanned and electronic versions could share an underlying source and should not be counted as independent corroboration.

## Compare without conflating

The [March–April correspondence](https://catalog.archives.gov/medialz/dc-metro/rg-237/7419198-9-11-faa/5-e-other/5-K90-25-Correspondence-Email-Voice-Recordings-second-request-for-tapes-REDACT-SEALED.pdf) describes an earlier tape with **High AR 1247–1253 UTC** and a later request for **1230–1600 UTC** even for blank air; the Cape response describes **1229–1600 UTC** as planned. The earlier [partial-transcript cover, AWA-332 p. 16](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/7601899), gives High Arrival **1247–1259 UTC**. These descriptions do not independently identify the attachments in the certification scans. Retain all time fields without assuming a single object, a continuous audio record or a correct preferred value.

K90-21's stated range corresponds to the range in the separately released gap memorandum, but the records do not establish that the same tape, attachment or catalog item is involved. The earlier report's competing K90-39 labels and unverified automated-recognition lead also remain open. No conclusion about motive, concealment, contents or complete delivery follows from the scans.
