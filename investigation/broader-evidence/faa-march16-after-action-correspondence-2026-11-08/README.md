# DoD’s March 16 reply identifies weekly production indexes

November 8, 2026 Eastern · B129 / PD01–PD04. A public reproduction of the Commission counsel’s DoD correspondence includes the February 20 request, the March 16 reply and the attached NORAD legal memorandum. The reply identifies a recurring **weekly delivery index** that could tie DoD’s account of production to item-level records on both sides of the transfer. [Marcus Box 3 reader, pp. 9–13](https://www.scribd.com/document/15556037/DM-B3-DOD-1-of-2-Fdr-Memos-and-Correspondence-Re-Document-Production-NORAD-After-Action-Reports-NOIWON-Conference-Call-268)

## What the documents say

| Record | Reported scope and production |
|---|---|
| **February 20, 2004 Commission request** | Cites May 9, 2003 **DoD Document Request No. 1, item 10**. It asks broadly for documents analyzing NORAD’s response, including interviews with personnel involved in air defense, and includes records created elsewhere within DoD. It asks for a list if production was complete, written confirmation if there were no responsive records, or outstanding documents and an explanation. The request asks for a reply by **February 26** and describes a Colorado visit then scheduled for **March 2**. Those are the stated deadline and planned visit date. Reader pp. 9–10. |
| **March 16, 2004 DoD counsel reply** | Explicitly responds to the February 20 after-action-report letter and identifies the NORAD staff judge advocate’s memorandum as its attachment. DoD describes a narrower scope: reports prepared for a higher level of command or supervision that describe the events and contain a conclusion or recommendation. It says it listed what it considered responsive on the index “delivered to you every week with our document deliveries.” The checked pages supply the reference to the indexes. The index entries and recipient acknowledgment remain unacquired. Reader p. 11, text lines 29–53 and 109–149. |
| **16 March 2004 NORAD–USNORTHCOM/JA enclosure** | Distinguishes the requested single comprehensive report from a set of potentially responsive records. The author reports an inquiry at NORAD, no single final and comprehensive report of the traditional kind, and a series of lessons-learned reports provided to the Commission. The continuation identifies the intended forwarding as an attachment to the DoD reply. The production statements come from the two sender-side records in the same correspondence chain. Reader pp. 12–13. |
