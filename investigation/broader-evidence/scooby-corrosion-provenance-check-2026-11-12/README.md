# Scooby report 17: corrosion provenance and timing source check

November 12, 2026. This checks selected recovered report-body claims against the actual agency reports, the 2004 event notice, and the 2006 publisher abstract. It does not verify every report claim, every cited attachment, or the full 2006 paper.

| Claim | Original source and assessment |
| --- | --- |
| FEMA linked Sample 1’s A36 mount to its photographed beam but qualified building identity. | [FEMA 403 Appendix C, pp. C-1–C-2](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2017/05/09/AppendixC-fema403_apc.pdf#page=1), says the first beam appeared to come from WTC 7, identifies its exact location as unknown, and describes Figure C-3’s mount as cut from the heavily eroded region shown in C-1. The document records the examiners’ identification and physical cross-reference; it does not establish an unbroken site-to-lab chain or a known original building location. |
| FEMA found an unusual process, not a measured clock or identified sulfur source. | [Appendix C, p. C-13](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2017/05/09/AppendixC-fema403_apc.pdf#page=13), says both the corrosion rate and sulfur source were unknown, suggests prolonged heating in rubble as one possibility, and also leaves pre-collapse onset open for study. The finding of oxidation and sulfidation is positive evidence of chemical attack, not by itself identification of an incendiary or its timing. |
| NIST found no unambiguous WTC 7 steel. | [NCSTAR 1-3, §7.7, printed p. 113](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3.pdf), limits the assertion to **NIST’s inventory** and says WTC 7 steel properties were estimated from literature. It is not a direct custody assessment of every FEMA/WPI sample or proof that FEMA’s tentative assignment was false. NIST’s [progress report, p. 84](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication1000-5v1.pdf), says its 236 cataloged elements were a small fraction of material examined in recovery yards; that count is NIST’s investigation inventory, not an exhaustive census of all steel. |
| Authors later favored rubble-fire exposure, but the exposure is not independently dated by the publisher abstract. | The [2006 publisher abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1361/154770206X129006) reports localized temperatures above 940 °C and says its analysis suggests removal during post-collapse rubble-fire exposure. This is a later interpretation by two original FEMA appendix authors, not independent replication or a direct time measurement. Full methods, provenance and any rate calibration remain unexamined because the publisher displays an abstract and subscription preview only. |
| The 2004 presentation covered both alloy classes; Figure 13’s conflict proves an actual specimen swap. | The [Northwestern event notice for December 14, 2004](https://dunand.mccormick.northwestern.edu/news/wtc-talk.shtml) describes both A36 and Cu-strengthened HSLA beams in the same talk. It does not authenticate the suspected proceedings scan, establish the image identity or resolve whether a caption, image or specimen assignment is wrong. The [earlier detailed repository check](../steel-wpi-furnace-paper-candidate-2026-11-06/README.md) documents the A36 experiment text versus the HSLA Figure 13 caption and published-image comparison; the scan was not re-rendered in this pass. A caption mismatch alone does not demonstrate a laboratory swap. |

**Open records and tests:** authenticated proceedings edition and proofs, the 2006 full paper, native micrograph and spectrum IDs, lab mount and specimen records, pickup and as-built location documentation, furnace-run controls and rate measurements. Any pre-collapse incendiary claim would need evidence discriminating it from the later rubble-fire exposure interpretation; the existing record does not attribute either event to UMS.
