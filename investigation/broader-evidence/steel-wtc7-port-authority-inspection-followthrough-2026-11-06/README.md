# WTC 7 fuel-tank support and FDNY inspection follow-up

## What the records establish

The City of New York's public scans document an inspection and escalation about a raised WTC 7 fuel tank and its steel support, fire protection and access to elevator pits. NIST later reported that inspectors initialed a list as they verified corrections to the Office of Emergency Management (OEM) system. The underlying list is the priority record for determining which deficiencies were addressed and what was inspected. NIST identifies Port Authority submittal **W98-7134**, which is a separate route to the design, review and approval records. ## Conflicting records and their limits

- **Tank capacity and A-8.** The visit memo gives approximately **6,000 gal** and reports **10,000** on A-8. The later FDNY memoranda and NIST describe a **6,000 gal** first-floor tank. The original A-8 and its revisions are still needed to identify the drawing's reference and compare it with the installed tank.
- **Directive identifier.** The March cover text cites **“F.P. Directive 6-54(R)”**. Its scanned attachment reads **“F. P. Directive 6-56 (Revised)”** and appears to list **“6-54”** in the revocation line, where a scan line crosses the text. The attachment is dated **May 22, 1978**. Both readings are retained pending a clearer directive or the issuing register. [Cover, PDF p. 2, attachment 2](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000169169.pdf#page=2) · [Directive, PDF p. 5, title and ¶3](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000169169.pdf#page=5)
- **Containment and corrective-action scope.** The March memo describes no dike at the access hatch or the elevator access doors. NIST's detailed report describes planned retaining walls and curbs for the fuel oil tank room. The summary describes initialed corrections without identifying the underlying items on that page. Locations, revisions and inspection dates matter here. The records reviewed do not establish that these statements refer to the same barrier or inspection date. [FDNY, PDF p. 1, paragraphs 1–2](https://sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us/apps/content/September11_MD/NYC-WTC_000169169.pdf#page=1) · [NCSTAR 1-1J, printed p. 26, §6.2](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-484f3a6f3708cdb44a8b7444631588d8/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-484f3a6f3708cdb44a8b7444631588d8.pdf#page=58) · [NCSTAR 1-1, printed p. 176, §12.1.3](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-1.pdf#page=238)
- **OEM transfer-pump unit.** NCSTAR 1-1 §12.2.4 prints **“700 gpm”**. NCSTAR 1-1J §6.2 prints **“700 gph”** and names model **LO-203**. If each unit is taken literally, the rates differ by a factor of 60. The installed specification, commissioning results and any analysis inputs are required to determine which figure was used where. [NCSTAR 1-1, printed p. 179](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-1.pdf#page=241) · [NCSTAR 1-1J, printed p. 26](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-484f3a6f3708cdb44a8b7444631588d8/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-484f3a6f3708cdb44a8b7444631588d8.pdf#page=58)

NIST's account of initialed corrections is evidence of a reported follow-up. The recovered City pages establish concerns and a documented process for action. Whether a given item was corrected, deferred, authorized through a variance or left open remains unverified. This review covers the inspection and record trail. NIST separately evaluated fuel oil scenarios and reported its conclusion in FAQ 19. [NIST FAQ 19](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation)
