## Separate observations

- **Indexed Chronicle account:** the relevant paragraph describes a short cellular-phone call and attributes a hijacking statement and a statement that “they” were kind to the passenger. It frames this as a call to her husband. Its printed wording and attribution are available at the publisher link; no access to its reporting notes or a contemporaneous print edition was obtained.
- **FBI account of the press and family:** SF 157 reports that an agent referred to a September 12 Chronicle column containing substantially that description. The same report records a family account that the answering-machine message did not mention a hijacking and that the newspaper account was inaccurate. The FBI report also records a family decision against an FBI recording for reasons of privacy and possible public release. It does not independently transcribe or authenticate the message. Do not select one account as controlling.
- **The press release and attachment:** the FBI report says a family press release was written and read to the press, and says the newspaper article was attached. Neither the actual press release nor the referenced attachment was found or examined here. The online article is a strong candidate match based on date, publication and wording; its identity with the specific attachment is not established.

The article, FBI report and family account have distinct evidentiary roles and may share source material. A match between the FBI's description and the indexed article corroborates the description's existence in that representation; it does **not** authenticate the original message, demonstrate the phone technology, or resolve who said what. Different message descriptions, flight labels and custody questions remain unresolved. No conclusion about fabrication, government suppression or motive follows.
