# Pilot-deviation recording rules before the FAA’s 2010 DEN retention provision

November 6, 2026 UTC · B129 / PD03–PD04. Three additional FAA change notices show a separate recording and preservation route for pilot-deviation files in 2008–2009. A newly recovered replacement list also identifies **GENOT 06-40** as an additional predecessor to seek. The earlier study recovered paragraph 72 amendments and accident-package custody rules. This comparison concerns the different record class under paragraph 114. ## Two separate routes in the same notices

| Clause | What the FAA directed |
| --- | --- |
| **114(c)(1)–(2)**, August 2008 and August 2009 notices, PDF p. 2 | In the specified radio-contact situation, capture the relevant voice transmissions from **5 minutes before** to **5 minutes after** the conversations or contacts. Include re-recordings of pertinent telephone conversations when they assist the investigation. When requested by one of the named offices, prepare and forward a certified partial transcript within **10 administrative days**. The five-minute windows concern recording coverage. The ten-day interval concerns delivery of a requested transcript. |
| **114(c)(4), (d), (e)**, both notices, PDF p. 2 | The filing facility notifies other facilities with potentially supporting data. Involved facilities retain originals under the reporting facility's **12-character pilot-deviation number** and forward copies. The report uses **FAA Form 8020-17**. This provides a record key for the facility file, copy-forwarding record and supporting material if an applicable deviation file is located. |
| **114(o)(2)–(3)**, both notices, PDF p. 4 | For the specified unauthorized flights, notify the **Domestic Events Network** coordinator within **15 minutes** of the occurrence. Prepare a pilot-deviation report if that coordinator requests one. The accompanying note says the notification is additional to the reporting requirements in FAAO JO 7610.4. The notice identifies notification and the creation of a pilot-deviation report as separate steps. It supplies no matching instruction to retain the central DEN recording on every notification. |

## Newly identified predecessor

The **January 2008 N8020.176** message gives effective **01/10/08**, cancellation **12/31/08**, and lists replaced GENOTs **06-35, 06-40, 07-03, and 07-18**. The January 2009 and January 2010 notices list **06-35, 07-03, and 07-18**. The 2008 notice supplies a positive locator for **06-40**. The subsequent omission leaves its disposition and role in each provision open. A replacement list cannot reconstruct the wording of a predecessor or establish which predecessor supplied a specific amendment. ## What this adds to the FAA follow-up

The pilot-deviation instructions predate the December 2010 DEN accident/incident provision. They supply a concrete way to distinguish facility records, a deviation investigation and a central DEN recording. The earlier Inspector General recommendation refers to preservation following notification of hijacked or suspicious aircraft. The inspected pilot-deviation clauses establish a narrower route and leave the broader trigger, the status of central recordings and the FAA's response to the recommendation open. No historical event has been assigned a pilot-deviation number on the basis of these later rules. [OIG recommendation 3](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cc2006085.pdf#page=13) · [2010 DEN provision](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/order/jo8020.16a.pdf#page=30)
