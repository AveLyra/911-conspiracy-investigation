## A more consequential proxy/validation limit

NIST's own review explicitly records disagreements between WJE's visual assessments and NIST's paint mapping. It further says **seven panels** had photographically observable pre-collapse fire exposure but neither recovered-piece technique indicated that exposure. It gives **M-2, over 16 continuous minutes of external flaming at floor 98**, as an example. This is a reported comparison in NIST's review, not an independently remeasured seven-panel result here. The paragraph does not provide the complete seven-piece list or show that the inspected recovered region coincided with every photographed flame. [V2 p.258 / printed 480](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3cv2.pdf#page=258); S11.

This creates specific tests: recover the seven-item list; match photographed locations and times to the recovered region and paint-sampling coordinates; inspect native observations, paint maps, temperature calibration and uncertainty. The absence of a recovered visual/paint indication cannot, on these passages alone, establish absence of prior fire exposure. Conversely, observed flames or soot do not establish a particular steel temperature, exposure dose or cause of structural failure.

NIST suggests a possible paint-degradation mechanism that need not raise local steel above **250 °C**, and discusses strain, abrasion and corrosion as alternatives. Preserve these as **source explanations**, not a reconciliation established by this review. They could weaken an inference of temperature-method error if supported by matched-region, calibrated results; lack of such support would leave the disagreement open. S09–S11.

The item-by-item review also preserves missing-data states: **S-10 and C-88b had no paint available for inspection**, so NIST made no paint conclusion; these are not negative temperature tests. Unidentified C-11 and M-4 lack accepted as-built fire-location context. Several observations agree, including aspects of impact, weld, core, truss and connection damage. The differing methods, targets and temporal conditions must be retained, not counted as independent confirmations of wrongdoing. [pp.255–258](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3cv2.pdf#page=255); S10–S12.

## What the report actually examined, and the originals it identifies

WJE says all **236 pieces** received general visual observation, while not all received detailed examination within the available on-site time. It does not give a detailed-examination count in that methodology paragraph. Observations were unaided; planned simple optical/ultrasonic aids were not used. Notes were **handwritten then transcribed into attachment tables**. Photography was primarily digital, with some film prints later digitized, and areas of interest had white **Z0–Z9** marks. Work was more than a year after collapse, and weathering/corrosion/paint erosion/fracture degradation were acknowledged. [p.178](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3cv2.pdf#page=178); S04–S05.

These identify concrete native-record targets, rather than another narrative retelling: handwritten sheets and transcription versions, full-resolution camera files/film scans, member/region/Z-mark crosswalks, examination dates and detailed-selection logs. Independent visual observations used **NIST-supplied accepted locations and identifiers**; independence of observation does not independently authenticate those locations, and shared location metadata does not erase the independent observations. S03–S05.

## Scope and contrary evidence

Volume I explicitly states that **WTC 7 steel was not evaluated in this tower damage/failure study**. In this particular downloaded file, that executive-summary passage is at **physical PDF 296 / printed xliii**. This is a scope statement about this study, not proof that no other NIST project ever considered WTC 7 steel. The six unknown-building worksheets do not override it. [V1 p.296](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3cv1.pdf#page=296); S01.

The 2004 committee's dissatisfaction/access concern and 2005 general-agreement statement remain preserved. The newly acquired published appendix includes explicit contrary observations and NIST's response. That is strong contrary evidence to a blanket claim that adverse contractor observations were absent from the public final record. It does not prove the October draft was preserved unchanged, the committee received it promptly, or all proposed complementary tests occurred. The native draft/comment/delivery/comparison chain remains necessary.

The strongest alternative to a misconduct interpretation is differing scopes, proxy measurements, sampling and publication-stage editing. None is selected as the cause of the count or assessment differences. These are record/version/method leads, **not evidence identifying an explosive, a demolition mechanism, deliberate concealment or a responsible person**. No collapse solver, physical/metallurgical experiment, paint calibration or original-note authentication was performed here.
