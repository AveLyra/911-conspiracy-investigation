## What the comparison actually tests

Different recipient/object instructions do not establish a contradiction about the same transaction. Neither record proves a uniform preservation rule. A hypothetical blanket claim that every distributed artifact had an identical nonalteration condition would need to account for Clearwater's contrary description. Conversely, a staff summary cannot certify the terms or implementation of its missing signed agreement. The strongest competing reading is item-specific permission or differing document scope; that remains an untested explanation, not a resolution.

The shared January 2007 inventory reference provides a concrete retrieval key, not a complete custody chain. The October 2004 completion account ([2005 minutes, p. 6](https://dspace.njstatelib.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/c960df66-1aba-40cd-9eb0-140585e3be89/content#page=6)), 2006 completion account ([2012 LMDC report, p. 5](https://www.renewnyc.org/attachments/content/pdfs/WTC_Historic_Resources_Report_January_2012.pdf#page=5)) and January 31, 2007 reference remain side by side. Those two earlier accounts are inherited from the [preserved study](../steel-preservation-decisions-2026-10-27/README.md), not newly source-read here. We have not obtained the editions, change history, item crosswalks or delivery records. No arithmetic on unlike collections, inferred alias match, authenticity finding, deliberate destruction or concealment inference is supported here. A physically unmarked item can have an administrative inventory number; that combination alone is not inconsistent.

The decisive tests remain obtaining each signed agreement and incorporated order/instruction, the referenced inventory entry/photo and revisions, and actual dispatch/receipt/cutting/condition logs. Compare permission, performance and later condition separately. Neither object has been joined to W14A/B, C12a/b, FEMA Sample 1 or a NIST-retained coupon. The inspection condition in the held Beverly Hills order is meaningful contrary evidence to a blanket loss-of-testing-rights claim; it is not proof of an experiment or a right applying to the public or to Clearwater's item.
