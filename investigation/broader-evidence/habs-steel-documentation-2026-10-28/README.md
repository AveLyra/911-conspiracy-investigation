## Location-specific records now available

The child caption index, physical page 2, identifies a blistered-paint detail as **NY-6369-A-26, column J3/10, level B2**, and a smoke-damage detail as **NY-6369-A-21, column J4/12, level B2**. Its third page contains a photograph-key plan. These are authored descriptions and locators; we have not checked the photographs against those captions or independently measured temperature, damage or causation. HBR02.

The parent index, physical page 3, describes corresponding-looking details under **NY-6369-31** and **NY-6369-29**. Both source identifiers, wording and their context remain separate. The proposed two-row [caption correspondence](CAPTION-CANDIDATES.csv) is a retrieval aid based on matching component/level/subject/direction descriptions; it does not authenticate identical negatives, exposures, edits, pixels or a common custody chain. The two indexes must not be counted as independent physical observations. HBR03.

The historical data's physical page 18 / printed 17 places the remnant substructure under **6 WTC**, while separately describing a vehicle ramp connected to Barclay Street under **7 WTC**. Adjacent geography does not turn a WTC 6 column into a WTC 7 specimen. It also distinguishes surviving pre-attack components from recovery-era tiebacks. HBR04.

The [earlier July 2005 implementation report](https://renewnyc.org/attachments/content/pdfs/S106_Report_January_to_June_2005.pdf#page=3) describes a smoke-scarred column and a blistered-paint column among four remnants moved to Hangar 17. The new grid-level captions make a comparison possible; **they do not identify either photographed column as either transported object**. Actual selection, cut/marking, removal, transport, receipt and object-number records are still required. No join to W14A/B, C12a/b, FEMA Sample 1 or Column 79 is established. HBR05.

## Version, date and label distinctions preserved

The data's physical pages 3–4 / printed 2–3 cite an **April 2005 Permanent WTC PATH Terminal Memorandum of Agreement** and photography spanning **June 2004–March 2005**. The parent caption index's first page has a **June 2004** heading, while entry NY-6369-2 expressly dates an aerial photograph **September 17, 2001**. The child index gives June 2004 photographer entries. Preserve these source-specific descriptions; do not impose one date on every image or infer a fabricated photograph from different scopes. The actual agreement, original manifests and version history would test how the sets were assembled. HBR01/HBR07.

The child PDF's first-page identifier visibly says **HAER NY-6369-A** under a Historic American Buildings Survey heading; its second-page identifier and handwritten plan notation use **HABS**. Both are preserved. This is a source-label variation relevant to retrieval, not evidence of a changed specimen, altered photograph or concealment. Editorial labeling or different cataloguing stages are untested possibilities, not a selected explanation. HBR06.

The data's agreement reference is retained beside the separate April 2004 LMDC programmatic-agreement trail. Their titles/parties/scopes are not silently merged. Nor does retrieving these PDFs authenticate them as the exact copies reportedly submitted to SHPO in August 2005. The earlier **October 2004 versus 2006 inventory-completion accounts remain unresolved**, and no actual inventory edition or receipt was acquired in this unit.

The [catalogue record](https://www.loc.gov/item/ny2014/) was located through an indexed representation which lists unprocessed field material **N1200** and 103 photographs. Direct catalogue/API opens failed in this session; the full catalogue response, field material and 103-photo corpus were not acquired. Those are located targets, not held/read evidence. HTTP Last-Modified dates are retained as response metadata, not source creation or submission dates. HBR08.

## What this permits next

The strongest substantive route is to compare the actual identified photographs and field material with the removal/transport/object registers, using component and region identifiers. A successful join could recover a specimen's former location and preservation history; a mismatch could exclude the proposed link. Similar wording alone proves neither outcome. Obtain the named agreement and actual submission/receipt manifests to test edition identity.

No native inventory, NIST attribution worksheet, paint calibration, coupon-to-region record or physical measurement was recovered. A caption's blistering/smoke description is not a calibrated thermal result or evidence of a particular incendiary. [Open records and discriminating tests](OPEN-RECORDS.md).
