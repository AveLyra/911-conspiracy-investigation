### 2. Four classes with different instructions

| Source class | Recorded disposition and trigger | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Page 2, item 1(a): original records and audio re-recordings | Permanent; transfer to NARA | An actual transfer; a complete master recording; every channel, decoder definition or clock reference; equivalence between an original tape and a re-recording. |
| Page 2, item 1(b): scanned images maintained by FAA as backup to images posted to its website, including redacted and unredacted copies | Temporary; cut off when the associated website images are removed; delete within **60 days of cutoff** | That the underlying original could be destroyed under this copy instruction, that a website removal occurred, or that a particular missing item was this kind of backup. |
| Page 2 item 2(a), continued on page 3: convenience/generation copies with no further administrative value after making the recordkeeping copy | Temporary; delete/destroy within **180 days after the recordkeeping copy is produced** | That no recordkeeping copy existed, that it was complete, or that all email was disposable after 180 days. |
| Page 3 item 2(b): dissemination/revision/updating copies maintained in addition to the recordkeeping copy | Temporary; delete when dissemination, revision or updating is complete | The date that task finished, lawful disposal of another record class, or a particular historical deletion. |

Pins S03–S06 provide exact page/item and local extraction-line locators. These are historical instructions described by the source, not directions to delete anything now. **The 60- and 180-day periods have different starting events and apply to different copies.** They are not competing answers to one universal retention period. The permanent original category must be preserved beside the temporary-copy categories when asking what survived.

## What can reasonably be inferred

**Moderate confidence:** identifying the actual record category and designated recordkeeping copy is necessary before a missing online copy can tell us much about the original's fate. This schedule provides specific classes and offices with which to test that question. **High confidence in the page-level readings of the class/trigger table**, supported by the preserved scan and full three-page visual review; **unresolved** applicability, supersession, completeness and actual custody/disposition.

The strongest objection to treating this as evidence of concealment is in the schedule itself: it expressly treats originals as permanent, while describing several copies as temporary. That does not prove preservation occurred. Conversely, permission to dispose of a qualified copy does not prove disposal occurred or that the original survived. An improper-disposal hypothesis and ordinary-copy-disposition hypothesis both require actual classification, transfer/hold and disposition records. No intent, coordination, wrongdoing or current compliance finding follows from these pages.
