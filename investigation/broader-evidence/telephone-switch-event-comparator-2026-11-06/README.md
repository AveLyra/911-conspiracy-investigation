## Inference and limits

The patent demonstrates that these stages can be distinct in a documented design. The later CPUC case illustrates why an apparent match on destination and date needs more independent fields. Together they motivate a test across the originating carrier, operator platform and terminating office. The patent describes an embodiment. The CPUC inquiry concerns different traffic, dates and systems. Applicability to Claircom or the specific AT&T platform used in 2001, the meaning of the printed duration field, and the existence of corresponding retained AMA records all remain unverified.

Separate call legs, an abandoned attempt, or a completed office connection belonging to another event are untested possibilities. A mistaken summary or classification is also possible. The released records in this unit provide no new basis for identifying the caller, establishing who spoke, or deciding which account describes the same event.
