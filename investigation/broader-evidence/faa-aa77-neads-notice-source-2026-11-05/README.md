# American 77’s 9:34 notice in the released NEADS clip

November 5, 2026 · B129 / PD01–PD04, with REC01–REC03 source context. The previous report preserved the Defense Inspector General's **9:34 hijacking notice** wording alongside the Commission's **9:34 lost-aircraft notice** wording. This pass acquired the published Washington Center–Huntress exchange, checked its transcripts and tested two other published excerpts for waveform overlap. [Prior comparison](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/19db38f818ec03535a0778ba6d8311f7ace7c648/investigation/broader-evidence/faa-norad-timeline-correction-routing-2026-11-05/README.md)

## Call content and source labels

- **What the call says.** Rutgers item 72 identifies the incoming speaker as the **Washington Center Operations Manager** and the other endpoint as **Huntress ID**. Its transcript reads **“we also lost American 77.”** The caller then describes Indianapolis Center losing radar and contact and being uncertain about the aircraft's location or fate. The separate *Transcripts from Voice Recorder* reproduction, **NCT0000066**, gives the same account and labels the words **13:34:02**. The Alderson reproduction also carries the lost-aircraft wording, with the channel label **DRM1/DAT2 Channel 4 ID Op**. These are representations of the same exchange, with potentially shared recording lineage. [Rutgers item 72](https://rutgerslawreview.com/wp-content/uploads/special/911/html/%2872%29%20093212%20AA11%20AA77%20ID%20Summary%20ZDC%20loss%20of%20AA77.html) · [Voice Recorder reproduction, NCT0000066](https://www.scribd.com/document/14142009/NYC-Box-2-NEADS-Transcript-Rome-NY-Fdr-Transcript-Pgs-1-82-1237-1417) · [Alderson reproduction, internal p. 0063 lines 19–22](https://www.scribd.com/document/14142075/NYC-Box-3-Neads-conr-norad-Fdr-Transcript-Neads-Channel-4-Id-Op)
- **Which call direction.** The Commission's narrative describes NEADS seeking information from Washington Center. The recovered excerpt starts with the Washington manager identifying himself and referring to earlier calls from Huntress. Keep the direction of this recorded leg separate from who initiated the inquiry and whether the clip contains the complete sequence. [Commission chapter 1](https://www.9-11commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch1.htm) · [Rutgers item 72, opening](https://rutgerslawreview.com/wp-content/uploads/special/911/html/%2872%29%20093212%20AA11%20AA77%20ID%20Summary%20ZDC%20loss%20of%20AA77.html)
- **Different time and channel fields.** Rutgers uses **093212** in the item title. Commission endnote **151** cites **ID channel 5** at **9:32:10** and **9:33:58**. The Voice Recorder reproduction labels the reported-loss utterance **13:34:02** under a transcript range headed **1227Z–1417Z**. The Alderson reproduction identifies **channel 4**. A Commission working-timeline reproduction distinguishes a **9:32:27** call start from **9:34:02** for the statement and separately lists a **9:34** MCC/T log entry describing American 77 as lost or unable to be located. The time labels, channel numbers and record categories are preserved. No channel or clock is selected as controlling. [Commission endnote 151](https://www.9-11commission.gov/report/911Report_Notes.htm) · [Working-timeline reproduction, rows surrounding 9:34](https://www.scribd.com/document/12992794/Timeline-of-the-Events-of-the-Day-of-9-11-Drafted-by-the-9-11-Commission)
- **Offline speech cross-check.** A pinned local Whisper model decoded the **85–155 second** portion of the Rutgers MP3. It returned **“We're also left American 77”** at displayed elapsed **104.000–105.960 s**, then detected the phrases about losing radar and contact around **130.600–136.360 s**. The first line differs from the published transcripts. The tool output remains verbatim and supplies a limited cross-check of the surrounding content. The displayed offsets are from the MP3 and the speech segments have model uncertainty.

These records support the missing-aircraft wording for this specific call. The Defense Inspector General's sentence assigns **9:34** as the actual time in a sentence about hijacking notice to **DoD**. It supplies no channel or source identifier there. This check leaves open whether it refers to this call, some other notification, or a different label for the same event. The record does not establish that this clip covers other DoD recipients or all military communications. [Defense IG memo, physical p. 1, lines 30–31 in the preserved OCR](https://web.archive.org/web/20120707002543id_/http://www.dodig.mil/Ir/reports/06-intel-12.pdf#page=1)

## Scoped waveform comparison

The Rutgers MP3 and the two split files from *9-11 Revisited* are three distinct downloads. They have decoded durations of **186.148625**, **90.174750** and **94.354375** seconds respectively. The frozen test decoded each file to 8 kHz mono with FFmpeg 7.1.1. It searched the entire Rutgers decode for three six-second windows centered at 25%, 50% and 75% of each split file using zero-mean normalized cross-correlation. The rule required all six peaks ≥ **0.95** and a lag spread ≤ **30 ms** per part.

| Source | Best peaks, in probe order | Rutgers minus split elapsed lag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Part I | 0.930568, 0.952075, 0.956903 | 1.255500 s at all three peaks |
| Part II | 0.997746, 0.996739, 0.997536 | 91.794750 s at all three peaks |

The complete frozen test **failed** because Part I's first peak was below threshold. The six peak positions still give consistent lags within each part. The strongest off-peak control, excluding ±1 second around each maximum, was below **0.067** and a silent probe was rejected. This supports overlap in the sampled regions, especially the Part II windows. It leaves the joins, completeness, processing history, native channel identity and absolute timing unverified.
