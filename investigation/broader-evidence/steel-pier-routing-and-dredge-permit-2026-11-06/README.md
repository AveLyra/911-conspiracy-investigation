# WTC recovery barging routes and the September 13 dredging permit lead

November 6, 2026 · Steel custody · B188 / H005 / STL01–03

The pier names in the prior load-ticket study need to stay tied to their sources. The 2006 court opinion says **Piers 6 and 26** in its Zone 5 footnote. Two official Commission transcripts give **25 and 26** and **25 and 6**, respectively. An October 2001 Army Corps publication also provides a dated locator for the original **Pier 25 dredging application and permission**. These records change which place names and files should be checked for the outbound shipments. The three pier pairs are different as written. A site plan, dated designation or operational change could bear on the difference between a zone and a loading berth. The contemporaneous permit and dispatch records would help test those possibilities. This review leaves the significance of Pier 26 unresolved.

## Separate the two permissions

The Army Corps’ *Engineer Update*, October 2001, vol. 25, no. 10, p. 1 under **Emergency permits**, reports that New York City requested permission on **September 13, 2001** to dredge **120,000 cubic yards** around **Pier 25** to allow larger vessels to support recovery. It says the North Atlantic Division commander granted permission to dredge and place the material in the **Newark Bay Confined Disposal Facility**. The article continues on p. 3 with a second request, five days later, for **60,000 cubic yards** between the Governor’s Island ferry terminal and the downtown heliport. The issue supplies no permit number in either passage. [Army Corps issue, pp. 1 and 3](https://corpslakes.erdc.dren.mil/employees/engineerupdate/Engineer_update_102001.pdf)

The September 13 passage concerns harbor dredgings. Holden’s reported verbal permission concerns steel from the WTC site. The application, issued permit, maps and disposal logs are the record targets for the first account. The second calls for a DDC decision, dated confirmation or contemporaneous routing instruction and recycler shipment records. The shared reference to a New Jersey destination supplies no link between those decisions. [Army Corps issue, p. 1](https://corpslakes.erdc.dren.mil/employees/engineerupdate/Engineer_update_102001.pdf#page=1) · [Holden transcript, p. 241](https://www.9-11commission.gov/archive/hearing1/9-11Commission_Hearing_2003-04-01.pdf#page=241)

## Test the published station figures before using them

| Station | Reported loads | Reported tons | Reported barges | Reported tons ÷ barges |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 59th Street | 4,515 | 38,160 | 62 | 615.48 |
| Hamilton Avenue | 17,879 | 263,993 | 447 | 590.59 |

The same exhibit page describes the broader DSNY operation as over **2,000 barges**, each containing **600 tons**. The exact station rows yield **960 tons more** and **4,207 tons less**, respectively, than multiplying their reported barge counts by 600. Those differences test only a literal application of the headline figure to the station rows. Whether 600 is a rounded description, a capacity figure or based on a different set of trips remains open. A load ledger and the station-level weight definitions are needed before using the headline to fill any gaps. [Exhibit, “by the numbers”](https://www.dsnyremembers.org/trucks-barges)
