# Flight 93 carrier packet: handwritten package-receipt lead

November 25, 2026 · TEL02–TEL03 / AUD016-A1. A newly acquired scan exposes a handwritten notation not transcribed in the earlier text-only review. It provides a specific document to check for the November 2001 carrier package; it does not establish the contents of that package or the attachment's bytes.

## Source and visual observation

We acquired the 41-physical-page [January 2009 Commission archive ZIP](https://archive.org/download/911datasets/NARA_9-11_Commission_Records_Released_Jan_14_2009.zip) member `911 NARA/Team 7 Files/Team 7 Box 13/T7 B13 Flight GTE Phone Records Fdr- Entire Contents 788.pdf`, by bounded range November 25. Verified ZIP path, size **1,597,265 bytes**, CRC `ea7caa8c`, SHA-256 `4e8a891c1b18ed60587d99d50222c7a8bdca2b5e8dbb56c544e2a3b43abd3d8b`. This is a Commission-release reproduction, not the native email or workbook. Physical pages **34–35** were rendered and visually inspected; other pages were surveyed by their extracted text, not comprehensively verified.

At the foot of physical page **34**, identified in the scan as **1A-1A65562 / M-NKA-00001342**, a handwritten notation appears to say a **FedEx package was received on 11/6/01 at 1:39 PM**, with the recipient's identifying field redacted. This is our visual reading of handwriting, not an authenticated delivery receipt or verified clock. No time zone is stated in the notation. The same reproduced page displays an email dated **November 6, 2001, 08:33:53 −0600**; it describes an attached Flight 93 spreadsheet named **`UAL_flt93.xls`**. A separate rendered page **35**, **M-NKA-00001341**, contains a table described as approximate seat locations. The scan's sequence and handwriting do not prove the package contents or that the displayed table is the emailed attachment.

The handwritten annotation and interview give a testable possible receipt cross-reference. They do **not** demonstrate that the email, FedEx package, printed pages, or later spreadsheet are the same version. They may share a source; do not count them as independent proof of each package content.
