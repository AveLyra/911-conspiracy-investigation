# American Airlines request 2 and the ground-office telephone records

November 7, 2026 UTC · **TEL01–TEL05 / AUD016-A1 / TC05–TC06.** A newly examined four-page public mirror of a Commission working-file packet preserves an internal email chain and an attached American Airlines document request. It identifies requests for telephone records from several **ground offices**, alongside a separate request for onboard telephone logs. The packet also preserves conflicting dates and an unresolved forwarding question. This is a reproduced paper record. Its originating email and external delivery have not been authenticated. ## Conflicting dates and document state preserved

- **Attachment, p. 3, lines 8–9:** the production deadline is **August 1, 2003**. **Attachment, p. 4, line 43:** its closing date is **December 4, 2003**. Neither value has been corrected or treated as controlling.
- **Email chain, p. 1, lines 8, 16 and 35:** the displayed messages are dated **December 17, 2003, 11:54 AM**, **December 17, 2003, 10:34 AM**, and **December 4, 2003, 6:03 PM**. The first two pages separately print **12/19/2003** at their foot. These are different printed fields, with no native message IDs or verified clock zones. The footer's generation event remains unknown.
- **Email, p. 1, lines 22–24 and 41–43:** the staff member asks colleagues whether to forward the material to the front office or airline counsel, or defer it, and describes the attachment as awaiting review. The packet establishes internal preparation and circulation. It does not establish an external send date, receipt or decision to defer.
- **Email list, p. 1 item 4, line 56:** the employee-related request ends before specifying a complete action. Physical p. 2 begins with another request grouping and item 5. That incomplete wording is retained. No missing continuation has been reconstructed. **Attachment p. 3 item 6** is visibly struck through. The change concerns a chart request, not the phone-log items. The author, date and approval of that mark are unknown.

A reused draft or clerical error is an **untested** possible explanation for the deadline conflict. A different revision history is also possible. The contradictory dates do not establish delayed compliance, concealment, or alteration of telephone evidence. An internal draft cannot establish the final scope delivered to a recipient.

## A separate briefing request

The independently acquired **one-page Team 7 item 259** is FBI briefing request 5, dated **July 29, 2003**. Its first topic covers events aboard the four flights, including calls to people on the ground. Its closing paragraph says the background briefing would not replace later interviews. It supplies a distinct request type and date. It supplies no carrier export, office log, briefing attendance record or completed response. Do not confuse the FBI briefing number with the American Airlines document-request numbers. Compare these against both printed dates and the December forwarding question. A sent counterpart could change the status assessment. A different final request could change the category mapping.
2. **Ground-office originals.** Locate the responses to items **2, 11 and 12**, including telephone bills, PBX or switch exports, line-to-desk assignments, clock definitions, production/Bates indexes, omissions and referrals. Separately locate the Clarke and SOC notes sought in the email list. Different endpoints may record separate call legs. An interview, requested category or monthly reliability report cannot substitute for those records.

Preserve incompatible time, count and service descriptions. Matching recipient, timing and leg fields could support a join. Incompatible fields or an unrelated line could weaken it. No call match or clock calibration was performed here.
