# Voorsanger archive route and its stated limits

November 7, 2026 UTC · **STL02–STL03 / B188 / H005 / AUD011 / AUD016.** A newly located institutional archive provides another place to look for project documentation associated with the firm that prepared the WTC preservation inventories. Its public description sharply limits that lead. This review did not locate an inventory volume, accession list, intake photograph, transfer manifest or WTC project finding-aid entry.

## Source observations

- **Archive scope page, paragraph 1.** The archive explicitly excludes **business documents, correspondence, emails, and related office materials**. It describes a collection focused on design development. That statement limits an assumption that the firm's emails, consultant contracts or administrative custody files are held there. [Archive's scope statement](https://voorsangerarchive.org/the-archive/)
- **Same page, paragraphs 2–3.** The page identifies the **University of St. Thomas Department of Art History** as the home of the full physical and digital archive. It distinguishes that collection from a curated selection of **25 projects**. It mentions a prospective finding aid without linking an item-level WTC inventory in the reviewed text. The actual extent and current status of a finding aid remain unverified. [Scope and curated-selection description](https://voorsangerarchive.org/the-archive/)
- **University pages.** The university's arts page, paragraph 12 of the saved paragraph extraction, identifies the archive and describes its physical and digital design collections. The department's archive announcement, paragraph 9, describes several kinds of digital project material and the selected-project database. These descriptions concern the same archive. They are not independent evidence of custody of a WTC specimen or of a particular preservation report. [University arts page](https://www.stthomas.edu/student-life/arts/) · [Department announcement](https://blogs.stthomas.edu/arthistory/2018/04/24/voorsangerarchitectsarchive/)

## What this changes

The university and the archive now provide a named institutional route for a **project-level finding aid**, while the archive's own exclusions caution against treating it as a repository for the firm's business correspondence. Neither shared architect identity nor a general list of design documents establishes that the missing WTC report is present.

The strongest limit on dismissing the route is the same page's broad inclusion of project documents and digital files. A preservation or inventory report could have been retained as project documentation. That is an untested possibility. Conversely, failure to find a WTC title in a short scope page or an inaccessible interface cannot establish that the full archive lacks it. No whole-collection absence claim is made.

The earlier **November 2002 Draft 2**, **October 2004** and **2006** inventory descriptions, and **January 31, 2007** inventory reference remain distinct and unresolved. Nothing recovered here joins those editions or changes the FEMA WTC 7 field descriptions, NIST's unidentified classifications, or the item-specific custody gaps. The prior source values and reports remain unchanged. Source hashes, byte sizes, access timestamps and paragraph locators are in [SOURCE-PINS.json](SOURCE-PINS.json). The linked selected-projects page returned a reader error and the database exposed only a JavaScript-required wrapper in that reader. No catalog search result, collection census or item-level absence was established. One unnecessary repeat of the same selected-projects reader failure was recognized and logged locally, not counted as new evidence. No archive staff were contacted and no request was sent. The public derivative contains original notes and source metadata, without source images, personal contact details or private case material. This is a retrieval clarification, not a new steel experiment, attribution decision or finding of concealment.
