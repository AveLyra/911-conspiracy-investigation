# Classic/Miami: original-page checks against the recovered report

November 12, 2026. This checks selected claims and table rows from the captured body of Scooby report 15 against the retained FBI scans. Some source pages were unavailable to the research chat, but were already preserved in the project; they were visually rechecked here. This is not a complete audit of the report or all its attachments.

| Claim or artifact | What the original record says and does not say |
|---|---|
| Miami lead and four employees | The [September 15, 2001 FBI communication, section 1 PDF p. 39](../../urban-moving-systems/fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=39), reports that Newark detained and interviewed four Classic International Movers employees on Miami information and that Miami believed the company was used by an unnamed alleged hijacker. The page does not identify the alleged customer or factual basis. It separately reports analysis of 16 computers seized from UMS; that sentence does not identify any particular later computer artifact or prove a Classic transaction. |
| Classic number in a notebook | The [September 2001 FBI memorandum, section 5 p. 50](../../urban-moving-systems/fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=50), reports that the company's number appeared in a notebook seized from the five detainees. It does not give the digits, identify the writer or prove a call. The later closure repeats the lead and cannot automatically be treated as independent confirmation. |
| Typed phone-list entry | The [section 3 scan, PDF p. 25](../../urban-moving-systems/fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-3.pdf#page=25), visibly lists “Classic International” and 888-461-6683 on a typed phone list, with handwritten “Computer #11” and “Item A.” This corrects the recovered chat's inability to inspect that artifact. The shortened name is a plausible locator, but the page does not independently establish the historical subscriber, connect it to the notebook entry or the Miami customer, or show that anyone called the number. A different release's page numbering must be checked against that release. |
| Return and possible grouping | The [October 4, 2002 communication, section 6 p. 6](../../urban-moving-systems/fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=6), lists 1B25/E01889034 and 1B25/E01889039, then a redacted case reference followed by 1B4/E01889578, 1B30 and 1B11. It reports that reviewed translated material was personal and a linguist saw nothing of foreign-intelligence nature in those documents. The syntax groups identifiers but does **not unambiguously map** 1B25/E01889034 as a component of 1B4/E01889578, nor identify which notebook contained the Classic entry. The recovered report's definitive arrow should therefore be treated as a hypothesis pending the original crosswalk, not as established by this page alone. The linguist's statement concerns the documents reviewed, not every investigative item. |
| Ridgefield fax | The [fax cover, section 1 p. 134](../../urban-moving-systems/fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=134), states 24 pages including the cover and asks for checks on four subjects. Its machine header says September 14, 2001, while handwriting appears to say September 4. The cover itself does not name Classic or establish the contents of 23 accompanying pages. The discrepancy and attachment identity require the original transmission or recipient package. |

The Miami source and return, customer/job records, historical subscriber record, actual notebook page, computer extraction/custody crosswalk, and Ridgefield attachments remain the discriminating documents. No checked page establishes that a hijacker used Classic, that UMS personnel contacted Classic, or that the companies coordinated an operation. Matching digits, if eventually established, would show a shared datum and would still require additional evidence of contact and purpose.
