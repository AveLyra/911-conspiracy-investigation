# FAA Domestic Events Network refresher training records

November 6, 2026 UTC · B129 / PD03–PD04. The 2006 Inspector General report says the FAA mandated refresher training for all air traffic controllers after the June 2004 Capitol evacuation, including communication over the Domestic Events Network (DEN). The dated training orders identify how such training would be documented, and a later DOT report identifies a separate response involving DEN training. [CC-2006-085, p. 12](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cc2006085.pdf#page=12)

## The requirement and the record of completion

- **June 22, 2005 — FAA Order 3120.4L.** Paragraph 2-12(a) requires documenting proficiency training on **FAA Form 3120-1**. Paragraph 2-12(b) calls for written annual facility refresher programs, including hijacking and other emergencies, with additional topics identified through Air Traffic and service-area offices. The radar-facility list in 2-12(b)(7) names three airspace-intruder topics, ending with Mode C and non-Mode C targets. It contains no explicit DEN item. The general subject list is open-ended, and the order provides for mandatory briefings distributed separately. [Order pp. 2-3-3–4, PDF pp. 35–36](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/ND/3120-4L.pdf#page=35)
- **A dated entry can be a scheduled class.** Appendix 1 permits coded subject entries if the facility maintains a corresponding master sheet that describes the training. For scheduled entries, it permits filling certain blocks in advance. **Block A** is the date of entry and **Block D** is the date completed. To verify whether DEN training occurred, join the subject code and the applicable master sheet to a completed entry. The appearance of a scheduled entry alone leaves completion open. Seek non-personal course records or aggregate completion evidence. [Appendix 1, §7, PDF p. 77](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/ND/3120-4L.pdf#page=77)

## Wording preserved across versions

| Record | What the text says |
| --- | --- |
| 2006 OIG, CC-2006-085, p. 12 | Describes a mandate applying to **all air traffic controllers** after the 2004 incident, including **communicating over the DEN**. This is the OIG’s report of an earlier action. [OIG report](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cc2006085.pdf#page=12) |
| March 15, 2011 — FAA 3120.4M, §6(e)(4) and §6(h) | The radar-facility annual training list prints **“Defense Event Network (DEN)”**. The separate managers and controller-in-charge provision expressly includes DEN notification. [PDF pp. 15–16](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/3120%204M%20FINAL%20with%20Signature%20110311.pdf#page=16) |
| January 30, 2012 — FAA JO 3120.4M Change 1, §§6(e)(4), 6(h) | Retains **“Defense Event Network (DEN)”** in the radar-facility list and the managers/controller-in-charge provision. [PDF pp. 8–9](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/3120.4M%20CHG%201.pdf#page=9) |
| September 30, 2013 — FAA JO 3120.4N, §5(b)(8)(d) | The corresponding airspace intruder training item prints **“Domestic Events Network (DEN)”**. Section 5(b)(12) also includes DEN notification in the manager/controller-in-charge training. [PDF p. 19](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/media/FAA-Order-JO-3120.4N-Air-Traffic-Technical-Training.pdf#page=19) |

These orders provide dated language and audiences. The two expanded names remain as printed. The 2005 omission in the radar-facility list and the later inclusion do not establish when training was first assigned or delivered. A test compared the extracted airspace-intruder items in the four versions and confirmed the two later literal forms. The earlier OIG account remains separate.

## The separate NTSB recommendation

The NTSB issued **A-10-42** on **March 18, 2010**, asking for standard procedures to document and share controller information such as contact with pilots. In its report to Congress, DOT reports that the FAA had developed and conducted radio communications training with NATCA **prior to receipt** of that recommendation. It says the training included DEN notification and identifies three orders for the related quality checks. This gives a separate lead to the underlying training package and dispatch and completion records. It does not establish that this was the same instruction described by the OIG after the 2004 incident. [Original NTSB letter, PDF pp. 1 and 6](https://www.ntsb.gov/safety/safety-recs/RecLetters/A-10-042-043.pdf#page=6) · [DOT report, printed pp. 136–137, PDF pp. 140–141](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2021-11/Report-to-Congress-on-Secretary-of-Transportation-Responses-to-NTSB-Recommendations-Final.pdf#page=141)
