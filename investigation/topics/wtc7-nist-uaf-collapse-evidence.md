# WTC 7 collapse: NIST, the University of Alaska Fairbanks and the evidence

**Answer:** NIST concluded that fire initiated a sequence of structural failures that led to WTC 7's collapse. A University of Alaska Fairbanks (UAF) modeling study disputed the fire conclusion. The disagreement does not by itself establish explosives, deliberate demolition, or involvement by Urban Moving Systems. [NIST FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation) · [UAF report](https://www.wtc7report.org/).

## What the models and observations do—and do not—show

NIST developed a fire-triggered structural sequence and performed some sensitivity checks. This project's review identifies questions about reconstructed fire histories, connection behavior, damage transfer and the independently unverified transition to global collapse. Some modeled burning differed from observations, but the effect on the relevant structural sequence has not been independently quantified. A modeling discrepancy is not evidence of deliberate manipulation by itself. [Technical cross-check and source links](../cross-chat-evidence-integration-2026-11-10/README.md#wtc-7-model-checks-and-their-limits).

UAF's study concluded that fire did not cause the collapse and modeled extensive column failures under its stated assumptions. Reproducing motion with imposed failures does not itself identify their physical cause. NIST's roughly 2.25-second near-free-fall interval concerns a tracked roofline point within a longer descent; it is an observation to explain, not by itself a blast signature. The UAF study concerns WTC 7, not the Twin Towers. [NIST FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation) · [Project causal-chain assessment](../wtc7/causal-chain-synthesis/report.md).

## Residues and physical evidence

NIST states that it did not test **Twin Towers steel** for explosive or thermite residues. This prevents describing its tower investigation as a negative residue test; it does not supply a positive chemical finding. The project's review discusses sample attribution and timing limitations for corroded steel, disputes over red/gray chips, and gaps in independent specimen-matched replication. [NIST towers FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-towers-investigation) · [Physical evidence review](../urban-moving-systems/counterevidence-and-records-2026-11-06/README.md).

Version-matched model inputs, independent reconstruction, calibrated recording comparisons and authenticated specimen histories could sharpen the competing explanations. Fire, damage and NIST's scenario-dependent blast analysis are important counterevidence to an explosives hypothesis; uncertainty about one model does not prove another.
