# WTC visitor card, Urban Moving Systems and Max Movers: what access records show

**Answer:** An FBI property receipt lists a World Trade Center visitor card, but the reviewed record does not establish its holder, issuing office, building, date or use. No primary record reviewed establishes a UMS or Max Movers job inside WTC 1, 2 or 7 before the attacks. [FBI receipt, section 2, PDF p. 81](../urban-moving-systems/fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-2.pdf#page=81) · [Access review](../urban-moving-systems/wtc-badge-record-trail-2026-11-06/README.md).

## The card and the companies

The receipt places the card in a fanny pack among inventoried property; its connection to a particular detainee's bag is unconfirmed. A card could reflect an ordinary visit, an unused or retained credential, or another holder. Identifying it is an important lead, not proof of access or of an explosives operation.

An August 2001 insurance fax names UMS and Max; a separate employee account describes shared ownership and operations. Neither establishes a WTC customer or the carrier for Zim's relocation. The relocation was publicly announced months ahead, and contemporary reporting did not identify the moving company. [UMS/Max and Zim review](../urban-moving-systems/zim-move-and-address-lists-2026-11-05/README.md) · [April 2001 announcement](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/zim-moves-regional-head-office-from-new-york-to-norfolk).

## Records that could resolve the lead

Seek a legible original card and property crosswalk, issuer and host records, dated entry logs, tenant authorizations, dispatches, invoices and dock/elevator bookings. Port Authority badge rosters are useful leads but their coverage of temporary visitors is unconfirmed; a roster entry is not proof of a particular visit. A well-documented unrelated holder or adequately scoped negative search could weaken this access claim. Even a confirmed visit or job would not alone establish explosive installation.
