## What this adds, and its ceiling

TN 1749 supplies a concrete **later** chain from experimental component comparisons and an attributed 2008 spring-law form to detailed/reduced connection models and a nine-test assembly comparison. It also records model fitting, incomplete experimental coverage of post-ultimate behavior, and mismatched failure modes. These are stronger and more specific evidence than a bare attribution, but they do **not** establish what the inaccessible 2008 article actually documented, validate WTC 7 fire-exposed connections, or authenticate the WTC 7 historical inputs.

The source is primary for TN 1749's own method choices and reported computational results; its descriptions of Rex/Easterling, Richard and Thompson experiments are **secondary to those original studies**, which this subtask did not acquire. Multiple curves or repeated citations do not create independent study families. No source-to-source or table arithmetic was recomputed here; percentages below are the report's statements, not independent checks.

## Disconfirming details retained

At **PDF 44 / printed 26**, the report says both models reproduce the main slip, flexural and catenary stages, and the steep resistance drops and successive failures. But:

- Both models predict bolt-shear fracture in every case; experiments also exhibit shear-tab tensile rupture and block shear. The report attributes this variability plausibly to nearby calculated capacities and strength variation, not to a demonstrated specimen-specific failure prediction.
- Vertical-load agreement is better than beam-axial-force agreement. Axial forces depend on axial restraint, initial gaps and unloading treatment. Another study's exterior axial springs are discussed as representing unknown test-frame stiffness; TN 1749 instead finds its gap representation improves that aspect of agreement.
- The modeled peak always accompanies initial failure; two experiments instead peak at a subsequent failure (4-bolt test 1 and 5-bolt test 3).
- Rotations reported in this study are about 5% greater than Thompson's because of differing length definitions. This is a comparison convention, not new physical ductility.

At **PDF 90 / printed 72**, a later floor-system modeling discussion selects abrupt post-ultimate fracture after comparing the consequence of that assumption and citing Thompson (2009) and Weigand et al. (2012). This supports preserving post-ultimate-law sensitivity as a consequential modeling choice. It does not establish the choice made in the 2008 paper or in the historical WTC 7 run. No floor-system capacity calculation from that page is adopted here.

## Precisely located unresolved sources and transfer

The **PDF 112 / printed 94** reference list identifies two exact original component-study leads:

- Rex, C. O., and Easterling, S. W. (2003), “Behavior and modeling of a bolt bearing on a single plate,” *Journal of Structural Engineering*, **129(6), 792–800**; TN 1749 selects test 41.
- Richard, R. M., Gillett, P. E., Kriegh, J. D., and Lewis, B. A. (1980), “The analysis and design of single plate framing connections,” *Engineering Journal*, AISC, **2nd Quarter, 38–52**.

The original Thompson (2009) experiment is a further named lead; its full bibliography entry was not inspected in this pass. The double-shear source Wallaert/Fisher (1965) is named at PDF 30, but its detailed comparison pages were not selected. These are finite next-source leads, **not acquired or verified originals**. No new search was undertaken.

The unavailable exact 2008 paper remains separately logged in [paper-source-review.md](paper-source-review.md). The remaining bridge is not erased by TN 1749: original 2008 methods/references → documented WTC 7 adaptation and calibration → location/property mappings and actual historical inputs. This later report can change confidence in specific component-model assumptions and reveal validation limits; it cannot itself supply that complete historical bridge or change a cause ranking.
