## Page-level observations and attribution

### FEMA 403 Chapter 5

**Printed 5-16 / physical 16.** The page text states that WTC 2 collapsed at
9:59 a.m. and WTC 1 at 10:29 a.m.; WTC 1 debris went into the streets and the
extent/severity of damage to WTC 7 was then unknown. It says Figure 5-14 shows
an aerial debris cloud just after WTC 1, Figure 5-15 shows WTC 1 debris between
WTC 7 and Verizon, and Figure 5-12(B) approximates that earlier debris. It
also states that the roof and east, west, and north elevations did not appear
significantly affected, while southwest-corner damage was photographed. These
are the report's narrative and attribution, not a new independent chain of
custody for the photographs.

**Printed 5-17 / physical 17.** Figure 5-12 is a three-panel plan diagram titled
“Sequence of debris generated by collapses of WTC 2, 1, and 7.” Panel A labels
WTC 2, panel B labels WTC 1, and panel C labels WTC 7. The WTC 7 panel depicts
a dark debris area around the WTC 7 label, extending visibly outside the drawn
WTC 7 rectangle into surrounding site areas; an annotation says debris hits
30 West Broadway to the north. The diagram has no visible scale bar or numeric
distance, and it is explicitly an approximation in the surrounding text.

**Printed 5-19 / physical 19.** Figure 5-15 is captioned “Debris from the
collapse of WTC 1 located between WTC 7 (left) and the Verizon building
(right).” The photograph visibly shows a substantial street-level rubble field
between those buildings, but the caption attributes it to WTC 1. Figure 5-16
shows a damaged southwest corner of WTC 7 from West Street, with smoke and
visible damaged façade; it is not a post-collapse debris-boundary map.

**Printed 5-24 / physical 24.** Figure 5-20 is a north-view photograph with
both mechanical penthouses visible; smoke/cloud obscures much of the lower
scene. The page text attributes WTC 7 debris as spreading mainly west toward
Verizon and south, says 30 West Broadway to the north was significantly
damaged, and says Irving Trust and the Post Office did not appear structurally
damaged. It reports an “average debris field radius” of approximately 70 feet
and points to Figures 5-12(C) and 5-26. No center, measurement protocol,
denominator, uncertainty, or definition of “average” is supplied on this page;
the number cannot be converted here into a mass fraction or a verified radius
from the building outline. The paragraph immediately below begins a potential
collapse mechanism and is not treated as a debris observation.

**Printed 5-28 / physical 28.** Figure 5-26 is a black-and-white aerial image
labelled “Debris generated after collapse of WTC 7.” It labels WTC 7, Verizon,
30 West Broadway, Irving Trust, the U.S. Post Office, WTC 5, and WTC 6. The
visible rubble/dark field is concentrated around the WTC 7 area but extends
into adjacent site areas; the image has no scale bar or explicit pre-collapse
WTC 7 outline, and the capture date/cleanup stage is not stated on the page.
The surrounding text explicitly calls the later fuel/truss explanation a
hypothesis based on potential rather than demonstrated fact; that mechanism
discussion is not used to authenticate the map.

### FEMA 403 Chapter 7

**Printed 7-8 / physical 8.** The page describes the Verizon building and says
its east (Washington Street) side was damaged from about the ninth floor down,
primarily by debris sliding out from the base of WTC 7, while allowing that
some damage may also have been caused by WTC 1 debris. It records damaged bays
and columns. The photograph is a report illustration, not an independently
authenticated deposit inventory.

**Printed 7-9 / physical 9.** Figure 7-7 is captioned “Verizon building — damage
to east elevation (Washington Street) due to WTC 7 framing leaning against the
building.” The photograph visibly shows large steel framing and floor/debris
material lodged against and through damaged portions of the adjacent building.
Figure 7-8 shows the broader east elevation with localized multi-floor damage.
The caption is a strong report-level attribution of identifiable WTC 7
structural framing beyond WTC 7's own plan outline, subject to the report's
source and attribution methods.

**Printed 7-10 / physical 10.** Figure 7-9 is a close-up of damaged Verizon
east-elevation column masonry/steel. The text distinguishes south-side damage
primarily from WTC 1 projectile debris from east-side damage attributed mainly
to lateral pressure of debris at WTC 7's base. It says two columns were
deflected inward up to about two feet, but did not buckle, and that structural
bays above remained essentially undamaged. The page therefore supplies both an
outside-building WTC 7 attribution and a counterexample to treating every
nearby impact as global structural failure.

**Printed 7-13 / physical 13.** The page identifies 30 West Broadway/Fiterman
Hall north of WTC 7 and states that its south exterior bay suffered structural
damage from impact by large debris from WTC 7. It also says its east and north
faces were not damaged and that the building had no fire. Figure 7-13 visibly
shows a large, localized, multi-story breach and exposed steel. This is a
directly depicted neighboring-building effect, but the debris-to-building
attribution remains the FEMA report's attribution rather than a recovered
piece-by-piece custody record.

**Printed 7-14 / physical 14.** The text reports concentrated south-face
damage, collapsed exterior bays, considerable debris on the eighth floor, and
steel beams that yielded but remained in place. It says the WTC 7 debris impact
was contained to exterior bays between roof setbacks and was more extensive
than Verizon's east-side damage; no damage was observed one bay away. The
photograph at the bottom is a different site view and is not itself a WTC 7
debris map. This page supports localized external impact and preserves a
limiting observation about its structural reach.

### NIST NCSTAR 1A

**Printed 4 / physical 46.** Figure 1-3 shows an overlay of the Con Edison
substation footprint and the WTC 7 footprint, with dimensions and streets;
Figure 1-4 is a pre-September-11 aerial view of the substation. The diagram
makes clear that the substation and the WTC 7 plan outline are different
geometries.

**Printed 5 / physical 47.** The text gives WTC 7's irregular-trapezoid
dimensions and says about half of the building rose outside the Con Edison
substation footprint. It describes the lower floors, transfer trusses, and
upper floors. This is site/structure context, not a post-collapse debris
measurement. It prevents treating the substation footprint as synonymous with
the full WTC 7 building footprint.

## Frozen claim assessment

**Literal claim:** “All identifiable WTC 7 structural debris remained within
the pre-collapse WTC 7 plan outline.” **Not supported; provisionally
contradicted at the report-attribution level.** FEMA's Chapter 7 captions and
narrative identify WTC 7 framing leaning against the adjacent Verizon building
and large WTC 7 debris damaging the exterior bay of 30 West Broadway. Those
are identifiable outside-building structural effects, not merely dust-cloud
spread. The conclusion is limited by the absence here of original photo files,
surveyed building outlines, exact capture times, and a chain-of-custody
inventory tying each depicted member to WTC 7.

**Weaker claim:** “Most collapse mass landed in a relatively compact area.”
**Consistent with the selected pages but not quantified here.** The FEMA map
and narrative depict concentration near WTC 7, and the reported approximately
70-foot “average debris field radius” is compatible with compactness. But the
radius has no defined center or uncertainty on the selected page, and the
outside-building deposits show that compactness cannot be restated as literal
containment.

**Causal implication:** None established. Debris outside the outline is
consistent with ordinary collapse-generated impact, a deliberate removal
scenario, or other mechanisms. These pages can test a footprint assertion; they
do not discriminate fire from demolition.
