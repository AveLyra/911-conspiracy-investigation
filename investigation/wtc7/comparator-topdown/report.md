# Top-down demolition: supported lowering is a different comparison

September 20, 2026 UTC. Research-only WP4 source/mechanics assessment, not a
structural certification, calibrated motion comparison or cause verdict.

## Result

The complete contractor paper documents a materially different removal process:
**a prepared roof enclosure remains supported while the building is dismantled
in stages and the enclosure is lowered under control.** It is not an example
of an intact high-rise entering an unsupported whole-building drop. This fills
the previously missing documentary top-down-method category, not the matched
motion or independent historical-validation requirements of WP4.

The full text makes the earlier overview more precise: typical work involved
two floors per cycle, not necessarily one lowering event per floor, and it
reports completion in early July 2013 rather than merely the June 2013 planned
end in the earlier publicity. Those are attributed execution claims, not
independently authenticated site logs. The distinction matters for source
accuracy, not WTC7's cause.

The reported broad structural descriptors are above-ground steel, below-ground
SRC, 138.9m and 39 storeys plus a penthouse (Table1,06-1). These do not supply a
matched floor system, connection inventory, lateral system, actual loading,
prior damage, protection or thermal exposure. Deliberate preparation, added
support, debris removal and staged work are major differences from an
uncontrolled failure hypothesis. No absence of prior damage/fire throughout
the site's history is inferred from the paper's silence.

The chronology also has distinct phase boundaries: September 2011 for
interior/asbestos work, June 2012 for structural work, and November 2012 for
the first lowering. The paper's aggregate durations refer to differing scopes;
none is a duration of continuous cap descent. The final phases include removal
of temporary works and retained roof structure, not just typical floor cycles.

## Physical relevance and strongest objection

For the specified slow, controlled cap process, substantial upward support is
part of the mechanism; material removal does not require the retained cap to
be in free fall. That is a mechanics interpretation, not a newly measured
acceleration or force history. It cannot be substituted for a demonstration
of WTC7's actual residual support or rapid loss of resistance.

Conversely, it would be invalid to use this slow method to argue that every
intentional removal must look slow. Explosive, mechanically initiated gravity
collapse, staged dismantling and other specified methods require separate
comparators. This case supports that separation; it supplies no new likelihood
ratio favoring fire or intervention. The current
[causal-chain assessment](../causal-chain-synthesis/report.md) is unchanged.

The strongest objection is participant selection and incomplete execution
evidence: a contractor's illustrated account can omit exceptions, mixed phases
or adverse outcomes. The detailed description supports its attributed method
claim (B), not complete physical validation (D). Raw monitoring/operation records
could qualify it. However, lack of those records is not a reason to erase the
positive published evidence of deliberate preparation and maintained support.
