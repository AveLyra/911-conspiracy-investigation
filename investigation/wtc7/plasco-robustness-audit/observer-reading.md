## Model and assumptions: what is actually disclosed

The model represents the top six storeys of a 17-storey building, not the full
falling tower and receiving lower structure (77/92, 91/106, 93/108). Its plan
has nine segments with four core columns and 38 perimeter columns, primary
beams, NS trusses and EW Vierendeel beams. Figure 3.7 supplies the local
SE/SW/NE/NW and SES/SEE identifiers. The construction drawings and photographs
inform geometry discussion, but this chapter does not provide a complete
machine-readable geometry/section/connection provenance join. The repeated
phrase ground reactions must not silently turn a top-six-storey model into a
verified full-height foundation model. Exact truncation/base restraints and
load transfer from omitted floors need the executable model.

The named OpenSees elements are displacementBeamColumnThermal and
ShellNLDKGQThermal. Steel thermal degradation is attributed to Eurocode 1;
concrete is layered plane-stress CDP and rebar is a biaxial plane-stress J2
layer (77/92). Fibre-section thermal input uses 15 points for an I section and
25 for the stated stiffened variant (93-94/108-109). These are method
descriptions, not verified parameter values, versions or implementation tests.
The chapter reports roughly 71,000 nodes and 105,000 elements, with mesh-size
ranges and an unclear column phrase (95/110); it supplies no mesh-convergence
demonstration within these pages.

Dead load is described as 6.2 kPa plus 1.3 kPa partition allowance, total
7.5 kPa. The cited regulation's live load is 5.0 kPa, while the estimated
minimum actual live load is 6.0 kPa (94/109). Later ratio calculations mention
DL+1.5LL (99/114). Those disclosures do not identify beyond doubt which live
load and load factor were applied to every thermal case; that mapping must
not be guessed from an adjacent paragraph.

Thermal exposure is intentionally idealized ISO 834 loading of selected
members/zones. The author explicitly declines a realistic reconstruction of
the historical fire and calls time-domain loading a convenience (77-79/92-94,
92/107). Section heat transfer produces temperature histories; gas/exposure,
slab-surface, through-depth and column temperatures are not interchangeable.
The one-primary zone discussion gives a slab bottom of 900 C and top around
85 C (104/119), whereas core redistribution refers to gas temperature above
550 C (101/116) and secondary-column buckling to column temperature 500 C
(109/124). None is an independently established historical steel temperature.

Table 7 (95/110) is materially important: gravity is Static/LoadControl with
ModifiedNewton, while the thermomechanical analysis is Transient/Newmark
with KrylovNewton; both list UmfPack, Plain numbering/constraints and
NormDispIncr. Gravity is applied in 10 increments; 3,600 seconds of temperature
loading is described in 100 steps. It would be wrong to call the whole chapter
a purely static analysis. It would also be wrong to infer a validated
falling-floor impact model from the word Transient: mass/damping details,
Newmark coefficients, step adaptation, tolerance values, contact, separation,
energy balance and completion/failure logs are not supplied here. The author
also cautions that sudden buckling dynamics could increase a predicted load
(116/131). Do not convert analysis steps to actual incident clock time, or
infer non-convergence simply because a plotted history ends.

Connection and composite assumptions are consequential. Weld/connection
degradation is expressly not modelled (98/113, 112/127). That supports a
conditional sustained-connectivity interpretation, not a verified assertion
that every joint is rigid or that every real connection remains intact. Full
composite action is historically uncertain; full/no-composite models are
mentioned for later parts of the thesis (91/106). This chapter describes a
composite floor contributing to redistribution, but does not provide every
coupling or slip constraint for these cases. Do not import a later chapter's
connection implementation into this one.

The retained stable cases matter physically: the chapter itself contains
load shedding that does not become global collapse. A connected floor can
transmit load to other columns, and cooler parts can brace overloaded ones.

that connection continuity universally prevents collapse or inevitably causes it.

## Exact next dependencies and permitted result

The smallest useful next technical distinction is not another uncalibrated
animation. It is a traceable case input/output package identifying:

- The six-storey node/element/section model, bottom restraints, floors and
  heated-element memberships, geometric imperfections and load combinations.
- Actual section heat histories, exposed faces, convection/radiation and
  protection/masonry assumptions, including why edge beams remain unheated.
- Material parameter/version files; slab/rebar/composite coupling and any
  connection, release, damage, failure or deletion commands, even if unused.
- Solver version, mass/damping, Newmark parameters, tolerances, step/log
  history, convergence evidence and outputs for stable as well as failing cases.
- Reactions/displacements and an unambiguous criterion distinguishing
  local buckling, loss of load, connection rupture, numerical termination
  and completed global collapse. Contact/energy accounting is additionally
  required for any claim about falling floors or a full propagation sequence.
- Source-to-capacity/exposure evidence and genuinely unused evaluation data
  if the claim is historical Plasco identification rather than an idealized
  structural-robustness demonstration.

This reading establishes a meaningful source distinction: reported thermal
load-induced deformation/buckling coexists with successful redistribution and
conditional stability. It does not establish how the real building's critical
connections behaved, authenticate a complete collapse pathway, or change a
WTC7 causal ranking. No numerical run, expert review, human gate, engine
acceptance, legal promotion, disclosure, acquisition, staging, commit or push
has occurred. Only this reader-owned working record was created. Chapter 7
