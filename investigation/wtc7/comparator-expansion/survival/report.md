# One Meridian Plaza and First Interstate Bank survival comparators

These two predefined cases document steel-framed office towers that remained standing after major multistorey fires. They do not show that fire-triggered collapse is impossible. They also do not validate any proposed WTC 7 initiating failure or propagation sequence. Their useful contribution is narrower: severe fire exposure and even large horizontal-member deformation do not invariably produce global collapse; any explanation of a different outcome must address the actual structural system, exposure, protection, damage and suppression history.

Status: non-canonical WP4 research; prepared during the September 8-9, 2026 research cycle. This is a source audit, not a structural certification, an independent reproduction of the cited engineering, or an expert opinion. The cases were assigned before this retrieval and were not selected from a population search. No base rate, historical uniqueness claim, causal likelihood or numerical probability follows from this pair.

## Evidence and pagination

`S-OMP-USFA` is USFA Technical Report 049, by J. Gordon Routley, Charles Jennings and Mark Chubb. The preserved file is a 99-PDF-page reissue carrying a Department of Homeland Security wrapper, not demonstrated original 1991 publication bytes. Its numbered report runs pp. 1-86; printed page *p* is PDF page *p + 12*. The printed February 1991 designation identifies the incident/report series; a foreword refers to the building still standing twenty months after the fire, and some figures carry November 1992 revision identifiers. FireDOC's catalog lists 114 pages. The reason for that pagination/edition difference is unresolved. Citations below refer to the preserved reissue and do not imply comparison against every earlier edition.[^1]

`S-FIB-USFA` is J. Gordon Routley's USFA Technical Report 022. Its preserved 41-page PDF also has a later DHS wrapper; printed page *p* is PDF page *p + 10*. `S-FIB-LAFD` is a historical-archive HTML transcription containing the executive summary, operational recommendations, and portions headed chapters 3 and 5 of the Los Angeles City Fire Department report. It is not a complete scan of that report. Use chapter and section locators; the HTML's page markers alone can be ambiguous.[^2][^3]

`S-FIB-NFPA` is Thomas J. Klem's original investigator-authored article, “Los Angeles High-Rise Bank Fire,” in *Fire Journal*, May/June 1989. The entire 136-page issue was acquired from a public mirror; relevant numbered pages are 72-74, 77, 79, 81, 83, 85-86, 88, 90-91 (PDF page = printed page + 2). ## Exposure and chronology limits

The duration labels cannot be compared as if they were furnace exposure times. From One Meridian's reported dispatch at 20:27 to control at 15:01 is **18 h 34 min** (a reproducible subtraction of reported clock values); from detector activation at 20:23 is **18 h 38 min**. Its overview also places reporting at approximately 20:40 and calls the total more than 19 hours. Exact ignition is not established by those times. The defensible summary is an approximately nineteen-hour incident with a documented eleven-hour interior-response/withdrawal chronology, not nineteen hours at an assumed steel temperature everywhere. Preserve the discrepancy rather than silently correcting the report.[^1]

At First Interstate, USFA's estimate of about 90 minutes of intense burning on each level differs from Nelson's estimate of 40-60 minutes of burning per floor (NISTIR p. 28). Nelson also places extension to floors 13, 14, 15 and 16 at approximately 23:00, 23:40, 00:20 and 00:50 (pp. 26, 28, 30). The LAFD schematic labels give 23:15, 00:00, 00:45 and 01:30. The descriptions may employ different definitions of first extension, established fire, intensity or local duration, but the retrieved records do not resolve that. They must not be averaged into a precise common fire history. NFPA p. 74 puts initial attack around 23:00; USFA p. 5 says approximately 23:10. Both describe severe initial attack difficulties and later successful suppression.[^2][^3][^4][^5]

Nelson's engineering presentation contains useful explicit limits. It uses modified ASET/FIREFORM for pre-flashover smoke-layer calculations and DETACT-QS for detector/sprinkler estimates. At 600 degrees C smoke-layer temperature it changes the assumed regime; after flashover it makes **no attempt to calculate the involved-space temperatures** (pp. 4, 6). These are not measured steel temperatures, and the report does not furnish a validated thermal-to-structural survival calculation. It assumes connected detectors responded as designed, uses partly judgment-based furniture growth histories, identifies alternative origins, cautions about the uniform zone approximation, and notes an unconfirmed period lacking photographs or eyewitness accounts (pp. 6, 8, 10, 12-14, 24). The separately mentioned detailed calculation report “in review” (p. 1) was not identified in this bounded search.[^5]

One Meridian's fire was unsprinklered on its main burned floors, but the whole incident cannot be labeled “no suppression.” First Interstate was not a freely burning tower allowed to burn to completion. Conversely, its suppression should not erase the early interval when attacks made little progress; NFPA pp. 74, 85, 88 describe the role of fuel consumption as well as firefighting in eventual control. Neither a nominal rating nor an exterior photograph establishes member temperature, restraint demand or connection capacity.[^1][^2][^4]

## WTC 7 comparability and decisive missing records

The pair shares broad attributes relevant to WTC 7 comparison: office occupancy, substantial height, steel framing, passive protection, concrete/metal-deck floors and fires on multiple levels. Those labels leave the determinants of a particular propagation mechanism unspecified. The present audit does not verify the WTC 7 side of the comparison anew or assume NIST's explanation. It identifies which comparator inputs must be matched before any result can be transferred.

1. **Structure and restraint:** acquire each comparator's original framing, connection, slab, diaphragm and lateral-system drawings, member schedules, loads, damage survey and any stabilization/repair designs. A column restrained by intact adjacent floors does not test a mechanism requiring loss of several floors of restraint. One Meridian's large horizontal deformation is a valuable constraint on an overly general claim that floor deformation necessarily produces column failure; it does not establish that WTC 7 had the same restraint or connection response.
2. **Thermal boundary conditions:** retrieve time-resolved photographs, original alarm records, hose/pump logs, firefighter floor observations, window-breakage history, fuel inventory and material/fireproofing tests. The strongest confounder against a simple duration comparison is actual suppression: it affected both cases, at different stages and by different means. Nominal ratings are comparison inputs, not universal survival thresholds.
3. **Original post-fire assessments:** First Interstate's underlying structural analysis is the highest-value specific gap. USFA and NFPA report reassuring results but do not expose the computation and complete inspection record. One Meridian needs the complete PFD investigative report and post-fire engineering surveys to establish member-by-member damage, residual capacity and the basis for continued standing versus occupancy decisions. Its 16-page Klem preliminary report is catalogued but not obtained.
4. **Source completeness and conflicts:** obtain First Interstate's complete 60-page NFPA report and complete LAFD report; resolve the fire-spread/duration definitions and source clocks. Seek the detailed Nelson calculations mentioned as in review. Compare TR-049's 99-page reissue against the 114-page catalogued edition before claiming edition completeness.
5. **Outcome labels:** retain local damage, loss of occupancy/use and global survival as separate fields. Do not turn First Interstate's department concerns during firefighting into measured imminent collapse, or turn its later no-danger assessment into an independently calculated safety margin. Do not relabel One Meridian as a global fire collapse because later decisions might have involved demolition; that later removal history was not independently established in this subtask.

The strongest evidence against misuse of these survival examples is in the primary reports themselves: successful but very different suppression; reported local structural damage and compromised barriers; partly shared investigative inputs; inconsistent duration estimates; and unavailable engineering assessments. The strongest evidence against an equally overbroad inevitable-collapse argument is One Meridian's reported coexistence of severe horizontal deformation, retained column support and continued global standing. Both conclusions can be true without selecting a WTC 7 mechanism.
