# Plasco thesis Chapter 6: bounded methods and negative-result audit

Research working note, 2026-09-09, under the investigation charter (supporting item not included in this public package). No legal fact, source/exhibit ID, solver reproduction, independent historical verification, or engineering certification is created.

Subsequent work: [Chapter 7 has now received a separate full documentary audit](chapter7/report.md). The scope statements below describe this original Chapter 6 pass and are preserved; they do not imply Chapter 7 remains unread after that follow-up. Neither pass executed the structural model or acquired the journal full text.

Chapter 6 adds a structural analysis to the already inspected Plasco fire-reconstruction programme, but its evidence ceiling matters. It computes substantial floor and column movements under selected thermal histories, infers likely connection failure, and then tests another model with corner floors already removed. That latter model remains stable under its selected northwest-column heating. The chapter also preserves a 13th-floor response mismatch and proposes recalibrating the thermal input before examining later collapse events. These are useful, source-pinned conditional results; they do not independently identify the historical connection rupture sequence or validate a whole-tower collapse calculation.

## Computed response, inferred rupture and prescribed damage

The ISO-fire calculation produces nonlinear downward floor movements and column displacement/force reversals; Fig.6.15 displays connection-region rotations (**194–197 / 209–212**). The next page reasons from large relative column movements to likely steel-connection damage. The composite calculation likewise proposes that thermal bowing, member buckling and partial composite action would impose severe connection rotation (**198 / 213; 205 / 220; 210 / 225**).

Within Chapter 6, no calibrated connection moment-rotation rupture envelope, tested weld/gusset failure capacity, fracture-strain limit, or explicit connection-deletion criterion is supplied. Large computed rotation is therefore not interchangeable with a computed, experimentally checked joint rupture. Nor does the displayed floor displacement alone establish the timing of detachment, falling-floor impact, or progressive propagation. This statement is scoped to the inspected chapter; it is not an assertion that the rest of the thesis or the unread journal paper contains no further connection analysis.

For the post-corner-loss calculation, the author explicitly creates **another model with fire-affected corner floors removed**, retaining peripheral columns and edge beams (**198–199 / 213–214**, Fig.6.17). This imposed geometry is clear in the complete figure. Its stated purpose is to work backward toward temperature conditions compatible with the observed corner-loss state. It is a conditional response calculation after assumed support loss, not a continuous independently predicted sequence from connection rupture through floor removal.

The chapter describes a selected **300–600°C column-temperature range** and reports roughly 40–50 mm corner-column movement, concluding that northwest peripheral-column heating did not destabilize the remaining model (**198–200 / 213–215; 210 / 225**). The calculated corner-column gravity share is about 2%, while core and other principal members carry much larger shares (**194 / 209**). These load shares are model outputs, not independent field measurements. The source also explicitly leaves the effect of batten weld quality on built-up-column buckling strength outside this study (**200 / 215**). The stable response must not become a universal claim of column safety under arbitrary heating, weld condition or later core damage.

## Contrary results and sensitivity retained

1. **Stable after prescribed northwest floor loss.** The above calculation is a genuine reported non-global-failure case under its stated assumptions. It weakens any claim that this programme shows northwest local heating or corner loss necessarily causes immediate global collapse. It does not show the later building should have remained stable under a different fire/damage state.
2. **Composite versus non-composite response.** The composite model initially bows more but later has a more gradual displacement increase; the non-composite model exhibits much sharper deformation growth. Figs.6.21–6.23 and the uncertainty discussion preserve a material modelling sensitivity (**202–205 / 217–220**). The shaded region in Fig.6.23 is the author's proposed response envelope, not a calibrated probability or joint-failure threshold.
3. **West-side lower response.** The CFD-driven model reports much less west/interior movement than in the northwest/north portion of the 11th floor, associated with its representation of the fire's limited western duration. This is a spatial negative response within the chosen calibrated exposure, not a matched independent fire-survival experiment (**206–207 / 221–222; 211 / 226**).
4. **13th-floor mismatch.** The calculation does not produce the expected comparably large 13th-floor deformation. The author calls for revisiting its cooling/thermal input and recalibrating upper-floor fires before Event 9/10, while retaining the 11th-floor match (**206–207 / 221–222**). This prevents converting the chapter's initial two-part-collapse aim into a demonstrated reproduction of both events.
5. **ISO versus CFD comparison.** Similar maximum local floor deformations are reported under two strong northwest exposures; the author specifically limits that similarity because later travelling fire has different growth/cooling phases in different parts of the building (**208–211 / 223–226**). It does not establish general equivalence of a standard fire curve and a reconstructed travelling fire.

The conclusion's claim of accurate Event-8 prediction and the argument that matching the 11th-floor pattern proves the fire model accurate are stronger than the documented verification supports (**207 / 222; 210 / 225**). The calibrated imagery, response-guided input reconsideration and unresolved 13th-floor mismatch do not form independent validation. A useful disconfirming test would hold the visual targets fixed, vary plausible fuel/cooling/suppression and composite/connection assumptions, and evaluate predeclared unused observations and failure/no-failure outcomes using disclosed cases. That test was not performed here.

## Version and precision cautions

The source contains internal references that should be checked before numerical reuse. At **208 / 223**, prose points to Fig.6.24 for the ISO result and Fig.6.23 for the CFD result, whereas those figures visibly show CFD contours and the composite/non-composite comparison respectively. At **204 / 219**, prose gives a 650–700°C non-composite runaway range, while **194 / 209** discusses more than 800°C compartment temperatures and the displayed curves require care about temperature definitions. Do not turn these different statements into one verified rupture-temperature threshold. No curve was digitized or corrected in this audit. Table14 is called Table1 in its preceding prose (**185 / 200**).

Root's full-page review adds a related range caution: the text specifies 300–600°C for the post-removal column study, while Fig.6.18(b), **199 / 214**, displays curves extending to 800°C. Its approximate 40–50 mm text summary must not be applied to that entire plotted range or converted to an experimentally validated stability threshold. Native histories and temperature definitions are needed before numerical reuse; the figure was not digitized here.

The thesis-wide conclusions (**263–266 / 278–281**) were read to prevent confusing Chapter 6 with the later work. They expressly distinguish the northwest substructure stage from Chapter 7 and make broader assertions about global collapse and an alternative with intact connections. Those summaries do not supply a substitute full audit of Chapter 7's methods, rupture/deletion choices, convergence, impact or validation. Only Chapter 6 was read in full for this task.
