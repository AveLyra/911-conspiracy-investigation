# Windsor fire and partial-collapse comparator

Windsor is a useful mixed-outcome comparator, not a steel-framed replica of WTC 7. The strongest record in this batch is an investigator's post-fire inspection and material-testing report. It supports examining damage, retained structure and thermal-model assumptions separately. It does not supply a validated dynamic reconstruction of every failure.

## Primary record and bounded findings

INTEMAC's December 2005 **NIT 2-05**, *Fire in the Windsor building, Madrid: Survey of the fire resistance and residual bearing capacity of the structure after the fire*, was prepared by José Calavera and six coauthors involved in assessing the damaged building for subsequent demolition. The preserved 40-page bilingual scan (supporting item not included in this public package) has printed technical pages 1–35; printed page = PDF page minus two within that body. Its purpose and engagement are on printed pp.10,16,34.[^1]

The report describes a concrete core/wall-column and steel-perimeter system, with two-way concrete floors and deep technical-storey transfer members (pp.4–8). It records upper peripheral collapse, surviving core/lower structure, buckled ninth-storey steel, and damaged beam anchors (pp.10–14; Figs.10–17). Fireproofing upgrades were incomplete. Fire spread and intervention times are approximate (pp.8–10). The English sixteen-hour “extinguished” summary conflicts with later chronology distinguishing control from technical extinction; the Spanish summary describes spread over sixteen hours. No common member exposure duration follows.

Its thermal estimates use test-calibrated assumptions about ventilation and damage depth (pp.28–33), not instrumented historical temperatures. Sampling excluded slabs for safety; additional specimens came from removed members (p.20). These limits matter when carrying its results into another building's analysis.

## Separate modeling source

Fletcher, Welch, Capote, Alvear and Lázaro's October 2007 draft (supporting item not included in this public package), *Model-based analysis of a concrete building subjected to fire*, has thirteen PDF pages and explicitly identifies itself as a workshop draft. It develops fire/thermal analysis and a simplified slab-beam strategy; broader structural modeling is future work (pp.6–12). Its p.13 references INTEMAC, so repeated post-fire claims are not another independent survey. The 2008 proceedings paper located in the catalogue is a distinct version whose equivalence is unverified.[^2]

The draft's p.11 characterization of surface temperatures as confirmed by post-fire analysis should be read against INTEMAC's assumption-dependent reconstruction. A laboratory calibration and a retrospective fit can constrain plausible exposure without uniquely identifying it. This source dependence is not evidence of fabrication; it is a reason to state a narrower validation claim.

## Comparison fields and unknowns

| Field | Disposition for this batch |
|---|---|
| Outcome | Partial collapse during the fire; later deliberate removal is a different operation, not a spontaneous total-collapse label. |
| Structural match | Mixed concrete/steel system. Use its retained-load-path and damaged-connection questions; do not treat it as the same framing/connection network as WTC 7. |
| Height/storey convention | Preserve INTEMAC's level diagram, p.4, rather than combining its office, technical, basement and roof counts into an apparently universal story count. No independent height survey performed. |
| Actual loading | Complete floor-by-floor gravity and combustible load inventories unestablished. Hypothetical office/archive thermal cases are not an inventory. |
| Prior damage/preparation | Refurbishment and incomplete protection are reported; a comprehensive pre-fire damage survey is not acquired. No deliberate pre-fire weakening is established here. Post-fire demolition preparation is temporally distinct. |
| Suppression/exposure | Reported intervention chronology, not a complete water/ventilation/temperature history. No new fire-area fraction or steel temperature measurement. |
| Recording provenance | Report-reproduced photographs and captions; original camera files, capture clocks, edit chain, calibration and multi-angle continuous coverage unestablished. No video motion/acceleration/audio measured. |
| Model reproduction | Neither report's underlying calculations rerun; no complete executable dynamic collapse case acquired. Report attribution is the verified layer. |

## Claim audit

Grades follow the charter's evidence convention. The following grades concern transportable propositions, not a verdict on the event or institution. Documentary pins above are checked; historical causation, laboratory results and engineering calculations are not independently replicated.

| Claim | Layer and grade | Best competing explanation / assumption | Discriminator |
|---|---|---|---|
| This is evidence relevant to both local failure and system survival. | Inference, B, conditional on the documented damage/outcome. | Post-fire inspection does not resolve the order or cause of every damaged connection. | Time-resolved original imagery and member-by-member inspection chronology. |
| A single incident-wide fire duration represents all member exposures. | Unsupported inference, E. | Moving fire, protection and firefighting yield different local histories. | Member-specific thermal evidence and validated exposure reconstruction. |
| A thermal fit uniquely determines the actual ventilation/fuel history. | Inference, D. | Several unmeasured histories may produce similar residual damage or exterior appearance. | Sensitivity/identifiability tests varying plausible histories while holding observations fixed. |
| Windsor demonstrates WTC 7's exact initiating and global sequence. | Unsupported inference, E. | Structural and exposure differences remain material. | An explicit WTC 7-specific chain with independently checked inputs and predictions. |
| Windsor's retained structure proves no high-rise could suffer a fire-triggered total collapse. | Unsupported universalization, E. | A surviving alternate load path in one system need not exist or persist in another. | Matched load-path, connection and bracing analysis; no inference from height alone. |

The valuable next test is therefore not a visual ranking. It is a paired failure/survival analysis: which paths continued to carry gravity loads, which were damaged, when that damage occurred, and whether a model predicts both outcomes without changing assumptions after seeing each result. For Windsor, obtain the original inspection/member maps, sample locations and full test data, thermal-case inputs and local exposure records. Expert review would then assess how far those results transfer to a materially different frame. The present batch does not select a WTC 7 mechanism.
