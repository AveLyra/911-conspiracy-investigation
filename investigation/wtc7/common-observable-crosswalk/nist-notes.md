## Time, case and evidentiary conventions

All three table columns set the start of east-penthouse descent identically to zero. Agreement at that origin is an alignment convention, not an independent prediction of onset. Negative table times are relative to that reference; they are not absolute video timestamps or clock time of day.

NCSTAR1-9A physical147–148/printed96–97 describes a global-model sequence with debris damage applied, exterior load redistribution and computed buckling. It does not restate a complete native case identifier or input-deck version on these two pages. Fig4-42's axis is calculation-reference time. Text/caption pairs include 5.5(21.5), 6(22), and 1.1(17.1)s, consistently differing by16s. That arithmetic relationship is apparent; these selected pages do not explicitly define the two origins. Do not silently authenticate the native time mapping or exact case join from that offset alone.

Debris damage is explicitly an applied condition in Fig4-42. The described downstream load changes/buckling are reported computed responses, not evidence that every buckled column was individually prescribed for removal. Conversely, these pages do not establish that every precursor, thermal load or transferred damage arose without imposed inputs. Full initialization and fitting history is outside this reading.

These are the authors' published observations and outputs, not independently reproduced measurements. The comparisons concern the same historical event/source family; no unused holdout is identified in this page set. Model agreement with an alignment event and the authors' favorable accuracy appraisal cannot count as independent validation.

### 1. East-penthouse onset

**Pin/identity:** NCSTAR1A p43 Table3-1: start of descent of the east penthouse is0 for observation and both Case B variants. A separate row reports buckling of Column79, quickly followed by80/81, at−1.3s with debris and−1.4s without; observation is unavailable. Cascading-floor failure is another event: approximately−6s in the observation column,−6.6s in both analyses; its observation footnote points to NCSTAR1-9 Appendix C/Chapter8, not a directly visible floor event demonstrated on these pages.

**Visible threshold:** Passing below the roofline is distinct from onset: observed2.0s; model with debris2.4/2.7s, without2.3/2.6s. The first modeled value uses a northwest/below viewpoint, the second a north/roofline viewpoint. No numbered camera or detection rule is supplied in this table for its single observation value.

**Comparison/falsifier:** Use an identically defined first visible descent threshold and projected component; never equate it automatically with hidden C79 buckling or disappearance. With zero aligned by definition, meaningful tests concern relative pre/post-onset events and trajectory, not whether the zeros match. A reproducible relative-sequence or disappearance mismatch outside justified detection/projection uncertainty would challenge the specified case. Needed: native run/time join and observed/view-projected thresholds; neither an uncertainty allowance nor fitted shift is invented here.

### 2. West-penthouse passage below roofline

**Pin/value:** Same table: observed9.3s; with debris6.9/7.3s; without debris10.6/10.9s, using the same two modeled viewpoints. The with-debris and without-debris cases lie on different sides of the observed table value. ### 3. North-façade onset and trajectory

**Pin/value:** Table3-1's feature is the **eastern section of the north-face roofline**, observed6.9s, with-debris6.3s, without-debris9.8s. NCSTAR1A p44 narrows this to northeast corner through the east side of the screenwall. It is not an observation that the entire building's center of mass starts moving at6.9s.

NCSTAR1-9 p276 says the visible north face was clearly falling as a unit by9.0s and had descended nearly two stories in2.1s, using visible floors above foreground obstruction in the West Street view. Compare a selected roof feature, camera projection and common relative origin. A single onset bracket is not a full trajectory match. A justified feature-track mismatch would falsify that implementation's trajectory claim; absent trajectory arrays/calibration/time mapping here remains a prerequisite, not permission to infer failure or agreement. No new Chapter12 reading was sought for this bounded crosswalk.

### 4. Lateral deformation

**Observed/derived pin:** NCSTAR1-9 pp276–280, Figs5-204–206. Camera4's handheld motion and zoom are addressed using two foreground-building edges to register the original northeast edge. Through its first five shown frames (6.7,7.7,8.8,9.7,10.1s), no perpendicular displacement beyond image resolution is reported; this permits downward motion **or** line-of-sight motion, not necessarily zero lateral displacement. At10.6s the northeast top is described as tilted west. Camera3's more oblique view/difference images indicate northward tilting, with northwest and northeast edges behaving differently.

Fig5-206 is a heavily processed Camera3 frame at10.3s. NIST estimates the top northeast roof corner displaced11±3m from its original footprint, primarily north when combined with Camera4. It is a derived **local corner** displacement, not whole-building translation or footprint containment. Building dimensions, camera distance, corner view angles and a line-angle construction are named, but the numeric input set, formula and uncertainty construction are not supplied here. Camera4 shown frames bracket10.3s; they are not an exact simultaneous frame at that time.

**Model pin:** NCSTAR1-9A pp96–97/Figs4-41–43 describe inward east-wall motion, a southward global motion, and X/Y displacement contours at1.1(17.1)s. The caption's−1m to1m contour range is not the late northeast corner's computed displacement at10.3s. Different times, components and physical directions cannot be scored as one residual. NCSTAR1A p44 describes differing buckling levels and wall deflections for the debris variants; later movement after the kink is explicitly beyond the model's reliability.

**Falsifier/prerequisite:** A direction/relative-shape mismatch can be meaningful before exact metric calibration, if the same component, camera and collapse stage are established. Testing11±3m quantitatively needs the missing geometric inputs/uncertainty and a matched model coordinate history; a whole-model translation or arbitrary animation view is not a substitute. Do not extrapolate a late-model reliability limitation into a false claim that no earlier lateral comparison is possible.

### 5. North-roof kink

**Pins/feature:** NCSTAR1-9 p276 associates an incipient kink by the9.0s view with a point just left of the east end of the46th/47th-floor louvers, near **perimeter Column47**. NCSTAR1A p44 §3.5.2 describes a north-face line/kink near the end of **core Column76**; northeast movement begins about8.8s and kink is visible at9.3s, two to three seconds after north-façade descent begins. These are different locators, not automatically contradictory column identities. “Has begun to develop” and “visible” also require a common threshold before treating9.0/9.3s as disagreement.

**Prediction/status:** NIST says the simulations form the kink, but assigns no case-specific kink time or quantitative shape measure on these pages. It classifies the feature as a consequence after global collapse initiation, not initiation itself, and states subsequent building movement is beyond the reliability of model physics. The favorable overall accuracy appraisal follows this qualification; it does not erase it.

**Falsifier/prerequisite:** Predefine the same roof/façade shape change and camera projection. Failure to form it, or formation in the wrong relative sequence/location outside justified visual uncertainty, would challenge the specified model. An imposed bend or tuned alignment could not count as independent prediction. Need native case-specific shape history, feature mapping and the uncertainty of its first visible appearance; a verbal “kink present” is not a complete temporal match.
