# Nonexpansion is not a calibrated acoustic result

The newly inspected author-posted text sharpens the earlier abstract-only
citation: it does not supply an established sound-detection threshold for the
WTC7 recordings. This is a source-scope result, not a test of historical cause.

## Source finding

The inspected methods use optical temperature measurements and high-speed
images to estimate propagation and specimen expansion. The pressure explanation
in section 3.5 is an ideal-gas argument, not a reported pressure trace. Its
proposed restraint against expansion is a hypothesis; section 3.4 also notes
cracking without noticeable expansion. No calibrated microphone or reaction-
pressure result was identified in these inspected sections.
[Polk et al., author-posted text, sections 2.3, 3.4, 3.5 and 4](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379464804_Identifying_a_combination_of_intermetallic_and_thermite_reactions_that_result_in_a_non-expanding_compact).

Separate powder ignition tests report few or no visually observed microexplosions
before wire failure (section 3.3); that is not a microphone measurement.

The implication is narrower than either causal claim: specimen nonexpansion
does not establish zero transient pressure or silence, but it also does not
establish a remotely audible blast. A quieter thermal pathway remains a
separate hypothesis; this citation cannot calibrate its likelihood or its
detectability in the historical recordings. The strongest contrary reading is
that material-level behavior can support a physical possibility without
providing an acoustic test. That limited use remains legitimate.
