# What generator shutdown can change in the published diesel analysis

This bounded source audit supports a specific conclusion: falsifying the heat-to-pump-power-loss premise would reopen a material exclusion in the northeast diesel-fire cases and could require revisiting the resulting thermal boundary conditions. It would not, by itself, falsify NIST's office-fire mechanism, establish a historical diesel fire, or demonstrate a different collapse cause. The rejection grounds differ across cases; several do not depend only on generator failure.

All primary page references below are to preserved NCSTAR 1-9. Physical 432-440 correspond to printed 366-374. The source is evidence of what NIST reported and assumed; it is not an independently reproduced equipment test or fire simulation. The [selection and deviation record](page-selection.md) and [coverage receipt](coverage.md) fix the limits of this review.

As attribution to NIST's published account, these claims are directly established by the selected pages (grade A). As historical equipment/fuel/thermal outcomes, the scenarios remain materially assumption-dependent (grade C); whether a shutdown-defeating installed configuration actually existed and survived is underdetermined in this source scope (grade D). These grades concern the stated propositions, not probabilities for collapse causes.

The reviewed pages contain no measured generator shutdown time, coolant-temperature trace, installed cooling-system transient or event-day pump log. Near-ceiling gas temperature above an engine is not a measurement of its coolant temperature. Even verified loss of pumping must be connected to remaining pool fuel and subsequent burning; Scenario 1a does provide a steady flow/burning-balance premise, so claiming that NIST gives no fuel-balance reasoning would overstate the gap. Conversely, the absence of a verified shutdown bridge does not demonstrate engine or cable survival.

The source separates ordinary floor combustibles from the hypothetical pumped diesel cases. This bounded review identifies the resulting input dependency, but does not independently inspect the full office combustible-load tables, Chapter 11 floor/connection model or initiation chronology. Therefore it cannot assess the numerical sufficiency of the office-fire mechanism or quantify how a changed diesel exposure would affect it.

## Critical review of the current parent equipment report

The parent equipment report's conditional wording is supported within this scope: it distinguishes gas from coolant/equipment temperature, requires all relevant power paths to be addressed, retains the fuel-balance and heavy-column-heating premises, and says the equipment-source follow-up changes the dependency map rather than the collapse ranking. No material categorical claim that generator survival disproves the office-fire account was found in the reviewed snapshot.

One narrowing addition would improve its opening description of the heat-to-generator-failure-to-pump-shutdown chain: identify it as a material rejection ground in Scenarios 1a/1b and preserve the different grounds in 1c/1d and Scenario 2. Suggested wording: "Equipment-specific verification could reopen the northeast sustained diesel cases and the resulting thermal-input exclusion; other cases additionally depend on local heat, observation coverage and compartment paths, and a surviving-generator branch already appears in Scenario 2." This is a precision recommendation, not a demonstrated correction to the parent report's conclusion. The NCSTAR 1-1J equipment claims, errata and inventory calculations were not independently audited in this task.

Supplemental physical 679-680 / printed 613-614 compress the diesel reasoning into Chapter 13's summary. They repeat the gas-to-coolant-boiling-to-engine-failure reasoning and acknowledge that the empty Salomon tanks do not distinguish pre-collapse consumption from later burning-rubble loss. The summary does not supply a new equipment test or independent source. Use the detailed scenario qualifications when explaining its broader language about smoke or fuel supply. These two pages were read under the disclosed selection deviation and do not expand the core table's evidentiary basis.

This was a separate computational reviewer of the designated source pages, with the task framed by the parent and the parent's reports read as leads. It is neither a blind holdout analysis nor an independent historical source or licensed engineering review. No solver was reproduced, and no record, bridge or legal authority boundary was crossed.
