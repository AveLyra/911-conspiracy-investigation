# Diesel scenarios: recovered source-review package

This package preserves a bounded 2026-09-11 review of NIST’s WTC 7 diesel scenarios from a retained investigation snapshot. It is not a new engineering analysis, a model run, or evidence by itself of who caused the collapse. Its central distinction is that NIST used different stated grounds for different hypothetical scenarios; equipment survival by itself would not establish a diesel fire or disprove the office-fire mechanism. See the [relevance report](report.md) and the [testimony and briefing crosscheck](crosscheck-report.md).

The reports and selection notes have a historical-context preface. Each coverage receipt is an explicitly labeled derivative with one local executable path redacted; all other original wording is retained. See [manifest.json](manifest.json) for exact original and modified hashes and change descriptions. The original receipts and research files remain in retained local storage. Original selected page images and LFS PDF copies are not included.

[NCSTAR 1-9](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-9.pdf), the [2006 testimony](https://www.nist.gov/el/disasterstudies/wtc/upload/TestimonySept8_06.pdf), and the [2008 technical briefing](https://www.nist.gov/el/disasterstudies/wtc/upload/WTC7RevisedTechnicalBriefing_111908.pdf) are official public NIST links. The latter two match the historical descriptions by title, date and page count; their current downloaded bytes were not compared with the original preserved copies. The historical receipt records checks by the prior reviewer. No simulation or full replication was performed while preparing this package; only the previously reported NCSTAR pump-power passage was separately checked in a retained copy. The saved Git LFS pointers for the testimony and deck carry the same SHA-256 values recorded by the historical receipt, but that comparison does not verify present-day web-file byte identity.

The [initial](coverage.md) and [crosscheck](crosscheck-coverage.md) receipts preserve the review’s claimed scope, failed methods, and non-blind-review limitations. Further tests require installed equipment and wiring records, evidence about actual fuel and operations, and scenario-specific thermal and electrical modeling.
