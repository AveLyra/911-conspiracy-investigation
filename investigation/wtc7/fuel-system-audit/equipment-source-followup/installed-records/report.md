# Installed equipment, fuel disposition and model relevance

The bounded search did not locate an original installed-generator specification, cooling package or dated control drawing. It did acquire two public NIST documents that sharpen the investigation: a 2006 testimony packet supplies a named maintenance-interview citation and earlier configuration descriptions; a 2008 briefing explicitly separates possible pre-collapse fuel consumption from later rubble-fire consumption. Neither supplies measured generator shutdown or resolves the thermal/electrical premise independently.[^1][^2]

The scientific consequence is specific. A defect in the assumed shutdown of diesel-fed generators could reopen particular diesel-fire scenarios and the decision to omit their heating from structural inputs. It would not, by itself, falsify the separate office-fire mechanism, establish a sustained diesel fire, or favor deliberate intervention. No collapse ranking changes on this source follow-up alone.[^3]

## What the new documents establish

Sunder's September 8, 2006 testimony was given at a New York City Council hearing concerning diesel storage at 60 Hudson Street, but its substantive discussion concerns WTC 7. Physical pages 2–4 describe NIST's then-working collapse hypothesis and explicitly say the diesel role was not finally determined. The appended NCSTAR 1-1 Chapter 12 is prior report material, not a new witness or an independent measurement. This testimony packet must not be confused with a final 2008 conclusion.[^1]

The testimony says that missing fuel could have been consumed in the building and describes evidence suggesting no underground soil leakage. Its own attached Chapter 12 is more qualified: physical page 12, printed page 181, describes some fuel contamination in bedding gravel and sand, none observed in the lower organic marine silt/clay, and an attribution ambiguity because the sand/soil extended under adjacent base-system tanks. It cites Langan 2002 for the environmental examination. The original report, sampling plan, dates, measurements and tank identification remain unacquired in this branch.[^1]

These statements do not support treating all soil as uncontaminated or calculating pre-collapse combustion from the tanks' later empty condition. Conversely, limited contamination described in the excerpt does not establish that all missing fuel escaped underground. The location, timing, possible sources and detection limits must remain attached to the observation.

The November 19, 2008 briefing's slide 49 expressly permits two explanations for missing Salomon fuel: contribution to a Floor 5 fire, or consumption/dissipation in the rubble fire after collapse. The briefing therefore cannot be cited as proof of a measured pre-collapse diesel release. Its slide 16 also retains an explicit footnote acknowledging removal of the American Express generator/day tank; omission of that note would misrepresent the displayed equipment table.[^2]

## Named interview and equipment dependencies

The attached report's printed page 180, physical page 11 of the testimony packet, footnote 52 names an interview with Mike Catalano, described as responsible for the Salomon systems. The citation is attached to the account that a fuel-delivery service maintained full tanks. The resulting event-day fullness statement is expressed as likely. The footnote supplies neither a transcript nor an interview date, original documentary exhibits or event-day gauge readings.[^1]

This is a more specific provenance lead than an unnamed source. It does not authenticate a join to the later report's “SSB Interview 1,” prove that two interview records are the same encounter, or turn their repetition into independent corroboration. The separately preserved Commission memorandum remains its own document with its own question/time boundaries.

The same appendix describes automatic startup of all nine generators, subsequent shutdown of unneeded units and possible restart as demand increased. It states that pumps operated while a generator ran. This is useful additional published control description, but it is not the point-to-point wiring, installed switchgear logic or an event-day operating log. Generator power, controller command, recirculation and discharge through a rupture remain separate quantities.[^1]

Three configuration differences require explicit source-version control:

| Published item | Precisely observed difference | Permitted conclusion / required discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| Fan sets, testimony appendix physical 11 / printed 180 | One paragraph describes seven four-fan sets, divided three east/four west; a later paragraph describes three northeast units plus six northwest units, each with four fans. The latter also lists 30,000 and 38,000 cfm per fan. | The page contains incompatible-looking totals without explaining different inventories or revisions. A dated fan schedule, equipment IDs and as-built arrangement are needed before selecting a total or inferring delivered ventilation. |
| Generator rating, appendix physical 8 and 11 / printed 177 and 180 | These call 1,750 kW the combined capacity of nine generators. Briefing slide 15 labels the generator group as 1,750-kW generators, quantity nine. | Do not silently substitute “each” for “combined,” or infer a factor-of-nine model error. Original generator schedules/nameplates and the actual model representation are missing. |
| OEM transfer pump, appendix physical 10 / printed 179 | This excerpt prints 700 gpm at 125 psi; the previously reviewed 1J component account gives 700 gph at 100 psi. | A further source-description conflict, not a measured flow or proven input error. Do not transfer OEM values to the separate Salomon loop. Original project specification/revision resolves the equipment basis. |

The briefing diagram's nine fan banks agrees with one of the earlier fan descriptions, but a later summary is not an as-built verification. No manufacturer model should be guessed from rated power. No heat-removal capacity or surviving runtime is calculated from these unreconciled descriptions.[^1][^2]

## Which physical proposition remains testable

The briefing's slide 50 groups several different diesel scenarios and rejection grounds: insufficient heating or duration, expected but unobserved smoke, or gas conditions said to cause all nine generators to fail before sufficient column heating. Its summary uses a less-than-three-hour coolant/engine argument. That is not necessarily a contradiction of a shorter timescale in a particular detailed scenario; the case and endpoint must first be matched.[^2]

The later detailed report distinguishes the cases. The generator-shutdown premise is material to the northeast over-ventilated cases. Other cases also invoke limited column heating or a visibility constraint. The southwest case explicitly contemplates a surviving generator and obscured fire but finds no open path for hot gases to Column 79 under its assumed geometry. Disproving one rejection ground does not automatically remove the others.[^3]

The physical inference requiring verification is not simply whether gas can exceed a quoted temperature. It is whether the specified exposure, cooling configuration, load, protective controls and electrical damage would disable every viable pump-power path on the needed timescale. A measured or simulated gas temperature is not the same variable as coolant temperature, and neither alone proves a particular engine trip. The acquired material does not provide the matched coolant composition/pressure, heat-exchanger arrangement, thermal transient, trip logic or historical event record needed for that inference.

Equally, the absence of these details here does not establish surviving machinery. A matched equipment calculation might support NIST's shutdown reasoning. A contrary surviving power/cooling path might weaken it. Either outcome would then need to be propagated through fuel flow, residual inventory, ventilation, column exposure and the model's omitted-versus-included diesel boundary. The section's conclusion affected thermal treatment of Floors 5/6 and explicit diesel inclusion on Floors 7/9, so the question is scientifically relevant without being decisive about the entire collapse.[^3]

## Claim dispositions and next discriminators

Grades concern the exact statement, not causal probability: A directly established; B strongly supported; C materially assumption-dependent; D underdetermined; E unsupported/contradicted as stated.

| ID | Claim / evidence layer | Strength, alternatives and falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| IR01 | A generator make/model and installed cooling package were found. Retrieval result. | E for this bounded search; no such original acquired. An actual dated equipment schedule or specification could change this. Search failure is not global absence. |
| IR02 | The 2006 packet names a Catalano maintenance interview for tank-fullness information. Documentary observation. | A for citation; transcript/date/event-day measurements and later-interview identity remain D. The source citation does not establish independent witnesses. |
| IR03 | The appendix records some contamination in upper strata and none observed in lower clay. Documentary observation. | A for published account; amount, source and timing remain unverified without Langan's original sampling/measurement record. E for “all soil was shown uncontaminated.” |
| IR04 | Empty tanks establish pre-collapse combustion. Historical inference. | E as proof; post-collapse loss is expressly retained in the 2008 briefing. Actual dated fuel accounting and release/combustion evidence would discriminate. |
| IR05 | Fan counts, combined/per-unit power wording and OEM pump units agree across these sources. Documentary comparison. | E as stated; exact conflicts are pinned above. Editorial error, inventory boundary or revision are alternatives. Original equipment and model-case files are needed before engineering consequences. |
| IR06 | Ambient gas exceeding a quoted threshold establishes historical failure of all generators. Physical/historical inference. | D here; equipment-specific response and power topology are missing. Either surviving or failed machinery needs affirmative support. |
| IR07 | Falsifying shutdown alone falsifies NIST's complete office-fire account. Logical inference. | E. It reopens applicable diesel cases and thermal-boundary choices; other rejection grounds and the office-fire causal chain require separate tests. |
| IR08 | The 2006 and 2008 statements prove concealment or intentional model manipulation. Intent inference. | E on these records alone. Published preliminary-to-final development and editorial differences are alternatives; actor/act/chronology/provenance evidence would be required. |

The exact installed-model search is now exhausted within its six-query boundary. Do not repeat it under a new generic label. Highest-value unresolved records are the dated Salomon generator/cooling and fan schedules, SOM E-5/E-R1 and approved revisions, the original cited Catalano interview and Langan environmental report, and the corresponding native diesel-case input/output files. A newly accessible exact record can justify a follow-up; more nominally compatible modern equipment cannot substitute for it.
