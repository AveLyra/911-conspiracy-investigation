# Fuel-system controls and the limits of the published reconstruction

NCSTAR1-1J supplies identifiable pump models, control functions, component locations and original-record references. It also explicitly identifies missing wiring and uncertain protection functions. It strengthens the basis for a specific equipment audit; it does not independently establish either a sustained diesel fire or its early termination. The later collapse report's heat-to-generator-failure-to-pump-shutdown chain remains a conditional analysis requiring equipment-specific verification.[^1][^2]

This research-only follow-up changes the dependency map, not the collapse-mechanism ranking. It does not run a fire or structural solver, identify a historical fuel release, or infer deliberate intervention. Acquisition, reading selection, reproduction and separate reviews are preserved alongside this report.

## Source identity and authority

The acquired official file is84 physical pages. Its title/imprint pages identify *Documentation of the Fuel System for Emergency Power in World Trade Center7*, Grill and Johnson, September2005. The catalog's December2005 date, the PDF's2008 creation/modification fields and its template metadata title are separate facts, not proof of a changed engineering edition. Physical9 retains a draft-for-public-comment header. These inconsistencies warrant version pinning, not an unsupported claim that the official download is fraudulent or a different substantive report.[^1]

The introduction says the account rests on located design drawings/specifications, plus certified as-built drawings for the Ambassador Construction project. It does not claim a complete as-built set for every system. Physical75/printed41 says not all component details were identified and that some functions or installations were inferred from brief design notes, typical operation, manufacturer applications or code requirements. Table9-1's legend is important: its affirmative entries indicate drawings/specifications; its negative entries indicate absence from those documents, not necessarily physical absence; other entries are explicitly assumed or unknown.[^1]

These are NIST's published documentary claims. The underlying original specifications, point-to-point wiring and operating records have not been acquired in this follow-up. NCSTAR1-9's use of the same equipment account is not independent corroboration of those underlying inputs.

The Salomon narrative leaves open whether valves connected one or both main tanks, the normal state of generator solenoids, the purpose of a second valve assembly, and whether the collecting-tank float switch stopped pumps. It describes a rupture upstream of the fifth-floor control assembly that could trigger pumping and discharge until the connected storage emptied. The immediately following page qualifies this: a leak detector might stop the pump, but the available documentation does not determine that function, and electrical malfunction effects need analysis. Reading only either half would distort the account.[^1]

For the base subsystems, physical76/printed42 likewise says a point-to-point control-panel wiring schematic was not located, so a possible electrical-malfunction scenario could not be evaluated. Other described protective routes include containment drainage to leak detection, fusible valves, check valves and isolation; their functions are conditional on actual presence, connections and operation. Neither complete safeguard success nor complete safeguard defeat is established.

## What this changes in the diesel-fire question

The2005 equipment report is not a categorical demonstration that accidental fuel discharge was impossible. It supplies plausible normal protective functions alongside acknowledged uncertainty and release scenarios. Its explicit gaps make the verification request more concrete: the missing object is not merely unspecified data, but the relevant point-to-point control wiring and its relation to pump power, level switches, leak detectors, solenoids and tank selection.[^1]

That finding does not by itself refute the2008 collapse analysis. NCSTAR1-9 §9.2 subsequently evaluates selected hypothetical fires and reasons about loss of fuel or electrical supply. In Scenario1a, printed369 describes gas temperatures above all nine water-cooled generators exceeding approximately100°C, and possible damage to distribution cables, as grounds for interrupting pump power within roughly one or two hours. It also states the required steady fuel-flow/burning balance and a separate column-heating timescale; those calculations cannot be erased from the account merely because other dependencies are unresolved.[^2]

NCSTAR1-1J does **not add a generator-specific cooling-system transient, coolant/pressure specification or actual shutdown record** in the reviewed equipment and system-performance chapters, particularly physical pages 46, 65–66 and 80–81; the [selection](SELECTION.md) defines the complete reading boundary. The base-generator alarm list is not a specification for the nine Salomon generators. The new source therefore does not close the step from modeled surrounding gas exposure to every relevant generator/power-path failure. Conversely, the missing detail does not show that any generator would survive that exposure.

A useful test must keep separate:

1. Electrical power available to a pump, the controller commanding it, and mechanical pumping actually occurring.
2. Pump discharge, recirculation, flow to generators, containment/drainage and fuel released into a burning pool.
3. Nearby gas temperature, equipment/coolant temperature, protective actuation, loss of cables or switchgear and the resulting power topology.
4. Supply interruption, remaining pool inventory and burn-out; piping capacity is not automatically residual burning-pool volume.

This separation is an analytical requirement, not a newly simulated outcome. The exact fire-case inputs and equipment response would need to show that the self-limiting sequence holds under the selected conditions and plausible contrary conditions. The source does not justify assigning a probability to those branches.

## Configuration, quantities and maintenance

Table9-2 estimates43283.8gallons across storage, tanks and piping under explicit fullness/drainage assumptions. It includes the American Express day tank and piping. Chapter8 calls its configuration an as-of-September11 depiction while describing locations approximated from design drawings. This is precisely why a2005 reconstructed inventory cannot simply be treated as a contemporaneous measurement or silently substituted for the2008 removal account.[^1]

The [inventory review](inventory-review/report.md) separately reconciles design quantities and units against the parent audit. None becomes measured combustion. In particular, system capacities, full-tank assumptions and recovered amounts need original dated records on the same equipment basis. The newly acquired report does not itself supply the GZA recovery tickets or establish a recovery date.

Two different component sets total three day tanks and 600 gallons: base (275), AmEx (275), Ambassador (50); or base (275), OEM (275), Ambassador (50). The aggregate alone therefore cannot establish that a later report retained removed AmEx equipment. The stronger documentary conflict is 1J's explicit event-day configuration including that equipment versus the later removal account. The parent report flags the three/600 wording for reconciliation without expressly proving its membership; the inventory review's description of the parent's earlier interpretation should be read with that qualification. Component IDs and the accounting boundary are needed before subtracting tanks or piping.

Declared unit arithmetic also explains a possible textual pattern: 70 U.S. liquid gallons/minute is 4.416313748 L/s, whereas 70 imperial gallons/minute is approximately 5.303771667 L/s; 12,000 gallons is respectively 45.424941408 or 54.55308 m³. These fit the rounded published pairs under different conventions. They do not establish the intended convention or actual model input, much less a demonstrated simulation error. Root independently reproduced the table totals and eight conversion checks using integer-tenths addition and exact rational arithmetic in the [arithmetic receipt](root-reproduction/inventory-arithmetic-receipt.json).

Chapter10 says maintenance-history documentation could not be located, while identifying a partial schedule/upgrade list and an April1997 inspection report noting missing fuel-line supports in the fifth-floor generator room. This is a specific dated condition lead. It does not show whether supports were repaired, which later rupture occurred, or what condition persisted in2001. Maintenance schedules are not completed-work or test logs.[^1]

## Published errata and inference limits

The two-page published errata, last updated June27,2012, lists **no correction for the six selected fuel discrepancy families**: recovery date wording, recovery-volume units, Floor8 removal versus retained listings, pump-rate conversion,250/275-gallon labeling, and the southwest/southeast scenario caption. That is coverage of this exact errata artifact only, not proof that no other correction or underlying explanation exists.[^3]

It does contain changes relevant to other structural-audit work: a2009 correction to an insulation-outcome sentence and figure references; a blast-analysis reference deletion with an assertion that another reference contains the pertinent material;2012 replacement of an erection-drawing citation with a structural-drawing citation; and corrections distinguishing5.5-inch axial travel from6.25-inch lateral travel, with a12-inch Column79 bearing seat. NIST expressly says the latter corrected values were already used in its analyses. The errata establishes that NIST made that assertion, **not that an independent investigator verified the input files**. Equally, corrected prose is not proof the solver originally used wrong values.[^3]

Use the corrected text when describing NIST's published position, preserve the earlier text as history, and test the actual drawing/input/output lineage where available. The existing [structural audit, SC01](../../structural-chain-source-audit.md) retained the superseded seat/lateral figures; an additive September 11 correction now distinguishes that original reading from the corrected published position. This is a scoped propagation, not a repository-wide errata audit. A text extractor flattens struck-out and inserted numbers, so the redline's complete visual pages control the correction direction.
