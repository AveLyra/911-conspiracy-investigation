# Technical review update relevant to the contracting/access audit

**Status:** Review note; source claims below require primary-source pinning before promotion. Not a causal finding or pleading text.

## What this review changes

The review identifies published work by Chandler, Walter, and Szamboti reporting multipoint roofline accelerations near 9.3–9.92 m/s² from a circulated Camera 2 copy. If the measurements and source lineage are confirmed, they strengthen the kinematic constraint beyond reliance on NIST's single fitted trajectory. The reported sampling and source limitations still prevent treating them as a complete independent video reconstruction. The work does not show simultaneous failure of every support or identify demolition.

The review also confirms the appropriate technical questions for any future model comparison:

- distinguish the roofline point and visible exterior segment from the whole building mass;
- compare identically defined onset and below-roofline events;
- preserve frame-rate, calibration, perspective, and fitting uncertainty;
- test the ANSYS-to-LS-DYNA damage handoff, not merely the final animation;
- sweep the 3.5-hour/4-hour damage transition and intermediate states;
- test floor-history substitutions for Floors 9, 11, and 13;
- identify whether UAF's P-delta treatment is present in the results being used for comparison;
- distinguish a prescribed support-loss comparator from evidence of a historical initiating act.

## Comparator corrections

The Hong Kong Amoycan example is reported as a prolonged fire in a concrete structure and therefore is not a close structural comparator. One Meridian Plaza is a closer high-rise fire comparison and should be source-pinned before use. Plasco is evidence that fire-induced global collapse can occur in a steel-framed building, but its height, fire protection, contents, and failure sequence differ materially from WTC 7. None supplies a numerical base rate for WTC 7 or a direct probability of demolition.

## Historical-context boundary

Warnings, intelligence failures, Saudi-linked assistance questions, inaccurate early official accounts, and later unsupported Iraq-related claims may reduce confidence in institutional assurances. They do not connect a sponsor or contractor to WTC 7 preparation. The contracting/access lane must seek that building-specific bridge rather than treating surrounding historical failures as substitute evidence.

## Highest-value source checks

1. Obtain and hash the 2023 multipoint-video paper, its measurement protocol, cited Camera 2/West-face files, and any frame-rate or calibration records.
2. Reconcile its event definitions with NIST NCSTAR 1A Table 3-1, especially the 9.3-second west-penthouse entry versus 6.9/7.3-second debris-model views.
3. Verify the exact UAF report pages, displayed P-delta treatment, and case-entry/model archive status.
4. Pin the primary Amoycan, One Meridian Plaza, and Plasco records before making comparator claims.

## Inference boundary

The strongest presently supportable statement is that NIST's precise causal chain deserves independent reconstruction and that near-gravitational exterior descent is a serious constraint. No reviewed item in this update identifies a commissioned technical team, sponsor, or intentional support-removal act.
