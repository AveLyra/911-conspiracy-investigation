### Affirmative support and limits retained together

These are reported physical tests, not merely an animation: pp7–19 describe
two full-scale35ft tower-floor specimens, furnace and steel thermocouples,
restrained/unrestrained conditions and differing temperature distributions.
They are not WTC7 and not new experiments separate from the cited1-6B tests.
Steel temperatures generally rise differently at mid-web, lower and upper
chords; Test1 shows greater variability. Spatial standard deviations in
Table1/Fig12 describe the measured distribution, not a confidence interval
for historical steel temperatures or a calibrated prediction-error model.

Baseline lower-chord/mid-web curves and many Test2 upper-chord comparisons
show useful agreement (pp24–31). The report also retains early/local
disagreement, large late Test1 upper-chord underprediction, and Test2-I
overprediction. Its possible omitted-bridging heat-sink explanation is a
hypothesis, not an independently tested correction. Test2-C's later measured
temperatures are absent from Table3 even though the plot shows a trace;
do not read a summary percentage as covering every plotted time/channel.

The model is a thermal subassembly: one main truss, half-width slab, without
end supports, bridging members or an explicitly modeled metal deck. pp21–23
give reasons for simplification, unit radiative view factors, convection
15W/m²/K, steel/slab emissivities0.7/0.6, initial25°C, and measured unexposed
slab temperatures as boundary inputs. The boundary histories are the stated
thermal-model difference between Test1 and2. These are disclosed assumptions;
the report does not establish they were all fitted to the observed outputs.

The pp32–33 exercise removes protection over2–3ft lengths, selects depths by
trial and error, and restarts at3800s (A0.5in; I/E0.75in) and4500s (C0.75in).
The log supports real damage and reports, but does not directly measure that
exact four-location removal history. Its Test1 entries include large reports
at68/78min, not a uniquely identified event at each model restart. Section5.3
also points to logs in AppendicesA/B, whereas the actual logs are C/D.
Timing references and fitted choices remain distinct; no invented alignment
or clock shift is supplied. Improved fits do not uniquely identify the loss
history, and Fig21 still has late A/E and pre-jump I discrepancies.

The source does not hide all these limitations. pp34–35 explicitly identify
missing moisture effects and future sensitivity/uncertainty work. p35 calls
for improving temperature predictions to approximately10°C agreement,
particularly above400°C, for structural use. That is an improvement target,
not an achieved tolerance or proof that a specific WTC7 prediction is wrong.
Kelvin-relative percentages, absolute temperature differences, spatial spread
and structural error cannot be treated as interchangeable metrics.

AppendixD p46 reports retained main-truss protection at108/120min in Test2;
p15 nevertheless suggests possible local loss atC around70min. Preserve both
as different source observations/inferences with limited viewing and sensor
coverage. Neither statement resolves the physical discrepancy by itself.
