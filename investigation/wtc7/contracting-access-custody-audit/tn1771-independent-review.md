## Page-specific findings

### Identity and experimental/model scope — pages 1–20

- Pages 1–3 identify *A Study of Thermal Behavior of a Composite Floor System in Standard Fire Resistance Tests*, NIST TN 1771, October 2012. The displayed cover/title names **Dilip Banerjee**, and page 2 supplies DOI `10.6028/NIST.TN.1771`. Page 37 uses the plural “authors”; that does not identify an additional author. The previously reported public-catalog authorship and the later paper's reference attribution are not silently substituted for this title page. The catalog was not opened in this pass.
- The abstract, page 5, says four tests were conducted but selects **two full-scale tests** for the present numerical comparison. Test 1 is the restrained 35 ft specimen and Test 2 the unrestrained 35 ft specimen; this is not a four-specimen FE comparison. Page 10's furnace-temperature figure includes four test curves, which does not expand the two-test model-comparison scope.
- Pages 7–9 give the substitute A572 Grade 50 steel rationale, nominal 0.75 in/19 mm fireproofing with ±1/16 in tolerance, deck/slab/truss geometry, furnace, and instrument arrangement. The source claims acceptable thermal/mechanical similarity for the substitute steel; this is a reported rationale, not a newly demonstrated equivalence to every historical tower or WTC 7 member.
- Page 9 identifies 24 furnace thermocouples and 88 steel thermocouples. Figure 2 maps north-truss locations A/G/C/I/E and south-truss B/H/D/J/F, with two upper-chord, two web, and four lower-chord positions per cross section. Figures 2–3 cite `[1]`, although page 36's reference 1 is ANSYS software and reference 2 is NCSTAR 1-6B; the body does identify reference 2 as the test source. These caption pointers are not independent primary provenance for the test diagram or furnace records.
- Page 11 reports a loud event at roughly 15 min in Test 1, deck bulging, and deck fireproofing falling, interpreted as concrete spalling. It also reports damaged Test 2 radiometers and Test 1 heat-flux measurements becoming unreliable after 104 min. These are channel-specific limitations, not grounds to discard every temperature measurement.
- Pages 13–16 distinguish possible explanations for measured differences: spalling, moisture, local convection, gas nonuniformity, view factors, and possible SFRM loss. Page 15's proposed Test 2 upper-chord loss near C around 70 min is an interpretation to compare with the later Appendix D observations, not an observed removal history established by a native measurement file.
- Pages 18–19 report cross-sectional means over ten locations, anomalous-reading exclusion, and standard deviations. Table 1's values match the corresponding later-paper values as visually inspected; Figure 12 uses **two-standard-deviation bars**, not a calibrated confidence interval or model prediction interval. This pass did not independently recompute the underlying means, exclusions, or standard deviations from raw channels.

### What was imposed and what was predicted — pages 20–24

- The analysis is transient heat transfer. Page 20 says its temperatures **will later be used** in a structural model. The formulation predicts steel temperatures; it does not predict the furnace fire and all thermal boundaries independently.
- Pages 21–22 model one main truss with a half-width slab, omit end supports and bridging trusses, assume uniform SFRM except above the upper chord, and assume 19 mm slab overspray. The thin steel deck is omitted on a cited thermal rationale. Solid70, Link33, and surface-effect elements are described; the report's mesh adequacy statement is not a documented mesh-convergence error study in these pages.
- Pages 22–23 use furnace view factor one, convection coefficient 15 W/(m²·K), steel emissivity 0.7 and slab emissivity 0.6, an initial temperature of 25 °C, and adiabatic longitudinal ends. The fire-exposed surfaces exchange heat with the E119 reference temperature through radiation/convection; the E119 temperature is not directly imposed as a steel temperature.
- Crucially, **measured mean unexposed-slab temperature histories are imposed as Dirichlet boundaries**. The source calls those histories the model difference between Tests 1 and 2. This leaves a meaningful comparison of predicted steel-channel temperatures conditional on measured slab boundaries; it is not a wholly independent end-to-end prediction of the entire thermal experiment.
- Page 23 describes an APDL procedure and weighted estimates at thermocouple locations from the FE temperatures, but does not supply the APDL script or a runnable case. Page 24 describes anomalous-channel exclusions and occasional use of south-truss counterparts. It points to reference 2 for erroneous thermocouples. Appendices C/D do not provide a complete machine-readable channel mask, selected sample timestamps, replacement map, or observation-operator implementation.

### Deliberately fitted loss scenario and structural-use wording — pages 32–37

- Page 32 expressly describes **trial-and-error** insulation removal for Test 1. It removes 2–3 ft lengths: at 3,800 s, 0.5 in at A and 0.75 in at I/E; at 4,500 s, 0.75 in at C. Figure 21 on page 33 compares the modified model with selected measured upper-chord channels, with intact cases also displayed. This is test-informed fitting of a proposed damage scenario, not an independently measured removal field or an untouched held-out prediction.
- The improved agreement supports the plausibility and thermal sensitivity of that explanation. The visible late differences and timing differences prevent converting the fitted scenario into a uniquely identified physical damage history. The logs support actual insulation loss, but not each specified upper-chord depth, length, location, and second of the fitted scenario.
- Page 32 calls Appendices A and B the test logs, but the logs are actually **C and D, pages 45–46**. This is a source cross-reference error, not permission to replace the record or infer intent.
- Page 35 acknowledges moisture effects/migration are not included and proposes further improvement to about 10 °C agreement, especially above 400 °C. That is a **future accuracy objective**, not an achieved acceptance criterion or a universal thermomechanical error bound. The source also suggests optimization with an appropriate objective function; this is not a reported completed optimization exercise.
- Structural wording is mixed: page 20 says the thermal histories **will later be used**, while page 35 says the data **are used** in a structural model. The latter is favorable evidence of claimed structural use and must not be erased by describing every mention as future-only. Nevertheless, this complete report supplies **no structural sag/result comparison, force history, structural input deck, or structural execution output**. The conclusion is absence of those deliverables from this report, not proof that no structural work existed elsewhere.
- Page 36 cites ANSYS Mechanical Release 12.1 (2011), while the previously reviewed later paper cites Release 13.1 (2013). These reference labels do not establish the exact executable or case version that produced every figure. TN 1771's ASTM reference is E119-73; the later paper's is E119-00. A bibliographic difference alone does not demonstrate a different furnace exposure actually executed.

### Test logs, clocks, and competing insulation observations — pages 45–46

- **Test 1, Appendix C page 45:** the published table begins at 21 min. It records concrete-spalling reports, deck/bridging deformation, and several insulation-loss observations, including a bridging vertical at 53 min and main-truss lower-chord locations at 87, 88, 92, and 93 min. These are positive evidence of actual observed deterioration. Their locations do not directly establish the fitted upper-chord loss field. A 63 min entry concerns deck clearance/bent diagonals, whereas larger reports are listed at 68 and 78 min; those rows are not exact observational timestamps for model changes at 3,800 and 4,500 s. Clock offsets, sensor response, approximation, and unlisted observations remain alternatives.
- The same Test 1 log expressly records **gas off/furnace extinguished at 116 min**, while the body/Table 1 and comparison tables discuss 120 min results and the model uses an E119 reference history. The timing question is now visible **within TN 1771 itself**, not merely between publications. Continued post-shutoff thermal measurements could be meaningful; they do not resolve whether the numerical boundary followed the actual shutoff, whether clocks differ, or which samples underlie the 120 min row. Those require native timestamps, furnace-control history, and case boundary history. No clock reconciliation is established here.
- **Test 2, Appendix D page 46:** at 74 min the log records approximately 2.5 ft of fireproofing falling from the top chord of the **east bridging truss**. At 108 min, and again at 120 min, it says all fireproofing remained on the **main trusses**. This is important limiting evidence for the page 15 interpretation of possible main-truss C loss. Visual accessibility, local/hidden damage, and the non-uniqueness of a thermal inference prevent calling this an automatic logical contradiction, but the log does not affirmatively confirm that proposed main-truss loss.
- The Test 2 log says observations were terminated for safety at **130 min**, and gas was turned off/fire test terminated at **146 min**. Observation termination and heating termination are different events. Neither the 120 min comparison horizon nor the observational stop is the test's 146 min gas-off endpoint.

## Strongest competing readings and falsifiers

The strongest favorable reading is a physically grounded, literature-property thermal model that predicts many measured steel temperatures reasonably well, distinguishes two tests, and responds plausibly to observed insulation damage. Prescribing measured slab temperatures does not make every steel prediction circular. Reported comparison failures are localized and have credible physical alternatives, including bridging-truss heat sinks, moisture, spalling, and spatially varying exposure. The earlier explicit Test 2 table/discussion makes an ordinary later reference/compression error more plausible than an unsupported claim of fabricated test identity.

The strongest limiting reading is that agreement is conditional on measured boundaries and channel selection, with a crucial damage scenario fitted by trial and error. The full source still lacks native data/masks, unrounded and continued property laws, exact executable/case identity, and structural outputs. The timing discrepancy and Test 2 insulation-observation tension cannot be dismissed by favorable aggregate prose. These limits constrain what is verified; they do not falsify all thermal calculations, establish misconduct, or select a WTC 7 cause.

The most discriminating existing-record package would connect the actual raw/time-aligned furnace, slab, and steel channels (including exclusions/substitutions) to unrounded material tables, continuation/interpolation rules, APDL/mesh/load-boundary inputs, executable/version identifiers, and result histories for the compared cases. It would resolve or preserve the 116/120 min issue and identify whether the 2012 Test 1/Test 2 table mapping was changed or merely miscited in the later paper. A claimed structural bridge separately needs the structural model, thermal mapping, load/restraint histories, and measured-versus-predicted sag outputs. This is a records/testing lead, not authorization to retrieve additional material or run models.
