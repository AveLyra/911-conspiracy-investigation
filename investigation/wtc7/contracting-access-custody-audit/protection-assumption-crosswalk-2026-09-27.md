## Result: an explicit model prescription, an unresolved historical join

The published material supplies more than unspecified insulation assumptions:
an installation-era sample, explicit uniform thermal-model thicknesses,
representative component calculations, and an author explanation of condition.
It does **not** establish a member-by-member link from the1997 missing-coating
observations through later repairs to protection on September11,2001.

The strongest supported criticism is that missing historical-to-model link,
not that NIST supplied no evidence or never analyzed uninsulated steel. The
strongest contrary explanation is that generally adequate protection coexisted
with localized construction damage that may subsequently have been repaired.
The reviewed record does not distinguish those repair outcomes. No quantitative
collapse-probability or cause-ranking change follows from this unit.

Thinner uniform protection, and no insulation versus insulation, produce faster
heating in the displayed component comparisons, conditional on their imposed
exposures. These cases do not test spatial nonuniformity. The selected model beam
thickness is below the published installation sample mean. Conversely, treating
a truly bare patch as coated would omit that local condition. These point in
different directions for different comparisons; neither supplies the net
temperature error, changed failure sequence or causal likelihood. Spatially
varying temperatures, connections, restraint and chronology prevent turning
a thickness difference into an assumed global outcome without the coupled test.
