### Prescribed loading and model versions are not measured test conditions

The 1-6C single-truss model is a cut-out of WTC1 Floor96, using a long-span
truss at Column143 and a40in slab strip (pp69–70). Figure5-25 labels a713in
span. This is not an identified35ft or17ft furnace specimen. Its imposed
gravity loads include self-weight,8psf superimposed dead load and13.75psf
live load, the latter25% of55psf design live load (p73). Do not add loads
from distinct cases or relabel this as a performed laboratory load.

Section5.4.4 p73 explicitly says its temperature history exercises the model
and does not come from fire-dynamics or conduction analyses. Over30min it
ramps steel and slab bottom from20°C to700°C, slab top from20°C to300°C,
then increases the maxima20°C/min. A linear through-slab gradient is assumed;
columns/spandrel receive no temperature load. These are prescribed structural
inputs—not furnace sensor measurements, inferred historical gas temperatures,
or proof that the actual fires attained them. Page81 itself warns that actual
tower failure modes/sequence may differ with actual versus assumed histories
and describes a later full-floor route using estimated histories. That route
has not been reproduced or evaluated by these single-truss pages.

Specific choices are disclosed: two-inch camber omitted; symmetry and lateral
restraints limit applicability (pp69–72); slab reinforcement/deck not included
in the detailed slab constitutive response as described onp69; post-cracking/
crushing negative slopes removed to improve convergence (p73); creep scaling
used here differs from a later formulation (p74). The debris-load case used
an earlier, different model without several break elements (p75). These are
version and applicability dependencies, not established errors. The report's
stated simplification rationale and future full-floor treatment must not be
discarded merely because an approximation exists.

The thermal solution switches from static to dynamic with5% Rayleigh damping
when convergence fails, resuming static analysis when velocity/acceleration
are small (p77). No convergence, damping, mesh or time-step study was rerun
here. Numerical nonconvergence is not by itself an observed physical collapse.
Likewise, calculated seat or knuckle failures are model outputs, not recovered
WTC member observations or furnace failures.

### Physical connection support, with the calibration boundary retained

The historical longitudinal tests used normal-weight concrete; the report
describes conflicts between the test specification and photographs over
reinforcement/confinement, an unexplained compressive-strength difference and
a weld-governed test. It derives approximately28.3kip per knuckle from the
first two tests, then scales by a concrete-strength ratio to approximately31kip
for in-place mature lightweight concrete. Transverse tests used lightweight
concrete; the reported16.9kip average is similarly scaled to approximately35kip
(pp61–62). These are adjusted estimates, not direct measurements of aged,
installed hot connections. Embedded historical test sheets are reproduced in
the public report; no separately held original drawings or test packets were
opened or authenticated here.

The FE study uses alternative fully bonded and frictionless interfaces,
imposed displacement and specified concrete properties/reinforcement (pp63–64).
Reported longitudinal strengths span15–35kip per knuckle depending on the
interface; comparison with test results favors a more bonded interface. The
transverse bonded case was terminated before ultimate strength (pp64–65), so
its endpoint is not an ultimate-capacity measurement. Figures5-21/23 are FE
curves, not independent experimental traces overlaid on predictions. The
FE curve captions use two knuckles, while the narrative capacities are per
knuckle; those denominators must not be conflated. Test
results inform the interface judgment and capacity choice; these pages do
not establish an out-of-sample, blinded or pre-test prediction. Calling this
test-informed capacity estimation and interface bracketing is narrower than
accusing it of improper fitting, and narrower than calling it independent
predictive validation.

The high-temperature extension is explicitly attributed to hand calculations
and engineering judgment (pp66–67), with assumed gas/steel/concrete temperature
relations, neglected differential thermal expansion in that estimate, and
temperature-dependent concrete strength. Room-temperature pullout capacity is
estimated, not identified as a separate pullout test here. Thus ambient shear
support does not independently validate the entire high-temperature law.
It is nevertheless affirmative empirical support that a sweeping “nothing
was physically tested” claim would wrongly erase. The report also attributes
resistance-weld data to Laclede tests (p74); their originals were not examined.

### Response-dependent agreement and preserved discrepancies

Page84 calls the simplified truss's overall behavior good agreement while
reporting web-diagonal buckling at530°C rather than565°C, and about2kip rather
than10kip tension at700°C. Figure5-38 onp85 visibly preserves both early
similarity and later differences in force and displacement. No pixels were
traced and no new numerical curve fit was performed. The source reports
that creep was included in the simplified truss comparison but omitted when
incorporated in the full-floor model because of convergence problems. The
simplified model also omits construction sequence and changes concrete and
connection representations (p84). Agreement in a precursor comparison does
not automatically verify the changed downstream version or every observable.
Figure5-38 labels horizontal reaction per column; it is not a total-building
force or an experimental reaction trace.

These are useful, explicitly published limitations—not hidden choices proven
to have caused a wrong historical result. The detailed truss also omitted a
strap-anchor weld failure that the authors say could allow earlier walk-off
(p81); some simplifications can retain support longer, so their net direction
must be tested rather than assumed. Section5.4.10 then lists that weld failure
among the simplified-model modes. Preserve model-specific membership.

Two printed discrepancies remain unresolved: Table4-2 gives ANSYS maximum slab
displacement0.695in, while Figure4-6 displays total-displacement DMX/SMX0.654969;
p77 says the analysis proceeded to727°C, whilep81 describes walk-off at730°C.
The latter may be rounding, and the former may concern an output/version
selection or reporting difference. Neither is silently reconciled or called
an established solver error. No historical probability is assigned to them.
