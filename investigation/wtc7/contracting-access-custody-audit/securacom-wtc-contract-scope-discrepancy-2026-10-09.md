# Securacom/Stratesec at the WTC: contract scope and continued-access discrepancy

**Record type:** Source-comparison note; not a finding of fact, expert opinion, or allegation of wrongdoing.
**Prepared:** 2026-10-09. Sources below were reviewed online on 2026-10-08.
**Rule:** Preserve the different statements as reported. This note does not reconcile them or choose which description controls.

## Question and boundary

The record supports that Securacom (later Stratesec) performed a substantial World Trade Center security-upgrade project in the 1990s. The sources reviewed here do **not** establish that the company controlled all WTC security, held WTC 7-specific operational responsibility on September 11, 2001, or retained unrestricted access after project closeout.

Conversely, a project-completion or “former client” description by itself does not establish that every separately scoped maintenance duty, punch-list item, warranty obligation, credential, or permission ended. Those are open questions, not evidence that such access continued.

## Source observations, kept separate

1. **1997 issuer filing, accessed through a filing mirror.** Securacom’s September 1997 S-1/A describes an October 1996 $8.3 million contract “as part of” the WTC security upgrade and says several projects, including the WTC project, were expected to be substantially completed in 1997. That is a contemporaneous forecast about a defined project; it is not the contract, a completion certificate, or proof that all security work or access ended in 1997. The copy reviewed is hosted by SEC Info, not the SEC archive. [Securacom S-1/A filing copy](https://www.secinfo.com/dsvRq.834g.htm)

2. **2003 press account of company and filing descriptions.** An archived American Reporter article says Stratesec continued to list New York City’s World Trade Center as a “former client” through April 2001. It also attributes to CEO Barry McDaniel a description of an ongoing “completion contract,” reportedly “not quite completed when the Center went down.” The article does not reproduce the relevant contract, a statement of work, or the underlying filing pages in the copy reviewed. These are two attributed descriptions in one secondary source, not two independent confirmations. [Archived 2003 article](https://www.mail-archive.com/ctrl%40listserv.aol.com/msg102103.html)

3. **2005 retrospective reporting on the 1998 transition.** Margie Burns’s *Washington Spectator* article reports that the company received about $9.2 million from the WTC job from 1996 through 1998; it attributes to former manager Al Weinstein the account that Securacom was “excused from the project” in 1998 because it could not fulfill the work, and says EJ Electric took over the electronic-security work. This is a reporter’s account attributed to a former manager, not a termination notice, final invoice, or transfer/acceptance record. [Article copy](https://informationdissemination.org/archive/pdf/marvin.pdf)

4. **A separate former-employee account in that 2005 article.** The article says Jeff Gallup left Stratesec for Landtek and quotes him describing himself as “intimately involved” with WTC security until September 12, 2001; it reports that the FBI took the WTC visitor database from his office then. This is a claim about an individual’s involvement and the reported location of a database. It does not, by itself, establish that Stratesec still held the contract, that Gallup had WTC 7 access on September 11, or who owned or administered that database at the time.

## Unresolved scope-and-timing ledger

Keep all of these statements visible together:

- the 1997 S-1/A’s forecast of substantial completion of the WTC project in 1997;
- the later report attributing a 1998 project excusal and electronic-security handoff to EJ Electric;
- the 2003 article’s report that the WTC was listed as a former client through April 2001;
- McDaniel’s reported statement that a completion contract remained unfinished when the Center fell; and
- Gallup’s reported personal involvement through September 12, 2001, after he had left Stratesec for Landtek.

These accounts may refer to different project components, personnel, or residual duties, but the materials reviewed do not test that possibility. Do not use it to harmonize the accounts. “Former client” does not prove every access permission had ended; “completion contract” or an ex-employee’s reported involvement does not prove company-wide or WTC 7-specific control continued.

## What the reviewed sources do and do not establish

- **Supported:** A substantial WTC security-upgrade project was awarded to Securacom in 1996, and the company’s 1997 filing forecast substantial completion in 1997.
- **Reported, not independently verified here from the underlying records:** 1998 excusal/transition; a later “former client” designation; McDaniel’s completion-contract account; and Gallup’s post-employment involvement claim.
- **Open:** Exact contract scope and end date; whether there were separate completion, maintenance, warranty, or service arrangements; the terms and date of any EJ Electric handoff; which systems and buildings—including WTC 7—were covered; and what credentials, administrative permissions, or site access any company or individual retained on September 11.
- **Not established by these sources:** Site-wide or WTC 7-specific security control by Securacom on September 11; unauthorized work; explosives or demolition preparation; or a connection between Securacom, the named managers, Urban Moving Systems, the five Israeli detainees, or Mossad.

The strongest ordinary alternative is that the sources describe a major finite upgrade followed by closeout or transition, while a narrower completion task or an individual’s later involvement persisted. That is a testable alternative, not a resolution: no reviewed contract or access log decides it.

## Records that would discriminate

The highest-value records are the original 1997 and 1998/2001 SEC filing bytes and relevant pages; the Port Authority award, contract, amendments, statements of work, invoices, completion/acceptance and termination records; any Securacom–EJ Electric transition or subcontract records; WTC 7-specific system/maintenance work orders; and dated credential, visitor, system-administrator, and access logs. The underlying interviews or contemporaneous records supporting the Weinstein, McDaniel, and Gallup statements would also help identify each statement’s scope and date. No such records are authenticated or reviewed in this note.
