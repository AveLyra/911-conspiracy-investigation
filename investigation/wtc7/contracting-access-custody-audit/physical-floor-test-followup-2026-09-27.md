## What limits a causal comparison

1. **Rating, damage, survival and stopping are different endpoints.** The
   rating includes temperature and heat/flame criteria, not only structural
   support (5–6,94–96). Cotton-waste ignition checks were not performed because
   of access/safety limits. Temperature limits were reached before test stops.
   Survival to a stop does not establish survival through continued heating,
   cooling or unloading; an imminent-collapse judgment is not an observed
   loss of support or a measured counterfactual collapse time.
2. **Restraint is an implemented boundary, not a complete stiffness description.**
   Printed5 says the cited standard does not prescribe restraining-frame
   stiffness. Welded/contact versus slotted/gapped setups are documented (22),
   but assigning those labels does not verify actual force–displacement history
   or an equivalent boundary in WTC7. The report treats real restraint as
   intermediate; this review has not proved that all responses vary monotonically
   between the two tests.
3. **Scale is not the only changed quantity.** The17ft design retains member
   sections, slab thickness and coating while changing span, web geometry and
   loading (9–10). The stated design rationale was to preserve heating behavior
   with unchanged sections/coating/slab and approximate prototype stresses by
   adjusting loads: a reasoned tradeoff, not evidence of arbitrary design.
   That rationale does not establish full response similarity.
   Toronto/Northbrook furnaces and reported coating densities
   also differ. This is not a geometrically similar, replicated factorial
   experiment. Printed106 explicitly identifies scale, restraint stiffness,
   connections, exposure, repeatability and laboratory reproducibility as
   unresolved research needs. Four specimens do not estimate a building-failure
   base rate or isolate a universal restraint effect.
4. **Thickness is not the sole intervention in3 versus4.** Assembly4 also
   changes bridging/deck protection (4,35,42). At about55min, ceramic-fiber
   insulation was placed over a slab opening to protect hydraulic equipment
   and continue the test (83–84). The opening affected a surface thermocouple,
   which then measured escaping hot gas (104). The later record must retain
   that intervention; its quantitative effect is not established here.
5. **Measurements and observations have coverage limits.** Assembly2's visual
   observations end130min, furnace termination146min (64). Its heat-flux probes
   were unavailable after damage during Assembly1 (47). The report warns that
   unedited data can become unreliable after instrument displacement or wire
   damage (64); the cited AppendixH validity listing is not reviewed here.
   Different heat-flux sensor types measure different radiative/total quantities
   (47). Do not equate all sensor curves, or treat stored values as validated
   measurements throughout the run.
6. **Damage is affirmative, but not a whole-building mechanism.** Logs49–50,
   63–64,74,83–84 describe deck/truss deformation, slab spalling and coating
   loss. Loud sounds were interpreted by the authors as concrete spalling from
   accompanying movements; exact location/extent was not fully ascertained.
   These laboratory descriptions are neither explosive-device evidence nor a
   calibrated acoustic identification of WTC7 recordings.
