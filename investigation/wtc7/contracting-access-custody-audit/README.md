# WTC 7 contracting, access, commissioning, and custody audit

**Status:** Active research work package; not a factual finding, expert report, or pleading theory.

**Purpose:** Test whether the documented ordinary commissioning and access structure leaves records that could distinguish legitimate building work, unauthorized work, and a deliberately commissioned technical operation. This package does not assume that an operation occurred and does not rank named people or firms as perpetrators.

Keep these propositions separate:

1. A firm or person performed ordinary work at or near WTC 7.
2. A person had access to a relevant area.
3. Work or access exceeded its authorized scope.
4. A technical team was commissioned for support removal.
5. A sponsor or authorizer directed that work.
6. The work caused the collapse.

Evidence for an earlier proposition does not establish a later one.

## Discriminating evidence

The operation hypothesis would gain material support only from convergent, primary evidence such as an unauthorized work assignment, relevant access, a payment or procurement trail, communications establishing purpose, and physical evidence with secure provenance. An ordinary work order, a company name, a political relationship, a missing file, or a post-collapse demolition contract is not sufficient.

Negative results must state search coverage. “Not found,” “not located,” “withheld,” “destroyed,” and “never created” are distinct outcomes.

## Guardrails

- Keep access/commissioning and post-collapse cleanup as separate chains until a record connects them.
- Do not infer organizational knowledge from an employee’s alleged conduct.
- Do not infer sponsorship from motive, association, later benefit, or capability.
- Do not use this package to amend pleadings, send requests, contact witnesses, or publish findings without separate review and authorization.
- Preserve negative and contradictory results; do not treat repeated documentary claims as independent corroboration.
