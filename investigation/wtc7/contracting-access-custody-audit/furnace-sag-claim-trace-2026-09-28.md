### Strongest counterarguments retained

Against an overbroad criticism: a sagging floor can retain support and transmit
force through surviving connections. The report openly describes different
restraints, damage and test limits; it does not assert that a full tower was
experimentally collapsed. Protected standard-test survival does not directly
test NIST's damaged-tower scenario. These qualifications matter, without
independently proving the modeled damage or its sufficiency.

Against an overbroad defense: unspecified magnitude agreement cannot establish
accurate timing, force transfer, ultimate failure or transfer to a different
building. Differences in scale, restraint, loads and thermal histories can be
material. The source itself identifies several of those as issues for further
study. A useful comparison need not be perfect or predictive in advance, but
its actual conditions and fitting history must be known before assigning that
stronger evidentiary role.

Keep the already-documented duration correction: v1 p54 says approximately
two hours with a116min minimum; v2 Finding8 says two hours for all four. The
[detailed1-6B audit](physical-floor-test-followup-2026-09-27.md) supplies the
116/146/210(or210.75)/120min stops and corrects the summary's35ft-unrestrained/
210min attribution. This unit does not reopen that completed discrepancy or
silently adopt its shorthand. Ratings are not collapse times, and no reported
failure before termination is not proof of unlimited survival.
