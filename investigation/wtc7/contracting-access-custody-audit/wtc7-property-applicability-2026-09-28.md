### What the public documents establish

1. **Material and thickness.** N9 printed34–36 identifies gypsum/vermiculite
   MK-5, reports manufacturer/inspection sources and describes uniform modeled
   thicknesses. These are meaningful building-specific prescriptions, not no
   information. But printed35–36 says7/16in beam analysis thickness, whereas
   printed390 says13mm/0.5in for modeled beams and girders. Preserve this
   published difference rather than choosing an executed value. A revision,
   different case or summary error could explain it. The stated sensitivity
   cross-reference is not newly reproduced here; the earlier
   [protection crosswalk](protection-assumption-crosswalk-2026-09-27.md) retains
   related context. Installation statistics and a1989 photograph do not prove
   protection condition everywhere in2001.
2. **A located property family.** N9 printed389/391 refers to the tower thermal
   method. Its cited1-5G Appendix A separately plots DC/F and Monokote specific
   heat, density and conductivity, alongside steel/concrete and a low-resistance
   damage placeholder. The temperature axes are Kelvin. Distinct curves defeat
   an automatic assumption of identical DC/F/MK-5 numbers, but neither a citation
   nor a generic product-family label closes the executed formulation/version
   join. The placeholder has an explicitly stated computational purpose; its
   label is not evidence of fabrication or actual WTC7 protection damage.
3. **The density concern is not DC/F-only.** 1-6A printed72 explains that elevated-
   temperature densities were calculated from mass-change and expansion
   measurements, rather than directly measured, and limits their validity to
   600°C. The paragraph concerns the SFRMs/each material. Table6-8 on74 includes
   MK-5 values of227.5kg/m³ at600°C,229.6 at800°C,269.3 at1000°C and369.4 at1200°C.
   These are printed table entries, not validated values beyond the stated
   domain. The separate Monokote density plot in1-5G also visibly falls then
   rises; no exact plot-to-table match was calculated. This makes applicability
   a concrete source question, not an established WTC7 implementation error.
4. **Heat storage cannot be judged from one table alone.** 1-5G FigureA-4,
   printed275, separately plots DC/F and Monokote volumetric enthalpy in J/m³.
   That is affirmative counterevidence to a blanket claim that no heat-storage
   representation beyond the smooth specific-heat curve existed. It does not
   prove which solver property was active, how reactions were represented,
   consistency among the displayed functions, or the WTC7 implementation. The
   selected pages do not resolve whether enthalpy replaced another heat-capacity
   representation or how density entered the calculation. Do not supply that
   missing rule from general software expectations. Phan2003 is a concrete
   reference locator, not an acquired concrete-mixture/assignment record.
5. **Validation has a property-version boundary.** 1-5G printed14–16 describes
   compartment experiments, component meshes and input choices distinct from
   the later tower-floor furnace series audited previously. Printed15 expressly
   attributes DC/F properties to Harmathy1983 because the NIST measurements
   were unavailable when these calculations ran. Printed16 calls the datasets
   generally comparable but reports lower high-temperature Harmathy conductivity,
   which can lower predicted steel temperatures. That is a reported possible
   effect, not a quantified WTC7 bias. These calculations may support the
   heat-transfer method in their tested regime; they cannot alone certify all
   later Appendix A properties, continuation rules or WTC7 MK-5 assignments.
   The source openly explains the chronology; it is not proof of concealment.
6. **The transfer stages remain distinct.** N9 printed389–393 describes gas-field
   averaging, reused/time-shifted floor exposures, thermal histories, CaseA/B/C
   gas-temperature variations, and different ANSYS/LS-DYNA temperature transfers.
   ANSYS uses histories and selected cross-section approximations; LS-DYNA uses
   an initiating-event temperature set, including slab-thickness averages for
   its single-layer shells. N9A printed56 identifies CaseB at four hours for
   the global model. These downstream prescribed temperatures cannot identify
   the upstream insulation law or local insulation temperatures. Gas ±10% is
   a reported scenario range, not an empirical confidence interval or a test
   of all material-property uncertainties. No new failure-timing inference is
   drawn from these reused method pages.
