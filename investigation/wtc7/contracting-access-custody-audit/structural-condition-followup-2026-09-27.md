## What the condition survey does and does not establish

### Scope and favorable observations

C 107–108/53–54 says the inspection was designed to minimize tenant disruption,
with accessible unoccupied service floors 3–6 and vacant space on floors 7,
10, 18, 23 and 25. It was primarily visual, with approximately 5% of critical
connections receiving a hands-on inspection; footnote 4 says the original
report did not define what that meant. Detailed Tables 3–9 through 3–12
identify selected framing locations at additional levels. Do not convert the
accessible-space prose into a complete list of inspected structural levels
or silently harmonize floor/ceiling/framing namespaces.

Column 79 appears in the column-splice inspection table at floors 5 and 23;
this is not a capacity test, whole-column certification, or inspection of the
later alleged initiating connection. Drawing S-3/S-8 references are preserved
as locators only. C 110/56 reports good condition of inspected framing and
no problems/deterioration at the reported splice, bracing and interior-beam
connection inspections. Footnote 5's definition allows minor, mainly routine
repairs. Floor slabs were also reported good; described shrinkage cracks and
construction-related deck damage were not considered structural problems.
C 111/57 reports a favorable Con Edison substation inspection. These findings
must not be dropped because deficiencies are more interesting.

### Deficiencies, recommendations, and one reported correction

C 110/56 reports fireproofing missing at utility-support installation locations
on all inspected floors, especially fifth-floor framing above the lobby and
second-floor framing above the loading dock. It recommends replacement.
The reproduced survey's **Routine Repairs item 8**, C 112/58, expands the
stated installation purposes to utilities, partition walls and special
equipment, and calls for fireproof coating where applicable. Its footnote
limits the throughout-floors statement to the floors inspected.

Neither passage quantifies damaged area, remaining thickness, specific member
coverage at the proposed initiation site, repair completion, or 2001 condition.
It is a lead for checking fire-protection assumptions, not a recovered thermal
boundary condition. Do not label the building wholly unprotected or assume
the deficiencies persisted until the fire.

Figure 3–4 has **one Immediate Action row, eight Safety Items and eight Routine
Repairs**. It is attributed to PANYNJ 1997 and reproduced with permission: an
excerpt of survey material, not merely NIST's narrative, but still not a
separately acquired/authenticated full original.

- The Immediate Action row, C 111/57, explicitly reports removal of loose
  concrete at column 51 in the floor-46 cooling-tower area by building-management
  personnel. C 110 identifies Silverstein Properties personnel. These are two
  presentations of the same attributed episode, not independent corroboration.
  Footnote 6 says size and exact location are not supplied.
- Safety entries address loading-dock ceiling storage, ladder/platform/anchor
  hazards, a concrete patch, a projection-screen support, and roof access.
  They are not a signed completion checklist. Safety item 7 says
  “as performed in Item 6 above,” while item 6 directs provision of anchoring;
  retain that textual ambiguity, not a blanket all-prospective or all-completed
  interpretation. Printed floor-48 references are retained as source labels,
  not silently corrected to the building's occupied-floor count.
- Routine entries concern roof/scaffolding support, cleaning/coating, sealing,
  roofing, window-washing brackets, fuel-line pipe supports in the fifth-floor
  south generator room (item 7), and fireproofing (item 8). The pipe-support
  recommendation is not evidence of a fuel leak or fire contribution.

The source separately recommends removal of delaminated concrete at a sixth-
floor patch and cleaning/painting roof steel. Missing follow-up records cannot
be used to turn those recommendations into uncorrected 2001 defects.

### Apparent differences that remain unresolved

to the 1997 survey, while separately attributing column reinforcement for

states that removed trading-floor slabs were later replaced when trading
floors moved, citing Cantor 1989. The passages do not supply a complete dated
alteration sequence. Different work, dates, floor conventions or a reporting
error remain alternatives; neither a direct contradiction nor the 2001 slab
configuration is established. Do not supply guessed chronology to fix it.

C 174/120 describes additional earlier alterations and missing further
documentation. It does not turn either 2000 filing-system project into an
undocumented operation. Original project/survey material is the discriminator.
