## Damage, method and interpretation limits

- Printed5-6: ASTM E119 uses underside standard exposure, applied structural
  load, and criteria for heat/flame passage, surface temperatures and load
  maintenance. Fire rating is not synonymous with a collapse time. The cited
  standard does not specify restraining-frame stiffness; varied components,
  spans, openings and actual fire conditions are outside a simple transfer.
- Printed49-50 and63: deck buckling, bridging/main-truss deformation, coating
  fall-off and concrete spalling were reported. Sounds described as reports
  were interpreted as explosive concrete spalling from associated movement;
  exact spalling location/extent could not be ascertained. These are not
  evidence of explosive devices or a calibrated historical acoustic comparator.
- Printed74 records Assembly3 increased deflection and limited coating fall-off
  while reaching210min. Printed83-84 records Assembly4 concrete spalling near
 55min; ceramic-fiber insulation was then placed over the opening to protect
  hydraulic loading equipment and allow continuation. This intervention and
  its possible exposure/measurement effects must accompany later observations.
- Printed94-95: cotton-waste ignition checks on unexposed surfaces were not
  performed, for safety/access reasons. Do not claim every potential endpoint
  was fully measured. Temperature limits were reached well before termination.
- Printed104: Assembly4's surface thermocouple near the slab opening recorded
  hot gas after slab failure. Printed104-105 compares *measured* center
  deflections and span-normalized deflections among the four assemblies. This
  is not a finite-element-versus-test validation plot. The reported agreement
  of normalized curves has no error metric supplied here and is not identity.
- Printed106 expressly questions17ft-to35-60ft rating scalability and names
  needed work on limit states, restraint stiffness, connections, loading and
  exposure, repeatability, furnace/laboratory reproducibility and realistic
  fires. Four differently configured tests are not a replicated factorial
  estimate isolating thickness, restraint or scale. Assembly4 differs in
  bridging protection and its slab-opening intervention, not only thickness.

## Claim ceilings and strongest counterreadings

The detailed report directly establishes what the publication reports (gradeA
for content attribution), and supplies primary reported empirical support for
substantial heated-assembly load-bearing survival with deformation/damage.
That is affirmative evidence against claiming there was no physical heated
floor/restraint evidence. Conversely, the same record prevents interpreting
survival as undamaged behavior, limitless reserve, or demonstrated load-bearing
failure at the fire-rating time. Imminent-collapse judgments and terminated
tests leave later behavior unknown; mere "no collapse" is not a complete
performance characterization.

These selected pages neither establish nor refute the accuracy of a particular
equivalent-coating model. They do not present a matched original/equivalent
coating reaction comparison, a blind predictive model test, or WTC7 geometry,
fire/damage/restraint histories. Measured floor-response agreement could be
useful empirical support for a properly specified model; the selected
cross-test plots alone cannot identify which assumptions were calibrated,
validated or historically executed. No causal ranking or intent finding follows.

Highest-value bounded follow-up: resolve the actual17ft applied-load route and
the missing Assembly2/termination/unloading continuation before asserting
complete load histories; for model validation, obtain the case-linked model
specification, predicted and observed histories, preselected metrics and tuning
history. Source pages or original data are needed; do not invent those results.
