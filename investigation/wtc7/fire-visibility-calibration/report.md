## Result

Separating incidental cautions from decisive exclusions removes the earlier
automatic-empty-subset failure in this new pilot. Both reviewers identify the
same six eligible candidate regions at the lowest opportunity threshold and
the same three at the middle threshold in the closer north-face image. Neither
has an eligible candidate at the highest threshold. The other image still
supplies no eligible candidate subset for the four sampled bands/fragments.

This does **not** produce a defensible burning-window percentage. The sample
was deliberately chosen for difficult cases, the opportunity intervals are
subjective, and three flame-versus-glow judgments remain disputed even with
identical geometry/opportunity decisions. At the middle threshold each reviewer
has one flame-like label among the three eligible candidates, but **they assign
that label to different candidates**. An equal aggregate is not corroboration
of the same observation.

The appropriate next step is broader facade/time coverage and better temporal
source evidence, not repeated relabeling of these two stills until agreement
appears. No temperature, model-overestimate magnitude, collapse cause ranking,
or finding about intent changes from this pilot.

## Consequences for the scientific question

This is a better separation of what an image shows from what an analyst can
infer. It does not settle NIST's thermal reconstruction. The report-attributed
floor mapping remains the earlier
[source comparison](/Users/admin/docs/911/research/sherlock-wtc7-investigation/fire-annotation/source-comparison.md):
the close-up's detailed rows are attributed to Floors 8 and 7, **not Floors
12 and 13**. Its local bright/dark contrast cannot directly validate or falsify
those other floors' temperature histories. That mapping remains attribution,
not independent architectural or clock authentication.

The earlier source-pinned questions about prescribed ventilation, uncertain
ignition, shifted floor histories and burn-duration mismatch remain open.
This pilot neither cancels them nor quantifies their thermal consequences.
Glazing, smoke, depth, saturation and the absence of calibrated detection
limits preclude converting nondetection into an interior heat bound.

| Claim | Type / strength | Limitation and discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The old blanket fields guarantee empty reviewer eligibility | Derived / directly established for saved v1 records | Does not imply all old visual judgments agree; actual code and records are preserved |
| Explicit severity gives matching eligible membership in this pilot | Derived from subjective annotations / directly established as bookkeeping | Same purposive images, shared geometry and rubric; not an accuracy estimate or physical validation |
| A matching flame-like total corroborates location or fire extent | Contradicted as a general inference by the middle-threshold example | Unit-level labels differ despite equal totals; inspect locators, not just aggregate counts |
| The ad43/U02 appearance difference primarily reflects single-label category priority | Supported within the two stated descriptions | Smoke identification remains unauthenticated; e43/U06 and ad43/R01C03 retain real opportunity/geometry judgments, not merely wording |
| This establishes exaggerated modeled heating or favors a collapse cause | Unsupported by this pilot | Requires floor/time-matched observation model, actual input histories and thermal/structural sensitivity, not more agreement on these stills |

## Verification and continuation

The independently authored bookkeeping passed 31 synthetic tests before
historical summaries. Root reran all 31; both historical executions succeeded
and produced byte-identical JSON. Tests cover rule boundaries and integrity,
not visual recognition. [Validation](validation.md) records commands, pins,
independent arithmetic review and remaining implementation limits.

Before further annotation, make the minimal versioned clarification that a
smoke description and no-discernible-flame description can coexist; preserve
target evaluability separately from the classification of a visible veil.
Keep the uncertain severity judgment explicit rather than forcing it from a
nuisance name. This is not authorization to relabel the frozen pilot.

Then expand the previously located image corpus's facade/time coverage with
localized observations and unknown/obscured regions. Where bright morphology
is decisive to a claim, prioritize adjacent, authenticated native frames and
source processing history. Do not hold the broader map hostage to obtaining a
window percentage; its required product is a reproducible visibility timeline
and input-specific agreement/mismatch record. Any additional quantitative
detection method still requires proper calibration and qualified review.
