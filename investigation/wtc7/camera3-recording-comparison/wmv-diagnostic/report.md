# Camera 3 WMV warning localization: public summary

> **Replacement summary.** This is a reconstruction from the previously published [investigation status](../../STATUS.md#2026-09-11--wmv-warning-localization-continuation), not the original diagnostic report. The source images, underlying logs and complete diagnostic artifacts are not included here, and this summary does not represent a fresh reproduction.

The earlier status reports that a bounded examination associated three decoder warnings with indices **37, 55 and 63**, at encoded times **2.466, 3.666 and 4.200 seconds**, respectively. It reports that both full settings and two retained runs produced that association. Encoded times are positions within an access copy, not established times of the events filmed.

The same status says two reviewers each inspected the 15 selected native images and reported countdown graphics in all of them; it says both saved PointMass arrays were disjoint from the three flagged indices. On that account, the identified warnings do not by themselves demonstrate corruption of collapse imagery or invalidate the saved annotations. Nor does the absence of later warnings certify later pixels. The warning localization does not authenticate the source footage or its original clock, establish that the saved point arrays cover every potentially affected observation, or validate cross-file matches and physical measurements.

For context, [NIST's WTC 7 FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation) describes its reported three-stage roofline descent analysis. The earlier WMV diagnostic does not independently verify or refute that analysis, and it does not identify a cause of collapse.

The distinction between the earlier reported checks and currently reproducible evidence is set out in the [public validation note](validation.md).
