## What can and cannot follow

The attachment supplies substantive, dated design-review questions previously
unread in this unit. That strengthens a concrete need to check the actual
responses, revised drawings and installed-state/inspection records, particularly
fuel-tank support, equipment cooling, detection and shutdown. It is not a
computed structural failure, an expert confirmation of inadequacy, a documented
2001 configuration, a link to NIST model inputs, or evidence of deliberate
support removal. Do not conflate the6,000-gallon fuel tank and unspecified
11,000-gallon tank merely because both appear in the packet.

Ordinary explanation has positive support: an identified design-review process
requested corrections and clarification before later construction. The strongest
objection to treating that process as assurance is that the responsive revisions,
approvals and actual closeout are absent from this ten-page packet. Conversely,
outstanding comments in a1998 review do not demonstrate they remained unresolved
in2001. Either inference requires dated follow-through records.

This PDF is an incoming2002 request with attached1998 material. It contains no
identified agency FOIL determination or production list; it cannot establish
what the City subsequently released. It shows the requester had copies presented
as1998 comments, but not their first public disclosure or independent historical
custody. The2002 request's breadth—including explosives—cannot be treated as
an affirmative contemporary finding of those materials. Modern2026 processing
metadata and portal Bates labels are not1998 creation or first-release dates.

No collapse-cause ranking change is justified by this reading alone. Stronger
evidence would be matching revised S-1/S-2/S-3/S-5 and equipment/fire-protection
records, identified written responses and inspections, or the actual FOIL
response/enclosures. The cover's unreviewed group needs actual returned item
locators, not a guessed sequence of Bates URLs. No automatic group expansion.
