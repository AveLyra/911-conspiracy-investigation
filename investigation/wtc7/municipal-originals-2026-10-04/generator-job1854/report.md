# July 1999 OEM generator test report and unresolved work

The five reviewed PDFs contain versions of **one August 2, 1999 Cosentini
report**, describing testing on **July 31, 1999**. This advances the earlier
planning-only evidence: a consulting engineering firm reported actual operation
and left the system described as ready for emergency demand. It also listed
seven unfinished corrective or investigative items. Neither half should be
omitted. This is research evidence of reported performance, not independent
validation of the tests, complete acceptance, or the installation's 2001 state.

## What was reportedly tested

Cosentini's letter to Swanke Hayden Connell identifies the Mayor's Office of
Emergency Management generator project, Job 1854. Page 1 reports a July 31
full-load test starting about 6 a.m.: engines 1, 2 and 3 ran for 2½ hours,
with H.O. Penn monitoring and recording generator characteristics. It also
reports individual-generator and total-system transient voltage load-step tests
recorded by APT. Cosentini's examination of those charts is still future-tense.
Actual load values, instrument traces, tolerances and chart-review findings
are not supplied.

Page 2 reports automatic transfer switches 2–9 transferring without difficulty;
an ATS 1 outage simulation starting the generators and bringing supply online
in approximately seven seconds; and a total-outage procedure followed by
service of transfer-switch loads. These are summary assertions, not timing
measurements independently made in this investigation. The load-service and
temporary building-pump descriptions support an **installed-system testing
inference**, rather than a factory-only demonstration. They do not establish
an exact floor, permanent fuel route, equipment serial identity, or coverage
of every tenant and system in WTC 7.

The attendee list is reported attendance, not separate testimony from each
organization or proof of who personally observed every test. Cosentini's
authorship also does not by itself identify its personnel as eyewitnesses.

Page 3 says the system was **left in automatic mode and ready to deliver
emergency power on demand**, while requiring these items to be addressed
promptly for proper design operation. It does not postpone all readiness until
completion of the list; equally, readiness is not a certificate that all design
functions or permanent arrangements were accepted. A total-failure narrative
and an unqualified-completion narrative both overstate this report.

1999 testing. This report does **not** say whether July 31 was a postponement,
an additional test or a retest. Either date may have had a separate event;
neither cancellation nor successful July 17 completion can be inferred here.

October 1998 cover reviewed previously.

Similarly, deletion of a consultant's separately priced test-witnessing service
cannot be used to say no generator testing occurred: this later report is
contrary evidence. It does not establish restoration of that exact contractual
service or satisfaction of a payment condition. No evidence here links the
reported defects to a 2001 fire, structural support loss, deliberate intervention
or intentional concealment. **Neither physical-compatibility nor overall
