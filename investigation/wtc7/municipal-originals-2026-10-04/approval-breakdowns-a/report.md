# Fuel route revisions and approval accounting at 7 World Trade Center

October 4, 2026. Research only. Three City-held schedules add specific leads
for a later fuel-line route, a fireproofing substitution, valve and hatch
controls, and reported project payments. They do **not** establish the
installed configuration, its structural effects or a collapse mechanism.
The most consequential change is that the March 1999 routing references
cannot be treated as the last documented route: all three schedules describe
a subsequently approved second-floor machine-room corridor route and a
credit for the earlier line.

These are dated versions of one recognizable accounting history, not three
independent witnesses to construction. Both prior-informed AI readers covered
all eleven physical pages before exchanging their frozen notes. Critical
comparison caught two errors in root's transcription; a separately declared
source recheck corrected them without rewriting the original records.

## Route and protection changes

Order 40 repeats concrete removal and new-to-existing trench rebar welding
with a three-inch overlap: $26,427 proposed, $21,300 approved January 25, 1999.
It still supplies no floor, member, joint or drawing identifier. The actual
joint and governing revision must be identified before comparing it with the
earlier trench drawing or calculating capacity. Sources: 171286 page 2,
171620 page 2 and 172947 page 2.

Those same pages identify order 44R as oil piping under revised FSK50/SK58,
dated March 19, 1999, with $85,000 approved April 8. Order 55 describes
electrical removal/reinstallation due to fuel rerouting, with $29,000 approved
March 11. These different date roles do not, by themselves, establish a
contradictory installation sequence or prove that order 55 followed the final
March 19 revision.

Order 88 supplies a more precise later location: revised fuel piping through
the **second-floor machine-room corridor**, $135,957 proposed and $130,000
approved July 7, 1999. A separate same-date credit lists $85,893 proposed and
$85,000 approved for the previously approved fuel line, matching 44R's amounts.
This strongly supports a commercial revision/credit linkage. It does not show
whether the earlier route had been installed, removed, partly reused or never
built. Counting both prices as two concurrently installed lines would be
unjustified. Sources: 171286 page 3; 171620 and 172947 page 2, with corresponding
budget entries. The July-dated version already contains this change.

Order 97 describes **two-hour-rated sheetrock fireproofing in place of a
two-hour vermiculite enclosure**, $4,560 approved October 27, 1999. It appears
in both the November and 2002 schedules, not solely the retrospective packet.
This is affirmative protective scope, not evidence that protection was simply
deleted. Nor does a stated rating prove tested performance or compliant field
installation. Sources: 171620 page 2; 171286 page 3.

The 2002 page 3 also reports order 112's anti-syphon-to-check-valve change for
fuel suction, $2,266 approved February 25, 2000; order 113's self-closing
tank-room hatch modification from a fusible link to an electric thermal link
tied to the suppression panel, $3,253 approved June 26, 2000; and later switch,
reserve supply and hatch-release disconnect work. Suppression-system spelling
varies in the source; no exact product identification or performance finding is
inferred from a normalized name. These are concrete records to reconcile with
underlying orders and the installed system, not an operational demolition chain.

## Contrary evidence and changing dispositions

The schedules describe ordinary project coordination, reinforcement and
protection as well as removal. Examples include additional steel at the
switchgear/water tank, seventh-floor Cantor-sketch work, microwave supports,
the hurricane-wall gap closure and reinforcing plates for doors. A claim that
every alteration was weakening would omit this affirmative contrary evidence.
Conversely, protective purpose does not establish sound design or execution.

Several wording distinctions prevent false inferences. Order 14 credits removal
of **Cosentini's witnessing** of a generator field test, not cancellation of the
test. Order 15 explicitly concerns cutting and sleeves on the **23rd floor**;
it is not a located first-floor trench record. Order 59 concerns electrical
wiring in an existing four-inch conduit, not necessarily the fuel-containment
conduit. Order 86's voided proposal to route ductwork around an existing beam
does not say the beam was removed. Sources: each schedule's pages 1-2 and
171286 page 3 for order 86.

Version-specific states also matter. Roof-radio-room work is “being revised”
in the 1999 schedules and “being voided” in 2002. Wall clips and a blast-resistant
door are HOLD in 1999 and being voided later. The UPS conversion row is
“not received” with blank amounts in July, HOLD with $1,276 proposed in November,
and being voided in 2002. Some July pending/not-to-be-approved items later
carry approvals. Heat tracing is $11,616 proposed with blank approval in
November, then $10,255 approved February 9, 2000 in the later schedule.
Normal administrative development is a plausible account of these changes;
they do not alone establish falsification. Zero, blank, HOLD, VOID and
being voided remain distinct source states.

## Reported payments and arithmetic

The November packet expressly labels four line items as amounts paid, totaling
$11,384,322, but leaves their dates blank. Its budget/payments headings also
include **to be approved**, **anticipated construction amount** and
**anticipated balance**. Do not describe every figure as a final completed
approval, or import payment dates from the later version. Source: 171620 page 3.

The 2002 packet reports seven dated payment lines from July 14, 1998 through
June 14, 2001: $3,500,000; $3,500,000; $2,500,000; $1,884,322; $300,000;
$340,007; and $504,232. They sum to $12,528,561, matching its total labeled
approved for payment; the stated balance to be paid is zero. The final line's
ordinal appears as “76th,” not evidence of 76 transfers. The packet's reported
remaining budget is $90,567. Sources: 171286 pages 4-5.

This is affirmative payment reporting, stronger than a proposal alone. It is
not a bank reconciliation, a voucher audit or evidence allocating each transfer
to a particular change order. The July packet supplies approved/proposed budget
scenarios but no payments page. Root's 16 selected arithmetic comparisons now
match, including payment sums and printed budget relationships; the peer
separately checked a broader set of 24 equalities. Neither summed and audited
every individual order, authenticated disbursement or verified construction.

- July row 87 has no visible amount. Root's $1,276 was incorrectly carried
  into that version; it belongs to later schedule states.

The [correction record](correction-review.md) preserves the targeted, unblinded
nature of those views and leaves both original readings intact. These are not
new independent initial agreements.

A separate date uncertainty remains. Both original readers read handwriting
beside the final 2002 change-order rows as apparently December 14, 2000, with
a bracket apparently spanning three items. The next budget page instead
prints December 18, 2000 for $40,485 and April 17, 2001 for $762. The amount
correction does not settle that date/bracket interpretation. Underlying approval
instruments are needed; neither page is silently repaired or treated as evidence
of concealment.
