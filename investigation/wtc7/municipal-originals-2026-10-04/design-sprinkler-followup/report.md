### Structural services

The proposed seven-part scope includes seventh-floor mechanical-equipment
supports (switchgear, generators, water tank); slab penetrations from seventh
floor to roof; review of new twenty-third-floor loads and reinforcement as
required; a roof radio room; new fuel-oil storage; construction/bid documents;
and construction administration through shop-drawing review and field questions.
These identify concrete design records worth locating. Slab penetrations are
not automatically beam penetrations or column cuts, and proposed reinforcement
is not proof of its installation or its adequacy.

Controlled inspection is excluded from this engineer's proposed fee; the
letter describes specialized inspection services as the usual economical
alternative. Building-department filing is assumed to be by an expediter.
This creates a specific separate-record question, not evidence that nobody
inspected or filed. An apparent author signature is present, while the client
acceptance signature/date lines are blank. Do not confuse the two or call the
entire letter unsigned. No conclusion about acceptance elsewhere follows.

Relocation of existing piping or adjustment of existing heads to remain is

The quote does not document an Inergen modification, completed work or a

## Evidence weights and strongest competing readings

**A, document contents:** the dates, proposed scopes, prices, drawing references
and exclusions are directly readable. **B, ordinary coordination interpretation:**
the documents show intended design/procurement roles rather than an absence of
planning. This does not demonstrate satisfactory execution. **D, actual geometry,
completed work and 2001 state:** none is established by these files. An executed
agreement would strengthen authorization evidence; actual calculations/drawings
would add technical content; a witnessed inspection or functional test would
address performance at its own date. None automatically establishes event-day
condition or a causal connection to collapse.

The strongest concern is that work might have been incomplete or inadequate.
The strongest ordinary alternative is that it was completed through records
not supplied here. The present evidence does not decide between them. Absence
of a calculation or certificate in a fee letter or quote has little exclusionary
weight because those document types need not contain it. Conversely, the
existence of professional correspondence is not engineering validation.
