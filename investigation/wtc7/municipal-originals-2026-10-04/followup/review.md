## Date correction and document joins

**May 4 is not the addendum date.** The unobscured cover says May 20, 1998; the accompanying lists say May 15; the specifications/drawings being modified are described as dated May 4. The catalogue selection used the May 4 reference, which matched the earlier requested resubmission deadline, but that coincidence does not prove a timely May 4 response or compliance. Preserve all these dates rather than flattening them into one date.

The two new documents explicitly share OEM, WTC7, SHCA and **project 5576A**. The parent April 1998 package names OEM/WTC7, SHCA, DRES and matching drawing families, but uses **DRES project 96-1346**, not the same numerical identifier. The common project description, architect, institutions and drawing IDs strongly support a related project-document family. These PDFs do not contain an explicit item-by-item statement answering the April 17/20 comments or a crosswalk equating the two project numbering systems. Do not claim that every April issue was answered merely because the project family is identifiable.

The structural list supplies concrete **May 13 S-1/S-2/S-3/S-5** versions to seek for the earlier support, location, beam and cross-reference questions. The documents do not supply those actual drawings. A-1-1 and relevant mechanical/fire-protection references also create narrower content-review leads, not known resolutions. The typography instruction matters: do not count every listed sheet as newly issued under Addendum 1, and do not infer a sheet was never revised because its row is not bold-italic.

The March 2000 memo advances the record beyond a purely hypothetical future inspection: an identified architect contemporaneously **reports** a preliminary walk-through and a particular follow-up request. That is meaningful evidence of a reported inspection-related process, while remaining distinct from the Port Authority inspector's own record or a documented completed correction. The process can be ordinary safety review; it is not affirmative evidence of deliberate disabling or concealment. Conversely, describing it as routine does not prove the hatch/interlock was installed or worked in 2001.

The memo concerns an OEM fuel-oil-tank room but supplies no tank capacity or enough geometry to independently identify it with the parent's 6,000-gallon tank, unspecified 11,000-gallon tank, another tenant's system or a NIST scenario. No such transfer is made. A generic closing mechanism described by Bilco does not itself demonstrate the requested interface to Inergen.
