# Generator test follow-up and limits on completion claims

The seven reviewed pages add a contemporaneous explanation for an unsupplied
test report, plans for corrective work, and a later pump-valve recommendation.
They do **not** supply the underlying generator measurements or establish
completion of the earlier seven-item work list. One keyword-selected file is
a preliminary door-lock packet, not generator test evidence.

The strongest contrary evidence to concealment inferred from nonattachment,
or to a total-failure narrative, is affirmative: the cover says the supplier report would follow
upon receipt, and the contractor reports present emergency-power readiness.
These statements do not prove later delivery, complete acceptance, or the
installation's condition in 2001. This is research-only documentary analysis,
not an engineering certification or a collapse-cause finding.

The named author, report date and test date substantively associate the cover
with the [previously reviewed three-page report](../generator-job1854/report.md).

This is a supported association, not a reconstructed original packet or custody

## Reported readiness coexists with unfinished corrections

Ambassador's August 4 letter to Silverstein reports the July 31 generator
load test and calls the listed problems minor. That severity characterization
belongs to the contractor, not this investigation.

The letter says the differential-pressure controller will be reprogrammed
on August 4 to close building valves and start the emergency condenser-water
system manually as designed. A proposed work date equal to the letter date
does not establish that work was completed. It also describes the ATS
manufacturer working on a solution involving added relays; corrective work
would precede the load-shedding test. No fitted-relay record or subsequent
passed test appears here.

The earlier Cosentini report calls for priority-load-shedding software work.
The different descriptions are not automatically contradictory: without switch
identities, circuitry and revision records they could concern related parts
of the same correction, or different work. Neither account closes the item.

The contractor nevertheless states that the generators were left automatic
and ready for power on demand, and that OEM is ready for a power emergency.
Its stated basis is Cosentini's attached report, not a separate validated test.
Do not make this present-readiness claim contingent on future work; equally,
do not turn readiness into acceptance of every design function. The referenced
attachment is not inside this one-page PDF.

## The later valve memo does not establish installed or event-day state

Cosentini's February 15, 2000 memorandum to SHCA identifies OEM–7 WTC,
Job 1854, and the fill pump set transferring oil from the building storage
tank to the tenant storage tank. Citing a reported field observation by the
equipment manufacturer's representative, it recommends removing an existing
anti-siphon valve and installing a check valve near the building tank, with
a spring-loaded valve specified for horizontal application.

This is specific advice about a described arrangement, not an unrelated
generic specification. But the representative's underlying report, pump
identity, valve model, route, flow/pressure measurements, implementation and
acceptance are not supplied. The shared project and pump subject justify a
follow-up lead; they do not prove this was the July temporary connection,
that one malfunction persisted until February, or that the recommended work
occurred. No adverse safety effect, harmful intent, 2001 configuration or
structural consequence can be inferred from the component names alone.
