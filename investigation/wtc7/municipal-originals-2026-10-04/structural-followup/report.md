## Consequences for testing competing explanations

**What is strengthened:** there is now a concrete OEM fuel-enclosure design
to seek in construction, inspection and model-source records. It is inaccurate
to treat this particular source as only an absent attachment, or to infer
unprotected conduit from the beam-penetration catalog title. Both sources
share the explicit OEM job5576A used by the earlier addendum/hatch records.
These source-content claims are grade A; the common project identifier does
not prove the two operations concern the same member or location.

**What is not established:** a beam cut, structural weakening, adequate load
capacity, specified fire-resistance performance, installed enclosure continuity,
rupture resistance, or event-day protection. Those remain underdetermined in
this scope (grade D). The drawing lacks a sufficient building floor/column
join here to assign it to a critical member or a particular NIST fuel scenario.
It cannot be transferred to another tenant's fuel system merely because both
use diesel. It also does not establish an answer to the April1998 design
comments or a revision of the May13 S-sheets.

The strongest ordinary interpretation is design/change-order coordination
with an expressly reported structural review and a support/enclosure detail.
The strongest unresolved concern is whether that design was correctly located,
implemented, inspected and retained, and whether any beam modification affected
a consequential load path. Neither ordinary documentation nor a drawing title
settles those questions. An installed protected assembly would weaken an
unprotected-release premise; a documented different or incomplete installation
would weaken reliance on the drawing as the historical configuration. Either
case still requires a supported fire/exposure and structural consequence.

There is no affirmative evidence of a deliberate weakening operation or
concealment in these three pages. The missing fax attachment is a scoped
completeness gap, not archive-wide absence or proof of removal. No inference
about intent follows from the drawing's existence or the change-order number.
