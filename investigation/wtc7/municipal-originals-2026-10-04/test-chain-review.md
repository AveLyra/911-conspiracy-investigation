## Conclusion and the question that actually advances

The record is no longer only a schedule, a witnessing credit and missing
results. It contains a specific engineering account of **July 31, 1999
installed-system testing**, affirmative present readiness, named shortcomings,
and contemporaneous follow-up. It therefore weighs against the narrow claim
that deletion of a witnessing fee demonstrates no testing, or that every
missing attachment indicates suppression. It does **not** establish permanent
fuel-system validation, closure of all seven corrections, or 2001 condition.

The useful next question is not whether someone planned a test or procured
service; those documentary questions are answered. It is **what equipment and
fuel/control configuration was actually tested, what changed afterward, and
whether records connect that configuration to the later relevant conditions**.
Raw electrical results can test the commissioning narrative, but are not by
themselves evidence of thermal exposure or structural support loss.

## Weight of ordinary and adverse interpretations

Within these pages, **ordinary commissioning with documented deficiencies,
commercial choices and follow-up is better supported than a coordinated
support-removal or evidence-suppression operation**. That comparison rests on
affirmative content: described load service, disclosed defects/workaround,
assigned corrective responsibilities, explicit pending-report transmission,
service procurement and an articulated witnessing-cost rationale. It is not
an assumption that official or professional sources must be correct.

The strongest adverse interpretation is also real: reported readiness was
declared while intended fuel pumping was not operating during the test,
protection/control work remained open, and underlying measurements and completed
technical review are not supplied. That prevents using the commissioning
account as independent verification of the final fuel/control design. It could
support investigation of inadequate acceptance or unresolved defects. Whether
those were ordinary temporary commissioning issues or consequential persistent
defects is unresolved; neither is deliberate sabotage on this record.

The retained accounting discrepancy is $85,000 between all thirteen dated
entries and the printed June subtotal, not a proven missing payment. Related
VOID/credit states provide a specific possible worksheet explanation, not a
verified repair. A's $11 mismatch and imported July $1,276 were **reader
transcription errors**, corrected under a disclosed protocol, not historical
anomalies. B's four disputed fields remain excluded; arithmetic does not settle
their readings. These administrative issues cannot substitute for an operation,
equipment or physical-mechanism link.

No overall WTC 7 cause ranking changes from this test chain alone. This is not
equal probability or a demand for perfect evidence: these records have useful
local discriminatory weight but very limited direct relevance to observed
support-loss timing. They supply no member-capacity calculation, event-day fuel
release/ignition/thermal exposure, or timed deliberate support-removal act.
Pump/control deficiencies could matter to a **specified** fuel/fire scenario;
one still needs the system, configuration, operational path and physical
consequences. Electrical test success/failure does not automatically select
either fire-induced structural collapse or demolition.

## Highest-value next discriminators, not an archive-reading queue

1. **Fuel/control configuration and correction history:** highest potential
   causal value here. Link intended/temporary pump, tank-to-tank circuit,
   shutdown controls and February advice/order 112 to equipment-specific
   completed work, permanent-system tests and later configuration. Such records
   could exclude a proposed operational fuel path, or substantiate a persistent
   defect/path needing a conditional thermal investigation. Neither outcome
   alone establishes ignition or collapse. Start from named records/held
   source associations; do not assume CO016 candidates are closure records.
2. **July 31 raw records and dated acceptance:** H.O. Penn measurements,
   APT traces and Cosentini evaluation, together with actual correction tests,
   can confirm or contradict the reported performance and identify tested
   scope. PA sign-off, punch-list closure and a voucher tied to the $1,884,322
   condition can clarify acceptance, not certify all unlisted systems. A
   July 17/31 bridge should be sought as part of that exact event chain rather
   than treating the two dates as a contradiction. Raw electrical performance
   alone has lower collapse-discriminating value than the fuel/configuration
   join and must not become a substitute for it.
3. **Factory data and approved witnessing scope:** certified Peoria data with
   engine identities, the actual witnessing-credit instruction and the
   October 30 enclosure could test whether promised data replaced witnessing,
   which test was affected, and whether equipment matches. This chiefly tests
   procurement/acceptance quality; absent a defect-to-site bridge, it is less
   causally valuable than items 1–2. HVAC acceptance/service paperwork is
   similarly conditional on an actual HVAC/thermal question, not a reason
   to review every commercially related record.
