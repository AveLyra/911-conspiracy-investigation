# OEM piping protection and the remaining drawing match

October 4, 2026. Research-only comparison of NCSTAR1-1J physical pages59-64
with the preserved municipal E5 and SK-58 readings. The initial three-page
scope was extended prospectively to the three directly following figure pages,
including one intentionally blank page. This tests documentary relationships,
not a fire or structural simulation.

**NIST already described protected OEM fuel piping.** The reviewed chapter
places a new storage tank on the first floor and describes a tank-to-pump
arrangement, but it does not explicitly identify E5 or SK-58 or resolve their
6-inch versus8-inch conduit labels. Its smaller fuel-carrying pipe dimensions
must not be substituted for either conduit dimension. The new drawing is not,
by itself, evidence that NIST omitted enclosure protection.

## What the figures add

Physical62/printed28 contains **Figures6-1 and6-2**, respectively first-floor
storage/fill/transfer-pump and seventh-floor day-tank/generator plans.
Physical63/printed29 contains **Figure6-3**, a schematic section that separates
OEM additions from named existing systems. Physical64/printed30 is explicitly
intentionally blank; it is included in the declared coverage, not a failed render.

The figures positively support the first-floor candidate for E5's tank/pump
run and distinguish it from the seventh-floor generator arrangement. They do
not give a protective-conduit diameter, E5/SK-58 reference, structural grid,
support/anchorage detail or visible north arrow for an exact comparison.
Figure6-1 depicts tank and pumps in the opposite page-left/right order to E5;
without a common orientation/grid this is a graphical difference, not a proved
geographic contradiction. The separate fill and transfer pump functions also
cannot automatically be merged into E5's generic pump label.

Figure6-3's generic existing-riser labels do not cure the text's express
riser-identity uncertainty. No graphical scaling, rotation, route fitting or
flow calculation was used to force a match. These report schematics may derive
from the same design family; they are not a second historical inspection.

## Effect on the competing interpretations

As **published source-content observations**, these claims are grade A within
the checked pages. Identifying E5's depicted run with the first-floor route is
a **plausible but still conditional inference** (grade C): named endpoints,
project context and enclosure type fit, but an explicit location/segment join
is missing. Actual installation, revision precedence and event-day condition
remain underdetermined (grade D).

The strongest ordinary explanation of the municipal difference remains a
related design/shop-drawing development, distinct segment, or documentation
error. The strongest unresolved technical concern is whether the final installed
assembly and its protections match any of these descriptions. An explicit
same-run revision would narrow that question; a different-run identification
would undermine treating the6/8-inch labels as conflicting specifications for
one component. Neither outcome follows from chronology alone.

This comparison rules out a particular overstatement: these drawings do not
newly demonstrate that NIST's **published OEM chapter assumed entirely bare
piping**. That chapter already includes enclosure protection. It does not show
how a specific fire/structural model represented that protection. Model inputs,
local exposure and consequences would require a separate source-linked test.
Conversely, repetition between a drawing and a report that may use related
design records is not independent proof of installation or effective protection.

No collapse-cause ranking changes. The result does not validate the office-fire
initiation/propagation sequence, establish a diesel fire, or support an intent
finding. It narrows the documentary question and prevents a component-type
or source-independence error in later calculations.
