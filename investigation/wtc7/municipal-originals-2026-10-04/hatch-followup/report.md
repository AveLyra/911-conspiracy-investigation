# Fuel room hatch proposal and inspection memo comparison

October 4, 2026. Research only. The new June 2, 2000 record identifies a
specific **proposed first-floor hatch replacement**, not completed work. The
other new file repeats the previously held March 21 inspection-request memo.
Neither establishes whether the requested automatic closure was installed,
tested or functional by September 2001. This narrows a documentary dependency;
it does not change the collapse-cause assessment.

The June fax also requires the contractor to verify dimensions and use the
existing opening and framing. It directs coordination with Bilco for the
automatic-closing device. These are concrete design/procurement instructions,
not evidence of structural-member removal or an executed operation.

The record affirmatively describes an **existing fire-rated hatch**. That
counts against silently assuming an unprotected opening from the mere fact
that replacement was requested. Conversely, the author's description does
not independently verify the existing assembly's rating, closing behavior,
condition or compliance. Both the described existing protection and the
requested change belong in the evidence account.

The June 2 form date is distinct from its June 21 forwarding overlay. The
former has a 12:01:56 PM form time, the lower fax mark June 2 at 12:20, and
the upper overlay June 21 at 13:57, P.04/04. Its form lists three pages but

verified delivery or completion events. No missing page's content is invented.

## Relationship and remaining uncertainty

The March and June records share the OEM project number, participants and
unusual requested function. That strongly supports treating June as followup
to the same hatch issue, although it does not explicitly cite the March memo.
The June record adds a first-floor location and a specified proposed product
and dimensions; those details must not be retroactively attributed to the
March memo itself.

The two March copies are one underlying assertion family, not independent
corroboration. Different portal identifiers and PDF hashes do not turn a
repeated memo into a second inspection. The catalog's abridged label is not
evidence of an altered operative statement: no additional or missing operative
sentence was identified in the direct comparison. Enclosure coverage differs.

The strongest remaining concern is whether the requested automatic closure
was completed and commissioned. The strongest ordinary alternative is an
ongoing correction/procurement process that was later completed. These records
do not distinguish them. A proposal request in June does not demonstrate
continuous noncompletion from March, much less a defect remaining in 2001.
Nor does the absence of closeout in these selected files prove none exists.
