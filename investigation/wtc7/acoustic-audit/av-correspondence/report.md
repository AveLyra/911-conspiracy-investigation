# Compilation edits and audiovisual timing limits

The selected *27 Angles* passages contain three abrupt changes of shot. One replaces a close building view with a dust-plume view without supplying a continuously observable descent across that transition. In the MSNBC passage, foreground turning, camera reframing and background visibility change at different times. The earlier machine-transcript marker is not a measured onset of any one of those events. These findings narrow which audio-to-collapse claims this access copy can support; they do not identify the collapse mechanism.[^1]

The comparator also needs a timing correction: local façade-edge changes are already visible in samples before 13 seconds. The more conspicuous multi-level plumes near 13 seconds cannot be labeled the first visible event. Neither their appearance nor the accompanying digital recording has been used here to identify a charge or its acoustic arrival time.[^2]

This is bounded, retrospective research under WP2/Q07/Q10, not an expert audiovisual-forensics report. Observations refer to preserved uploaded tracks, not authenticated original camera clocks. The declared [protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [single refinement selection](REFINEMENT.md), [verification](validation.md) and [separate visual review](independent-review.md) specify coverage and qualifications.

## Audio at the observed edit coordinates

The audio calculation uses the already full-decoded, stereo 44,100 Hz derivatives from the parent audit (historical local reference `../report.md`; not included here), without input-side seeking or gain adjustment. Each visual first-new-frame PTS maps exactly to an integer sample in those derivatives. That is a **shared uploaded-track coordinate convention**, not an independently verified original A/V alignment.[^4]

For each of the three visual cuts, the declared calculation measures both channels over 0.1 seconds immediately before and after the boundary (4,410 sample frames per window), and over twenty 0.02-second bins spanning ±0.2 seconds (882 frames per bin). RMS includes any DC component; peak is the maximum absolute retained digital sample. The [complete result](audio01/measurements.json) preserves exact sample indices, rational time boundaries, linear amplitudes and dBFS. Zero-valued windows have null logarithms in JSON, not an invented finite floor.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 435 s | edited: 220500 | −34.792 / −34.792 | −22.427 / −25.859 |
| 6628/15 s (441.866667) | edited: 523320 | −20.872 / −24.136 | −22.163 / −24.496 |
| 17423/30 s (580.766667) | msnbc: 122010 | All samples zero in both channels | −22.532 / −22.532 |

The 435-second cut has higher digital RMS in its following window. The 441.866667-second cut does not have the same pattern: its following window has lower RMS. Before the MSNBC cut, the particular 0.1-second derivative window is digital silence; its following window is not. **A visual cut need not coincide with an audio level jump, and a level jump does not identify an explosion.** Silence in an edited access-copy window also cannot establish silence at the original scene. These contrasts discourage treating waveform changes alone as physical-event clocks.

No actual sounds were listened to in this pass. There is no fresh word-onset annotation, acoustic-event classifier, microphone calibration, propagation correction, source attribution or opportunity-to-detect calculation. The earlier transcript labels are preserved for navigation, not silently moved to these visual or sample coordinates. In particular, no physically calibrated “20 seconds before collapse” interval follows from this work.

## Consequences for the competing explanations

These results address recording continuity and claim precision, not the fire-to-failure chain. They do not explain rapid support loss, validate NIST's detailed sequence, or demonstrate deliberate removal. They also do not validate the earlier broad negative claim that a characteristic explosive pattern was absent across independently sourced WTC 7 recordings. Such a claim needs genuine listening, source-family authentication, synchronized original recordings and justified detection limits, not merely these repeatable digital metrics.

No overall cause ranking changes from this bounded audit. The consolidated assessment (historical local reference `../../../wtc7-collapse-evaluation-synthesis.md`; not included here) retains two distinct scopes: conditional fire-first weight when disputed published case-specific engineering analyses are included, and **no defensible strict ordering** from the independently checked subset alone. The latter is not a numerical tie. The present correction removes unjustified precision from some audiovisual support; it does not supply affirmative evidence for any initiating mechanism or for intentional editing to conceal evidence.

## Highest-value remaining tests

1. Reconcile the exact between-cut material with its highest-generation continuous source and edit/source identity records. Similar-looking footage or a shared attribution is insufficient to recover chronology or soundtrack custody.
2. Obtain a genuinely reviewed sound annotation on the preserved channels, with candidate intervals separated from speech, vehicles, edit artifacts and indeterminate sounds. This is a prerequisite to stronger event-source or nondetection claims, not a request to classify every transient as a blast.
3. Use authenticated recording locations and several shared events to test original A/V alignment and inter-camera timing with uncertainty. Do not align clips to force agreement with a favored collapse sequence.
4. Resume calibrated multi-feature motion constraints where the existing footage and review gates permit them. Foreground reactions and dust arrival cannot replace tracks of identified structural features. The first-visibility limit in this one comparator does not justify indefinite comparator expansion.
