## Controls from the rest of the spreadsheet

All 13 spreadsheet pages were visually read, not only the WTC7 entries. They demonstrate why a common-looking letter or descriptive phrase is not a specimen join:

- Physical 22 / summary 8, numbered items 98–100, describes **KB coupons A, B, C** from tower components. These are not the CM items A/W14A and B/W14B. Physical 25–26 / summaries 11–12 uses **FK A through K**, again another namespace. An identifier needs yard, date, object type, and source context.
- Physical 23 / summary 9 explicitly records coupons from K10, K11, K12, K13, K14, K15a, K18 and K19 brought to GMS. Physical 24 / summary 10, item 116/K50, records division into three sections and transfer to GMS. Physical 26–27 / summaries 12–13 records seven GZ connection coupons attributed to WTC5 and brought to GMS. Those affirmative coupon/transfer descriptions do not extend to unrelated neighboring rows.
- Physical 25–26, items 139–149, records FK member and marked-coupon/photo identifiers. A marked coupon location is not itself a completed shipment or laboratory receipt. Nothing inspected connects these FK entries, or the earlier mixed-debris handling audit, to WPI Sample 1.
- Physical 23 / summary 9, item 109/K16, notes thickness variation and asks whether corrosion or fire was involved. That is a field observation/question with a photo/video lead, **not** a WTC7 attribution, a thermite finding, or a Sample 1 identification.
- Physical 15 / summary 1 reports accidental processing of previously marked pieces. That supports a documented preservation problem at the report's scope. It does not establish a specific instruction to destroy demolition evidence. Appendix D physical 2–3 also depicts mixed salvage piles and ordinary cutting operations; neither proves how an unjoined specimen was altered.
