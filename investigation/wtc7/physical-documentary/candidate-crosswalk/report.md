# WTC steel candidates: FEMA and NIST identifier crosswalk

This standalone summary replaces a previously linked, unavailable detailed audit. It compares published inventory entries; it does not recover original photographs, prove physical-item continuity, determine the origin of either piece, or establish a collapse mechanism.

## What the entries say

The spreadsheet in FEMA 403 Appendix D, PDF page 16, lists item 20 as **A or W14A** and item 21 as **B or W14B**, and describes both in its remarks as WTC 7 candidates. The same page lists C12a and its attached component C12b separately in the remarks for item 24. The spreadsheet’s “Trip Date” is not, by itself, a shipment, intake or recovery date. The [historical FEMA Appendix D link](https://www.fema.gov/pdf/library/fema403_apd.pdf#page=16) returned an access error to the checking tool; the page was read from a previously preserved copy, whose hash is given below. FEMA’s [accessible Appendix D narrative](https://www.fema.gov/pdf/library/fema403_apd_x.pdf) describes the collection but does not by itself provide the spreadsheet rows.

[NIST NCSTAR 1-3B, Table A-1, printed p. 64 / PDF p. 92, and Table A-7, printed p. 68 / PDF p. 96](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3b.pdf#page=92) lists **W-14A or A** and **W-14B** as heavy wide-flange pieces in the unidentified category. NIST explains that its inventory records samples received and generally retains earlier engineer-assigned identifiers for consistency with the FEMA report. [NCSTAR 1-3B, §3.1, printed p. 5 / PDF p. 33](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3b.pdf#page=33).

These matching identifiers support a documentary connection between the FEMA-attributed candidates and two pieces recorded in NIST’s inventory. They do not supply the original intake photographs or uninterrupted chain of custody, and are not independent physical verification that the two inventory entries describe the identical pieces. NIST stated that none of its steel pieces could be unambiguously identified as originating from WTC 7. [NCSTAR 1-3B executive summary, PDF p. 27](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-3b.pdf#page=27). FEMA’s field attribution and NIST’s later uncertainty are both part of the record; neither proves which building the pieces came from. The reason for NIST’s classification is not established by the cited rows.

The crosswalk does not identify either piece as FEMA’s metallurgical Sample 1, establish the subsequent custody of C12a or C12b, or demonstrate destruction, concealment or intentional interference. The two NIST tables are presentations of the same inventory, not independent custody confirmations.

## Next records

Seek the original photographs and indexes for the FEMA candidate entries; transport and intake records; NIST receiving photographs, identification and attribution worksheets; and any renumbering or coupon records. A documented match of distinctive geometry and identifiers through the chain would strengthen physical continuity; inconsistent images or an authenticated renumbering explanation could weaken the proposed match. Building origin requires its own evidence. The [later public inventory-delivery review](../../../broader-evidence/steel-inventory-delivery-roots-2026-10-28/README.md) identifies additional inventory routes but does not supply candidate-specific attribution.
