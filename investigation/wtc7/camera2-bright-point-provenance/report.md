# Camera2 bright-point source check

2026-09-24. Working research for Q07/Q10, not an expert opinion or completed
investigation. The [bounded critical review](critical-review.md) is complete;
this is not source authentication or human acceptance.

## Result

**The two bright-point appearances remain unidentified.** The documents
inspected in this finite search do not identify the apparent foreground
upright, and the inspected stored source comparison does not supply exact
alternate-frame counterparts for Camera2
indices 6925 or 6970. This is not a finding that counterparts or identifying
records do not exist.

There are useful positive holdings: a related Tilted Camera MP4 and DistantView
AVI/MP4 representations. Their bytes and finite stored coverage are now
reconciled. Different encodings are not independent historical witnesses;
similar scenes do not authenticate an emitter or a specific exposure.

One incidental discrepancy has been resolved: two preserved PNGs labelled as
Camera2 frame 6925 have different encoded hashes but **identical complete
decoded pixels**. Independent mechanical checks confirm this. That is not a
change in the bright point's appearance or evidence of tampering, and it does
not explain the light's physical origin.

No new image was displayed, video decoded or physical quantity measured. The
only new mechanical pixel decoding was the prospectively declared two-PNG
integrity exception. The preceding light-screen observations remain unchanged.

## What the documents say about the scene

The analysis paper calls Camera2 CBS footage. A preserved third-party index
instead calls this the NBC Leaning Cam and places the camera at 30 Rockefeller
Center. The lab calls the similar tilted view an unmanned spare camera. These
are attributed statements, not an authenticated resolution of operator or site.
NIST's table describes a high midtown camera north of WTC7; that broad location
does not settle the more specific attribution or identify the foreground object.

The [scene locator](scene-locator.md) searched 30 named working text records,
three exact catalogue/manifest paths and the extracted text of the held paper,
lab instructions and floor sheet. It found no explicit upright identity,
address, light-device type or attachment in that scope. Image-only labels,
other vocabulary, omitted metadata and unsearched records remain outside the
negative result. Root checked the material paper/lab passages and NIST's
camera-table text; no graphic attribution was newly inspected.

The catalogue's `24:49` marker belongs to the leaning-camera entry, not its
separate Window Shot entry. Its named later BBC segment is not the earlier
BBC access file already held. The restored index repeats the old lineage,
so it cannot be counted as an independent source. Existing failed DVD-copy
requests remain historical attempts; none was rerun or converted into an
absence or concealment finding.

## Integrity anomaly: tested rather than interpreted

The inventory's initial assumption of cross-copy PNG-byte equality failed.
Both files were preserved. Under the [declared exception](INTEGRITY-ADDENDUM.md),
root checked their own receipt chains, complete chunks/CRCs, filtered rasters
and Pillow-decoded pixels. A separate checker reconstructed every pixel using
PNG filtering logic without Pillow, with synthetic filter and CRC-tamper
controls. Both produce the same 307,200 grayscale bytes.

The seven encoded differences comprise three IDAT payload bytes and four CRC
bytes. This establishes different encodings of the same decoded image under
the checked paths, not why the encoders emitted different bytes. See the
[root check](integrity-check.md) and [separate replay](integrity-review.md).
No source file, original pin or prior annotation was replaced.

| Different file hashes prove different historical observations | Unsupported inference, E | Distinct captures require provenance, not a changed container encoding |
| This source check establishes or excludes deliberate support removal | Unsupported by this unit, E | A specified mechanism prediction plus authenticated observations and justified detection opportunity |

The strongest objection to a negative locator is unsearched or differently
described evidence. The strongest objection to cross-copy corroboration is
shared ancestry: an earlier artifact can survive in every later version.
Conversely, shared ancestry does not make comparisons useless. A documented
upstream/downstream relationship can test a particular processing stage even
without authenticating the original light. The [method review](method-review.md)
sets out that conditional test and the alternative explanations.

## Next useful test and stopping boundary

The finite locator is complete, not the source-authentication problem. A next
target-specific test can use the already-held Tilted clip: declare a
point-independent scene-correspondence search for Camera2 6924–6926 and
6969–6971 against its finite 476-frame domain. Reuse existing safe extraction
contracts, verify every decoded-frame identity, preserve ambiguous neighboring
matches, and freeze selection before comparing the bright points. The existing
eight anchors must not be interpolated into an allegedly observed exact join.
This proposal has not been run; new method, synthetic controls, representation
checks, independent review and applicable human gates remain required.

Without a demonstrated processing branch, its maximum outcome would be
cross-representation appearance agreement/disagreement, not exclusion of an
upstream artifact. A missing point in a poorer or mismatched representation
would not establish physical absence. The distinct documentary route is the
specific clip/DVD/broadcast lineage and authenticated scene identification;
this local unit neither acquires those sources nor abandons them.
