## Bounded prospective arithmetic protocol

Acceptance is a complete, independently checked computation from the printed inputs with explicit method limits. A mismatch, ambiguity, or inability to identify the historical method is a valid result. Do not choose a window, rounding tolerance, or derivative after seeing which one best matches the authors.

1. **Freeze transcription and provenance.** Use all Camera 2 rows for t, reference x/y and the four roofline y/v pairs; preserve all blanks and literal decimal strings. Keep any additional source columns clearly separate. For west use all ten columns and all 25 rows. Resolve disagreements against full-page images before arithmetic; retain both original transcriptions and the reconciliation. Schema/range checks cannot replace visual checking.

2. **Separate direct published-velocity fitting from position reconstruction.** First fit the printed v values to `v = a*t + b` by unweighted least squares with a free intercept and nominal printed t, reporting signed `a`, downward magnitude `−a`, `b`, sample times/count, residuals, RMSE and descriptive R². This is a fully specified reconstruction choice, not a recovered Tracker configuration. Do not force zero velocity at the onset label. Do not fit a line through a screenshot or assume the red line’s drawing extent is its data membership.

3. **Freeze a finite window set.** For NE use the annotated 8.0 s start and last printed velocity 9.2 s as the base window. For EC/WC/NW use two co-primary candidate windows, 8.2–10.6 s and 8.2–10.8 s, which bracket the earlier approximate 2.5-second description. Label both as analyst choices. A compact sensitivity set is the Cartesian product of starts `{7.8, 8.0, 8.2}` and ends `{8.8, 9.0, 9.2}` for NE; starts `{8.0, 8.2, 8.4}` and ends `{10.4, 10.6, 10.8, 11.0}` for each other track. Include both endpoints and only nonblank velocity values. Report every member (9 NE plus 12 each for EC/WC/NW), not merely the closest slope. Add one onset-to-last-printed-velocity fit per track as an explicitly extended-window contrast; keep duplicate windows identified. A later expansion is exploratory and must retain this set.

4. **Distinguish printed rounding from measurement uncertainty.** Each two-decimal v has a nominal rounding interval ±0.005 m/s, under a stated rounding-to-nearest assumption. For a fixed grid and free-intercept linear fit, calculate a worst-case slope perturbation from linear slope weights: `0.005 * sum(abs((t−mean(t))/sum((t−mean(t))²)))`. Compare this with each author magnitude’s own two-decimal rounding interval ±0.005 m/s². Record exact printed-value mismatch and interval compatibility separately. Do not use this narrow printing bound as annotation, metric-calibration, timing, or physical uncertainty. Do not retrofit a broad percentage tolerance solely to obtain a pass.

5. **Test a differentiation hypothesis.** For every interior row with both neighboring positions, compare printed v to `(y[i+1]−y[i−1]) / 0.4`. Do not synthesize values where support is missing, including the first NW velocity. Under independent nearest-hundredth rounding of the two positions and velocity and exact nominal spacing, residual magnitude up to 0.030 m/s can arise solely from printing. Record full residuals and failures. Compatibility supports consistency with this stencil, not proof of its use or the absence of smoothing. Any alternate stencil/filter is a separately declared sensitivity, not a repair of the source.

6. **Position-first cross-check.** On the same declared time windows fit printed y to a free quadratic in centered t, with all available position rows in that interval, and report `2*c2`, residuals and sample membership. Optionally add an explicitly labeled expanded-support quadratic over one adjacent row on each side to reflect the centered derivative’s support. These are distinct estimands; compare rather than force equality. A velocity sample near onset can use a future/past position and smear a sub-step transition. Overlapping finite differences also produce correlated velocity errors; ordinary iid regression standard errors would not automatically provide a valid physical confidence interval.

7. **Camera 2 reference sensitivity.** Preserve the published columns as primary inputs. Quantify the reference y drift/noise and an explicitly hypothetical subtraction branch `y_target−y_ref`, with the same windows and derivative rule, if needed to test common camera motion. State that this correction may be inappropriate if the published coordinates already contain it. Reference x supplies a separate stability check, not a y correction. A single stationary point cannot remove changing zoom, camera rotation, target/reference depth-dependent parallax, or local facade deformation.

8. **West table arithmetic.** For each target/row compute `relative_y−(raw_y−reference_y)` and report literal residuals. A nearest-hundredth rounding bound is ±0.015 m for these three printed quantities. Next test whether the center and SW `adjusted_y−relative_y` differences each admit one constant offset: intersect every row’s `[difference−0.010, difference+0.010]` interval. Display all per-row offsets/residuals and the common-offset interval or empty intersection. Visually the center differences are around 4.32/4.33 m and SW around 8.23/8.24 m; do not substitute these impressions for complete arithmetic. Per-row rounding compatibility is weaker than one globally consistent hidden-precision table; if joint consistency is claimed, enforce common reference/raw/relative variables and one offset per target in a documented feasibility check.

9. **Western clock and scope.** The printed 0.03/0.04 s increments are compatible with a rounded `8+n/30` grid, but the table does not authenticate 30 fps. Since this unit audits position transformations, do not add acceleration fits to the west table. Report onset labels as the paper’s assignments. A horizontal baseline adjustment and NW-based synchronization cannot independently establish exact simultaneity, rigidity, or a physical free-fall acceleration.

10. **Report three distinct outcomes.** (a) transcription and arithmetic verified or failed; (b) a declared method’s numerical compatibility with the published coefficients; (c) original measurement/method identification unresolved. No arithmetic result in this unit identifies global center-of-mass acceleration, surviving support forces, an initiation mechanism, intentional intervention, deception, or legal facts.

## Strongest contrary tests and evidence ceiling

- **Window dependence is the main challenge to an overconfident reproduction.** If reasonable declared endpoints materially shift acceleration, reporting one near-match conceals model-choice uncertainty. Conversely, agreement across the full finite window set would support robustness of this printed-data result within those choices, even while the original endpoint selection remains unknown.
- **Position/velocity disagreement can reveal preprocessing dependence.** A failed centered-difference test outside printing bounds would falsify that simple reconstruction under nominal timing; it would not automatically prove bad source measurements. Alternative timing, hidden preprocessing, source rounding rules, or a transcription error must remain possible until checked. A pass does not establish the absence of smoothing.
- **The four north-face points share a source, clock, scale and possible camera error.** Four similar slopes provide spatial information but are not four independent calibrations or independent event replications. The NE record is substantially shorter and smoke-limited in the source discussion on p. 10.
- **West agreement has two built-in dependencies.** The common-height transform removes baseline differences by construction, and the NW descent supplies the absolute timing anchor. Their compatibility is useful for comparing relative motion within this footage; it does not independently authenticate metric acceleration or the absolute cross-camera clock.
- **Printed onset labels do not establish a zero-duration physical transition.** Page 15 expressly gives 0.2-second tracking intervals and page 16 invokes limited measurement resolution. To distinguish a sharp transition from a shorter-than-resolution gradual change requires a declared onset model and original-frame localization/timing uncertainty, not only the printed labels or straight-line fits.

The strongest defensible result available here is reproducible arithmetic on the authors’ printed tables with source-pinned method gaps. A near-gravity result can be quantitatively meaningful for the assigned scale and chosen interval without establishing a whole-building force balance or unique cause. The source’s broader p. 38 conclusion is an interpretation; this unit neither adopts nor purports to test it.
