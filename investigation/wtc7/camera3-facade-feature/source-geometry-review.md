## Result

The available public report provides a **substantive architectural join**:
the conspicuous central dark upper-north-facade band is identified by NIST as
a bank of vertical louvers serving cooling towers at mechanical Floors 46
and 47. Its photograph, facade graphic and Camera 3 illustration support
identifying C as the **apparent lower-west corner of that louver bank**. This
is stronger than a generic resemblance or a floor-spacing guess.

It does **not** authenticate a particular blade, frame, fastener, slab edge
or structural node at C. The sources inspected here do not establish the
corner's exact elevation, depth, attachment or continuously tracked material
identity. They therefore do not supply a validated A-C physical distance or
camera projection. The NIST facade graphic is a report derivative assembled
from drawings and images, not an inspected original architectural sheet.

floor, material designation or attachment. This observation concerns one
frame; I did not assess C's temporal persistence or enter coordinates.

## Material, dimension and projection limits

**Material correspondence.** The supported description is an apparent
corner of the louver field in the facade. At this resolution the junction
could localize a blade-bank boundary, surrounding frame/trim, a shadowed
recess edge or a mixed pixel across those surfaces. The architectural
identification makes a building feature more likely than a transient
unstructured patch, but does not settle which surface supplies each edge
as the viewpoint, lighting, deformation or visibility changes. A repeated
contrast corner is not automatically one persistent material point.

**Dimensions available, but not a C scale.** Figure 5-13 provides a useful
Floor 46 neighborhood. It does not specify that the observable's lower
contrast edge coincides exactly with that slab elevation. The report's
typical-floor 30-in slab-to-window-bottom offset cannot be imported into
the nonstandard mechanical-floor louver detail. Likewise, the lower-floor
aluminum-louver descriptions encountered in text search cannot establish
the material or dimensions of these upper louvers.

For a concrete *conditional datum comparison only*, combining NIST's
parapet value and the kit's Floor 46 value gives
`925 ft 4 in - 893.750 ft = 31 ft 7 in = 9.6266 m`.
This arithmetic was checked exactly with rational numbers. It is not a
measured C elevation or A-C length: it assumes compatible datums, exact
corner/slab correspondence, and an A point at the relevant parapet height,
none of which this pass establishes. The vertical datum difference is also
not the spatial distance between two horizontally separated features.
It must not become a new fitted metric scale through this note.

**Projection.** The public map fixes a useful view region, not camera
intrinsics, height, exact coordinates, aim, lens distortion or a current
frame transformation. Even points initially on a common facade plane can
change their image separation under perspective, rotation, camera motion
or plane motion. The louver recess and parapet may also lie at different
depths. Conversely, a constant image separation does not prove structural
rigidity. No pose or homography was solved here; the root's image-coordinate
tests remain tests of projected separation within their own declared scope.
