# Camera 3 louver-bank corner and relative motion

A more distinctive second facade feature can be followed through 20 of the
22 selected images. Its motion is compatible with the roof corner retaining
one constant **two-dimensional image separation**, within both observers'
original placement ranges simultaneously. No widening of those ranges is
required. This strengthens the image-correspondence basis for comparing
apparent facade motion; it does not demonstrate exact physical rigidity,
whole-building free fall or the cause of support loss.[^1]

A separate architectural check supplies a useful new identification: the
dark rectangular band corresponds to the north-face louver bank at Floors
46/47, making the tracked junction its apparent lower-west corner. That join
is supported by published photographs, description and a facade graphic,
not a newly inspected original architectural detail. Exact material point,
attachment, depth and metric scale remain unresolved.[^2]

## Defined feature and direct observations

**C** is the intersection of the dark band's lower sloping edge and its
image-right termination, below the upper building rim. It is neither the
band's upper-right corner nor the overall roof corner **A**. Unlike the
previous fixed-column upper-silhouette sample B, C follows a distinctive
local junction in both image coordinates. The feature definition, selected
images, window families, and constant-separation/widening tests were declared
before the new coordinates.[^1]

Two computational observers each viewed all22 native images and all22
existing enlarged crops: frame258 and288..348 in steps of3. The crops repeat
native pixels threefold; they add no resolution. The selected images are
reused public-access-copy derivatives, not new cameras or an unused
historical holdout. Their receipt hashes agree. The existing A annotations
are reused explicitly, not counted as fresh observations.[^3]

Both observers localized C in frames258 and288..342, and independently
rejected345/348 because the apparent junction merges with foreground
occlusion. A surviving dark segment is insufficient to establish that its
original corner is still visible. All20 paired C boxes overlap in both
coordinates. The central estimates differ by at most2 native pixels in x
and1 in y. The boxes remain subjective judgments of blurred-edge placement,
not calibrated confidence intervals or independently measured error rates.

The separate observer froze C before reading root C, earlier A/B positions,
fits or architectural conclusions. Root knew earlier A/B work and received
non-numerical architectural leads during the later image pass; these limits
on independence are retained in the frozen notes. Neither observer is a
human annotator or a licensed structural reviewer.[^3]

## Constant-separation test

For one axis at one frame, the allowed difference A-C is
[A_low-C_high, A_high-C_low]. Intersect these ranges across every readable
sample. In the combined scenario, first intersect the two observers' A
boxes and their C boxes at each frame. This requires one set of positions
to meet both observers' original constraints; it does not average their
coordinates or alter an annotation.[^4]

All six declared scenarios (each observer and both together, over all22 or
late21 selected samples) are feasible without additional box widening.
The combined results are:

| Coverage | Readable / selected | Constant A-C x interval, px | Constant A-C y interval, px |
|---|---:|---|---|
| All selected, including258 | 20 / 22 | [92,95] | [-47,-44] |
| Late only,288..348 | 19 / 21 | [92,95] | [-48,-44] |

For example, a separation of **(93.5,-45.5) native pixels** has a constructed
position sequence inside both observers' A and C boxes at all20 readable
samples. The witness is a calculation showing feasibility, not another
measurement or a physical reconstruction. Its positions are not asserted
to be the most likely ones. All scenarios omit345/348, and the gap between
258 and288 remains unsampled here; no continuous trajectory is supplied.

This does not prove a rigid facade. Perspective, rotation, different depths,
camera motion, deformation and changing contrast boundaries remain possible.
Conversely, failure of a constant image vector would not alone disprove
physical rigidity under a projective camera. The result specifically shows
that these observations do not require differential motion of A and C
beyond their placement ranges.[^4]

## Architectural evidence and limits

NCSTAR1-9 printed p101 (PDF145) identifies the central dark upper-north-face
area as vertical louvers serving cooling towers at mechanical Floors46/47.
The credited morning photograph and flanking panel arrangement support
the same architectural-region association. Figure5-13, printed p105
(PDF149), depicts that field across the two floors, with its bottom near
the Floor46 slab guide. The accompanying pp103-104 text says the graphic
was made using design drawings and images. It is not itself an original
dimensioned louver-detail sheet.[^2]

AppendixC identifies Camera3's view and the image-right outer edge as the
northwest corner. Under that orientation C is the lower-west louver-bank
corner. NIST's p600 reference at the parapet aligned with the **east** edge
of the louvers is a different feature. Its stated elevation cannot simply
be transferred to C. These are publication-backed source joins, not an
independent verification of every architectural statement or of NIST's
collapse model. The image-coordinate calculations use no NIST target track.

The report's p100 curtain-wall description distinguishes cladding/supports
from the exterior steel frame. The visible C junction could involve trim,
a louver boundary, a recess/shadow edge or mixed pixels. Neighboring
wall-backed glazing is another relevant distinction. The original
dimensioned north elevation and lower-west louver detail would be needed
to establish trim-to-slab offset, recess depth and attachment. Without that
join, this feature cannot supply a verified physical acceleration, steel
node displacement or reaction force.[^2]

## Claim assessment and implications

| Claim | Layer / strength | Discriminator or limiting evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C is consistently localizable in20 selected images. | Observation; B for shared-image correspondence. | Both readings agree on coverage; a different visible junction or higher-quality source could change placement/identity. |
| C has pronounced late downward curvature. | Conditional calculation; B for image motion, C for physical interpretation. | Clock, material correspondence and projection are not newly validated; windows differ. |
| A and C require different physical accelerations. | D, underdetermined. | One constant image vector satisfies both observers' original boxes; better geometry/material tracks could discriminate. |
| C corresponds to the lower-west louver-bank region. | Source-informed identification; B for region, D for exact material/metric point. | Original detail/as-built records and clearer imagery could refine or contradict the join. |
| This establishes simultaneous universal support loss, whole-building free fall or a collapse cause. | Not established by this unit. | Moving mass, force boundaries, hidden structure and the fire-to-failure or removal chain were not measured. |

The previous ambiguity at a fixed-column silhouette is not resolved by
pretending that point was a material landmark. Instead, C supplies a better
specified, architecturally supported comparison feature with an explicit
visibility cutoff. The result adds support for approximately common apparent
motion in the covered facade region; it does not quantify exact physical
symmetry throughout the building or select fire versus deliberate removal.

The next motion-specific record is the original north-elevation/louver-detail
join described above. To move the comprehensive investigation beyond repeated
trajectory checks, the next substantive work package should return to the
newly released model inputs: map actual damage-set elements and heated nodes
to member/part geometry, without executing a solver or equating deletion
lists with historical failures. That work remains subject to its existing
privacy/inspection boundaries and the separate structural-audit protocol.
