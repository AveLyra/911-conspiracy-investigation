the principal rectangular building and raised roof outline, dense smoke to
its left, a narrow bright-bordered vertical strip at the far left, layered
foreground roofs, and the distinctive pair of tall background buildings to
the right. Their sampled smoke arrangements and changing roof/visible-facade
arrangements closely correspond across the sequence.

In the Camera2 samples, the raised outline is visible at 6613 and 6681; it
remains identifiable at 6748/6749, 6817 and 6884 despite changing smoke. At
6952 the upper outline is lower/altered; at 7020 substantially less of the
principal facade remains visible above the foreground; at 7088 that roof is
no longer visible above the foreground and dust/smoke. These are descriptions
of sampled images, not onset times, measured displacements, named structural
failures or an interior-to-exterior causal sequence.

The adjacent candidate images 6748 and 6749 are very similar. Visual inspection
does not resolve which original exposure, if either, a resampled Tilted frame
represents. No unique source-frame choice is claimed from visual inspection.

## Interpretation boundary

Together with the declared arithmetic, these observations support shared
footage/content. They do not authenticate the original camera clock, identify
all editing/transcoding steps, prove exact exposure equivalence, establish the
paper's historical project version, validate the assigned physical scale, or
distinguish fire-triggered from deliberate support removal. Similarity is
expected for an edited/resampled derivative and is not independent causal
corroboration. The displayed Y plane omits color information and cannot support
a fresh fire-temperature or full fire-extent assessment.
