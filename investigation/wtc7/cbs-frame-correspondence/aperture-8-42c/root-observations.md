## Disposition before peer exchange

This reading resolves an important identity risk: the readily seen pale patch
in the isolated corner pair is associated with8-42B, whereas the report's
further-loss claim names8-42C, beyond the wide pier. The close-up includes only
part ofC. The wide candidates locate its neighborhood but do not support a
reliable C-only surface comparison. A source-guided same-neighborhood join is
possible; a resolved same-aperture glass-loss transition is not established.

The observed appearances are compatible with a damaged/open-window reading,
but dark areas and ragged contrast alone do not distinguish glass from opaque
material, interior/background or reflection. No material was observed detaching
in this still-image selection. The completed counter study also leaves filming
order unresolved: a close/wide difference cannot by itself validate the claimed
one-minute direction. Do not claim loss atB instead, or infer that NIST'sC
assertion is false because it cannot be confirmed here.

The strongest objection to this limited result is that better original imagery,
the complete sequence, or NIST's original condition worksheet may support the
specificC observation more strongly than these access copies. Conversely,
authenticated chronology or a well-resolved original same-aperture comparison
could contradict the asserted loss or ordering. No heating, ventilation-input
mismatch, structural consequence, intent or cause ranking is derived here.

reader's separately recorded source/geometry interpretation, not human approval,
