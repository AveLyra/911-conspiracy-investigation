## Result: a usable qualitative join is recovered

The response diagram's upper-right region can be related, with strong
source-supported qualitative confidence, to the building's northeast
corner: the eastern end of the north face and the northern end of the east
face. In the published architectural namespace, the corner is Column 42.
On the north elevation this is the **left** end of the displayed facade;
on the east elevation it is the **right** end. The plan's right-hand corner
must therefore not be copied untransformed to the right side of both
elevation graphics.

This result is more specific than saying that some unidentified northeast
region might be involved. It establishes the shared corner, the two adjacent
faces and the direction of their architectural labels. It does not establish
the exact endpoint of the predicted window-loss region, a response-station
to individual-pane lookup, or historical failure of any pane.

### 1. Cardinal orientation is supplied by source prose and geometry

NCSTAR 1-9 printed 98 expressly describes the north side as the long side
of the trapezoidal upper building, identifies the rectangular core as offset
toward the west, and states that perimeter numbering begins at the northwest
corner and progresses counterclockwise. Figure 5-7 places Column 1 at the
upper-left corner, Column 42 at the upper-right, Column 15 at the lower-left
and Column 28 at the lower-right. It shows the core toward the left with the
additional eastern interior supports to its right.

Those mutually consistent landmarks support the displayed plan orientation:
top is north, right is east, bottom is south, left is west. This is not an
assumption that north must be up in every drawing. It follows from the
explicit source prose joined to this particular labeled plan. The asymmetric
core offset supplies a useful reflection check; the long-versus-short
perimeter sides supply a north/south check. Printed 98 describes Figure 5-7
as locating columns for Floor 8 and above, so Floor 12 is within its stated
applicability.

Printed 14 Figure 2-3 supplies compatible framing and core geometry and is
captioned for Floors 8 through 45. Its prose is narrower in describing
typical framing for Floors 8-20 and 24-45. Floor 12 is included in both;
this small distinction must not be converted into a claim that every
intervening floor had identical framing. The plan attributes its basis to
Cantor 1985 structural drawings. Figure 5-7 separately cites McAllister
2002. These are published source attributions, not independent inspection
of those underlying originals.

### 2. The partitioned layout has a real public counterpart, with a ceiling

Printed 56 labels Figure 3-7 as a Floor 12 schematic and attributes it to a
NIST drawing derived from material supplied by the U.S. Securities and
Exchange Commission. Footnote 7 identifies the Commission as the floor-plan
source. The surrounding prose says Floors 11 and 12 had similar layouts and
that one plan was used for their fire simulations. It describes perimeter
offices, interior corridors and another band of offices outside the core.
This is positive public evidence for a heavily partitioned Floor 12-type
layout, not merely an assumed empty typical floor.

The response figure and the Floor 12 schematic display a compatible
trapezoidal footprint, an off-center core with elevator/service spaces and
partitioned perimeter/interior office bands. Those features support the same
coarse orientation as Figures 2-3 and 5-7. This is a visual qualitative
comparison, not an overlay, dimension check or proof of mesh identity.

The decisive qualification remains in NCSTAR 1A printed 27: the response
figure is described as a highly partitioned tenant floor, with Floor 12 as
an example. That language is not the same as an explicit statement that
Figure 3-1 reproduces the exact historical Floor 12 layout. Nor does the
technical report's shared-plan statement for the **fire simulations** prove
that the separate response calculation used an identical geometry file.
The present pages do not establish that model-input identity. Consequently
the defensible label is a **Floor 12-like, highly partitioned illustrated
layout**, not a newly verified as-built Floor 12 reconstruction.

This qualification does not nullify the recovered corner/face orientation.
It limits transfer of a particular predicted pattern between floors and
layouts. An observation on another floor cannot silently be substituted for
the illustrated floor context. The source also states that a less partitioned
floor produced more extensive breakage; this is a model claim on the summary
page, not a result reproduced here.
