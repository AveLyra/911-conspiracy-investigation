### Strongest favorable and adverse readings

Favorable: this is a physical outward-effect prediction for a specified model
family, not an argument solely from whether a collapse looks like demolition.
If surviving imagery resolves the relevant glazing and would reveal that
predicted pattern, its absence can genuinely weigh against the corresponding
scenario. Laboratory-perfect cameras are not required before any qualitative
negative evidence can have weight.

Adverse: the reported photographic limit does not by itself cover the same
place and time as a hypothesized lower northeast event near collapse. The
report acknowledges the obstruction. Without a same-domain observation and
adequate resolvability, nonobservation cannot be converted to intact glazing
or a demonstrated failure of the prediction. These three summary pages also
leave the pattern's pane-level extent unspecified, preventing a quantitative
mask-agreement claim here.

The strongest objection to overusing the adverse reading is that a conspicuous
multi-window effect might remain observable in some suitable scene even when
other regions are hidden. Coverage incompleteness alone does not establish
that the proposed effect would be invisible everywhere. The actual scene and
event opportunity must be tested, not presumed in either direction.

### What an actual matched observation would need

An eligible test must join the same north/east facade region near the named
structural location to visible, identifiable windows; distinguish the earlier
approximately4p.m. domain from the above-Floor25 collapse-time domain; retain
pre-existing damage and ordinary fire/impact/occlusion alternatives; and have
source-linked time and view coverage capable of detecting the stated pattern.
A before/after pair or continuous sequence is particularly useful for new
failure, but a reliably timed clear post-state could itself constrain a
specified persistent damage pattern. Do not impose a universal before-frame
requirement unrelated to the precise hypothesis being tested.

Dark panes, smoke, reflection, disappearing structure or a facade/floor tag
alone do not establish failed glazing. Another roof-corner track or a bright
tip candidate is not an independent window-state observation. A clock derived
from changing fire/window patterns cannot independently validate the same
timing assumption without disclosure of that dependence. Consequential new
classification requires the separately declared human/method controls.
