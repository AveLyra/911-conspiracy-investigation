The observational test needs a defined predicted visible damage region and a
record showing its relevant state with adequate visibility and temporal meaning.
At least one clearly observed, predicted affected region can be informative;
seeing both entire faces or every pane is not a universal prerequisite. The
strength of a nondetection depends on the predicted effect, its persistence,
initial glazing condition and the actual image's ability to distinguish it.
For a newly occurring transition, before/after information is normally needed;
for persistent damage, a sufficiently informative post-state can suffice.

This unit does not validate the window-response model, its damage threshold,
initial glazing state or downstream acoustic calculation. It also does not
assign a probability of detection, mechanism or intent.
