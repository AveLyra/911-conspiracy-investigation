## What changed and what could falsify the assessment

1. **Directly established source fact:** a published response diagram and a
   defined window-state coding method exist. The earlier missing-output task is
   resolved at the published-figure level, not at the executable-model level.
   Correct source edition/page attribution or contrary primary content could
   change this finding; the held source and page labels are pinned below.
2. **Remaining inference limit:** the inspected citation is chapter-level, not
   a recovered record of the precise image regions and times compared with the
   predicted regions. A located worksheet/source-image/model-region join would
   materially improve reproducibility and could strengthen or undermine NIST's
   observation claim. This finite search does not show that such a record never
   existed, was withheld, was destroyed or is absent from all published pages.
3. **Important semantic restriction:** time-integrated fire-or-breakage maps
   cannot alone establish intact post-event glazing. That is a property of the
   stated map definitions, not an allegation that NIST itself used them wrongly.
   Time-specific window-state sheets, correctly matched imagery or a suitable
   resolved post-state could supply the missing observation.

The strongest objection to overemphasizing the gap is that an extensive,
conspicuous, persistent damage pattern can sometimes be tested qualitatively
without a pane-by-pane numerical mask, native model files or continuous video.
The output figure and qualified observation statement therefore retain
conditional evidentiary value. The strongest objection to treating the visual
argument as a universal exclusion remains its time/region coverage: early
views and later upper floors cannot automatically establish a later lower-
corner state. Neither objection, alone, identifies a historical mechanism.
