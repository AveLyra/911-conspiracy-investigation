questions, but do not close the fire-to-global-failure chain or establish an
executed support-removal mechanism. The most important changes are actual
municipal design, approval and commissioning records; concrete but incomplete

## Competing explanations after the additions

**Physical compatibility remains distinct from historical identification.**
Rapid widespread loss of effective restraint is compatible with deliberate
support removal and with some nonlinear progressive-collapse pathways. That
statement does not show either pathway occurred. The unresolved question is
whether a specified initiating process actually produces the required timed
loss of surviving capacity under defensible building-specific conditions.

**NIST's exact sequence:** visible fire, early interior-related motion,
developed released structural work and narrower physical tests provide genuine
support. The chain above is nevertheless not independently closed. New model
anchors reduce a blanket claim of uninspectability, but do not verify the
critical thermal, connection, handoff and propagation steps. No robust overall
upgrade or downgrade follows from A01–A11.

**Other fire pathways:** not automatically falsified by a defect in NIST's
specific sequence, but not rescued by an unspecified possibility either.
OEM system evidence can inform a defined fuel/control hypothesis only after
system, time, route, release, ventilation and structural-exposure joins.
Ordinary office fires and fuel-system scenarios must not be merged.

**Deliberate support removal:** remains mechanically compatible at a broad
level. The terminal motion is not unique to a device or an operation. A01
narrows one proposed transient appearance; it does not exclude all flashes.
The retained conditional acoustic evidence weighs against studied conspicuous
loud scenarios, not automatically against quiet thermal/mechanical methods.
Those alternatives still need case-specific mechanism evidence; freedom to
prescribe any support-removal pattern is not demonstrated explanatory success.
Identifying a perpetrator is not a prerequisite to weighing physical evidence.

**Other, mixed or unresolved possibilities:** remain open but receive no extra
credit simply by accommodating every observation. Each useful proposal needs
specific predictions that could fail. No numerical probabilities or 50–50
conclusion are supported by this reconciliation.

The strongest objection to withholding a total ranking is cumulative rather
than newly discovered: actual fires, early interior-related motion, developed
mechanics and empirical component support can rationally support a weak
provisional fire preference. This report does not claim agnosticism is forced.
The objection to making that preference strong is the unverified historical
link from exposure to timed global loss of the surviving load paths. The
relative evidential weights are not measured here. The retained assessment is
therefore a partial comparison, not a new claim that demolition is likelier or
that official correctness is established.
