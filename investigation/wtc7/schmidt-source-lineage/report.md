## What the source check adds

Both separately frozen readers inspected six complete report pages and four
native report images. Figure 5-30 (physical 167, printed 123) and Figure 5-127
(physical 256, printed 212) have matching vehicles, visible people, street
features and irregular fire/smoke appearance, not just a shared viewpoint.
They are strongly consistent with two processed versions of one exposure.
The first has fewer native pixels: 378x504 versus 477x636. No higher-detail
original or independently established additional exposure was obtained;
provisionally do not count these reproductions as separate historical samples
or corroboration. Fewer pixels alone do not prove less usable detail.
A near-static sequence
or undocumented shared derivative remains an alternative; no exact camera-file
identity or image-registration proof was obtained.

The report's physical 164-168 (printed 120-124) describe three worksheets per
face/time: fire, smoke and window condition. They explicitly retain unobservable
or indeterminate conditions. The published window key has open/broken-out,
intact and unobservable categories, without a quantitative partial-opening
field or an explicit coding rule for retained portions of glass. This does
not establish that no fuller original observation existed.

The combined facade map is **not a display of the complete window worksheet**.
Its stated rule gives priority to the fire code where fire is present or the
fire value is unassignable; the window code is displayed otherwise. A fire
color can therefore mask a known or unknown glass assignment. That composite
cannot be inverted into a complete window worksheet: fire-present and
fire-unassigned cells conceal the underlying glass code. Eligible no-fire
cells can display the window code under the stated rule.

The panels also use different color keys. In Figure 5-31, yellow means an
unresolved fire condition and red combines fire classes 1-3. In the facade key,
yellow means spot fire and blue means unresolved. Reading one panel through
the other panel's legend would create an analytical error. **There is no
finding that NIST's analysts made that error.** These are published display
choices, not independently verified historical window or thermal measurements.
