## Result

The images do not support calling the pictured fires uniformly faint or
inconsequential-looking. Two separately frozen AI readings identify flame-like
forms in nine of the 25 newly reviewed report images; three additional images
receive that label from only one reader. These are image-judgment counts, not
independent fires, burning-floor counts, detection accuracy or heat measurements.

The stronger claim that interior heating was mild is **not established by
these photographs**. Neither is its opposite: the images do not establish
steel temperatures, loss of connection capacity, or sufficient conditions for
NIST's collapse sequence. Fire visibility and structural sufficiency remain
different questions. This result changes the defensibility of a premise, not
the ranking of collapse mechanisms.

A concrete check is Figures 5-129/130: the report identifies the second as an
intensity-adjusted enlargement of the first exposure. Both readers identify no
luminous feature in the small wide image, but both identify warm luminous
features in the enlargement. They disagree about resolved flame morphology.
This demonstrates representation-limited observation in this pair. It is not
a calibrated enhancement experiment, two independent exposures, or evidence
of deceptive processing. A dark distant view cannot by itself establish low
interior heating.

The most consequential source finding is **dependent timing**. Some assigned
photograph times use fire development or comparisons of fire/window patterns.
Those timestamps cannot independently validate the same assumptions used to
derive them. This is a concrete limitation on corroboration, not proof that
the estimates were false or fabricated.

These are disclosed reconstruction assumptions. Relative dating from physical
scene changes can be reasonable; its existence alone does not establish
misconduct or model failure. But source-derived clocks and window states
cannot be relabeled independent holdout evidence for the same reconstruction.

The source also expressly acknowledges observational limits: west windows
above floor 8 hidden by smoke (physical 263); glow not establishing flashover
(267); lower-plume transport versus local fire unresolved (272); a reflection
alternative for a floor-9 patch (274); and little exterior-image information
about fires remote from walls (286). These qualifications matter in both
directions and are not erased by the report's stronger interpretations
elsewhere.
