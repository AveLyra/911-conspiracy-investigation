# NIST Camera 3: source lineage and measurement-method audit

**2026-09-08. Research-only, bounded source audit.** No new acceleration fit, source-video authentication, causal ranking, expert certification or legal-record promotion. This note attributes methods to NIST's preserved reports; it does not independently reproduce their measurements. The evidence-audit discipline separates that strong attribution from unresolved physical validation.

## Result that changes the next action

NIST identifies an earlier-generation, much longer Camera 3 recording: a VHS recording of a news broadcast imported as 720 x 480 DV NTSC at 29.97 frames/s. Appendix C describes 13,558 images in its approximately 7.5-minute vibration analysis. The present 232-frame, 15 fps access copy (its timing audit is not included in this public copy) is therefore not established as NIST's actual analysis input. Its near-repeat image pattern (its follow-up is not included in this public copy) cannot simply be imputed to NIST's measurement chain. The earlier DV file, broadcast/tape lineage and exact frame correspondence are concrete retrieval targets.

NIST's reported gravity-compatible interval came from a regression on selected velocities calculated from displacement differences. It was **not** merely a second derivative of a fitted displacement curve. Conversely, the raw displacement table, exact landmark pixels, calibration transform and statistical uncertainty needed to reproduce that regression are not supplied in the reviewed method section. These are specific reproducibility gaps, not evidence that no such records exist or that the result was fabricated.

## Reconstruction target: distinguish the two numerical paths

The printed displacement fit is

`z(t) = 379.62 {1 - exp[-(0.18562 t)^3.5126]}` (feet, seconds).

The report describes a general `A {1 - exp[-(t/lambda)^k]}` family and least-squares parameter estimation, selected to have zero displacement, velocity and acceleration at onset. Those initial conditions are fitting assumptions, not three separately measured initial conditions. The printed exponent is compatible with the stated zero initial velocity/acceleration; that is not true for every unrestricted possible exponent in the general family. The fitted function is an empirical smooth representation, not a structural-force law.

Its derivative is drawn in Figure 12-77. A separate path starts with measured displacement samples. The footnote's formula is algebraically

`v_(i+1/2) ≈ (z_(i+1)-z_i)/(t_(i+1)-t_i)`.

This is an adjacent-sample slope assigned at the interval midpoint, described in the report as a central-difference approximation. It is not the three-position stencil centered at `t_i`. Selected midpoint velocities are then fit with `v(t) = -44.773 +32.196 t`. Reproduction should implement the published indexing before testing alternatives; it should not quietly replace it with a different stencil and label the result an exact rerun.

Both numerical paths share the same displacement observations. Their agreement is a method cross-check, not independent historical corroboration. Adjacent velocity differences also share displacement endpoints; any uncertainty calculation must account for that dependence. The late numerical-velocity points visibly sit above the fitted derivative in Figure 12-77/1A Figure 3-15, while the smooth derivative turns downward near the end. No discrepancy size or significance was calculated here. The published plot itself therefore supplies a reason to preserve residuals and test derivative/interval sensitivity instead of treating a close displacement fit as sufficient velocity/acceleration validation. A separate main-analyst check of the printed analytic expression is planned; that is not a reproduction of its original input dataset either.

The text first identifies a near-center tracked point, but the third-stage distance sentence in 1-9 p602 calls its feature the northwest corner; 1A p45 says a north-face corner. It does not explain a point switch, extrapolation or equivalence on these pages. That needs resolution, especially because Chapter 5 pp273-275 describes early nonuniform motion: larger screenwall/eastern movement, little corner movement in a difference image, and view-dependent disappearance of rooftop structures. These descriptions are not a proof of whole-facade rigidity. Nor is loss behind a parapet proof of complete physical descent through the roof; NIST expressly notes that limitation on p275.

## Appendix C is a different measurement

The vibration procedure uses the near-vertical **northwest** edge, image-right in Camera 3. The marker is the image row where fitted grayscale intensities of pixel columns 517 and 519 intersect. Vertical movement of that intensity-intersection marker magnifies small **horizontal** edge motion; it is not vertical physical descent. In pp683-684, the stated conversion is 100 +/-10 vertical marker pixels per horizontal image pixel, north-face width 301 +/-4 horizontal pixels and reported real width 329 ft, yielding approximately 1.1 +/-0.1 ft sideways per 100 marker pixels.

The automated method exports each frame to an image, converts RGB to grayscale in Photoshop, extracts intensities over approximately rows 140-340, fits each column's profile with a third-order least-squares polynomial and finds the intersection using Mathematica. The report acknowledges image defects/outliers and camera movement; p686 attributes long-duration changes probably to the camera. These details are more explicit than the descent coordinate extraction, but grayscale weights, software versions, exact root-selection/rejection procedure, per-frame arrays and camera-reference corrections remain reconstruction dependencies. The Appendix C conversion is **not** a vertical-descent calibration for our roof-corner track.

Preserve textual mismatches without inflating them: p683's calibration paragraphs and Figure C-7's caption sometimes say northeast while the principal text consistently locates the analyzed edge northwest. Figure C-8's caption describes a period before penthouse movement, but its plotted axis spans about -15 to +8 s with penthouse onset at 0 and main collapse near +6.9. These are documented label ambiguities; the plotted axis and associated definitions must travel with any extracted data. They do not independently establish a faulty measurement or intent.

## Clock conventions are not contradictory timestamps by themselves

Section 5.2.3/Table 5-1, pp89-90/PDF133-134, distinguishes database-relative clock labels from broadcast-adjusted times. The report says it added 5 s after comparing four television broadcasts. Thus 5:20:47 p.m. in the relative column and 5:20:52 p.m. in the adjusted column are deliberately different conventions, not a newly discovered five-second inconsistency. The time chain includes calibration of a photographer's timestamps using tower images, matching a WTC 7 photograph to an untimed video frame, then a 6.2 s interval to visible global onset. None of those raw calibration inputs was reproduced here.

Table 5-1's LDEO WTC 7 entry visibly prints an a.m. suffix; surrounding afternoon context differs. Keep the literal source and a notation of the apparent labeling error, not an unmarked corrected field or an inference about tampering. Appendix B seismic timing and the event responsible for its signal are separate tests, not resolved by this camera-method audit.

This audit neither verifies NIST's physical causal explanation nor refutes it. It strengthens the investigation by replacing an unspecified complaint about inaccessible data with particular retrievable records, explicit method choices and disconfirming tests. It also rejects an unsupported shortcut: the known deficiencies of a modern access copy are not evidence that NIST used that deficient copy.
