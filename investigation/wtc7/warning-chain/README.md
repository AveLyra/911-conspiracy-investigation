# WTC 7 warning accounts: recovered source review

This package preserves an independent computational review, its claim ledger and protocol from a 2026 investigation snapshot. The reviewer was given the source selection and questions and was not blind to the originating inquiry. The review distinguishes retrospective first-person accounts, a contemporaneous CNN broadcast transcript and an interview memorandum. It identifies an observation-to-concern-to-withdrawal account while finding that the complete warning chain and the precise time of individual later CNN remarks are unresolved. These records do not establish a deliberate operation.

The included documents are historical research, not a new source acquisition or analysis. The independent review’s public links were changed to current publisher and National Archives pages; byte identity with the previously captured copies was not checked. The three-page Catalano memorandum, source captures and media are not included; the catalog link identifies the item but does not itself display the interview PDF in this package. The ledger’s pointer to a separate historical report is marked unavailable. Each document has a historical-context preface. [Manifest](manifest.json) records original and derivative hashes and snapshot paths. The retained originals were not changed.

As a bounded current check, CNN’s transcript is labeled 16:58 ET and contains the later collapse discussion, but that header alone does not give the wall-clock time of each later utterance. National Archives’ public index lists Catalano item 2609722 as a three-page open memorandum dated January 16, 2004. The Firehouse publisher carries Hayden’s interview. These checks do not authenticate the original MFR or establish the movement of a warning between officials and newsrooms. A time-calibrated broadcast master, contemporaneous command records, and source call or wire logs would help test that missing chain.
