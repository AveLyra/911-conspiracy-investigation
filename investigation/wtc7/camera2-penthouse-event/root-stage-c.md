## Continuity, reappearance and uncertainty

The broad progression from a raised middle/right outline to a short right bump
supports a continuity hypothesis. It is stronger than choosing an isolated
dark pixel. The strongest competing interpretation is that, as the outline
merges, the observer begins following an outer corner or another visible edge
instead of the disappearing assembly. Larger display pixels do not decide
between them. The upper projection and the right-hand step are not silently
treated as interchangeable material markers.

I observe no confident below/behind-parapet state followed by an independently
identified reappearance. That does **not** exclude an earlier crossing and
reappearance: ambiguous merged outlines, sparse earlier context and the
6979–7012 enlarged-display gap prevent a finality claim. Even a three-frame
local crossing would not, by itself, establish final disappearance.

Confidence is moderate in a shrinking raised roof profile and the existence
of the 6958 bump; insufficient in the component-specific occlusion event.
The display preserves censoring. Stage B's root lower enclosure of
24101/2997 seconds (about 8.0417 encoded seconds) remains **only conditional on
the original component and event association**, and concerns final
disappearance after the last associated visibility—not necessarily first
crossing if reappearance occurred. This pass supplies neither a stronger
component-specific bound nor a new finite upper end. It does not independently
validate that old lower bound's physical interpretation.
