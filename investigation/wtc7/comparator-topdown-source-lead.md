## Method classification and claim strength

**Documentary observation:** TD-1 reports project application, and TD-2 later
describes an executed removal. Together they support an actual application,
not merely a proposed technology. **Grade B** for that attributed method/use
claim: source participation and specificity are strong, while underlying
execution and completion records have not been checked.

**Classification inference:** the supported category is controlled top-down,
section-by-section dismantling under an upper enclosure. This is distinguished
from an intentionally initiated whole-building gravity drop, from HBI's
upper-section fall, and from bottom-up removal that lowers the remaining
entire building. Here the roof/enclosure lowering is part of a staged work
process; it must not be described as uncontrolled whole-building descent.
The term floor-by-floor identifies sequential work, not a verified claim that
every operating cycle removed exactly one floor. No particular crushing tool,
cut sequence, jack arrangement/capacity, load-transfer design or rate is
inferred from the pages or from unviewed imagery.

**Permitted narrow comparison:** method diversity. A documented demolition can
remove a high-rise by sequential dismantling; the word demolition therefore
does not by itself identify a common rapid-collapse signature. **Grade D** for
matched WTC 7 motion or causal equivalence: those comparisons were neither
specified nor measured here. No cause ranking or base rate changes.

The strongest objection is that participant publicity may simplify the actual
operation or overstate its performance. Records could reveal mixed methods,
different phases or a narrower application than the accounts suggest. Such
evidence would qualify the method label; a source-label check must precede any
attempt to classify recording segments. The counter-objection also matters:
being an unmatched comparator does not erase the documented existence of a
different removal method.
