## Claims and tests

| Topic | Evidence and implication | Limits and alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Column 79 | NIST's proposed initiation involves loss of floor support and bracing around Column 79, followed by broader progression. The column is on the northeastern edge of the interior array; perimeter columns remain beyond it. | The position alone does not prove or disprove propagation. A roofline point is not automatically the center of mass of a rigid body. [NIST WTC 7 FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation). |
| Orabi transfer-truss route | Orabi et al. analyze a separate possible mechanical-room fire affecting Truss 2 and Columns 77–78. The paper reports approximately 0.8 m of regional floor deflection after two seconds, with bridging resistance from upper floors, and discusses additional failures needed for progression. | This preliminary partial model is not a complete demonstration of global roofline descent. Actual fuel supply, exposure, fire protection and connection behavior require evidence; fixed joints and non-failing studs can overstate retained resistance. [Authors' manuscript](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/92640/1/Orabi_Collapse_World_Trade.pdf), pp. 13–25; details reported in the supplied audit and checked in the prior chat, not rerun in this update. |
| Resistance and free fall | For a defined mass with downward positive and no other unaccounted external forces, ma = mg − R. Significant net upward resistance reduces acceleration below g. | Loss of effective support *before* a later free-fall interval is mechanically possible; whether a specified sequence achieved it is a separate question. Internal connections within a freely moving assembly do not necessarily transmit support to foundations. Test reactions, fracture, load redistribution, energy and multiple observed points with one timebase. |
| Fire exposure and competing models | The supplied audit identifies NIST's objections to certain mechanical-room diesel scenarios, including partitioning, pumping and smoke observations. NIST's and Orabi's sequences have different candidate initiators. | Examine the actual partition, fuel, ventilation and exposure evidence; rejecting one scenario does not exclude all alternatives. [NIST NCSTAR 1-9](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=861611), §9.2; the present update did not independently recheck every cited page. |
| Visual and acoustic observations | East-penthouse movement preceded exterior descent, placing timing constraints on any mechanism. The supplied report describes no pre-collapse sound attributed to WTC 7 in specific distant NIST-reviewed recordings. | Neither observation directly depicts each lower-floor connection or confirms a cascade. The audio statement is reported from the draft section reproduced in [NIST's public comment compilation](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2017/05/09/combined2008publicComments-2.pdf), not a fresh inspection of the final audio section. Test source strength, obstruction, recorder response and clock alignment before inferring audibility or silence. |
| Energy calculation | Korol et al. state a five-floor normal-concrete thickness of 101.6 mm and 32.01 GJ. Using their stated 3,853 m², 2,400 kg/m³ and 4,900 J/kg gives 5 × 3853 × 0.1016 × 2400 × 4900 = **23.018 GJ**. [Published paper, pp. 25–31](https://www.challengejournal.com/index.php/cjsmec/article/download/50/41), especially p. 30. | This is an unresolved input/arithmetic discrepancy, not a determination of the correct effective thickness. Slab geometry and equivalent volume require reconciliation. The supplied audit's conditional recalculation retains the paper's overall inequality; available dissipation is not necessarily unavoidable work on every collapse path. The arrest-floor sensitivity in the supplied audit is a reconstruction, not an author workbook. |
| Numerical settings | The audit proposes checking upper-building mass and boundary representation, frequency-dependent Rayleigh damping, accelerated heating and energy at the thermal-to-dynamic handoff. | Coefficient arithmetic alone does not identify actual nonlinear modal damping or numerical error. Obtain native input decks, run configuration and outputs; compare controlled scenarios without choosing parameters to fit a preferred result. [OpenSees damping documentation](https://opensees.github.io/OpenSeesDocumentation/user/manual/model/damping/rayleigh.html). |

## Consequence for the working theory

These considerations sharpen tests of both fire and deliberate support-removal scenarios. They do not identify an explosive material, installation, actor, UMS job or intelligence operation. A controlled model that fails to reproduce an observation under one setup does not prove all fire-based models impossible; a prescribed failure sequence that matches motion does not identify what caused it. Seek independently supported fire and protection histories, connection and material tests, version-matched model inputs, complete reactions and energy histories, and synchronized multi-camera and acoustic records.
