# WTC 7 investigation — public master lead register

October 25, 2026. Research by AveLyra. **134 overlapping entries: the preserved 131-entry compilation plus three recovered-record additions—not 134 independent errors or evidence items.**

This is a scientific and documentary research register, not an expert report, agency finding or conclusion of conspiracy. Criticisms, qualifications, contrary findings, unresolved disputes and unperformed tests remain separate.

All original IDs and scientific wording are retained. [CORRECTIONS.md](CORRECTIONS.md) adds source-backed qualifications to our compilation without overwriting conflicting evidence. The [earlier edition](AS-COMPILED.md) is retained with citation relocation only.

Source links lead to pinned public studies where available, or to [selected technical excerpts](SOURCE-EXCERPTS.md) with whole-source hashes and line locators. The [inherited bibliography](INHERITED-BIBLIOGRAPHY.md) retains the earlier index section-source links as well. Unpublished machine-specific pointers are identified in [SOURCE-LINKS.md](SOURCE-LINKS.md), not represented as working public sources. Full private case files and raw productions are not included.

<a id="m067"></a>
### M067 — Does the proposed lower-floor fire sequence reproduce the upper-façade free-fall interval?

**Status:** Inherited research observation or question; read its explicit limits. **Family:** Motion and validation.

67. **Does the proposed lower-floor fire sequence reproduce the upper-façade free-fall interval?** NIST identifies the sustained uncontrolled fires on Floors 7–9 and 11–13, including Floor 13, while acknowledging approximately 2.25 seconds of essentially free-fall descent of the north face. This spatial separation makes the propagation and loss-of-support sequence a specific quantitative question. It does not establish a contradiction merely because the descending upper structure was above the principal fire floors.

    **The question to preserve:** Does NIST’s specified lower-floor fire damage and ensuing failure sequence actually produce sufficiently extensive loss of support, with the timing and residual forces necessary to reproduce the observed upper-façade acceleration, coherence, and motion? Relevant tests include matched model displacement/acceleration, member-force and contact histories, the failure chronology, and measurement and model uncertainty. This makes explicit the connection implicit in item 9 and historical inventory item 58.

    **NIST’s explanation and the mechanical qualification:** Upper stories need not themselves be heated to descend near gravitational acceleration if their supports fail below them. NIST attributes the interval to negligible support after lower exterior-column buckling. Compatibility with that explanation in principle does not independently validate its particular historical sequence; describing the process as progressive collapse does not replace the quantitative comparison.

    **Force constraint and observation limit:** For a defined fixed-mass body, sustained center-of-mass acceleration near gravitational acceleration requires a correspondingly small net non-gravitational vertical force. Substantial upward resistance cannot coexist with that motion unless physically justified downward forces offset it. A tracked roof point is not automatically that body’s center of mass. The body, interval, relative motion, projection, and uncertainty must be established or bounded. This limitation must be tested, not used to invent unlimited hidden motion or unexplained cancelling forces. ## Preserved contrary findings

**Earlier criticisms that our own investigation narrowed or rejected**

These qualifications belong with the inventory:

- **“NIST denied free fall” is not an accurate description of its final account.** The final treatment recognizes the approximately 2.25-second interval; the remaining question is its mechanical reproduction, preserved in item 67. The draft-to-final history must not be used to erase the final acknowledgment. Source: [NIST FAQ, question 32](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation).
- **“A36 and A572 were modeled identically” is contradicted for the examined released upper-floor definitions.** The [October 24 material check](SOURCE-EXCERPTS.md#source-grades) compares their complete curves and finds distinct values. The pinned printed-table duplication remains a publication issue, while this result positively supports a publication-error explanation for those definitions. Historical invocation, effective assignments, physical grade validity, and version equivalence are not established; do not retain a claim that these grade-specific definitions are unavailable.
- **“No sensitivity studies existed” is unsupported.** The [structural audit, SC10](SOURCE-LINKS.md#ref-665a18b75415c247) records the 3.5-hour non-collapse control and prescribed-removal cases; the published account also compares debris/no-debris variants. The unresolved issue is adequacy, coverage, and reproducibility, not complete absence.
- **“All temperatures were inflated” is not established.** Repeated coefficients, fire-timing discrepancies, and uncertain inputs do not demonstrate a uniformly upward temperature error. Establish effective solver values, their derivation, and consequences before asserting bias. See items 14–19 and 69–71.
- **“No potentially relevant steel was examined or received” would erase affirmative evidence.** The Sample 1 examination, candidate inventory, and NIST receipt findings must remain alongside their unresolved attribution and custody limits; see revised item 59 and the [physical-documentary review](SOURCE-EXCERPTS.md#source-physical).
- **“Every discretionary modeling choice encouraged collapse” is not supported.** Capacity-enhancing assumptions and formulation-dependent fracture effects are contrary evidence; see items 78, 82, and 88. Their existence also does not prove the model historically accurate.
- **“NIST omitted all residual support” is unsupported.** The reviewed models include bearing, contact, discrete resistance, and retained supports.
- **Suspected connection-table ratio errors were not substantiated after allowing for display rounding.**
- **The seat-dimension erratum does not establish that the simulation used the erroneous printed dimension.**
- **Our proposed seamless join between two CBS clips failed.** It cannot support a continuous fire-duration measurement.
- **Our image work has not produced a defensible burning-window percentage or demonstrated temperature inflation.**
- **UAF’s prescribed broad support failures do not identify the historical cause of those failures.**
- **Other fire-driven pathways remain possible.** Weakening NIST’s exact sequence would not eliminate the entire fire-mechanism family.
- **Near-free-fall façade motion does not by itself identify initiation, whole-building force balance, or intent.**
- **No reviewed anomaly establishes an executed conspiracy or deliberate falsification.**

The strongest objection to treating this as a list of demonstrated failures is that **many entries identify what our available record cannot yet resolve**. Missing replication is weaker evidence than a reproduced contradiction. The highest-value next evidence remains the exact executed runs, paired sensitivity cases, processing histories, and independently testable output—not another accumulation of unexplained-looking details.
