# Flash-observation opportunity: partial visibility, no absence finding

2026-09-24. Working research under charter Q07; a spatial pilot, not completion
of the flash/acoustic workstream or an expert opinion.

## Result

**The selected views offer some opportunity to see outward luminous events,
but do not directly expose every potential source region.** Two separately
frozen AI readings of eight full-frame grayscale images describe substantially
visible upper-wall/roof regions, alongside foreground and cloud masking.
Neither reader identifies the target ground-contact line. Very light sky,
cloud edges and nearby surfaces create brightness confounders.

This is not evidence that flashes were absent. Sparse stills do not establish
transience or cover the intervening footage; a visible wall does not establish
that an internal source would emit through it or be recorded. Conversely,
occlusion does not make the footage useless: a sufficiently conspicuous outward
event or illumination of an exposed region might be observable. This pilot
does not establish the probability or threshold for either outcome.

No cause ranking changes. It supplies neither a negative test of explosive
demolition nor affirmative evidence of deliberate intervention. It does not
validate NIST's fire-to-failure chain or test low-light/nonluminous mechanisms.

## What was actually reviewed

The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) fixed eight frames before new image views:
Camera2 6593/6717/6958/7013 and Camera3 WMV 258/300/330/348. They sample familiar
earlier/later appearances, not a blind or random selection, synchronized
instants or continuous temporal coverage. Each reader viewed each complete
native stored-resolution image once and saved its record before the next.
There were sixteen presentations of eight shared images, not sixteen
independent exposures. No extra frame, enhancement, crop or new decode was used.

The [source check](source-check.md) verifies selected file bytes, dimensions,
source-index/encoded-clock joins and underlying source pins. Camera2 uses a
converted 640×480 access copy; its extraction retains a known audio-layout
diagnostic. Camera3 uses the longer 442-frame WMV, **not** the older 232-frame
MP4 with the same camera label. Its 720×480 derivatives retain diagnostic-only
status and three recorded corruption-warning mentions. The selected indices
do not coincide with the recorded warning indices, which does not establish
clean pixels or rule out propagated effects. Neither file supplies an
authenticated original exposure clock or calibrated photometric response.

## Claim ledger and strongest objection

| Claim | Type / strength within scope | Support and limits | What would change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The eight files match their recorded source joins. | Integrity observation, A within checked bytes/metadata. | Fresh source check and root byte/header/clock replays; not new pixel decoding or historical authentication. | A failed pin, wrong index join or evidence of an incorrect earlier transformation. |
| Some upper exterior regions are visible, while other regions are masked. | Image observation, B within these rendered derivatives. | Two prior-informed AI readings of the same eight images; coarse category disagreement retained. | Better source imagery or qualified review showing mistaken target/occluder identification. |
| Every potential event location was concealed, so footage has no evidentiary use. | Unsupported inference, E for this universal claim. | Both records preserve exposed surfaces/background; actual sensitivity is unmeasured. | A specified event/optical geometry demonstrating no detectable outward manifestation for that particular mechanism, not for every alternative. |
| No visible flash occurred during either collapse recording. | Not established, D. | No continuous temporal inspection or calibrated detector was performed here. | Complete candidate review plus source/recording-specific detection constraints, or an authenticated visible event. |
| These images favor a particular initiating mechanism. | Unsupported by this pilot, E. | No specified mechanism prediction or discriminating event observation was tested. | Verified event/source identification and fair comparison against alternatives. |

The strongest objection is shared error: both readers are prior-informed AI
systems using the same processed grayscale copies and broad schema. Agreement
does not calibrate detection accuracy, authenticate the imagery or replace
human/qualified review. Unknown exposure and source processing are real limits,
but do not establish that a large conspicuous event could never be detected.

Earlier research statements that the reviewed record lacks an unambiguous
flash identification must remain statements about **what was established**,
not be promoted into a validated absence-of-flashes finding. This pilot does
not by itself prove or disprove those broader documentary summaries.
