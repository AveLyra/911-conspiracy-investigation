# DEP preservation anomalies and unresolved recovery outcomes

October 15, 2026. This update examines New York City Department of Environmental
Protection (DEP) records about preservation, collection, computer disposal and
later records searches. It is not a finding about WTC 7's collapse cause or
intentional destruction of evidence.

The records support two conclusions at once: there was reported collection and
return activity, and there were specific preservation vulnerabilities. They do
not yet establish which unique records were permanently lost or recovered.
[Methods and source identities](METHODS.md) distinguish document assertions,
observations, interpretations and unresolved outcomes.

## Preservation risks reported in 2003

The September 4, 2003 memorandum recording a September 3 DEP meeting reports
these conditions. Source:
[NYC-WTC_000153950, pages 1–4](../municipal-preservation-2026-10-08/remaining-records/sources/NYC-WTC_000153950.pdf).

| Item | What the memorandum reports | What remains unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Computers | Some PCs used by employees during the months after 9/11 were already gone. Computers were salvaged or reassigned; records did not necessarily identify former users. | Which devices and contents; whether drives were erased; whether copies survived. The PC passage does not establish that those employees had left DEP. |
| Former-employee email | Some accounts may have been deleted before the preservation letter; archives were to be checked. | Whether deletion occurred, which accounts, and completed archive-check results. “May” is not an admission of a specific deletion. |
| Backup tapes | Tape recycling was occurring. Stopping recycling and buying new tapes were proposed actions. | Which backups were overwritten, their contents, and whether corrective actions occurred. |
| ACP5/ACP7 databases | The databases were not currently backed up. | This does not say the live databases were deleted or their data lost. |

DEP also reported keeping former employees' accounts open since the preservation
letter and having hard copies of all mail. These assertions were not independently
verified; paper copies do not establish complete native-email or metadata
preservation. They must be considered alongside the possible earlier deletions.

The same memorandum reports nightly incremental and weekly full backups,
distributed PC-local documents and standalone devices. Backups could have
preserved material, but their coverage of affected PCs/accounts is unknown.
Missing hardware does not establish irretrievable data loss.

instructions. They also prevent treating signed paperwork or returned survey

## Institutional records narrow the next search

The official [2002 DEP data-center audit](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/7A02_069.pdf)
(printed pages 5–6 and 9–10) and
[2004 follow-up](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/7F04_065.pdf)
(printed pages 9–12) identify recovery-plan and inventory deficiencies. The
2002 report names IFMS/FMS Fixed Asset Inventory Reports; the follow-up describes
continuing problems and an outdated inventory. DEP also reported narrower
restoration tests, contingencies and improvement work. Those responses matter,
but underlying execution records were not obtained. Repeated earlier findings
are not independent corroboration of a specific loss.

A [later DEP Records Control Sheet](https://911records.org/doc/NYC-WTC_000159728)
was located, but its later tort-claims/administrative-order entries and 2013
footer do not establish the sought 2004 crosswalk. Blank barcode/date fields
and N/A box cells do not prove a completed transfer. This mirror-hosted copy
was not freshly byte-matched against the City host. No claimant was pursued.

Public [City records](https://www.911healthwatch.org/files/Mooklal-AFF.pdf)
and [E-Discovery](https://www.911healthwatch.org/files/Nduka-AFF.pdf)
affirmations identify nearly 20 relevant Law Department Relativity workspaces
and later search results in certain workspaces. These are repository leads,
not a demonstrated historical ingestion chain or recovery result. The reported
100,000-plus population already includes roughly 43,000 direct hits plus
families; it cannot be subtracted from portal counts to quantify missing records.

## Later testimony exposes a verification gap

The public [July 1, 2026 DEP appeals-officer deposition](https://www.911healthwatch.org/files/Filed-Exhibit-1.pdf),
filed July 9 as NYSCEF 88 in Index 155678/2024, concerns a February 29, 2024
appeal. The witness testified that:

- He had not ascertained the physically searched locations (76:24–77:7).
  This is not testimony that nobody searched.
- He believed DEP had an archive of old records but could not specify its
  location (77:8–24; 78:1–17). This does not identify an email archive.
- He believed the search diligent, based that conclusion on counsel's advice,
  had not interviewed searchers and identified no other basis (79:2–17;
  80:1–5; 81:5–12). Privilege objections limited further questioning.
- He believed he had asked who performed the search, received no answer and
  had not learned their identities by deposition (90:9–91:25). He reported
  no predecision follow-up and explained that he had been instructed to await
  a response (92:16–93:10).

These are concrete limitations in this witness's verification of the later
search. They do not adjudicate search sufficiency or resolve the 2003 mailbox
checks. Counsel's statements about unproduced appeal correspondence are not
adopted as admissions, judicial findings or proof of historical destruction.

## Unresolved outcomes and discriminating records

The bounded searches did not locate and verify a target PC-salvage transaction,
the 2004 control-ID crosswalk, or completed historical former-employee archive
checks. The most useful next records are:

1. Asset/serial-to-user assignments, dated salvage/reassignment transactions,
   drive-copy receipts and surviving-content inventories.
2. Bureau/server/account populations, archive or backup inputs, execution
   dates, methods, errors and identifiable search/restore outputs.
3. The actual collection/scan/return register and migration crosswalk.
4. Later search instructions, searchers, repositories, coverage and returns,
   connected where possible to the historical systems.

Unindexed records, other versions and surviving copies could change these
findings. A verified surviving copy would disprove irretrievable loss for that
item. A documented destruction event still requires a sufficiently complete
search for surviving copies before concluding that its information was lost.

The anomalies warrant targeted inquiry. They do not establish deliberate
destruction, an irrecoverable WTC 7-specific dataset, deletion of NIST model
files or a collapse cause. Publication does not promote this research into a
legal record or certify expert acceptance.
