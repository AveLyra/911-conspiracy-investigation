# Five-bolt descent and six-bolt broken crest: source recovery

October 8, 2026. Research-only result. Two separately frozen computational
readers completed the previously missing F5 Im3 descent and F6 lower-Im1
broken-crest targets. They preserve locally attributable pieces and unresolved
contacts rather than reconstruct a smooth full curve. All four originals and
their differences remain intact. This is evidence about the published graph,
not an independently validated connection law or historical collapse cause.

## Agreements and differences

Both readers attribute a local continuous blue descent in F5 and separately
recognizable broken blue pieces. Red/blue contacts near the top, middle and
lower boundary retain unresolved attribution. F6 has local broken red crest
and arm pieces, with red/green contact uncertainty. The corrected earlier
inventory places this broken crest in Im1, not Im2.

Neither reader attributes solid F6 cells inside this Im1 target. The solid
crest is in Im2 and was previously investigated there. Empty local attribution
is not physical absence, zero force, zero support or a new common-domain claim.

## Evidence ceiling and next discriminating work

The strongest objection to treating this as model validation is decisive:
correctly recovering a raster graph does not establish its simulated inputs,
connection behavior under the historical exposure, or its effect on global
failure. Even agreement between readers cannot prove pre-raster enclosure or
continuity through hidden/shared ink. These claims remain unestablished.

This completes these finite source targets, not the fourteen-pair coverage or
the42 paired human-review slots. F5 rise/origin/tail and seams, F6 Im3 irregular
shoulder/downstream regions and seams, and the other inventory obligations
remain. Do not treat context-only pixels as newly annotated target support.
