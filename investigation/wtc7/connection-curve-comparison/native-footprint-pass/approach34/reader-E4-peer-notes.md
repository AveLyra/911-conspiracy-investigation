bolts. The locally continuous gold approach and the separately visible lower

rule. An initial working possibility that the early single gold band required
both-route ambiguity was reconsidered after this reader completed later raw
blocks: the separated lower bodies show a distinct dashed local route. That
change is based on this reader's own source inspection, not another annotation.

The early red/gold neighborhood, green contacts and blue crossings retain
route-specific unassigned bands. A candidate `solid` band does not imply the
dash route contributed ink there. Its candidate means an unresolved possible
E4 contribution alongside another color. The export schema names only E4
routes, so the other-color ownership described in the reason is not enforced
by the machine. This is an aggregate ownership limitation, not proof that all
cells in the band belong to E4.

The solid is split into local fragments at unresolved contacts. Each
distinguishable gold dash body has a separate local ID. Pale body-edge material
may be fringe-only. Wholly unselected columns do not bridge a dash gap, and
fringe at the ends of bodies is not a supported historical interval. The
strongest alternative is that some pale RGB disturbances are JPEG/color
interaction from neighboring curves rather than gold edge ink; fringe and
unassigned choices explicitly retain that vulnerability. In particular,
lower-body attribution first becomes distinct in this reading around x284;
earlier empty dash records are a visibility/attribution limit, not a finding
that the shell curve is absent there.
