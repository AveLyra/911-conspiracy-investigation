## Manual decisions and limits

Core and fringe are manual attribution-confidence labels, not calibrated
pre-raster bounds. The script merely expands these literal inclusive row
runs and constructs the required schema; it never chooses cells from RGB,
thresholds, smoothing, tracing, interpolation or a fitted path.

- The rising solid pieces are split around the mixed-color loss at column
  153 and the later interruption. Separate local IDs do not assert continuity
  across an unassigned region. Pale edges and isolated broken-body pieces
  remain literal, including multiple memberships in one column.
- The top-edge region around 360–367 retains alternative solid/dash ownership
  where red and other colors meet. Separated visible red material receives
  local attribution only where supported by this reading.
- Columns 384–385 retain shared red material as candidate bands for both
  styles. They are not duplicated as attributed cells or forcibly assigned to
  the visually smoother trajectory.
- The descending broken pieces keep the irregular shoulder changes and
  separated bodies. They were not made monotone. Blue, green, purple and cyan
  contacts remain unassigned where ownership was not resolved; two disjoint
  bands are retained at column 427.
- Later solid candidates merge with purple-colored material and meet the
  lower source edge. Unassigned bands preserve that uncertainty; no automatic
  red continuation is extended through the remaining source.
- Long inspected nonassignments, including central columns 225–359, do not
  establish true absence. Material may lie outside this strip, be overwritten,
  or not be uniquely attributable. No source-edge observation establishes a
  physical endpoint, continuation into another strip, or zero response.
- Context columns 440–441 were read but are not target selections. The red
  broken candidate and mixed-color contact at column 439 retain the target-right
  boundary and cannot establish a terminal physical domain.

The strongest alternative to any local attribution is that raster compression,
overprinting or neighboring colored ink accounts for part of the selected
footprint. Those limitations are particularly material near the preserved
contact bands. Another independent reading, source-qualified cross-pair
conflicts and the required actual human review could change the attribution;
successful code replay cannot resolve that issue. Neither context duplication
nor repeated viewing creates new scientific evidence.
