The summary's search of the van and occupants must not be expanded into a finding that explosives were chemically detected **on the five men**. The chemical-related paragraph specifically describes the vehicle and interior swabs. An item/person or material/operation attribution requires additional evidence.

## New object-description mismatch: blankets absent versus blanket samples inventoried

The next page of the same September23 summary, [Part5 physical45/printed3](https://archive.org/download/fbinewarkdivisionfullfieldinvestigation/FBI%20Newark%20Division%20Full-Field%20Investigation%20Part%205.pdf#page=45), first paragraph, says equipment typically used in a moving company's daily duties was not found, expressly including **work gloves, blankets, straps, ropes, boxes, dollies and rollers**. The Part1 inventory nevertheless lists **blanket samples**.

Keep both statements. They create an unresolved **object/scope/source correspondence question**: what material was sampled, where and when was it obtained, and what objects did the summary's not-found account cover? A retained fragment or sample versus usable equipment, a different collection context/date, or an inaccurate description are possible tests, not resolved explanations. Neither source alone establishes planted material, a false laboratory result, a substituted vehicle or deliberate misstatement. The original search inventory/photos, sampling worksheet, sample envelopes and submission manifest would discriminate. The SA-glove controls are not automatically the work gloves described as missing.
