# UMS: Newark closure, headquarters inquiry, and record leads

Working source review, November 5, 2026. This is a research note, not an agency finding or court determination. The FBI records linked below are apparent released scans hosted by a third party; agency custody and completeness have not independently been authenticated. This review checked a locally held OCR derivative and visually inspected PDF pp. 38 and 61; redactions and OCR errors limit what can be concluded. PDF page references are one-based.

## Newark closure and the headquarters inquiry

The [July 10, 2003 Newark closing memorandum, PDF pp. 38–39](https://archive.org/download/fbinewarkdivisionfullfieldinvestigation/FBI%20Newark%20Division%20Full-Field%20Investigation%20Part%206.pdf#page=38), reports no factual or substantive circumstantial information corroborating alleged videotaping of the attacks, or indicating that the five men were on the garage roof videotaping before what it calls the first aircraft striking tower 2. That description warrants care: the first aircraft struck the North Tower, WTC 1. It states that none of the pictures developed from the film in the identified 35 mm camera depicted the towers before the attack. It also reports that other circumstantial information supported accounts of going to the garage after learning of the attacks. The memorandum's later discussions report no basis to conclude that the individuals had prior attack knowledge.

The photo statement concerns the identified camera's developed film. It does not establish that every possible image was recovered or independently prove when the group arrived. Likewise, an investigator's no-foreknowledge finding is evidence against the claim of advance knowledge, not conclusive proof that no person had it. The underlying negatives, witness interviews, clock calibration and competing evidence remain important.

An [April 14, 2004 Newark evidence-retention communication, PDF p. 61](https://archive.org/download/fbinewarkdivisionfullfieldinvestigation/FBI%20Newark%20Division%20Full-Field%20Investigation%20Part%206.pdf#page=61), reports that Newark's counterintelligence inquiry had closed in July 2003 while an FBI headquarters matter was still pending and Newark awaited a summary relevant to evidence disposition. Redactions obscure part of the description. **The pending-status communication is dated 2004**, so it should not be represented as a July 2003 finding or as establishing the headquarters inquiry's eventual result. A [February 10, 2004 communication, PDF pp. 59–60](https://archive.org/download/fbinewarkdivisionfullfieldinvestigation/FBI%20Newark%20Division%20Full-Field%20Investigation%20Part%206.pdf#page=59), directs reopening and reassignment for evidence tracking; that administrative action does not establish a renewed finding of foreknowledge.

The final headquarters inquiry, its scope, database results, supporting evidence and disposition remain targets for retrieval. An unresolved inquiry is not evidence of an affirmative intelligence-affiliation finding or a link to the hijackers.

## Zim relocation and Classic lead

An [April 3, 2001 trade report](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/zim-moves-regional-head-office-from-new-york-to-norfolk) announced a planned September relocation of Zim's U.S. corporate office to Norfolk. This weakens an inference that the September move was necessarily an emergency evacuation prompted by advance attack knowledge. The report identifies no mover; it does not establish whether UMS or another company performed a WTC job. Seek original contracts, dispatch sheets and building access records.

The [Newark closing narrative, PDF p. 47](https://archive.org/download/fbinewarkdivisionfullfieldinvestigation/FBI%20Newark%20Division%20Full-Field%20Investigation%20Part%206.pdf#page=47), describes Miami's belief that Classic International Movers had moved one alleged hijacker. The locally held OCR associates a Classic telephone number in a seized notebook with the inquiry; the obscured text should be checked against the page image and earlier records. The passage does not identify the alleged customer, establish a job or show that a notebook holder called the company. Obtain the original Miami lead, notebook page and translation, customer and payment records, interviews, and any positive or negative comparisons. Earlier and later summaries may repeat one source rather than independently corroborate it.

An [FBI 2023 FOIA log](https://vault.fbi.gov/foia-log-2023/FOIA%2520Log%25202023%2520Part%252001%2520%2528Final%2529) search result lists `1609818-000`, opened November 3, 2023, subject Classic International Movers. The log is an indexing lead, not proof of the request's outcome or the existence or content of any particular production. The direct FBI Vault page returned an access error during this check; confirm the row in a directly accessible official copy.

## CCTV and remaining test

The [October 17, 2001 FBI communication, Serial 325, printed p. 4](https://www.israellobby.org/urbanmoving/1169683-001%20---%20303A-NY-C237934-Serial%20325%20---%20Section%201%20%281037758%29.pdf), describes a possible Exxon surveillance match and expressly conditions its timing inference on the van being the same vehicle. A match, occupants and calibrated time were not established in the reviewed passage. Obtain the original recording, camera clock and calibration, comparison and enhancement records, and final disposition; distinguish the Exxon footage from other video and other vehicles.

These records supply investigative leads and contrary evidence; they do not establish UMS or Max Movers' WTC access, explosives placement, Mossad tasking, or coordination with hijackers or the Saudi support network. Each proposed link requires independent, contemporaneous evidence. A verified mismatch, negative identification or an unrelated business explanation would narrow the hypothesis; authenticated job, access, communications or tasking records could strengthen particular links.
