# WTC badge and access records: a separate records trail

**Working research, November 6, 2026.** Public agency documents identify potentially useful badge-list and aggregate access-record trails beyond the WTC 1 and 2 occupant list previously discussed. They do not identify the holder of the WTC visitor card listed on an FBI property receipt, establish that a detainee entered the WTC or connect UMS to explosives.

## Three separately described records

1. **Seven-building badge roster.** An April 2006 World Trade Center Health Registry manual says New York City health officials provided RTI International with a Port Authority list in May 2004. It describes 95,442 people issued security badges for one or more of the seven WTC buildings during the preceding five years. This establishes what the manual says about roster scope, not that the list contains a particular person, covers temporary visitor passes, records actual entries, or remains available. The same manual identifies RTI project 08692 and contract 200-2002-M-00836 as possible search locators. [Registry manual, section 4.5.3, printed p. 25 / PDF p. 35](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/911health/downloads/pdf/wtc/wtc-datafile-manual.pdf#page=35); [project information, PDF p. 3](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/911health/downloads/pdf/wtc/wtc-datafile-manual.pdf#page=3).
2. **NIST occupant lists.** A NIST data-collection summary published in July 2004 lists a Port Authority WTC occupant badge list under May 2003 and an updated list under August 2003. The publication date is not the same as the listed receipt dates. The descriptions do not establish whether those files are identical to the 2004 registry roster, their complete coverage or present custody. NIST's separate survey documentation describes a WTC 1 and 2 occupant frame containing people absent on September 11 and former occupants, so inclusion in a roster does not alone prove a visit on any particular date. [NIST data-collection summary](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2004/07/status-data-collection-efforts); [NIST SP 1000-5 vol. 6, Appendix O, p. O-2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication1000-5v6.pdf).
3. **Aggregate tower accesses.** A 2009 article attributes to a March 2005 Port Authority written communication a count of 14,417 card accesses to the towers between midnight and 8:47 a.m. on May 8, 2001. An access count is not established as a count of unique people or as proof that individual event logs survive today. The original communication, query definitions, underlying events and retention status remain unlocated. [Murphy, *American Journal of Public Health*, PDF pp. 2–3](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/201614/cdc_201614_DS1.pdf#page=2).

## What would connect the records

The [FBI property receipt](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-2.pdf#page=81) lists a WTC visitor card, but its holder, building numeral, issuer, date and use remain unresolved. None of the aggregate or roster descriptions above is a match to that card. A routine visit, an unused or retained credential, a different holder and record mismatch are alternatives to investigate. Even a verified entry would not establish a moving job, foreknowledge or an explosives installation.

Seek the original card image and property crosswalk; issuer, host and temporary-visitor records; the May 2004 roster transmittal and processing history; the NIST badge-list versions; and the March 2005 communication with any retained source and aggregation records. Request appropriately scoped searches or releasable nonidentifying results rather than unnecessary personal or health data. A positive or negative comparison must be interpreted according to the actual list's coverage, dates, retention and identification quality.
