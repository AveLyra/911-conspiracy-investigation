The file is an FBI-origin release, not an independently authenticated original case file; its SHA-256 is `e589c01d1dc29f157ae364d4deaac9bce8072937905de6197a2e6548c9f506ea`. ## September 10: moving company and building witness

The [first interview, PDF pp. 71–72](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=71), records that a tenant said on September 9 that Urban Moving Company would move the tenant and spouse on September 10. A second message reportedly placed the expected move around 9 a.m. on September 10. The interviewee reported obtaining a certificate of liability insurance from the company and giving it to the agent. The form says the investigation was September 12 and transcription September 14. The certificate, booking and underlying tenant records are not reproduced in the reviewed pages. The witness's characterization of the timing as odd is a contemporaneous opinion, not evidence of the tenants' motive or knowledge.

A [separate interview, PDF p. 73](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=73), records a different interviewee's account that Urban Moving Company moved tenants out on September 10, using three men and a white box truck with a red cab. The interviewee said none of the photographs shown were of those movers. One photographed person nevertheless looked familiar according to the same report; the two statements have different scopes. This is evidence of a reported UMS business job at the same apartment complex, not evidence the September 11 van or any detained person was on that job. An ordinary move is a plausible reason for company personnel to be there. The tenant's name and reasons are redacted; no inference about nationality, occupation, destination or foreknowledge follows.

A [third witness account, PDF pp. 61–62](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=61), records the witness's confident recognition of person 5 in a five-person photo array as someone seen inside the building around 3 p.m. September 10 and during the prior weekend. The reported array contained photos of the five investigated people; the person's name is redacted on the page, and no name-to-array crosswalk was verified here. The witness reported a second man who was not in the photos. The visible account does not link either man to the tenant's move. It contains differing interview/date labels and calls September 10 “the day before,” so the precise interview timing needs the original record; the reported sighting date is September 10. The FBI's later closing account says the lineup consisted only of photographs of the five detainees; an array without known fillers and an unverified crosswalk limit identification weight. Confidence is a reported observation, not independent confirmation. The witness also could not recall the nature of work the man said he was performing; the redacted continuation does not establish a WTC job or reason for the visit. ## September 11: observed van and timing

A [September 11 interview, PDF pp. 64–65](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=64), records that a witness saw three men on a white van after the first WTC fire and before the second impact. The witness said she wrote down registration JRJ-13Y as the vehicle pulled away and later gave the paper to an agent. The record is evidence for an identification and an observation between impacts; it does not establish when the van arrived, the exact departure time relative to the second impact, all five occupants' identities, or advance knowledge. The reported behavior and apparent photography are observations attributed to the witness; motivation and video capture are not independently established by the account. The 2003 Newark conclusion found no corroboration of pre-impact videotaping or foreknowledge within its inquiry; that is counterevidence with a defined investigative scope, not a complete location history for everyone.

Earlier reports of an unidentified utility-marked van and a Gulf visit remain separate from this identified-plate account. Their witness identities, vehicle match and calibrated times require independent comparisons; they cannot automatically establish a pre-first-impact arrival or be counted as independent observations of the same vehicle. ## Effect on the hypothesis and next records

The prior-day commercial job and a separate prior-day identification lead strengthen the question of how particular workers and customers came to know the site. They do not establish that the pictured person participated in the move, that a tenant was a hijacker, that any visitor had WTC access, that explosives were present or placed, or that UMS acted for an intelligence service. The reported job supplies a commercial explanation for some prior company presence; a mistaken photo identification or a separate visit remain alternatives. Positive identification of a specific worker on a verified dispatch could strengthen a narrower association; contemporaneous rosters showing the person elsewhere or reliable alternate identifications could weaken it.

Seek the September 10 move order, dispatch and driver roster, truck identifiers, invoice and certificate fax/insurance schedule, apartment move and elevator logs, source messages and the FBI 1A indices or copies. Compare original photo array, filler selection, administration notes, witness statements and the redacted number-to-name crosswalk; do not publish tenant identification without a separate privacy review.
