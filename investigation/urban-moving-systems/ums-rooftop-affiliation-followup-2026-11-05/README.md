# Urban Moving Systems: rooftop call and FBI follow-up

**Research assessment, November 5, 2026.** An FBI Philadelphia report records an interviewee's account of a September 11 phone call from a UMS supervisor about people going to a roof. A later FBI Newark memorandum says photographs showed the five van detainees on a UMS warehouse roof while the World Trade Center was burning. These accounts could describe the same rooftop visit, but their connection, participants and sequence relative to the call are not established. The reviewed records do not establish advance knowledge, a Mossad operation, or a UMS link to the hijackers or Saudi support network.

This note is a source-based working assessment, not an independent authentication of the historical records. The FBI documents are public scans or reproductions held by third parties. Redactions, attribution and missing underlying materials limit the conclusions.

## Reported call and rooftop observations

The September 21, 2001 Philadelphia communication, FOIPA **1169683-001**, **303C-PH-C91946 Serial 2**, PDF page 4, recounts an interview in which a UMS worker said a supervisor called a redacted recipient's cell phone at approximately **10:00 a.m.**, labelled EST. The caller reportedly said unspecified people had gone to a roof for a better view of the towers; the account mentions a forgotten camera. The report does not disclose the numbers, identify the rooftop group, provide a carrier record, or establish how the interviewee learned all details. The approximate time, whether the EST label means local daylight time or literal standard time, is after both aircraft impacts. [Serial 2](https://www.israellobby.org/urbanmoving/1169683-001%20---%20303C-PH-C91946-Serial%202%20---%20Section%201%20%281037749%29.pdf#page=4).

The caller account comes from an interview of one of two additional UMS workers on an Ohio/Illinois delivery trip, distinct from the five people detained in the New Jersey van. The report cites toll receipts for September 10 as corroboration of parts of the crew's travel. That corroboration does not independently verify the later phone call or the rooftop account. [Serial 2, PDF pages 2–4](https://www.israellobby.org/urbanmoving/1169683-001%20---%20303C-PH-C91946-Serial%202%20---%20Section%201%20%281037749%29.pdf#page=2).

The July 10, 2003 Newark closing memorandum states that developed 35 mm photographs showed three detainees at the Doric apartment parking lot and that photographs also showed **all five later on the roof of a UMS warehouse** observing the burning towers. This is an FBI description of photographs, not an independent examination of the underlying images in this review. It makes the five a plausible group for the earlier call's unidentified "they," but neither record establishes that identification, the precise roof-visit time, or whether the visit preceded the call. [FBI scan, PDF page 44](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=44); [third-party online text](https://www.scribd.com/document/993617086/FBI-Report-Section-6-text-5-Dancing-Israelis).

An earlier Philadelphia report repeats a claim that the five were on a company roof before either impact. That passage attributes the claim to an unspecified report; the July 2003 account describes photographs of the towers burning and does not independently establish a pre-impact roof visit. [January 24, 2002 report, Serial 18, PDF page 1](https://www.israellobby.org/UrbanMoving/1169683-001%20---%20303C-PH-C91946-Serial%2018%20---%20Section%201%20%281037776%29.pdf#page=1).

## Testing the parking-lot timeline

Newark's closing memorandum describes several imperfect checks. It reports that E-ZPass data recorded the UMS van entering the Brooklyn-Battery Tunnel at **7:48 a.m.** but gave little help locating it at the time of the attack. It describes a comparison of the detainees' still photographs with news footage which, in the FBI's assessment, supported their account of traveling to the parking lot after learning of the first impact; the report itself calls that comparison non-scientific. It also identifies an early photograph containing what agents believed was a news helicopter and describes FAA radar data showing a candidate helicopter in the relevant area from about **8:50 to 9:03 a.m.** The inference depends on correct helicopter identification, concerns the timing of a photograph, and does not establish when the van first arrived. [FBI scan, PDF pages 48–49](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=48).

The memorandum also recounts a second witness who reported seeing a white van in the parking lot as early as **8:00 a.m.** It does not establish that this was the detainees' van. Agents attempted to time a different witness's observation using records of a local telephone call, but the memorandum says the provider did not maintain the relevant electronic records. Preserve both the witness lead and the limitations of the photographic and phone evidence. [FBI scan, PDF pages 42 and 49](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=42).

## Notebook number and competing explanation

The July 2003 Newark communication describes a detainee's notepad containing numbers that prompted checks. It says one number appeared to correspond to an overseas telephone subscriber in South America whom the FBI described as having ties to Middle Eastern militants. The same report says the number was later identified as a legitimate bank-account number belonging to a friend who had lent the detainee $200; Newark stated that it ruled out a nexus between the detainee and the South American person. Names, digits and portions of the explanation are redacted, preventing independent reconstruction here. Do not present the initial apparent match as a proven contact or omit Newark's contrary finding. The passage does not establish a hijacker or Saudi support-network connection. [FBI scan, PDF pages 55–56](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=55).

## Intelligence inquiry and polygraph material

Newark's July 2003 memorandum says its investigation was completed and reported no factual or substantive circumstantial information corroborating witness claims that the five videotaped the attacks. It also reported no factual or substantive circumstantial evidence that they were on a parking garage videotaping before the attack, and said circumstantial information supported their account of going there after learning of the events. Its wording refers to "tower #2" in discussing the first aircraft strike; preserve that apparent discrepancy when locating the underlying record. It separately reports no basis to attribute advance knowledge to the investigated men, while substantial affiliation-related passages remain redacted. These are Newark's investigative conclusions, with the stated record and redaction limits. [FBI scan, PDF pages 38 and 52–57](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=52).

The same closing communication says FBI headquarters and intelligence-community personnel reviewed the case in April 2002 to assess possible links to other Israeli nationals and a possible clandestine human-intelligence collection network. It reports the headquarters inquiry was **not complete in July 2003**. An April 14, 2004 Newark evidence-retention communication says the headquarters matter was still pending and that Newark awaited a summary. Neither document provides the final headquarters findings. Newark's closure does not establish the separate inquiry's eventual outcome; the existence of the inquiry does not establish an intelligence operation. [FBI scan, PDF pages 56–57 and 61](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=56).

An FBI New York communication dated November 28, 2001 reports interviews and polygraph testing on advance knowledge and foreign-intelligence contact. A January 3, 2002 quality-review communication says an examiner judged the subject's responses not indicative of deception on advance knowledge but indicative of deception on intelligence affiliation; a headquarters supervisor concurred after reviewing the examination materials. The records' subject and dates suggest they may concern the same November testing; redactions prevent confirmation, and the quality review is not an independent test. The January document lists an **FD-497 worksheet, FD-498 report, 11 polygraph charts and a computer disk**, among other enclosures. A polygraph opinion is not proof of deception, affiliation or advance knowledge. Its synopsis says 2002 while the detailed section says 2001; retain the discrepancy. [November communication](https://www.israellobby.org/UrbanMoving/1169683-001%20---%20303A-NY-C237934-Serial%20339%20---%20Section%201%20%281037762%29.pdf); [January quality review](https://www.israellobby.org/UrbanMoving/1169683-001%20---%20303A-NY-C237934-Serial%20368%20---%20Section%201%20%281037763%29.pdf).

## Address and entry-date lists and Classic Movers fax

**Record-location assessment, November 5, 2026:** We located the documents identifying the address and entry-date lists and a 24-page fax, but not the lists themselves or the fax's substantive pages. These documents do not disclose a comparison result, a named hijacker customer or a Mossad affiliation. Sources below are publicly circulated copies of FBI records; the release chain and omitted materials have not been independently authenticated.

An FD-302 transcribed **September 16, 2001** describes a Newark investigation on September 15. It states that agents compiled a list of current and prior addresses and U.S. entry dates for identified hijackers and suspected associates, and a comparable list for the five detained UMS workers; both lists were said to be attached. The following page in this release begins a different interview. The FD-302 does not state whether the comparison produced any match. Neither list was located in targeted searches of OCR for the six section releases or in targeted public searches; OCR gaps and the possibility of copies elsewhere prevent a claim that no released or surviving copy exists. [Section 5, PDF pages 28–29](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=28).

A **September 17, 2001** Newark communication says it attaches information concerning Classic International Movers employees detained by Ridgefield police. The next page is a Ridgefield Police Department fax cover, with a September 14 transmission header, addressed to the FBI Command Center and asking for urgent checks on four subjects. It states **24 pages including the cover**, implying 23 substantive pages in the stated transmission. The cover does not itself name Classic or identify the subjects. Its placement after the Classic communication is a useful lead, but does not by itself prove the two refer to the same people or that the missing pages were interview reports. The substantive pages were not located in targeted searches of the six section releases' OCR or in public searches in this review; their omission here does not establish destruction or absence from all other files. [Section 1, PDF pages 133–135](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=133).

Newark's July 2003 closing memorandum provides the better-substantiated bridge: it says the FBI Miami division believed Classic had been used as a mover by one of the 19 hijackers, that Newark detained and interviewed four Classic employees based on Miami's information, and that Classic's telephone number appeared in a notebook seized from the five UMS detainees. The passage does not identify the alleged customer, establish the move or show who owned the notebook or placed a call. The underlying Miami lead, company job records, notebook page and investigative disposition are needed to test the account. A number for another moving company could reflect ordinary business contact; it is not proof of surveillance, shared purpose, advance knowledge or Mossad direction. [Section 6, PDF page 47](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=47).

The FBI's public [2023 FOIA log](https://vault.fbi.gov/foia-log-2023/FOIA%20Log%202023%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/at_download/file) identifies **1609818-000**, opened November 3, 2023, with the subject Classic International Movers. The log establishes a request entry, not whether records were found or released. The original Miami lead and any prior production were not located in this pass. The two covers also do not determine the outcome of the separate headquarters intelligence-affiliation inquiry discussed above.

## Van laboratory result, business records and WTC access

**Records assessment, November 5, 2026:** This targeted search located pre-attack vehicle and insurance paperwork and FBI accounts of moving jobs, but did not locate a final chemical report for the stopped van, a complete UMS dispatch or customer ledger, or a WTC access record for UMS. The materials examined do not establish that UMS transported or planted explosives in the towers. The underlying FBI release scans are third-party-hosted copies, not independently certified productions.

The September 23, 2001 FBI memorandum reports a bomb-detection dog's alert on the van, collection and laboratory submission of interior samples, and says final results remained pending on that date. Newark's inventory identifies fabric item **15 / E01889035**, glove and bomb-suit control swabs **16–17 / E01889036**, and blanket samples **34 / E01889037**. An alert or the description of an intended test is not a chemical identification; the controls are not separate positive findings. Neither a later final report nor a cancellation or nonexamination record was located in this pass. This does not establish that testing was never completed or that any result was positive or negative. [September memorandum, section 5, PDF page 44](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=44); [inventory, section 1, PDF page 7](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=7); [earlier source review](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/README.md).

An October 18, 2001 FBI laboratory report in the releases concerns enhancement of video frames showing white vans. Its lab number is not a demonstrated accession for the fabric or blanket specimens; it is not the missing chemical report. [Section 5, PDF pages 69–71](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=69).

An August 2, 2001 fax on UMS letterhead requests insurance coverage for six vehicles. Its listed van VIN, **1GCEG15W4Y1142815**, matches the VIN and license JRJ13Y in a September 16 FBI vehicle-registration summary. The schedule calls the vehicle a GMC; the FBI summary calls it a Chevrolet. The matching VIN supports identification of the vehicle in UMS's pre-attack business paperwork, but the insurance request does not establish where it traveled, what it carried, or whether insurance was ultimately issued on the proposed terms. [Vehicle schedule, section 2, PDF page 34](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-2.pdf#page=34); [FBI summary, section 1, PDF page 89](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=89).

An FBI interview states that an agent showed a UMS worker a **bill of lading**, but the actual bill was not located in the materials checked. A separate Philadelphia report says agents contacted customers who corroborated furniture deliveries in Ohio and Illinois on September 11 by two other UMS workers in a rented truck. That crew was separate from the five occupants of the stopped van; the corroborated deliveries were not to the WTC. These accounts show avenues for locating specific job records, not a complete customer history or proof about other cargo. [Interview, section 3, PDF page 7](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-3.pdf#page=7); [Philadelphia report, section 5, PDF page 74](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=74).

A third-party [reproduction of a December 13, 2001 New Jersey consumer-affairs announcement](https://www.ancreport.com/urban-moving-systems-owner-flees-the-country-and-abandons-customers-stuff/) says the state obtained a court order for access to UMS's Weehawken warehouse and filed a consumer-protection lawsuit. The original agency announcement, court file and warehouse inventory were not retrieved in this pass. If retained, those records could identify customers or goods; the reproduced announcement is not evidence of a WTC delivery.

No pre-September 11 UMS WTC delivery authorization, loading-dock record, tenant order, access log or witness account placing UMS people or cargo inside the towers was found in the materials searched. This bounded negative finding does not prove that such records never existed or remain unavailable elsewhere. The [Port Authority records-access form](https://www.panynj.gov/forms/af/port-authority/public-records-access-form.html) offers a route to ask about surviving historical records; their creation, custody and retention are unverified. NIST concluded its tower investigation found no evidence supporting controlled demolition, while expressly stating it **did not test tower steel for explosives or thermite residues**; that is not a negative residue-test result. [NIST tower FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-towers-investigation).

## Max Movers and next tests

In Serial 2, an interviewee described **Max Movers** as the long-distance name and UMS as the local-moving name, with common ownership and facilities; the suggested business reason was the interviewee's own inference. A separate summary repeats a same-company account, which may derive from overlapping interviews. This review found no verified incorporation, ownership, DBA or customer records establishing the legal relationship. [Serial 2, PDF page 4](https://www.israellobby.org/urbanmoving/1169683-001%20---%20303C-PH-C91946-Serial%202%20---%20Section%201%20%281037749%29.pdf#page=4); [Serial 357, PDF page 4](https://www.israellobby.org/UrbanMoving/1169683-001%20---%20303C-PH-C91946-Serial%20357%20---%20Section%201%20%281037756%29.pdf#page=4).

Priority tests, seeking results in either direction:

1. Obtain less-redacted interviews, contemporaneous notes, any surviving FBI-held call records, subscriber information and dispatch records identifying the reported call's participants, time, roof and group. Test the alternative that it concerned other employees.
2. Obtain the original or surviving photo sequence, film attribution and chain of custody, FBI image-comparison work, underlying news footage, FAA radar data, helicopter identification and witness interviews. Assess what each source can and cannot establish about arrival and exposure times.
3. Seek the headquarters analytical inquiry's final disposition and underlying search results, including contrary and inconclusive findings; separately seek the FD-497, FD-498, charts, disk contents and quality-review materials where available.
4. Obtain the notebook-number trace, comparison inputs, subscriber and bank-account attribution, interviews and records explaining Newark's negative nexus finding. Do not equate matching digits with a completed call or shared purpose.
5. Seek dated Max Movers incorporation, ownership, trade-name, dispatch and customer records and compare identities and dates against UMS, with ordinary business and social explanations retained.
6. Locate the two lists identified in the September 15 investigation/September 16 FD-302, their source data and any actual comparison results. Also seek the September 14 Ridgefield transmission and all pages, the September 17 Classic-employee attachment, the originating Miami lead, Classic job and customer records, and subsequent determinations. Establish whether the fax and employee attachment are the same records instead of assuming it.
7. Seek the van laboratory submission and accession records, worksheets, report or documented cancellation, controls, and chain of custody using JRJ13Y and the listed sample barcodes. Compare any result with dated vehicle use, cargo and WTC access records; a van result alone would not establish delivery to or placement in a tower. Seek the FBI-held bill of lading and business/dispatch files, New Jersey consumer-affairs and court records, and any surviving historical Port Authority or tenant delivery and access records.

These are research targets, not representations that any record survives or will be releasable. This public note does not characterize any request's current processing status or authorize agency contact.
