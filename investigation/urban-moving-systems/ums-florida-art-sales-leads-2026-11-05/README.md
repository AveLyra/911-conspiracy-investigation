# Urban Moving Systems, Florida and art-sales leads

**Assessment, November 5, 2026:** The examined materials document an FBI-reported connection between Urban Moving Systems (UMS), two additional workers' Florida travel, and a separate group of people in moving businesses who knew of UMS and described art sales among opportunities for young Israelis. A separate DEA security report describes suspicious contacts at government facilities. The sources do not establish that the UMS workers were the DEA subjects, that any particular person was a Mossad operative, that anyone monitored a hijacker, or that the episodes formed a coordinated 9/11 operation.

## Philadelphia interviews and the UMS business scene

An apparent FBI Philadelphia report dated January 24, 2002 says two UMS workers detained in York, Pennsylvania, told interviewers that, after arriving separately in New York, they flew to Miami to obtain Florida driving licenses, then returned to New York and began working for UMS after answering a newspaper advertisement. The same report describes a separate group of six people detained in October 2001 while working for moving companies: five reportedly worked for Advanced Moving Systems in Florida and one for Isaac's Moving and Storage in Philadelphia. It states all six knew of UMS and three described moving, door-to-door art sales and mall-kiosk work as available jobs. These are reported interview accounts, not independently verified employment histories. [FBI report copy, FOIPA 1169683-001, 303C-PH-C91946, Serial 18, pages 1–2](https://www.israellobby.org/UrbanMoving/1169683-001%20---%20303C-PH-C91946-Serial%2018%20---%20Section%201%20%281037776%29.pdf#page=1).

The report does **not** identify either UMS worker as one of the DEA investigation's art sellers or show contact with a hijacker. It also repeats an account placing the five detainees on a company's roof before the attacks; the cited passage presents it as a report, and the rooftop and timing claims require separate corroboration. Its first page says the document does not contain FBI recommendations or conclusions. The apparent FBI release is a third-party-hosted copy; its complete chain from FBI production to this hosting has not been independently authenticated in this review.

## DEA art-student inquiry

A publicly circulated report headed DEA Office of Security, June 2001, describes reported approaches by people presenting themselves as Israeli art students at federal facilities and employees' residences. Its authors considered organized intelligence gathering possible and reported that telephone numbers obtained from one person were linked to ongoing MDMA investigations. These are the report's investigative assessment and reported leads, not proof that every subject was an intelligence officer or that the activity concerned 9/11. It also records ordinary art-sales behavior and some approaches to whole neighborhoods. [DEA report copy](https://antiwar.com/rep/DEA_Report_redactedxx.pdf); [separately reproduced report in the appendix to Gerald Shea's memorandum, PDF pages 57–58](https://www.yale64.org/news/shea.pdf#page=57).

The appendix is contained in a private advocate's later memorandum; the advocate's hypothesis is not a finding by the 9/11 Commission or the DEA. Both report copies are third-party reproductions; this pass used accessible extracted text, not an authenticated DEA original or complete review of the 60-page document. No shared person, telephone number, employer or preattack contact between the Philadelphia interviews and the DEA inquiry has been demonstrated by these passages.

## Reported surveillance and warnings

In an October 1, 2002 [press release](https://www.presseportal.de/pm/9377/384837), *Die Zeit* reported that Mossad had monitored at least three future hijackers and passed U.S. officials a list of suspects. The release does not state that the list contained all 19 hijackers. The underlying list, surveillance records and information-sharing traffic were not located in this source review; the claims remain reported allegations.

The [DOJ inspector general's account](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter5.htm) describes CIA personnel's August 2001 review of earlier cables and U.S. travel records, a request to place Mihdhar and his travel companions on watchlists, and their August 24 watchlisting. This is a documented competing account of the steps leading to the watchlisting; it does not prove no Israeli information existed or resolve all intelligence-sharing questions.

The [Los Angeles Times' September 21, 2001 correction](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-sep-21-mn-48188-story.html) says its preceding account of a warning involving up to 200 militants was erroneous: its U.S. official's source was a British newspaper account, not independent information. The CIA denied that particular reported warning. These stories cannot be counted as independent corroboration; the correction does not resolve every possible Israeli warning.

## White House records lead

The National Archives' February 10, 2026 [presidential-records notice, request 2025-0218-F, PDF page 16](https://www.archives.gov/files/foia/pra-notifications/pdf/bush43/rn-plgwb-2026-023.pdf#page=16) lists 13 assets, ten withheld in full and two in part, with one proposed for opening in full and two in part. The underlying request used alternative terms including UMS, Israeli art students and unrelated names and subjects. The notice describes a security notice and press-guidance emails proposed for opening; it does not identify which search term matched any particular asset or establish that its contents concern intelligence operations. This notice records a proposed release; actual access, contents and later disposition were not verified in this pass.

## Tests that could strengthen or weaken the connection

1. Obtain the underlying FBI interview reports, job applications, dispatch and pay records, and dated company records. Compare named individuals and employers with DEA source records, controlling for spelling variants and uncertain identity.
2. Compare authenticated subscriber histories, phone lists, addresses and dates. A matching entry is a lead, not proof of a call, a shared operation, surveillance or intent. Preserve mismatches and ordinary business or social explanations.
3. Seek the DEA's originating records, later findings and any actual FBI/DEA cross-references; obtain *Die Zeit*'s underlying warning/list documents and the relevant CIA and FBI routing and watchlisting records. Seek negative, qualifying and inconclusive findings as well as supporting material.
4. Obtain the proposed White House releases and item-level descriptions. The mixed-scope notice alone cannot establish that any asset concerns UMS or the DEA inquiry.

No full name or telephone-number cross-match was performed in this bounded source review. No original report, carrier record or interagency message establishing a UMS–hijacker or UMS–Saudi support-network connection was located here. This report does not update or infer the disposition of any FOIA request.
