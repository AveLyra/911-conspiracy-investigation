# Urban Moving Systems and related records: November 5–6 source update

**Working research, November 6, 2026.** This document separates records, investigative leads, interpretations and unresolved allegations. It consolidates selected recent work on Urban Moving Systems (UMS), Max Movers, the detained movers, Saudi-linked assistance, the CIA information-sharing record and post-attack policy. The material reviewed does not establish that UMS planted explosives, coordinated with the hijackers or Saudi intermediaries, or acted under a plan to create a pretext for war. A finding not made in a bounded search is not proof a record never existed.

## FBI inquiries and potential connections

**Early and later inquiries.** A September 24, 2001 headquarters communication reports Newark and New York's early determination that the five detainees had no prior information about the attacks and were not actively engaged in clandestine intelligence activity in the United States. It is an account of field findings, not a second independent investigation. Newark's July 2003 closing account reports no factual or substantive circumstantial support for foreknowledge, while describing a separate FBI headquarters/intelligence-community analytical inquiry started in April 2002 and incomplete at the time. A 2004 evidence-retention memo identifies a pending redacted HQ matter; its identity and link to the 2002 inquiry are not visible. The eventual HQ assessment has not been located in the reviewed sources. [September 2001 communication, section 5 pp. 58–60](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=58); [2003 and 2004 communications, section 6 pp. 56–57 and 61](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=56).

An August 26, 2002 email describes an August 20–21 HQ visit and an expected report to Newark. It does not establish that a report was completed or that the visit and later analytical inquiry are the same. Administrative entries record an identified foreign-intelligence-service/terrorist front or cover organization and positive intelligence, but their subjects and supporting references are redacted. They do not visibly identify UMS or Mossad. A separate negative photographic comparison has redacted subjects and cannot be assumed to concern the detainees. Retrieve the original visit report and transmittal, underlying source serials, subject identities, final analysis and positive or negative comparisons. [Section 5 pp. 103–104, 109–110, 128–130](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=103).

**Notebooks and Classic.** An October 4, 2002 FBI communication says a contract linguist considered the translated notebooks, journals and address books personal and found nothing of foreign-intelligence significance. This negative opinion concerns the translated material reviewed, not every seized item or the final HQ assessment. A translation cover names WFO job **6643 / as02260f.nsd**; a separate questioned-document report names lab **020924003 LV**, notebook Q1 and sheets Q1.1–Q1.6, with writer identity unresolved. The two records are not shown to concern the same item. Full translation attachments and item-level crosswalks remain unlocated. [Section 6 pp. 5–8 and 36–37](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=5).

The 2001 FBI summary separately reports that a Classic International Movers number appeared in a detainee's notebook and that Miami believed an unnamed hijacker had used Classic. The source does not establish a call, a customer identity, the transaction or a shared operation. A separate undated list naming “Classic International” has not been shown to be the same notebook entry. Seek the Miami lead, actual notebook, subscriber and call records, customer documentation and negative as well as positive comparisons. [Section 5 p. 50](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=50); [related public source review](../ums-rooftop-affiliation-followup-2026-11-05/README.md).

**Exxon footage.** FBI laboratory records identify VHS tape **K1966 / AGBH009**, twelve input prints and an October 18, 2001 examination that digitized white-van frames with minimal improvement. Its transmittal uses **011001250 HC JD** and the examination sheet **011001250 JD**; another earlier draft used **HC ID**, an additional search variant rather than an established independent laboratory case. The report does not identify a van or validate the recorder's clock. An October 17 memo describes a *possible* match and makes its timing inference conditional; it does not visibly identify K1966, so the specimen crosswalk remains unproved. Seek the original tape, custody, time checks, output images and identification or exclusion. [Section 5 pp. 64–66 and 69–71](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=64); [New York serial 325](https://www.israellobby.org/urbanmoving/1169683-001%20---%20303A-NY-C237934-Serial%20325%20---%20Section%201%20%281037758%29.pdf).

## WTC card and moving-company work

A September 11 FBI property receipt lists a WTC visitor card in a black fanny pack. The police report's description of the van driver's bag makes an association plausible, but the receipt-to-bag join, cardholder, building numeral, issue date, use and purpose remain unproved. A receipt for a card does not establish building entry, a moving job or explosives placement. [Section 2 pp. 72–73 and 81](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-2.pdf#page=72).

NIST's June 2004 report says the Port Authority supplied WTC 1 and 2 **occupant** badge lists for its survey, including absent and former occupants and test entries. An entry on such a list would not alone prove attendance or access on a particular day; temporary-visitor and WTC 7 coverage remain unestablished. A contemporary security-system account separately describes visitor authorization and entry records. The continued existence, custody and releasability of records matching the seized card are unknown. Seek the original receipt and card image, issuing and host-tenant records, visitor entry logs and associated job documentation. [NIST SP 1000-5, vol. 6, Appendix O p. O-2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication1000-5v6.pdf); [2001 security account](https://www.ecmweb.com/content/article/20889492/towering-security).

The August 2001 UMS insurance fax names UMS and Max Movers together. An employee separately described related ownership and local/long-distance work; the fax is not proof of ownership. An April 2001 public trade report announced Zim's planned September relocation, weakening a claim that the move itself was a sudden secret evacuation. The actual carrier remains unidentified in the reviewed records. Seek Zim/Hunter vendor and payment files, dispatch, tenant moving approvals and loading/access records. [Insurance fax, section 2 p. 13](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-2.pdf#page=13); [April 2001 trade report](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/zim-moves-regional-head-office-from-new-york-to-norfolk).

## Insurance and regulator data

Public federal carrier histories list UMS and Max under separate carrier dockets and show two shared insurance policy numbers. This corroborates an insurance relationship, but named-insured schedules and endorsements are required to establish coverage or ownership. Federal authority-history, revocation and insurance tables have date fields that are not reconciled for the two companies. A contemporaneous UMS-only Hanover endorsement cannot be substituted for a shared-policy schedule. Original federal and New York notices, orders and endorsements were not found in this bounded search. [UMS insurance history](https://data.transportation.gov/resource/6sqe-dvqs.json?docket_number=MC320465); [Max insurance history](https://data.transportation.gov/resource/6sqe-dvqs.json?docket_number=MC398463); [UMS authority history](https://data.transportation.gov/resource/9mw4-x3tu.json?docket_number=MC320465); [Max authority history](https://data.transportation.gov/resource/9mw4-x3tu.json?docket_number=MC398463).

## Saudi and CIA records

A June 14, 2017 FBI communication reports that Omar al-Bayoumi received a Saudi General Intelligence Presidency stipend and describes reporting through Ambassador Bandar. The underlying source and reporting details are substantially redacted. This is evidence of a reported intelligence relationship, not a CIA recruitment order or proof of knowing participation in the attacks. No UMS–Bayoumi operational link has been established in the reviewed records. [FBI communication reproduced in Senate hearing pp. 55–60](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-118shrg53703/pdf/CHRG-118shrg53703.pdf).

The CIA inspector general's review reports that a January 2000 draft conveying information about Mihdhar was put on hold and not formally disseminated; it could neither confirm nor refute a cable's claim of informal FBI delivery. A separate defense-investigator declaration describes allegations of CIA-directed Saudi-mediated recruitment; those claims are not independently established by the hold. A documented hold does not establish its purpose or advance knowledge of an attack. [CIA OIG report, printed pp. 53–55](https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/cia/DOC_0006184107.pdf); [DOJ OIG chapter 5](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter5.htm).

The public 2024 plaintiffs' filing already described Bayoumi–Awlaki footage and reported earlier contact, so the allegation of an association was not first made public in 2026. Parties dispute its significance; a reported 1998/1999 recording-date discrepancy and the precise video crosswalk require original files and testimony. An FBI interview memorandum records an agent's recreational interpretation of paintball, which is relevant contrary evidence but does not establish the purpose of every event. Neither a social relationship nor a video establishes a UMS link. [2024 public filing, PDF pp. 241–242](https://www.motleyrice.com/sites/default/files/documents/AT_HR/9-11/20240802%20Redacted%20public%20averment.pdf); [2003 Commission interview memorandum](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/4172440/Document-12-Memorandum-for-the-Record-October-16.pdf).

## Physical samples and competing explanations

A September 2001 FBI memorandum records a dog alert, van-interior swabs and pending laboratory results. The alert and an “explosive residue” inventory label do not establish a positive chemical result. A later FBI production reviewed in this research reports disposal of specific physical sample barcodes and a separately inventoried group including notebooks. That production is not reproduced here; the reported disposal date of a communication is not an item-specific destruction date, and the record does not establish a final analytical result, destruction of every copy or a concealment motive. Seek the matched accession, controls, final positive/negative/inconclusive result, original disposition authority and surviving derivatives. [Public laboratory and custody review](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/README.md).

NIST concluded that impact and fires caused the tower collapses and said it did not test tower steel for explosive or thermite residues. UAF's WTC 7 study disputes NIST's fire-collapse explanation for that separate building; neither the lack of a residue test nor UAF's model alone establishes the method or an installer. FEMA documented unusual steel corrosion; a 2006 abstract suggests post-collapse rubble-fire exposure. The full methods and specimen crosswalk remain to be reviewed. Harrit and Millette examined different dust samples; their opposing interpretations are not a same-specimen replication. [NIST towers FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-towers-investigation); [UAF](https://www.wtc7report.org/); [FEMA Appendix C](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2017/05/09/AppendixC-fema403_apc.pdf); [corrosion abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1361/154770206X129006).

## Iraq and contracting

The 1998 Iraq Liberation Act states a regime-change policy while separately limiting implications about armed-force authority. The 9/11 Commission describes officials raising Iraq after the attacks and the president initially prioritizing Afghanistan. A later Defense inspector general criticized presentation of Iraq–al-Qaeda intelligence assessments as inappropriate but did not decide their validity. GAO concluded that KBR's November 2002 oil-planning order was outside the existing contract's scope; DOD disagreed. GAO assessed the subsequent sole-source award separately. Such records warrant scrutiny of later decision-making, but do not establish an authenticated pre-attack UMS–hijacker–Iraq-policy agreement. [1998 Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-105publ338/html/PLAW-105publ338.htm); [Commission report](https://www.9-11commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch10.htm); [DOD IG testimony](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-110shrg35438/html/CHRG-110shrg35438.htm); [GAO report](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a242676.html).

## What would resolve the central question

The most discriminating evidence would be authenticated records connecting a named person's WTC access, actual materials and installation to contemporaneous instructions or communications with other alleged participants. Original visitor records, employer/job records, final FBI analyses, complete translations, independent specimen testing and source-to-subject crosswalks can each narrow one link. Negative or ordinary explanations and incomplete searches must be preserved alongside affirmative findings; no one link proves the others.
