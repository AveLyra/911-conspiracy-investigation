# Public release status of the FBI van explosives test

As of October 6, 2026, this research did not locate a publicly accessible final chemical analysis of the samples taken from the Urban Moving Systems van occupied by the five Israeli nationals detained in New Jersey on September 11, 2001. The released scans establish a canine alert, sample collection, and a September 23 report that the final results were pending. Later records establish administrative closure of laboratory leads, but do not identify the chemical finding.

**The supported conclusion is that the result remains unlocated in the public sources examined.** It is not established that the FBI never completed testing, never released a result to any requester, or obtained a positive or negative chemical result.

This investigation concerns the van bearing New Jersey registration JRJ13Y. It does not treat other vans, photography examinations, handwriting examinations, or intelligence trace checks as the same test. It is a research record, separate from the NIST litigation record and current pleading packet. No request, contact, filing, or transmission resulted from this research.

Their official forms and internal consistency support their use, but the original agency production was not independently certified. Hash checks confirm the downloaded Internet Archive files match that archive’s metadata; they do not authenticate the archive against the FBI’s originals.

## The specific sample identifiers

The [FD-192 inventory](https://archive.org/details/DancingIsraelisFBIReport/fbi%20report%20section%201/page/n6/mode/1up) identifies property group **1B4**, the source vehicle **JRJ13Y**, and the owning office **Newark**.

| Item | Sample description | Evidence barcode |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Fabric submitted as an explosives-residue sample | E01889035 |
| 16 and 17 | Control swabs from an agent’s gloves and bomb suits | E01889036 |
| 34 | Blanket samples collected for explosives-residue examination | E01889037 |

Item 35 is described as vacuuming and grouped with other property under E01889578. The visible inventory does not establish that it was part of the same chemical submission, so it is an additional lead rather than a confirmed laboratory specimen.

The glove and suit samples are explicitly controls. Counting these four item numbers as four confirmed explosives findings is unsupported. The phrase describing a sample’s intended examination also cannot substitute for a test result. The barcode is an evidence identifier; a laboratory accession number linking it to a completed examination remains unlocated.

## Confidence and what would change the answer

**High confidence:** the date and pending-results wording are present in the September 2001 scan; the sample barcodes are present in the inventory; the July 2003 administrative laboratory-lead instruction is present; the alleged trace-result pages do not visibly identify the van’s chemical examination.

**Moderate confidence:** no identifiable final chemical result appears in the readable portions of the principal public collections searched. OCR has limits, and many pages and passages are withheld or redacted. The Section 6 deleted-page sheet lists 201 deleted pages, including privacy withholdings and duplicates; that total is not 201 proven laboratory records.

**Unknown:** whether a report was completed, what it concluded, whether a later requester received it privately, whether it remains in a separate laboratory record, and whether the sample barcodes were eventually destroyed or returned.

The strongest objection to a claim of nonrelease is that later productions can be released to a requester without being posted or indexed publicly. A final report might also be withheld or present in files outside the available mirror. Neither this search nor the 2001 pending status can exclude those possibilities.

A publicly accessible chemical report tied to JRJ13Y or the identified evidence barcodes would change the release conclusion. A verified later determination letter expressly describing release of that specific report would establish a release event even if the report itself remained unposted. Records expressly documenting nonexamination or cancellation would clarify the test’s disposition, but would not establish a chemical finding.
