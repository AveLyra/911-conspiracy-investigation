# WTC access and Exxon lead: source cross-check

November 7, 2026. This working review checks specific source pages relevant to UMS access and the New Jersey observation-site timeline. It does not identify a WTC job, establish that a particular van was UMS's, or show advance attack knowledge. The source page images and the original local research notes are preserved separately; the links below point to the existing public copies, not a new production.

## Observation-site vehicle

A redacted [FBI interview, section 2, PDF p. 82](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-2.pdf#page=82), records an interview on September 11, transcribed September 14, describing a white, windowless utility van with red electric-company lettering in a rear lot. The visible passage provides neither an exact time nor a plate. A separate September 15 account gives an approximately 8:15 a.m. estimate for a utility-marked van. Similarity justifies comparing the accounts, but redacted witness identity leaves it unresolved whether the accounts concern one witness, two witnesses, or the same vehicle. Neither identifies the UMS van. See also the [earlier sighting review](../wtc-hijacker-source-recheck-2026-11-06/README.md).

## Fuel and call entries

An [FBI inventory, section 3, PDF p. 54](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-3.pdf#page=54), lists Gulf receipts attributed to UMS and plate JRJ13Y dated August 17, August 24 and September 6, not September 11. The original receipts were not obtained in this check, and the listing cannot establish how many station visits occurred on September 11. A separate [inventory entry, section 1, PDF p. 128](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=128), lists a Gulf business card and a separate receipt without a visible date, time or amount. It does not establish that the receipt is the one referenced in the FBI's later timing account.

The [FBI page at section 1, PDF p. 45](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=45), lists an incoming 2:14 p.m. and an outgoing 9:01 a.m. entry under telephone-call headings. They are not purchase receipts or independently verified carrier records. Names and numbers are redacted; this page does not establish the participants' identities or locations. Original carrier and account records, with time-zone and clock information, would be needed for a comparison.

## Badge and building-record leads

The [New York City health-registry manual, PDF p. 35](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/911health/downloads/pdf/wtc/wtc-datafile-manual.pdf#page=35), describes transfer in May 2004 of a Port Authority list of 95,442 people issued badges for one or more WTC buildings during the preceding five years. A badge-holder list is not an individual entry log and does not establish that temporary visitor cards are covered or that the list survives today.

[NIST's June 2004 progress report, PDF pp. 99–100](../../wtc7/contracting-access-custody-audit/nist-sp1000-5v1-source01.pdf#page=99), reports receipt of elevator/escalator contract and maintenance information, while separately reporting the loss of WTC 1, 2 and 7 construction and maintenance logs in the collapse. It does not establish loss of all loading-dock, visitor or tenant records.

The reported mover for Zim's relocation remains unidentified. The original Exxon tape, station clock checks, purchase receipt and final vehicle comparison remain unlocated in the reviewed material. A laboratory enhancement reported minimal improvement; that result alone does not identify or exclude a van. Newark's negative foreknowledge finding is relevant counterevidence within the scope of its inquiry. The highest-value tests are original visitor and vendor records, tape and comparison worksheets, calibrated timestamps, fuel-account records, and unredacted witness-identity crosswalks. A missing record alone does not establish concealment.
