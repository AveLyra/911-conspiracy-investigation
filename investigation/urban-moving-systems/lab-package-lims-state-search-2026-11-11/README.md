# Van-sample package copy, LIMS and New Jersey search

Source check, November 11, 2026. The supplied research identifies useful retrieval paths, not a chemical result or a UMS–WTC connection. The six-page underlying report itself was not supplied in this review; its quoted claims were checked against the records identified below.

## Package copy and transfer

The [FD-192, PDF p. 3](../fbi-notebook-custody-2026-11-06/raw/fbi-selected-disposition-and-inventory-pages.pdf#page=3), is marked “Package Copy,” with a handwritten consolidation reference to an EC dated September 12, 2002. It lists property group 1B4 and fabric, control-swab and blanket sample barcodes. The date of acquisition shown is September 12, 2001; the form also has a July 29, 2003 print timestamp. These dates must not be conflated. A package copy does not by itself show laboratory transmission.

The [September 12, 2002 Newark evidence transfer request, section 5 PDF p. 136](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=136), is a possible match for the EC date. It lists multiple 1B groups including 1B25, with destination and other information redacted. It does not visibly name 1B4, the three sample barcodes, or a laboratory. A separate [handwritten entry, section 5 PDF p. 132](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=132), equates 1B25/E01889034 with 1B4/E01889578; because groups contain multiple items, that entry does not establish the transfer of each sample, identify a chemical examination, or prove the two EC references are the same document. The same date and related grouping justify a targeted crosswalk inquiry, while coincidence, a different contemporaneous EC or other administrative grouping remain possible.

Seek the complete EC referenced on the package copy, underlying 1B25-to-1B4 consolidation documentation, item-level movement and FD-192 histories, and receiving-location records. A contemporaneous item-level match would support the proposed administrative link; conflicting case IDs, subject or inventory history would weaken it.

## FBI laboratory search routes

The [2017 FBI LIMS privacy assessment](https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/pias/pia-laboratory-information-management-system-lims.pdf#page=16), Appendix A, defines **Historical** as submissions migrated from a pre-LIMS Laboratory Division Microsoft Access database. Its [Appendix B](https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/pias/pia-laboratory-information-management-system-lims.pdf#page=19) defines the Evidence Agency # in management reports as the contributor's evidence identifier, with FBI 1B and barcodes as examples. Its capabilities and migration coverage are documented as of 2017; inclusion, retention, current function and searchability of this specific case are unverified. A missing LIMS result would not establish no examination. Ask for any existing matching Historical submission, Evidence Agency # report, predecessor database records and associated legacy electronic case files alongside lab-unit and paper files; do not assume barcode indexing was complete.

## New Jersey State Police decision

In [Lawyers Committee for 9/11 Inquiry v. New Jersey State Police](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/appellate-division-unpublished/2020/a1204-18.html), the unpublished January 8, 2020 decision describes a 2017 request including fabric and blanket sample records and laboratory analyses. It recounts state-police certifications and the court's conclusion that the agency conducted a reasonable search. The forensic photographer lacked knowledge of the samples; a logbook entry produced by agreement concerned unspecified film and was not clearly responsive to the requested van photographs. The appellate court affirmed. The opinion concerns an NJSP search, not a determination of FBI laboratory holdings or whether these samples tested positive, negative, inconclusive or at all. Plaintiff's allegations in the opinion are not judicial findings that explosive residue was identified.

## Implication and records to pursue

Prioritize the original-to-consolidated evidence mapping and any matched laboratory submission, receipt, specimen identifier, report, cancellation and return trail. The existing FBI request seeks submissions, accession, results and custody broadly; these system names and the dated EC are more specific locators, not proof of an accepted search scope. Any results must be assessed with controls, chain of custody and detection limits. None of these findings establishes a final chemical result, deliberate concealment, or an explosives installation.
