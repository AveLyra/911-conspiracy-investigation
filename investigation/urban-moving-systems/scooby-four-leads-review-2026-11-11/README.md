# Scooby follow-up: visitor card, Classic and FBI headquarters

Review date: November 11, 2026. All four requested chat responses were available. The chat-access metadata did not expose a reliable indication of whether any response used Deep Research. This report distinguishes the chat's leads from checks against the locally preserved FBI and police scans. A complete independently retrievable copy of each chat's supplemental report was not available in this review.

## Visitor card and moving work

- The [FBI receipt, section 2 PDF p. 81](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-2.pdf#page=81), lists a black fanny pack containing a WTC visitor card. Its received-from field is redacted; the receipt does not visibly identify the cardholder, building, issuing office, date or use.
- The [East Rutherford police report, PDF pp. 2 and 4](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/police.pdf#page=2), says the driver was handling a black pouch, and later refers to an attached FBI receipt for that bag's contents. The report supports a proposed association with the FBI's fanny-pack receipt, but the reviewed copies do not independently prove the specific attachment-to-receipt match. Even a confirmed bag match would not necessarily identify who owned or used the card.
- The reviewed September 14 employee interview identifies boxes being prepared for an overdue Manhattan delivery; the customer, address, assigned crew, vehicle and actual completion remain unknown. It supplies a dispatch-record target, not evidence of a WTC assignment. [FBI section 5, PDF pp. 21–22](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=21).

**Tests:** obtain the full police-to-FBI receipt package, card image or transcription, issuing authority and date, visitor-system crosswalk, and original job, dispatch and tenant records. An identified non-WTC card or a dated unrelated job would weaken the specific WTC-access inference; an authenticated matching access and job trail would strengthen it, while leaving the alleged explosives role a separate question.

## Classic number and Miami lead

[FBI section 3, PDF p. 25](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-3.pdf#page=25), contains a typed entry for **Classic International**, 888-461-6683, on a list bearing handwritten “Computer #11 Item A.” Its compiler, date and relationship to the separately reported notebook entry are not established. The list does not demonstrate an outgoing call, subscriber identity at a relevant time, a business transaction, or a connection to a hijacker.

[FBI section 5, PDF p. 132](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=132), has a handwritten cross-reference “1B25 E01889034 = 1B4 E01889578.” The latter package contains multiple items; the cross-reference by itself does not identify the notebook page bearing the reported Classic number. FBI reports describing Miami's belief and a number in a seized notebook leave the underlying Miami basis, customer and disposition unresolved. Repeated summaries may share a single source.

**Tests:** recover the originating Miami lead, customer and transaction records, original notebook image and translation, full evidence inventories, and relevant call records with dates and participants. A verified shared person or transaction would support a narrower connection; a documented negative comparison with known search inputs would weigh against it. Neither an address-book entry nor a moving job alone establishes coordinated attacks.

## Headquarters inquiry and contrary findings

The [July 2003 Newark closure, section 6 PDF pp. 56–57](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=56), reported a distinct FBI headquarters/intelligence-community inquiry initiated in April 2002 that was then unfinished. The [August 2002 email, section 5 PDF p. 130](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=130), promises a report of a headquarters visit, but the visible text does not establish that the visit was for that same inquiry. The [April 2004 record, section 6 PDF p. 61](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=61), records a pending redacted headquarters matter; whether it is the inquiry described in 2003 is unestablished. No final headquarters assessment was located in this bounded review.

Newark's closure reported no factual or substantive circumstantial basis for foreknowledge. That is relevant contrary evidence within the scope of that inquiry, not a finding that every possible issue had been resolved. The chat also identified an October 2002 linguist's assessment that reviewed materials lacked foreign-intelligence significance; its scope must not be extended to material not reviewed. Redacted front/cover and positive-intelligence entries cannot be assigned to UMS, Mossad, or the Saudi support network without identifying the underlying subjects and results.

**Tests:** request the promised visit report, final headquarters assessment and attachments; preserve distinct case identifiers, dates, search inputs and negative as well as positive results. A confirmed source connecting the companies or individuals to intelligence tasking would be materially different from a redacted database category. None of the sources examined here establishes coordination with a Saudi support network.

## Van samples, results and disposition

The [January 27, 2014 FBI disposition excerpt, pp. 1–2](../fbi-notebook-custody-2026-11-06/raw/fbi-selected-disposition-and-inventory-pages.pdf), reports evidence destroyed or disposed of and lists E01889035, E01889036 and E01889037. Both pages were visually checked. The date is that of the communication; individual execution dates and the complete authority, method and custody trail are not established. The first page describes legal consultation, but does not establish that all requirements were met.

The [property inventory, section 1 p. 7](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=7), labels fabric and blanket samples for explosives-residue examination and identifies glove and bomb-suit swabs as controls. The controls do not establish the detainees possessed bomb suits. The September 2001 account says final results were pending; no final chemistry report, laboratory accession or barcode-to-specimen crosswalk was located. The proper result is **unlocated**, not a positive, negative or inconclusive result. The 2003 discontinuance of administrative leads is not a chemical finding. A report of vehicle interior swabs has not been matched to the three barcodes.

The chat flagged a possible discrepancy: the September narrative describes moving blankets as absent, while the inventory lists blanket samples. These could refer to different objects or circumstances; the collection records are needed. It also located a [contemporary U.S. Attorneys’ Bulletin](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/usao/legacy/2006/06/30/usab4905.pdf#page=24) discussing laboratory and specimen identifiers. Requesting the field-barcode-to-accession crosswalk, laboratory submissions, signed findings, controls and custody/return documentation offers a testable path; this does not establish those records survive. The chat's further references to evidence-control procedures and release-log records remain leads to verify separately.

Routine post-case disposal, incomplete testing, and misfiling are possible explanations. Improper disposal or concealment requires evidence of a preservation obligation, conflicting instruction, altered finding, or other specific misconduct. Disposal does not establish the test result, destruction of laboratory records, or a link to WTC material or named persons.
