## Manhattan mural-truck account

The 2003 Mineta Transportation Institute study [Saving City Lifelines](https://transweb.sjsu.edu/sites/default/files/02-06.pdf#page=28), PDF p. 28, printed p. 20, describes a mural-bearing panel truck stopped near a temporary Manhattan emergency command post and says NYPD searched it and found an innocent delivery vehicle. The preceding paragraph places a mobile command center near Sixth Avenue and 36th Street. The study describes the mural as an airplane flying into the WTC and says the vehicle was rented to a group; it does not give a plate, rental contract, incident number, exact time, photo, name of the carrier, or name or direct affiliation of any occupant. It does not identify the truck as UMS or connect it to the five New Jersey detainees. The report is a later institutional reconstruction; the excerpt has no incident-specific source citation. We have not inspected the incident report, dispatch audio, vehicle or artwork, bomb-squad records, interviews or the report's underlying notes. Thus the exact mural and the asserted clearance are reported conclusions, not independently verified physical observations in this review.

The [FBI's separate 2003 Newark closure](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/main/investigation/urban-moving-systems/fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-6.pdf#page=53) and related investigation describe a different detention in New Jersey. The locations and narratives differ, but without identifiers we cannot conclusively identify or exclude a shared vehicle, person, or relation. Do not join the mural incident to UMS based on resemblance, nationality or internet repetition, or assert the delivery account conclusively settles every separate report of a suspicious van. Seek contemporaneous NYPD complaint and bomb-squad records, dispatch and incident timestamps, original artwork photographs, vehicle plate/VIN/rental transaction, identification and disposition, and compare them with authenticated UMS van identifiers. The already submitted NYPD camera request seeks recordings and comparison records, not expressly this distinct incident's complete police file; do not assume it will cover it. No new request is sent.

## Philadelphia customer and travel record — visually checked

I rendered and inspected [FBI Philadelphia Serial 357, 09/21/2001, local PDF pp. 2–4](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/irmep-13.pdf), building on the prior OCR-only [access/lab/hijacker cycle](../developing-theory-2026-11-06/README.md). This is an FBI communication summarizing interviews and contacts, not the underlying customer statements, toll receipt or independently verified dispatch records.

On printed p. 3 (PDF p. 3), the account says two men left New Jersey the evening of September 10 for Ohio, were stopped in Pennsylvania at 11:30 p.m., and delivered furniture in Worthington on September 11. It says agents contacted the Ohio customer, who verified a redacted subject's statements. The same page reports a Chicago Skyway toll receipt from September 11 and an Illinois customer who told investigators that two men delivered furniture about 10 p.m. that day. Names and exact source attribution are redacted; the report contains the customer's description but the ethnicity assigned by a witness is not proof of identity. On printed p. 4 the FBI says one interviewee initially denied being in Chicago; the report raises limited English as a possible reason, not an established explanation. It also reports employment-related inconsistencies and a subsequent admission, together with the bureau's later assessment that the subject lacked advance knowledge. Polygraph findings in the same document are not reliable independent proof of truth or deception and do not determine the conclusion.

**Effect:** The released FBI page image corroborates that investigators reported customer contacts and a toll-receipt lead, supporting a real commercial-delivery alternative and the specific travel narrative. It does not establish an alibi for the five separately detained New Jersey men, every UMS employee, other dates or any WTC access. Nor does one interviewee's inconsistent response establish a plot. Obtain the actual customer FD-302s and contemporaneous notes, deliveries, payment and dispatch files, stop and toll documentation, and an identifier crosswalk before attributing a particular act or building a complete route.
