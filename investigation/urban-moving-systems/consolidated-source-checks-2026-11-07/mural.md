# Manhattan mural-truck and UMS van: identifier search

November 7, 2026. Targeted search of the 9/11 Commission/NARA finding aid, the Mineta study, public source leads and the preserved East Rutherford and FBI page images. No original NYPD incident or bomb-squad report, original mural-truck rental agreement, authenticated mural-truck photograph or agency-authenticated recording of the specific dispatch was located. This is not proof those records do not exist. The four agencies' live correspondence was not checked in this cycle.

## UMS van: directly examined records

The [East Rutherford preliminary police report, PDF p. 2](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/police.pdf#page=2), incident CSRR 014157, records September 11 at 15:56 on the Route 3 east service road, mile 7.9. The officer describes the broadcast as a white 2000 Chevrolet van with registration JYJ13Y, then states that he observed rear plate **JRJ13Y** and sought confirmation of the one-letter discrepancy. Five persons were detained. These are the officer's report and reported broadcast, not a direct examination of the vehicle by this reviewer.

The [September 13 FBI FD-302, section 1 PDF p. 80](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-1.pdf#page=80) describes receipt of 76 black-and-white photographs and negatives from the New Jersey State Police forensic unit in connection with a white Chevrolet van bearing **JRJ-13Y**, listed to UMS, VIN **1GCEG15W4Y1142815**. It calls the vehicle a **2001** model, whereas the contemporaneous East Rutherford report calls the broadcast van a **2000**. Both documents visibly use the same registration for the stopped/investigated van. The year conflict and provenance of the vehicle data require the original title, registration and inspection records; do not silently choose a year. A receipt for photographs does not establish that every image depicts the van, that the images survive, or what a photograph shows; this cycle did not examine the 76 negatives or prints.

## Manhattan incident: bounded comparison

The [Mineta 2003 study, printed p. 20, PDF p. 28](https://transweb.sjsu.edu/sites/default/files/02-06.pdf#page=28) reports a mural-bearing panel truck near a temporary Manhattan command post, a rental to a group and a police/bomb-squad examination. It says investigators treated it as an innocent delivery vehicle. The preceding paragraph describes the command bus dispatched to Sixth Avenue and 36th Street; the paragraph does not state a precise stop address or time and does not connect the vehicle to UMS, give a plate, VIN, rental agency or individual names, or identify its source for the passage. The reported rental and examination are not the underlying records. Its conclusion is a secondary institutional account, not a chemical test report.

A separately circulated [third-party report of a purported police-radio clip](https://911warroom.com/kendoc/mossad-truck-bombs/) places a described mural truck on King Street between Sixth and Seventh and links an archived `firehouse.com/audio/wtc10.wav`; it is not itself authenticated NYPD dispatch or the underlying report. We did not obtain and listen to an authenticated, complete original recording. The King Street and 36th Street-area descriptions have **not** been shown to describe the same incident; the sources differ in location and specificity. Nor can the quoted account reliably identify a vehicle, prove an actual explosion, or resolve the Mineta report's conclusion. Treat the King Street incident and the Mineta incident as possible, unjoined leads, rather than calling them definitively the same or different incidents.

The source records do not yield a match and do not exclude prior travel or separate business ties. A matching plate/VIN, named renter or vehicle/operator record could support an identification; reliably different identifiers, a complete rental/custody chronology or a well-documented other vehicle would weigh against it. Overlapping descriptions such as white van or panel truck cannot establish a match. We did not authenticate any image sometimes circulated online as the mural truck; some sources describe a computer-generated reconstruction. Do not use such imagery as a vehicle photograph without provenance.

## Newly identified archive search targets

The [National Archives 9/11 Commission finding aid, PDF p. 134](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/9-11/finding-aid.pdf#page=134), Box 26, lists item **00098**: 14 NYPD First Division dispatch tapes, 08:45–19:10; **00099**: 14 Special Operations Division dispatch tapes, 08:46–19:00; **00100**: 15 citywide dispatch tapes, 08:45–19:15; and **00101A**: 12 of 45 NYPD Ground Zero video tapes from September 11–20. The finding aid labels these special media withdrawn **from the textual records**. That designation is not proof of destruction, denial, public access, digital availability, or that any particular tape contains the incident. Determine present custody, preservation, access restrictions, tape index, timestamps and transcript before review; do not represent these recordings as retrieved or heard. The [same finding aid](https://www.archives.gov/files/research/9-11/finding-aid.pdf#page=132) lists item 00040, a 9/11–12/31/2001 tape-reproduction request log, as a separate possible locator.

NYPD or NYC emergency management may hold incident, complaint or SPRINT/ICAD reports, dispatch logs, bomb-squad request and outcome, evidence photos, tow/inventory, interviews, registration and rental documents, and records of transfers to prosecutors, FBI or Commission. Custody is unconfirmed. A researcher [claims](https://mybigfatanti-zionistlife.blogspot.com/2012/04/the-fbi-nypd-and-9-11-2001-king-street.html) prior FBI/NYPD no-records responses, but this pass did not verify the underlying agency letters, search terms or agency search scope; do not treat this as a verified response or proof of a cover-up.
