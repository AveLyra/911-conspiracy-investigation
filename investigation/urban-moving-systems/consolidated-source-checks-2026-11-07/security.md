# WTC cameras, canine deployments and power-down: records search

November 6, 2026. Scope: targeted public-source and local-tracker search, not an exhaustive archive search. Separate firsthand accounts, extant footage, original operational records, and inference. A record not located in this search is not proof that it was destroyed or withheld.

## Cameras and van

A 2001 [security-system installer account](https://www.ecmweb.com/content/article/20889492/towering-security) describes cameras covering areas including the plaza, lobbies and streets, and tape recording. It does not establish survival, field of view, clock accuracy or the content of a particular recording. We did not locate original WTC security-camera footage, an index of tapes, access and retention records, or any comparison identifying a UMS vehicle near the complex.

The official [Moussaoui trial exhibit P200015](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/prosecution/P200015.html) is an actual compilation of 14 publicly available video clips; its [trial stipulation](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/prosecution/ST00004.pdf) describes the clips, including street-level scenes after the attacks. The exhibit can be graphic. We inspected the exhibit catalogue and stipulation, not the video itself; the material is not established here to be WTC security-camera footage, and no claim about the presence or absence of a UMS vehicle follows. The [September 11 Digital Archive](https://911digitalarchive.org/collections/153/) also catalogues an eyewitness video. These are potential comparison sources, not a completed vehicle search.

The FBI Exxon tape K1966 concerns a **New Jersey** camera, not a WTC camera. The lab reported minimal enhancement improvement; the separate possible van match and timing remain conditional and unverified. [Local custody and comparison review](../research-update-2026-11-06/README.md). An early white utility-marked van near the New Jersey observation site remains unidentified. No footage located in this cycle connects UMS to WTC access, explosives or hijackers.

**Follow-up:** identify custodians and seek surviving original WTC and surrounding-street recordings for relevant dates, camera maps and field-of-view diagrams, timestamp synchronization and clock checks, recording schedules, tape inventories, transfers, retention/disposition records, and any police/FBI vehicle or plate comparisons. Obtain time and location metadata before drawing conclusions from a negative visual search. Confirm whether each potential source was actually recording and whether it survives.

## Canine deployment

Port Authority police officer David Lim's [March 2003 written statement](https://9-11commission.gov/hearings/hearing1/witness_lim.htm) places him and his explosives-detection dog Sirius at WTC 2 on September 11 and describes his job checking vehicles at the truck dock. In [oral testimony](https://9-11commission.gov/archive/hearing1/9-11Commission_Hearing_2003-03-31.htm), he said they had searched numerous trucks that morning. This is evidence that at least one team was working, not a complete deployment roster or proof all access points were screened.

A [September 12, 2001 Los Angeles Times wire brief](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-sep-12-mn-44946-story.html) attributes to guard Daria Coard a report of heightened security, bomb dogs being removed, and Tuesday as the first day without extra security. A [2007 reproduction attributed to a September 12 Newsday article](https://groups.google.com/g/alt.sports.football.pro.ne-patriots/c/_MAllntkkf0) says the dogs were removed Thursday (September 6 if the attributed publication date is correct), and quotes the same witness. The original Newsday page has not been independently verified; these accounts do not provide two independent witnesses. The [Loose Change Second Edition transcript](https://www.scripts.com/script/loose_change_2nd_edition_12812) places both dogs and the guard shift change on September 6; the reproduced report differentiates the Thursday dog change from the witness's Tuesday account of extra security. Check the original article and film version before treating this as a settled contradiction.

Lim's testimony contradicts a blanket claim that all explosives-detection dogs had been removed from the WTC before the attacks. It does not resolve a possible withdrawal of supplemental dogs or a change in private security on other days or posts. Neither account establishes advance knowledge, a security stand-down to enable an attack, or UMS involvement.

**Follow-up:** contemporaneous PAPD canine assignment and handler logs, security alert and threat notices, command orders and cancellations, private guard schedules and post orders, and records identifying actual coverage and its changes September 6–11. The underlying deployment records were not located in this cycle.

## Reported weekend power-down

A [2004 reproduced account attributed to Scott Forbes](https://www.serendipity.li/wot/forbes01.htm) describes a September 8–9, 2001 South Tower power-down, reporting an approximately 36-hour period and a reason given to him involving cabling. His further account about security systems is a witness claim; we have not located maintenance work orders or independent records proving what floors, circuits, cameras, recording systems, electronic locks or backups were affected. A [later attributed interview](https://killtown.blogspot.com/2005/12/scott-forbes-interview.html) describes his workplace on the 97th floor and approximates the scope of the outage. The installer account above describes redundancy and backup, but system-level design alone cannot establish which devices remained operational during a particular shutdown.

The allegation is a useful lead, not established proof of unrestricted access, explosive installation or a role for UMS. **Follow-up:** contemporaneous WTC 2 electrical switching and work orders, approved outage and tenant notices, contractor logs, floor and circuit schedules, UPS and backup records, security-system power dependencies, alarms, access logs, restoration records and related incident reports. Relevant custodians may include the Port Authority, facility management, utility, contractors and affected tenants; custody and survival have not been verified.
