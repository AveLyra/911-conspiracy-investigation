# Zim's relocation, Urban Moving Systems and the FBI address lists

**Source review, November 5, 2026.** A 2005 article alleges that Urban Moving Systems (UMS) moved Zim-American Israeli Shipping from the World Trade Center's North Tower. This search did not locate a Zim moving contract, invoice, dispatch sheet, building access record or independent firsthand account identifying UMS as the mover. A separate FBI form identifies two address and entry-date lists that were said to be attached, but the lists and any comparison results remain unlocated in the material checked. These are substantive leads, not established operational connections.

## Zim's move: allegation and contemporaneous records

Wayne Madsen's September 21, 2005 article asserts that UMS carried out an emergency move of Zim from the North Tower. The article does not give an identifiable firsthand witness, job date, invoice, contract or dispatch record for that assertion. [Online Journal article, reproduced PDF](https://www.gbppr.net/nytimes_ww2/09-21-05_Madsen.pdf); [separate reproduction](https://www.mygen.com/users/ufo/9-11%20Memorandum%20Commission%20Select%20Committees%202004%20-%20Israeli%20Surveillance%20-%201z.htm). The PDF returned a gateway error during this review; the relevant passage was checked in the indexed PDF excerpt and the other reproduction. Repetitions of the same Madsen claim are not independent corroboration.

Zim's planned relocation was public months before the attacks. [American Shipper reported on April 3, 2001](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/zim-moves-regional-head-office-from-new-york-to-norfolk) that its U.S. corporate office would move to Norfolk in September, reporting the company's business reasons. The Norfolk developer [reproduces an April 2001 Virginian-Pilot report](https://www.geesgroup.com/portfolio-create/zim-international-shipping-co/) locating Zim on the 16th floor of 1 WTC and reporting an expected September 4 opening. Neither identifies the physical mover. These accounts undercut treating the move alone as a sudden, secret response to a warning; they do not by themselves resolve the actual work schedule or the identity of each contractor.

The October 18, 2001 [Journal of Commerce report, reproduced here](https://s3.amazonaws.com/911timeline/2001/journalofcommerce101801.html), says Zim relocated headquarters shortly before September 11 but retained 20 people assigned to the WTC office, 10 of whom were there that day and escaped. A [reproduction of a September 13 Virginian-Pilot report](https://stevenwarran.blogspot.com/2012/05/zim-israel-navigation-company.html) quotes Zim's president saying the company had people in New York but none in the Trade Center. Those accounts conflict on remaining personnel; neither establishes that every Zim employee left the building before September 11. The precise number and the scope and timing of each move remain to be established.

## A relocation-management lead, not a mover identification

A third-party [reproduction of an April 25, 2001 Real Estate Weekly report](https://stevenwarran.blogspot.com/2012/05/zim-israel-navigation-company.html) identifies **Hunter Management Corp.** as owner's representative for the Zim relocation, with responsibility for coordinating development and the transition. The same compilation reproduces a November 2001 report attributing a later Staten Island project to Hunter. These are useful leads for identifying retained project files, vendor selection, invoices and subcontractors. The reports do not identify the company that transported furniture and equipment. Hunter's role as representative does not itself refute UMS subcontracting, and it does not establish UMS involvement.

Independent records naming another carrier, with a complete account of subcontractors and the relevant work, would weigh against the Madsen claim. An authenticated invoice or access record naming UMS and Zim, dated to the WTC job, would support it; evidence of a job alone would not show explosive cargo, placement, advance knowledge or cooperation in the attacks. No such identifying record was found in the targeted searches performed here.

## The FBI's two address and entry-date lists

The original released scan of [section 5, PDF page 28](../fbi-van-lab-release-2026-10-06/raw/section-5.pdf#page=28), is an FD-302 transcribed **September 16, 2001** concerning a **September 15** Newark investigation. Visual inspection confirms that it describes compiled current and prior addresses for identified hijackers, suspected associates and the five UMS subjects. It says two attachments formed part of the FD-302: a list of addresses and U.S. entry dates for hijackers and suspected associates, and a comparable list for the UMS subjects. Some information sources are redacted. The next page in this release starts another interview; searches of the available section OCR and public sources did not locate the two lists themselves.

The document records compilation and intended attachment; it does **not** report that a comparison was completed or produced a match. Its absence from these released pages does not prove the lists were destroyed, never released to anyone, or unavailable elsewhere. The scan is a publicly circulated copy of an FBI record rather than an independently authenticated FBI master. The list contents, criteria, source data and any positive, negative or inconclusive follow-up remain unknown.

## Other records and search limits

The prior research note identified a [CIA response to a 2007 request](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/DOC_0001500712.pdf) that listed Zim, UMS and other subjects and gave a neither-confirm-nor-deny response. Those were the requester's subjects, not an agency finding of a connection. In this pass the CIA document URL redirected to its reading-room landing page; the result is retained as prior reported request history, not a freshly inspected source.

The [INTERPOL Washington FY 2007 FOIA log](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/interpol-washington/legacy/2012/04/30/foia_log_2007.pdf) separately records a Zim request, 2007-0166, and a UMS request, 2007-0168, each marked “No Record.” These are log entries for that office's handling, not proof that other agencies had no records and not a substantive finding about either company.

This bounded search does not establish that the Zim moving contract, WTC records or FBI attachments no longer exist. The highest-value next records are Zim and Hunter's 2001 relocation procurement and subcontractor files; Port Authority or building-manager tenant move approvals, insurance certificates, freight-elevator and loading-dock records; and the FBI lists plus source records and any comparison outcome. Requesters should distinguish original contemporaneous records from agency summaries and later retellings. No agency contact or new fee authorization resulted from this review.
