October 26, 2026. Local research follow-up to the four leads in the user's
message. Sources, earlier assessments, redactions and conflicting observations
remain unchanged. These are newly located records in this follow-up, not claims
that the records themselves were recently created or first discovered here.

## Result

The most consequential addition is an actual **October 18, 2001 forensic-video
examination report**, not just a request for analysis. It reports minimal image
enhancement results and identifies the VHS, prints and CD-R outputs. An earlier
FAA comparison memo, contemporary witness interview reports, a January 2002
computer-image receipt and sample inventory now provide more precise record
chains to test. Several apparent timing and record-description discrepancies
remain unresolved. No original video/CD contents, chemical result, computer
examination findings or White House message body were recovered in this pass.

The evidence establishes what these public copies say. It does not independently
authenticate their agency custody or reproduce the historical comparisons. No
verified preparation of false al-Qaeda attribution, attack participation, or
WTC 7 operational connection was established.

## 1. Exxon tape: a request-to-result chain is now identifiable

P5 pp. 64–66 is a September 28, 2001 request from Newark to Quantico FAVIAU.
It describes an FD-340a envelope containing an original surveillance tape and
12 color photographs. The stated purpose is to identify distinctive markings
on **two white vans** from an Exxon station near the business. Page 66 specifies
the event-on-tape interval **09/11/2001, 08:29:00 to approximately 08:31:00**.
The recording/acquisition-method text immediately following is redacted under
b7E. It does not expose a clock-calibration procedure, exact camera position,
camera channel or recorder identity.

P5 pp. 69–71 supplies the October 18 response referencing that September 28
communication and receipt on October 1:

- **K1966:** TDK T-160 VHS, marked **AGBH009**.
- **K1967–K1978:** 12 submitted color prints.
- The transmittal's laboratory number is **011001250 HC JD**; the examination
  sheet prints **011001250 JD**. Both recorded forms are retained.
- FAVIAU located and digitized the best white-van frames, applied brightness,
  contrast, sharpening and unsharp masking, and described the results as minimal.
- Outputs were FUJI prints and CD-Rs. The transmittal describes three sets of
  13 prints and three CD-Rs sent with the submitted material to an Explosives
  Unit examiner; two sets/two CD-Rs were for New York. A separate paragraph
  describes two sets of 13 prints/two CD-Rs for Newark and a retained file CD-R.

This identifies an examination and concrete output/custody targets. The visible
report **does not claim a successful identification of either van** or certify
the surveillance clock. Sending images through an Explosives Unit examiner is
not a chemical detection finding. Submitted prints and generated sets have
different counts; this is a documented input/output distinction, not proof of
missing or substituted frames.

**Still needed:** K1966 and its original acquisition envelope; source recorder,
camera/channel/location and time checks; K1967–K1978; the 13-image output set,
CD-R directory/file hashes and examiner notes; any subsequent vehicle-match
decision. The time printed in the request must not become a calibrated historical
timestamp or proof of pre-impact positioning without those joins.

## 2. Helicopter/FAA comparison: originals are more specific than the summary

P3 p. 23 is an FD-302 for September 17, 2001, lead **NK 2405**, recording receipt
of two videocassette copies from Helicopters Inc. at Linden Airport. Its index is:

| Tape position, not historical time | Recorded source label |
| --- | --- |
| 00:00–21:00 | N8BQ, Metro Traffic |
| 21:00–25:40 | N42F, Metro Traffic / WABC-TV |
| 25:40–end | N27M, WCBS-TV |

P3 p. 22 contains a related pilot interview: covering 880 AM traffic, seeing the
first impact, proceeding toward the WTC and hovering near the Holland Tunnel,
then leaving after the second impact. Radio and television affiliations must
not be interchanged; the receipt describes a compilation, not one continuous
camera clock. The repeated receipt in P5 p. 40 is an OCR locator for the same
document, not independent corroboration.

P5 pp. 62–63 describes an **FD-340-enclosed ATC CD-ROM** containing selected
continuous flight-path records for AAL11, UAL175, AAL77 and UAL93. Page 62's
header says **09/23/2001**; page 63's continuation says **09/19/2001**. Both are
preserved. Page 63 reports:

- The pictured helicopter was **most likely N8BQ**.
- It was in the relevant vicinity from about **08:50 until 09:00**, after which
  it moved southwest out of the photograph's field of view.
- Comparing the burn progression with N8BQ video led to an opinion that the
  photograph was taken around **09:00**.
- ATC radar information was **not viewable from 09:03 to 09:07**.
- The text prints **08:46:35am EST** and **12:46:35am GMT**. Those labels and
  date/time wording are not silently normalized here.
- The memo expected no further radar analysis at that time.

P6 pp. 48–49, printed pp. 13–14 of the July 10, 2003 account, instead describes
hovering/presence from **08:50 to 09:03**, without a precise time, and calls the
photo/video comparison non-scientific. This is an unresolved difference in the
documented bounds, not a newly measured flight path. Neither reported photo
window establishes a pre-impact photograph. A photograph's date also does not
establish when the people first arrived.

**Still needed:** the actual NK 2405 cassette and source masters; which source
segment was used; the ATC CD-ROM, viewer and raw tracks; why the 09:03–09:07
interval was not viewable; helicopter identification and projection worksheets;
original photograph/negative/frame sequence. The absent raw inputs prevent a
reproducible reconciliation of the different reported bounds.

## 3. Underlying witnesses and photographic custody

P1 pp. 63–65 is a contemporary FBI interview report, transcribed September 12
for an interview on September 11. It places the neighbor's call at approximately
**09:00**, followed by observation of smoke and three men on a white van. It
reports apparent photography/video, smiles, hugging/high-fives, the absence of
the second explosion when she first noticed them, and contemporaneous recording
of plate **JRJ-13Y**. It says the police call occurred later, after her husband
returned. This is the underlying released interview report, not an acquired
audio recording or independently authenticated signed witness statement.

P5 p. 27 records a detective relaying the complaint/plate and collection of three
cigarette butts. It is not a second independent observer of the same behavior.
P1 p. 33 separately summarizes another witness's **08:00 white-van sighting**.
That allegation remains preserved; the original supporting interview and a
verified van/occupant identification were not located in this pass.

Other checked reports add uncertainty rather than supply a clean single clock:

- P5 p. 25's maintenance observer describes less than five minutes after the
  first North Tower impact, but also estimates **09:00–09:10**, and reports not
  recognizing the men in photographs. A parking-lot diagram is identified as
  enclosed in a **1-A envelope**. Relative and clock estimates remain separate.
- P5 pp. 21–22 is the employee report underlying the **08:58 time-card** lead.
  It describes redacted people in dispatch, subsequent recognition of the attack,
  and office-roof observation. The actual time card was not inspected. Office,
  parking-garage and van-roof observations are different locations/events.
- P6 p. 49 acknowledges that a later employee interview did not verify the prior
  statement as intended. It also describes the inability to obtain the local-call
  timestamp. Redactions prevent mapping every named-in-the-original person to a
  particular detained individual.

P1 p. 10 documents development/reproduction of seized **original 35 mm film on
September 13**. P1 p. 3 separately inventories **1B14**, three copies each of
76 black-and-white 8×10 photographs and film negatives, barcode **E01889076**,
connected with the JRJ13Y vehicle investigation. It does **not**, by itself,
establish that all 76 are sequential exposures from the detainees' camera.
That join requires the contact sheets, negative envelopes and lab records.

P1 p. 35 expressly notes that the then-current recovery log did not make clear
which collected items belonged to which detainee. This is a specific early
attribution problem, not proof it was never subsequently corrected.

## What can change the assessment

The [structured record targets](chains.json) distinguish located descriptions
from absent payloads. Original media and calibrated clocks could strengthen or
weaken pre-positioning. Final chemistry could identify a substance, a negative
result or an inconclusive examination; all require controls and custody. Computer
findings could identify relevant communications or unrelated material. White
House messages could contain ordinary public-information handling or evidence
of misleading statements. None of these outcomes is assumed in advance.
