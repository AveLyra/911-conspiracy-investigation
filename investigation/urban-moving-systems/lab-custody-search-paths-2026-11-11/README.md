# UMS van samples: FBI lab and evidence-control search paths

Research update, November 11, 2026. This is a source and request-coverage review, not a new laboratory result. The final chemical findings for the three listed evidence barcodes remain unlocated in the records reviewed. No FBI contact or new request was made.

## New and refined findings

1. **A more specific transmission trail.** The FBI's released [Manual of Administrative Operations and Procedures, Part II, § 2-4.4.11](https://vault.fbi.gov/maop/MAOP%20Part%2005/at_download/file), describes an evidence-control-room binder for the package copy of an automated FD-192 sent to an FBI or DEA laboratory, and a Collected Item custody entry. If U.S. registered mail was used between an FBI field office and FBI headquarters, it also describes FD-441a and the return of a completed receipt for the investigative file's 1A section. **FD-441a is conditional on the shipping method; the review did not establish that this van evidence was mailed, used registered mail, or reached the FBI Laboratory.** The publicly released manual's applicability and revision effective on the relevant dates have not been verified. It is an index of plausible records, not proof that they were created or retained.
2. **A laboratory crosswalk is a concrete search target.** A September 2001 [U.S. Attorneys' Bulletin, printed pp. 33–34 / PDF pp. 24–25](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/usao/legacy/2006/06/30/usab4905.pdf#page=24), describes the handling of evidence in major bombing examinations: laboratory and Q/K specimen identifiers, chain-of-custody tracking, examination reports and a table correlating identifiers. Its case study is a fictional bombing scenario. This contemporary description supports asking for such records, but does not prove that the van submissions were handled under that process or assigned Q/K numbers.
3. **February disposition counterpart inspected.** I visually inspected the FBI-produced `efoia.1.pdf` physical PDF pages 4–5, held in the local October 26, 2026 production under request 1761252-000. They appear in reverse order: p. 4 is the continuation listing E01889035, E01889036 and E01889037; p. 5 is the February 24, 2014 cover, which reports evidence destroyed/disposed of and lists FD-192s as enclosures. Its substantially overlapping list and language do not establish a second disposal, item-specific execution dates, the underlying authority, or what chemical testing found. This confirms the counterpart's contents rather than independently proving a separate event. The January communication and inventory remain publicly reproduced in the [three-page excerpt](../fbi-notebook-custody-2026-11-06/raw/fbi-selected-disposition-and-inventory-pages.pdf).

## Request coverage and next useful comparison

The locally preserved November 7 sent supplement under FBI request 1761252-000 expressly asks for collection, submission, receipt, accession, testing, controls, reports, transmissions, custody and disposition records for the three barcodes, plus related or differently indexed submissions. The preserved tracker records it as sent and records an automated response. It does **not** establish that the FBI accepted every locator, searched these repositories or found records. Its broad language plausibly includes the evidence-control and laboratory crosswalk records, but the FD-192 binder, Collected Item custody history and conditional FD-441a are not expressly named in the examined text. They are additional locators, not demonstrably new substantive categories. The original request's attachment has previously remained unidentified; the supplement does not resolve its exact scope.

If a follow-up becomes appropriate, ask the agency to search any surviving Newark evidence-control binder entries and associated FD-192 package copies and Collected Item histories; any applicable FD-441a receipt if registered mail was used; laboratory submission and receipt indices; any actual field-barcode-to-laboratory/Q/K crosswalk, examination or cancellation and return/consumption records; and the FD-192 enclosures referenced in the February communication. Seek both completed and uncompleted or unavailable results, without presuming an accession or examination exists. Do not confuse **011001250** (image enhancement) or **020924003 LV** (document examination) with an established residue accession. No separate fee commitment follows from adding a locator to the pending request.

## What this changes

The records make the missing chain easier to search: field identifiers → transmission/custody → possible lab accession and specimen IDs → actual report or nonexamination record → return/consumption → disposition. They do not show explosives, link a specimen to WTC material, or establish concealment. The strongest counterexplanations remain routine post-case disposal, incomplete or canceled testing, and a report held elsewhere; an instruction to suppress results or an actual contrary preservation order would be more probative of misconduct. Conversely, a reliable negative report with controls and complete specimen mapping would weaken an explosives-residue claim for those tested items, subject to detection limits and sampling scope.
