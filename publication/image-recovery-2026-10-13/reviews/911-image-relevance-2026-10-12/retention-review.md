# Retention review of the recovered 9/11 investigation images

October 12, 2026. **Keep the 1,566 core study-input objects. Hold the other 913 native frame objects pending individual relevance and recovery review. Keep one copy of each of the six tiny method-test images. The 50 distinct navigation sheets have lower retention priority. Nothing was deleted, moved, uploaded or accepted as a new scientific finding.**

**Confidence clarification:** the core populations have documented uses in the fire-source, motion and continuity studies, including negative controls and poor candidates needed to reproduce those studies. Individual investigative value has not been established for every context/diagnostic frame. The initial keep recommendation for those 913 objects was precautionary. This review examined study records and 260 preview cells representing 245 distinct objects; it did not freshly inspect all 2,479 native objects at full detail. Some context images show presenters, source leaders, off-target scenes or failed decoder output. Their usefulness as source-search history does not establish that every PNG must stay locally forever. A verified source-video and restoration route could justify a smaller local derivative collection after an exact approved proposal.

This recommendation concerns usefulness for investigating and testing competing explanations. It does not certify historical authenticity or establish a conspiracy. Some images document fire, some constrain motion, and others preserve failed matches, obscuration, processing failures or the scope of the search. Those different uses should remain visible.

The 5,344 recovered paths already share **2,535 distinct image objects totaling 1.633 GB**. The same pixels may occur in repeated runs, separate studies or distinct frame positions. Keeping their position/provenance records matters even when their bytes can share storage. Removing a hardlink alias would usually save directory bookkeeping while leaving the underlying image bytes allocated.

## Recovery qualifications

The image vault remains at [outputs/911-image-recovery](${SOURCE_WORKSPACE}/outputs/911-image-recovery/RECOVERY.txt). No additional full-resolution image copy was made by this review.

- **4,967 paths** match saved original file hashes under the recovery record.
- **188 paths** are same-path published archive copies without a located independent original hash. Their bytes match the saved recovery hashes, not a newly discovered original-byte reference. These correspond to the refused Camera2/compilation derivatives.
- **189 paths** reconstruct the earlier refused Clip 3 run. They match the accepted v2 image content, which has original-hash verification, but that does not certify the old refused run's original bytes or erase its refusal.
- Consequently, **188 distinct objects** have no original-hash-verified path anywhere in this recovered inventory. Preserve that qualification even when keeping their content.

All 2,535 distinct objects passed a fresh SHA-256 check in this review. A matching hash proves integrity relative to the stored reference; it does not authenticate original camera custody, source time, interpretation or physical cause. Published Git/LFS references were not freshly tested for remote download in this task. No new cloud backup was made.

The strongest argument for eventually removing derived native frames is that a preserved source video, exact selection map, extraction code and toolchain could recreate them. Many frames were previously recovered that way. This review did **not** test a complete present-day restoration route for all 2,479 native objects, and those images are already compacted to shared storage. A source-preserving, fully verified offload could change the local-storage recommendation; mere topical similarity or a failed preferred-source match would not.
