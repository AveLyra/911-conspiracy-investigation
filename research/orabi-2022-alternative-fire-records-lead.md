# Orabi et al. (2022) — alternative fire pathway as records lead

**Prepared:** 2026-09-16  
**Status:** Working research / case-strategy framing. Not pleading text, not a send draft, not a scientific finding, and not authorization to amend or transmit.  
**Paper:** Orabi, Jiang, Usmani, and Torero, *The Collapse of World Trade Center 7: Revisited*, *Fire Technology*. [Paper and accepted manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10146056/).

## Answer first

**Yes — use it as a concrete reason to pursue underlying records, not as proof that NIST’s explanation is false.**

The paper models an alternative fire-driven pathway: a diesel/hydrocarbon fire in the mechanical space damages the transfer structure, removing support from core columns and potentially initiating progressive failure. Its results establish a **possible mechanism under the modeled assumptions**, not that this mechanism actually occurred on September 11.

## How it can help the FOIA case

It gives specific subjects for locating and evaluating existing records within DOC-NIST-2024-000233:

- **Fuel and fire assumptions:** fuel availability, pump power, generator shutdown, ventilation, fire duration, and fireproofing.
- **Structural assumptions:** transfer-system exposure, column support, and the consequences of different initial failures.
- **Model testing:** existing alternative runs, sensitivity analyses, calculation outputs, and documentation explaining why scenarios were included or excluded.
- **Search completeness:** whether NIST searched the locations containing those responsive records — not simply the previously withheld model-file collection.

Those are illustrative subjects, **not a closed list or a condition on discovery**. Keep the lawsuit focused on access to records capable of testing competing explanations, rather than asking the FOIA judge to decide structural engineering.

Related technical work already in the repository includes the [fuel-system audit](sherlock-wtc7-investigation/fuel-system-audit/report.md) and the [model-access audit](sherlock-wtc7-investigation/model-access-audit.md) (Orabi engine/case-file leads). Those audits do not change this FOIA framing.

## Important counterargument

**NIST did consider diesel fires.** Its published explanation rejects their contribution based on fuel duration, heating, smoke observations, and other scenario-specific considerations. It also concludes that transfer structures did not play a significant role. Do **not** allege that NIST “never considered” this general mechanism. The meaningful question is whether its actual assumptions and calculations adequately address the pathway identified in the later paper. [NIST FAQ, questions 18–19 and 20](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation).

An alternative **fire-driven** explanation also cuts against the false choice that either NIST’s exact sequence is correct or deliberate destruction must have occurred. That makes the paper useful for testing — and potentially falsifying — a conspiracy hypothesis. See the [counterfactual inquiry](counterfactual-state-corporate-conspiracy-inquiry.md) and [collapse synthesis](wtc7-collapse-evaluation-synthesis.md).

## Recommendation

**Use the paper as supporting context and a records lead; do not add a separate scientific-merits claim or amend solely because this paper exists.** Scientific disagreement does not itself defeat a valid FOIA exemption, and FOIA does not require NIST to conduct a new simulation. [FOIA statute](https://www.foia.gov/foia-statute.html).

Preferred framing:

> Existing records can help distinguish competing fire-driven mechanisms and evaluate the assumptions underlying NIST’s conclusions. Plaintiff seeks access to responsive agency records, not a judicial determination of the correct collapse mechanism.

## Next useful step

An assumption-by-assumption comparison of the paper against NIST’s analysis, identifying the corresponding existing-record leads. That would strengthen a substantive follow-up without sending another repetitive email or making unsupported allegations.
