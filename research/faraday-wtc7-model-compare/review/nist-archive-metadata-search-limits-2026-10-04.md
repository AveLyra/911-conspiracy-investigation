# NIST archive catalog and embedded-metadata limits

**Record type:** Source-pinned archive-policy observation and narrowly stated research implication. This is not a finding about alteration or authenticity of any particular WTC 7 file.

## Source

- National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), “Disaster and Failure Studies Repository,” WTC investigation archive page: <https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/photos-videos-and-simulations>
- Page states “Created November 26, 2019, Updated August 19, 2026”; accessed 2026-10-04.
- Relevant page section: “About Tags, Attributes and Metadata,” especially its statements on non-exhaustive tagging and embedded metadata. No local snapshot of the live page was captured, so no source-file SHA-256 is available. The URL and access date identify the page inspected, not immutable page bytes.

## Source observations

The current NIST page says that only a subset of collected photos and video clips was organized into a searchable database with tags and attributes. It expressly says that the process did not assign every possibly appropriate tag or attribute to every item; copies of the same media may appear in other archive headings without those tags. The page further states that tag-based searches return only the organized collection, while title search returns across the repository.

Separately, NIST states that some photos and videos contain camera- or photographer-embedded metadata and that NIST takes no position on the accuracy of such metadata. The page describes the archive as a compilation of collected materials and NIST-generated research materials, with separate categories for them.

## Limited implication for the AVI provenance inquiry

This is a first-party, archive-wide warning about catalog coverage and embedded metadata. It means that an absent tag is not, by itself, evidence that an item is absent from the archive, and an embedded metadata string should not be treated as NIST-authenticated provenance solely because it appears in a file. For the AVI under review, this is consistent with keeping the `NDW Ltd. 2008` / `JPGVideo` strings as observations rather than treating them as proof of authorship.

The page does **not** say that the specific AVI is mistagged, misattributed, replaced, or altered. It does not explain the AVI's conflicting dimensions or codec descriptions, identify an original/master file, or establish how any particular metadata value was created. This is not evidence of a cover-up. Search results should be checked across the archive's available categories and by title as well as tags; the present source review did not conduct a new full NIST repository search.

## Evidence classification

- **Observed in primary source:** NIST's live archive page contains the catalog-coverage and metadata-accuracy qualifications above.
- **Limited inference:** archive tags and embedded strings alone cannot authenticate the identity or completeness of a particular video record.
- **Unknown:** whether this AVI appears under multiple NIST headings; whether its embedded strings are native, inherited, or introduced during export; and whether a source/master copy exists elsewhere.
- **Alternative explanations remain open:** incomplete cataloging, ordinary export/metadata behavior, derivative-copy lineage, or some other history. This source does not discriminate among them.
