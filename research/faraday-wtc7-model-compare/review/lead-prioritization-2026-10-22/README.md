# Priorities for 67 WTC 7 research leads

October 22, 2026. Research assessment by AveLyra.

The strongest existing criticism is the acknowledged difference between predicted late exterior deformation and the footage. The most consequential unfinished tests concern support loss during rapid descent, the thermal assignments actually applied, and whether the proposed connection and damage-transfer sequence remains robust under justified alternatives. These are different kinds of evidence. An unanswered high-consequence question is not already a demonstrated model error.

All [67 original leads](INVENTORY.md) remain available with their numbering, conflicting values, qualifications and contrary findings. This addendum records research priority, not deletion, adjudication or replacement of any source. The entries overlap and must not be counted as 67 independent failures. Lower current evidentiary weight does not mean a lead should be discarded.

## The earlier damage control needs a precise description

Items 21–22 deserve priority, but the comparison is not two complete independent 3.5-hour and 4.0-hour fire histories. NIST describes the earlier-damage control as **4.0-hour temperatures with 3.5-hour ANSYS damage**.

The published control ends at solver time 12.76 seconds, or 4.26 seconds after the damage application at 8.5 seconds. First Column 79 buckling in the higher-damage case is reported at 14.7 seconds, or 6.2 seconds after damage application. The control's reported followup is therefore 1.94 solver seconds shorter.

NIST also says the control had stabilized and no initiating event developed. Keep that statement beside the duration difference. A stabilized lower-damage system need not run as long as an unstable higher-damage system; the difference does not show that the control would later collapse or that its stopping decision was invalid. The decisive next comparison needs stability criteria, displacement and velocity, kinetic and internal energy, damping and termination settings, and an extended equal-horizon check. Neither a sharp physical threshold nor an invalid control is established here.

Sources: [NCSTAR 1-9, printed p. 568, physical p. 634](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-9.pdf#page=634); [NCSTAR 1-9A, printed p. 105, physical p. 156](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1-9a.pdf#page=156); [preserved October 21 comparison and arithmetic](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/c79-and-timing-crosschecks-2026-10-21/README.md#the-earlier-damage-control-has-a-finite-and-shorter-reported-horizon).

## Conditional and secondary leads remain open

**55–58** test the scope of acoustic exclusions. Detection opportunity, obstructions, recording conditions and the specified intervention matter. Weakening a particular exclusion is not affirmative evidence that an explosive was used.

**59–60** concern physical material and custody. These could become highly consequential with reliable building, member, collection, timing and mechanism attribution. The current provenance gaps limit their present weight. The unavailability statement in 59 must not erase the separate FEMA Sample 1 or unidentified-candidate provenance questions.

**31 and 62–64** concern a counterfactual or related property/validation questions. Their link to the exact WTC 7 executed case needs to be established before generalizing.

**65–66** remain preservation and accounting leads. Municipal DEP record vulnerabilities are not evidence that NIST destroyed records, and an accounting tension does not identify a unique lost record or a suppression decision.

Sources and contrary qualifications remain alongside these entries in the [preserved inventory](INVENTORY.md). Their priority rises if an authenticated record supplies the currently missing causal or execution link.

## Lower current weight does not mean removal

**14, 20, 43, 45 and 48–53** deserve preservation but currently carry less weight as evidence of a physical model error or intent than the specific tests above.

Item 14 is a broad historical count requiring its own exact-input and method check. Labels, comments, dates and names in 20 and 48–53 can be informative but do not establish what was executed. The erratum in 43 corrects publication text while NIST says the analyses already used the corrected values. The prose/table sequence in 45 needs event definitions and component histories before calling it a solver contradiction.

The same item can be lower-weight evidence of misconduct and a high-value records target. Item 48 is deliberately retained in both discussions. A matched run or export history demonstrating a material consequence could raise its weight; a documented harmless naming or editorial distinction could lower it. Neither outcome is presumed.

## What the prioritization supports

The reviewed inventory supports a stronger present challenge to reproducibility and adequacy of a particular model than to deliberate concealment or a specified conspiracy. That is an assessment of the current evidentiary links, not a presumption that NIST is correct or that misconduct is impossible.

A consequential discrepancy tied to the actual executed run would materially strengthen an engineering criticism. Authenticated evidence that a material contradiction was known and misrepresented would additionally strengthen a concealment hypothesis. Conversely, authenticated source/run mappings and independently justified sensitivity results that account for the observations would weaken the relevant criticism. No competing historical cause or actor is selected by this prioritization.
