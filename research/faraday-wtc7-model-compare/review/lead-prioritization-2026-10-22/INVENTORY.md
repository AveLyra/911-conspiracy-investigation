## Preserved numbered inventory

The investigation has identified **a real late-stage model/video mismatch, specific anomalies in released inputs, consequential assumptions that remain insufficiently tested, and gaps in the reproducible modeling record**. Those findings differ substantially in strength. Calling all of them “NIST errors” would overstate what we have established.

Below is the consolidated inventory I could recover from the project records. It focuses on WTC 7, with related tower-validation and preservation issues labeled separately. **It is not certification that every historical conversation has been recovered.** Some entries overlap because they concern different parts of the same unresolved causal chain.

**1. Model predictions versus observed collapse**

1. **Late-stage upper-wall deformation differs from the footage.** NIST acknowledges that its predicted large inward deformation of the upper exterior walls is not visible in the videos. This is the clearest documented model-to-observation mismatch.

2. **Omitted nonstructural stiffness and strength.** NIST says its analyses excluded contributions from cladding, interior walls, and partitions. That is a documented simplification whose importance to the exterior-motion mismatch remains unresolved.

3. **Reduced confidence after global collapse begins.** NIST acknowledges increasing uncertainty during breakup. Late-stage animation therefore provides weaker validation than a presentation of the entire sequence as a demonstrated match would imply.

4. **West-penthouse timing discrepancies.** The project’s source audit records an observed disappearance below the roofline at approximately 9.3 seconds, versus 6.9/7.3 seconds in the debris-damage model and 10.6/10.9 seconds without debris damage. The paired model values represent different views, not uncertainty intervals.

5. **North-roofline onset discrepancies.** The same comparison reports observed eastern north-roofline downward onset at 6.9 seconds, versus 6.3 seconds with debris damage and 9.8 seconds without it.

6. **Penthouse timing is partly an alignment convention.** Setting east-penthouse descent to zero aligns the comparison; agreement at that reference point is not an independently predicted absolute event time.

7. **The decisive interior sequence is not directly observed end to end.** Column 79 buckling and cross-core buckling have no direct observation times in the cited comparison table. Exterior motion constrains that sequence without confirming every hidden step.

8. **Calibration and validation partly overlap.** Photographic information helped direct simulations. Agreement with information used to construct or select a run carries less independent evidentiary weight than agreement with observations withheld from that process.

9. **Independent reproduction of the free-fall interval remains incomplete.** The reviewed released record does not supply a fully reproduced baseline roofline acceleration and resisting-force history demonstrating the model’s correspondence to that interval.

Sources: [NIST’s own FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation), [project simulation/footage comparison](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/research/wtc7-video-comparison/nist-simulation-footage-crosscheck-2026-09-30.md), and [structural source audit, especially SC08–SC09](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1a.pdf#page=84).

**2. Fire reconstruction and thermal inputs**

10. **Floor 11 and Floor 13 histories derived from Floor 12.** The use of shifted or substituted histories creates a consequential dependence between floors rather than three independently reconstructed fire histories.

11. **Acknowledged potential overestimation of some fires.** NIST’s published discussion recognizes possible overestimation of duration and window-adjacent burning for Floor 12 and its derivative histories. This is a bounded qualification, not proof that all modeled temperatures were too high.

12. **Some photograph timing depends partly on assumed fire development.** Those estimated timestamps cannot independently validate the same fire-development assumptions used to derive them.

13. **Visible flames do not determine interior steel temperatures.** Our image work has not established the quantitative temperature field required to confirm—or falsify—the thermal analysis.

14. **Repeated, unequal thermal assignments.** The earlier inventory reports unequal repeated coefficients for 170,491 node IDs. That broader historical count should remain attributed to its original analysis until rechecked against its exact input and method.

15. **A more tightly verified Column 79-region anomaly.** The later trace found 63 selected nodes with two unequal coefficients each. Every one connects to a single modeled part, so sharing between different parts cannot explain all these repetitions.

16. **Regular spatial grouping of those repetitions.** The 63 nodes form seven groups of nine at regularly spaced model elevations. That supports investigating export or segment-boundary handling; it does not identify the explanation.

17. **Effective solver temperatures remain unresolved.** Repeated rows are not automatically added, averaged, or interpreted as separate historical times. We have not established which values the historical solver actually applied.

18. **The LS-DYNA temperature generator remains unidentified in the reviewed release.** Having the destination temperature file does not reproduce its spatial mapping, source selection, or duplicate-handling procedure.

19. **Thermal transfer and structural-damage transfer are separate branches.** Earlier shorthand sometimes conflated them. NIST describes separate thermal datasets for ANSYS and LS-DYNA; we have not established that LS-DYNA temperatures came from a completed ANSYS structural-response solution.

20. **Case-dependent thermal packaging remains unexplained.** Examples include the Case B `+10%` directory label, its additional `SLNo` inputs, and differences in Floor 7 driver inclusion. These are configuration questions, not demonstrated physical errors.

Sources: [thermal-assignment trace](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/thermal-assignment-trace/report.md), [thermal-transfer crosswalk](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/thermal-transfer-crosswalk/report.md), [fire-image review](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/fire-coverage-batch3/report.md), and [NIST’s published discussion of fire reconstruction](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=909254).

**3. Initialization, damage transfer, and collapse sensitivity**

21. **The reported 3.5-hour/4.0-hour transition is consequential.** One damage state did not initiate global collapse; the later state did. A threshold is physically possible, but the reviewed record does not demonstrate its robustness across justified intermediate histories and perturbations.

22. **The paired earlier-damage control remains unavailable in the inspected package.** The supplied 4.0-hour damage material does not provide the complete 3.5-hour counterpart needed for a controlled comparison.

23. **Hours of accumulated damage are initialized in a seconds-long calculation.** Gravity, debris damage, temperature ramping, settling, and imported fire damage are applied in a numerical sequence. Its physical equivalence needs testing rather than assumption.

24. **Ambiguous published initialization wording.** One summary describes temperatures and damage as applied “at the same time,” while the more detailed schedule separates them. Actual run and restart records would resolve the implementation.

25. **Instantaneous damage application.** The transfer imposes accumulated damage at a particular simulation time. We have not established whether credible gradual-transfer alternatives yield the same onset and progression.

26. **Coarse mapping of partial connection damage.** Detailed damage states are translated into reduced connection representations and element-removal rules. Their equivalence in residual stiffness, resistance, and energy absorption remains a validation question.

27. **Element deletion requires more than a visual plausibility check.** Removing elements while retaining mass may be defensible, but requires checking the resulting force paths, contact, energy, and post-buckling behavior.

28. **Residual-state continuity is unresolved.** The live released deck does not establish a complete transfer of residual stress and deformation from the preceding analysis. That does not prove these quantities were absent from every historical run.

29. **The supplied master configuration is not a self-contained collapse replay.** Its live termination and loading settings, together with commented damage hooks, require continuation or restart information to reproduce the later sequence.

30. **Floor-cascade arrest and propagation need explicit checking.** The reviewed work has not independently reproduced floor-by-floor impact forces, receiving-floor capacities, surviving paths, and arrest possibilities.

31. **The “Truss 2 failure was not essential” proposition remains a counterfactual claim.** It needs an identified alternate run or equivalent analysis, rather than treatment as an observed historical fact.

Source: [structural source audit, SC02–SC07](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/NCSTAR/ncstar1a.pdf#page=84) and [overall causal-chain synthesis](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/causal-chain-synthesis/report.md).

**4. Connections, restraint, geometry, and material behavior**

32. **Connection calibration is not equivalent to independent experimental validation.** Some reduced connection laws were fitted to another numerical representation. Solver agreement and physical accuracy are different propositions.

33. **Matching dissipated energy does not ensure matching force throughout failure.** Published spring/shell comparisons can show similar terminal energy with different force-displacement histories. The global consequence has not been quantified.

34. **Connection grouping introduces approximation.** Geometry-based groups, averaging, and outlier handling require location-specific checks. The reported coefficient-of-variation guideline is not a guaranteed uncertainty bound for every connection.

35. **Transfer of failure criteria across temperature requires validation.** The report describes using the same strain-failure criterion at room and elevated temperatures. That is a specific applicability question, not proof that temperature-dependent strength was ignored.

36. **Later connection experiments reveal failure-mode differences.** The audited later comparison predicts bolt shear where experiments also produced tab-tension and block-shear failures; experimental and modeled peak loads can occur at different failure stages.

37. **Later experiments do not automatically validate the historical WTC 7 implementation.** The audited assemblies lacked the heated composite-floor conditions of the building, and some parameters were assessed against the same experimental responses being compared.

38. **Column 79’s remaining restraint is more complicated than “unsupported.”** NIST describes retained seated girders and direction-dependent support. The actual stiffness and capacity of these paths remain unquantified in our reconstruction.

39. **The support diagram can be misread without its annotations.** Some “No support” coloring accompanies explicit north-only restraint and vertical-support qualifications. A diagram label alone cannot establish zero resistance.

40. **Released material references remain unresolved.** The material/run audit found 23 used master-region parts referencing 19 undefined material IDs in the inspected inputs, covering 16,324 shell records. Numerical counterparts exist under transformed IDs, but copying them over would be an unauthenticated repair.

41. **Specific spring-curve dependencies remain missing.** References LCD602 and LCD803 remain unresolved in the inspected released numeric packages.

42. **Construction detail, installed condition, and historical model use remain incompletely joined.** A drawing detail alone does not establish what was installed at the relevant floor or what the executed model used.

43. **Seat dimensions were corrected in published errata.** NIST’s correction specifies a 12-inch seat and 6.25-inch lateral travel and says the analyses already used those values. This establishes a report correction; it does not independently establish a solver error.

44. **The A2001 stiffener/preliminary-analysis issue remains a records lead.** Drawing 9114, NIST Response 2020-001, preliminary loads and temperatures, and seat deformation warrant comparison. We have not established that paired with/without-stiffener runs existed.

45. **The Column 44 event sequence needs run-level reconciliation.** Narrative descriptions and Table 8-2’s seat-bolt, beam-buckling, and top-plate-bolt sequence require component histories and precise event definitions. Those reported seconds are accelerated-simulation times.

Sources: [connection-calibration audit](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/connection-calibration-audit/report.md), [experimental-source audit](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/assembly-test-source-audit/report.md), [Column 79 restraint audit](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/c79-restraint-audit/report.md), [material/run crosswalk](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/material-run-crosswalk/report.md), and [original-sheet evidence boundary](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/c79-original-sheet-review/report.md).

**6. Forensic exclusions and investigation limits**

55. **Acoustic exclusion depends on actual detection opportunity.** NIST’s reviewed propagation calculation omits neighboring buildings and qualifies the loudness estimate for unobstructed paths, whereas the cited recordings were distant and obstructed.

56. **The urban source-to-receiver bridge remains insufficiently demonstrated in the reviewed pages.** Qualitative discussion of reflections does not reproduce detection conditions at each camera.

57. **A universal “nothing was heard before global collapse” summary is inaccurate.** NIST itself describes an earlier reaction/hearing attribution at one location. That does not identify the sound as an explosion.

58. **A modeled loud-blast exclusion does not exclude every intervention mechanism.** Conventional explosive, thermal, mechanical, and mixed proposals have different signatures. Each needs its own affirmative evidence and detection analysis.

59. **Identifiable WTC 7 steel was unavailable for the direct examinations at issue.** That limits testing of critical members for heating, fracture, cutting, or explosive effects across competing explanations.

60. **Physical-material claims remain insufficiently connected to the building and mechanism.** Dust, residue, thermitic-material, and sulfidation claims require authenticated custody, location, timing, and discriminating analysis before they become evidence against NIST’s particular sequence.

61. **Matching thermal expansion does not alone validate restrained thermal force.** Our reproducible synthetic test demonstrates that inference can fail unless additional properties, including compliance, match. It does not establish that a particular NIST model made that error.

62. **Fireproofing-property applicability remains unresolved.** The reviewed property report qualifies calculated density above 600°C. WTC 7’s MK-5 specification narrows the issue but does not establish the actual case-linked implementation or resulting temperature error.

**8. Explicit follow-up question: lower-floor fires and upper-façade free fall**

67. **Does the proposed lower-floor fire sequence reproduce the upper-façade free-fall interval?** NIST identifies the sustained uncontrolled fires on Floors 7–9 and 11–13, including Floor 13, while acknowledging approximately 2.25 seconds of essentially free-fall descent of the north face. This spatial separation makes the propagation and loss-of-support sequence a specific quantitative question. It does not establish a contradiction merely because the descending upper structure was above the principal fire floors.

    **The question to preserve:** Does NIST’s specified lower-floor fire damage and ensuing failure sequence actually produce sufficiently extensive loss of support, with the timing and residual forces necessary to reproduce the observed upper-façade acceleration, coherence, and motion? Relevant tests include matched model displacement/acceleration, member-force and contact histories, the failure chronology, and measurement and model uncertainty. This makes explicit the connection implicit in item 9 and historical inventory item 58.

    **NIST’s explanation and the mechanical qualification:** Upper stories need not themselves be heated to descend near gravitational acceleration if their supports fail below them. NIST attributes the interval to negligible support after lower exterior-column buckling. Compatibility with that explanation in principle does not independently validate its particular historical sequence; describing the process as progressive collapse does not replace the quantitative comparison.

    **Force constraint and observation limit:** For a defined fixed-mass body, sustained center-of-mass acceleration near gravitational acceleration requires a correspondingly small net non-gravitational vertical force. Substantial upward resistance cannot coexist with that motion unless physically justified downward forces offset it. A tracked roof point is not automatically that body’s center of mass. The body, interval, relative motion, projection, and uncertainty must be established or bounded. This limitation must be tested, not used to invent unlimited hidden motion or unexplained cancelling forces. The linked project force calculations are conditional analyses of separately identified published motion data, not an independent reproduction of NIST’s exact 2.25-second measurement or a measured historical force history. Neither the spatial separation nor free fall alone establishes simultaneous failure of every column, deliberate intervention, or a conspiracy.

    Sources: [NIST FAQ, questions 8–9 and 32](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation); [finite-interval force analysis](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/finite-interval-force/report.md); [roof-point and center-of-mass identifiability analysis](https://github.com/AveLyra/911-conspiracy-investigation/blob/99a624a8089c8b57e6bf300674b9d7495fdf1065/investigation/wtc7/body-com-identifiability/report.md).

**Earlier criticisms that our own investigation narrowed or rejected**

These qualifications belong with the inventory:

- **“NIST omitted all residual support” is unsupported.** The reviewed models include bearing, contact, discrete resistance, and retained supports.
- **Suspected connection-table ratio errors were not substantiated after allowing for display rounding.**
- **The seat-dimension erratum does not establish that the simulation used the erroneous printed dimension.**
- **Our proposed seamless join between two CBS clips failed.** It cannot support a continuous fire-duration measurement.
- **Our image work has not produced a defensible burning-window percentage or demonstrated temperature inflation.**
- **UAF’s prescribed broad support failures do not identify the historical cause of those failures.**
- **Other fire-driven pathways remain possible.** Weakening NIST’s exact sequence would not eliminate the entire fire-mechanism family.
- **Near-free-fall façade motion does not by itself identify initiation, whole-building force balance, or intent.**
- **No reviewed anomaly establishes an executed conspiracy or deliberate falsification.**

The strongest objection to treating this as a list of demonstrated failures is that **many entries identify what our available record cannot yet resolve**. Missing replication is weaker evidence than a reproduced contradiction. The highest-value next evidence remains the exact executed runs, paired sensitivity cases, processing histories, and independently testable output—not another accumulation of unexplained-looking details.
