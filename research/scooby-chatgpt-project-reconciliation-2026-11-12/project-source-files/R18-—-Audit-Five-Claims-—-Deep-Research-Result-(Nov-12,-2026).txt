2. Findings from the original footnotes

The original notes establish two important qualifications.

First, multiple interrogation reports are not necessarily multiple independent witnesses. Chapter 5 note 35 identifies KSM interrogations dated July 12 and November 6, 2003, alongside accounts attributed to Abu Zubaydah and Khallad. The Commission explicitly acknowledged that neither of the latter two claimed to witness KSM's original 1996 proposal. Thus, their accounts cannot independently authenticate that meeting. 
9-11commission.gov
+1

Second, chapter 5 note 38 expressly acknowledges inconsistent KSM accounts of whether Bin Laden approved the operation in late 1998 or early 1999. The underlying reports are identified as August 18, 2003; January 9, February 19 and April 3, 2004. Repetition across those dates is a single-witness series, not four independent confirmations. 
9-11commission.gov
+1

Correction to the fourth preselected claim: The Commission did not conclude that Bayoumi's restaurant encounter was coincidental. On printed p. 218, it explicitly left chance versus design unresolved. The actual affirmative finding to audit is its separate assessment, on the same page, that it had found no credible evidence Bayoumi knowingly assisted extremist groups. This correction prevents testing an assertion the Commission did not make. 
9-11commission.gov

3. Five-row source-dependency audit

The test here removes contested detainee interrogation reporting, including later summaries derived from it. It does not assume the reporting was false.

Finding after source removal




1. KSM's 1996 proposal

	

Report pp. 148–149; chapter 5 notes 33–36. The core account comes from KSM's July 12 and November 6, 2003 interrogations. Abu Zubaydah's May 16, 2003 account and Khallad's April 22, 2004 account are indirect. A separately described 1995 Philippine interrogation concerns an earlier aircraft-attack idea, not proof of the specific 1996 meeting.

	

Specific meeting and proposal remain uncertain. Earlier aircraft-attack concepts survive, but they cannot independently establish KSM's conversation with Bin Laden.

2. Bin Laden's late-1998/early-1999 approval

	

Report pp. 149–150; chapter 5 notes 16, 36, 38–39. Several dated accounts all originate with KSM. Note 38 acknowledges conflicting dates. No independently authenticated contemporary authorization record was located.

	

Specific approval date and conversation remain uncertain. Later preparation for the operation is compatible with leadership approval, but does not independently date the original decision.

3. Al-Qaeda financing and absence of substantial domestic financing

	

Report pp. 171–172; chapter 5 note 131. Sources include FBI financial investigations, Adam Drucker interviews, the July 13, 2004 FBI response and KSM/Binalshibh interrogations. The financing monograph draws substantially on the same investigation—not an independent second investigation.

	

Most transaction evidence survives. Overseas transfers, U.S. bank activity, payments for training and approximately $26,000 returned overseas remain supported by financial analysis. Exact total cost, complete ultimate funding attribution and categorical exclusion of other assistance are less secure.

4. No credible evidence Bayoumi knowingly assisted extremists

	

Report p. 218; chapter 7 notes 15–19. Underlying sources include Bayoumi and Bin Don interviews, an October 8, 2001 FBI interview, hotel records, employment records and FBI assessments. Note 19 also cites KSM's denial of knowing Bayoumi. These are mixed evidence types, not uniformly independent corroboration.

	

The assistance survives; its purpose remains disputed. Later FBI intelligence reporting and the August 2025 judicial findings substantially qualify the 2004 characterization. Neither establishes that Saudi leadership authorized 9/11 or resolves each allegation of operational knowledge.

4. Interrogation conditions, destruction and editorial influence

The Senate Intelligence Committee's December 9, 2014 study documents coercive interrogation of KSM beginning in March 2003, including waterboarding and subsequently identified fabricated reporting. That provides a concrete reliability concern. It does not establish that every later statement by KSM was false, or determine how any particular interrogation method affected the July 2003–April 2004 reports cited above. An item-level assessment requires the original report, interrogation chronology and relevant corroboration. 
govinfo.gov
+1

The November 2005 destruction of 92 CIA interrogation videotapes was a separate event concerning Abu Zubaydah and Abd al-Rahim al-Nashiri. Released November 8–10 communications document authorization, destruction and subsequent internal reactions. Those tapes must not be described as destroyed recordings of KSM's planning account. 
National Security Archive

On editorial influence, I could establish an actual source-access restriction: Commission researchers were denied direct detainee and interrogator access. The documented effect was reduced ability to assess credibility, clarify answers and control questioning. I did not establish a particular politically motivated draft alteration or fabricated passage. NARA identifies managing-editor files containing report drafts, but the necessary version-to-version comparison was not completed.

5. Linked originals and archival gaps

The principal inspectable sources for this audit are the July 2004 Commission Report, its original numbered endnotes, the August 2004 terrorist-financing monograph, the December 2014 Senate interrogation study, and August 28, 2025 judicial decision, ECF 11182.

The most consequential missing originals are the individually cited KSM interrogation reports; the complete Bayoumi and Bin Don interview memoranda and relevant financial workpapers; the underlying transaction exhibits for a complete financing reconciliation; and the FAA transcript's full source and timing metadata. Where these are unavailable, the dependent findings above stop at the supported documentary level.

NARA's Commission series finding aid and access guidance identify the appropriate Commission-record route through the Center for Legislative Archives, including record-specific review. The Commission's legislative-branch records are not subject to FOIA. An FBI or CIA copy of a document does not establish identical contents, annotations or custody within the Commission's own archival holdings.
