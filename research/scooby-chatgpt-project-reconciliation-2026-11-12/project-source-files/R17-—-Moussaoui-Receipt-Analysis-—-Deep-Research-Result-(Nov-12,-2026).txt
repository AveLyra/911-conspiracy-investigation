## New source finding: defense Exhibit 633

[Defense Exhibit 633, physical p.1, Bates `M-MP1-88000402`](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/633.pdf#page=1) is a contemporaneous displayed email **August 22, 2001, 10:13 a.m.**, Harry Samit to Charles Frahm and G. Jones, subject **“747 Information.”** The original page image was visually inspected. Its later January 24, 2005 declassification note is not an authoring or delivery date.

Samit says the **opening EC was attached** and had originally been routed to an Iran/Hizballah unit when investigators thought there was a Hizballah connection; by this message he wrote they had **pretty much ruled out** that working theory. He provides named identity and bail-assistance leads in Norman, Oklahoma and Minneapolis and describes a still-contested name match with a Memphis bank robber. Thus the message is not simply a bare terrorist allegation: it contains a correction of an early hypothesis, uncertain identification, and explicit tasks. But the one-page court representation does **not contain the attached EC**; it supplies neither embedded attachment bytes nor recipient acknowledgment or reading log.

It also summarizes **attributed French reporting**: a person connected with a Chechnya group led by al-Khattab and an interview reporting Moussaoui recruited someone to fight there. This is a **source-reported recruitment allegation**, not authenticated original French interview notes, demonstrated formal affiliation with al-Qaeda, or specific advance knowledge of an aircraft attack. The email closes by saying more information may follow, marking the intelligence as incomplete. Retain the exact distinction between attributed source allegation, FBI inference, and proven operational relationship.

A second court representation, [Exhibit 736, p.1](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/736.pdf#page=1), reports a written response attributed to Charles Frahm on August 24: beginning name/telephone traces, requesting photographs, and questioning why the French information did not support the proposed foreign-group connection. It supports **receipt of some information by an actual counterpart and an expressed trace intention**. The time labels in the reproduced exchange are internally awkward, and a subject-line case-number digit differs from the `199M-MP-60130` used on other records. Without the native thread and source headers these differences cannot be normalized. The original task returns are not thereby proved. Source wording was examined through the court-file representation and its earlier preserved text; fresh high-resolution PDF capture for Exhibit 736 was not available in this pass.

## Narrow sensitivity: which missing or altered facts could affect which decision?

**FISA foreign-power review (HQ → NSLU → possible OIPR).** The operative issue was a showing that Moussaoui acted for or on behalf of a qualifying *foreign power*, not simply that his beliefs were extreme or aviation training suspicious. Exs 346, 633 and 693 indicate attributed Chechnya recruitment/Khattab links; Exs349/350 show disagreement over precisely when supporting organizational material should be added. DOJ OIG Ch. 4 §§IV.B.4–6 explains that the more plausible Chechen-group theory and an unnamed-group alternative were not adequately explored; **no one consulted OIPR**, and the four orally briefed NSLU attorneys saw no source documentation. OIG also says Pakistan travel and the interviews about beliefs were omitted from those briefings. The omitted material **could have altered the questions asked, the legal options considered or the readiness to submit**, but **does not prove FISA probable cause or a likely FISA Court approval**. FBI concerns about new/unrecognized foreign-power pleading under pre-9/11 norms were genuine counterconsiderations. [DOJ OIG chapter 4, especially §§IV.B.4–6](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter4.htm).

**Accuracy of field/interview editing.** Ex349’s concern over “preparing to fight” versus defensive training, an inadequate versus absent explanation, hearing an alleged plan, and “dangerous one” might have shifted factual weight for an honest legal reviewer. But 693 and redacted 347 do **not identify the exact criticized attachment stage**; some visible clauses already preserve field wording. Maltbie’s email says some material was to be added later and that he corrected wording. The OIG found some edits meaning-changing and also found most stylistic, openly circulated for comments, and attributed to a planned new foreign-power section. Without original interviews, native drafts and the package counsel saw, the chain **stops at a documented dispute and incomplete review**, not proof of a knowingly falsified final application. [Exs349/350/693 as above; DOJ OIG chapter 4, §§IV.C.3–4](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter4.htm).

**Criminal-warrant/prosecutor route.** An August 16 Minnesota AUSA reportedly believed the limited facts then presented were close to warrant-ready and offered follow-up; other prosecutors declined to state that a warrant certainly could have issued. The August18 request for OIPR authorization before further criminal contact was an **attempted pathway**, not proof that the pathway was closed by a written refusal. Rowley recommended trying FISA first because of perceived primary-purpose/“smell test” risks; after FISA halted, agents did not revisit the criminal application. A complete affidavit and actual prosecutor review could have altered the opportunity to search the computer; the Sept11 *postattack* criminal warrant is not an independent preattack probable-cause determination. [OIG chapter 4, initial USAO contacts and criminal-warrant analysis](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter4.htm).

**FAA notice and possible operational response.** The Minneapolis LHM’s explicit possible 747-takeover assessment and aircraft-controls particulars could have changed the salience of an institutional FAA warning. The Sept4 HQ teletype did not transmit that assessment in the same text, **but local FAA received the teletype and was orally briefed on the threat on Sept5**. It is therefore inaccurate to say FAA universally received no warning. The missing two printed Ex810 pages and both complete dissemination chains matter to *which audience had what* and *what operational measure followed*, not directly to whether a FISA warrant was legally feasible. [Ex810 physical p4/printed6](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/810.pdf#page=4); [DOJ OIG chapter 4 §II.H.8](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter4.htm).

**French option and field tasks.** A French source offered eventual custody/search, safeguarding evidence from return to Moussaoui; U.S./British sharing was permitted subject to source protection. There was a genuine **August30 plan, August31/Sept4–6 HQ disagreement and September10 headquarters concurrence** on an FBI escort. That is documented progressive follow-through, but not an actual French search before Sept11. Likewise, Oklahoma’s September6 reported **negative identity comparison** constrains the earlier Memphis/Norman lead; without the original Oklahoma response and Memphis return we cannot compute a complete task success score. [Ex59B p4–6; Ex642 p6–7; Ex643 p1](https://coop.vaed.uscourts.gov/moussaoui/exhibits/defense/643.pdf#page=1); [DOJ OIG chapter 4 §§II.E, II.H.7](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter4.htm).

## Source dependence, counterevidence and stopping rule

The key primary sources are linked within the table. The Department of Justice Inspector General’s [Chapter Four](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter4.htm) is a **single retrospective reconstruction** combining interviews and official case records; do not count multiple OIG retellings of the same interview or multiple court exemplars of a single French fax as independent witnesses. Ex327’s repeated jail-call descriptions trace to a single detention log, not 13 connected calls. Ex633 and Ex346 may describe substantially overlapping French reporting, without proving independent source corroboration. Ex59B and Ex642 also derive from the same French supplement and may represent distinct versions; counting both as corroboration is inappropriate.

Contrary evidence remains material: the initial Hizballah lead was discounted; a Memphis suspect match was later ruled out; instructors saw genuine interest in aviation; there were alternative, sometimes contradictory explanations for interviews and travel; pre-9/11 foreign-power standards and practical timing constrained FISA applications; HQ discussed and pursued some additional leads; the local FAA did receive warning and a threat briefing; and the France proposal progressed to HQ escort concurrence. These limit single-cause or motive conclusions. The OIG nevertheless found serious failures, insufficient legal review, some meaning-altering edits, omitted field threat assessment in external teletype, and failure to reconsider the criminal warrant after FISA screening. The evidence supports those particular deficiencies but not a proven plan to let an attack happen.

**Dependent tests stopped:** exact native 633 opening-EC attachment; native LHM attached to 349/350 with full draft/version metadata; final uploaded EC promised in 642, its original translation, and actual delivery; versioned named-recipient NSLU/OIPR/USAO packages with read/decision timestamps; full native serials 3/10/13/16/19 and 1A enclosure manifests; missing 810 printed pages 3–4, complete Aug31 local warning and Sept4 HQ teletype, FAA receipt/action records; original Oklahoma/Memphis task returns and identity evidence; and full contemporaneous France plans, escort clearance/transport/custody/search results. Their absence **prevents package-level conclusions** but proves neither suppression nor misconduct.
