Scooby — Contractor procurement audit
1. Established findings

The November 11 financial review identified substantial post-attack contracting advantages for KBR, alongside documented procurement-control failures. The original GAO findings establish two separate issues:

November 2002 LOGCAP planning order: GAO determined that the Iraq oil-contingency planning assignment exceeded the existing contract's scope. DOD disputed that interpretation and cited a contemporaneous legal opinion.

March 2003 Restore Iraqi Oil (RIO I) award: GAO concluded that the subsequent sole-source award generally complied with applicable competition requirements. That determination does not establish that the selection process was optimal, free of influence, or adequately supervised.

These conclusions are compatible. A legally defensible later award does not retroactively settle the earlier scope dispute.

A consequential distinction is that DOD accepted payment of most questioned costs while reducing the base on which fees were calculated. Neither the $112.163 million adjustment nor the $5.826 million fee effect represents recovery of the entire questioned amount.

GAO also documented that all ten task orders were definitized more than 180 days after work began. The first formal award-fee board convened in July 2004, after nearly all fieldwork was finished; contractor notification followed in January 2005. The available documentation did not permit GAO to reconstruct every criterion-specific fee-board score.

3. Additional findings

Three documentary details sharpen the existing assessment.

The February 4, 2003 LOGCAP contingency plan references the November 8, 2002 planning statement of work and its December 20 extension. This strengthens the dated chain connecting the planning assignment to KBR's subsequent readiness advantage. 
esd.whs.mil

A September 18, 2012 DoD Inspector General report, drawing on an earlier investigation, identifies an April 30, 2003 modification expanding RIO task order 3 beyond emergency repairs into broader oil-facility restoration. This introduces a separate postaward scope issue; it does not establish that the original award was unlawful. 
2012 Update

GAO's $26.158 million sustained-cost figure includes roughly $17 million removed from earlier proposals and approximately $9 million comprising disallowed and reallocated costs. It cannot be described as $26.158 million recovered from KBR. 
gao.gov
