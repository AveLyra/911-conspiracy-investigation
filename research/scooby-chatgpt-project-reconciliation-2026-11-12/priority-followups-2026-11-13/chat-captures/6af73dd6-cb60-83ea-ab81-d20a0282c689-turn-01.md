RJ Lee's printed pp. 16–19, including Figure 21, interpret particles as evidence that iron-bearing materials underwent high-temperature processes during the event. That is a legitimate analytical interpretation of observed morphology and chemistry. It is not a direct measurement of molten structural steel, nor a method for identifying when each sphere formed.

There is also a meaningful chemical distinction: a spherical particle containing Fe and O may have a different thermal and oxidation history from a metallic Fe droplet. Converting iron-oxide evidence into a universal steel-melting temperature would be unjustified.

USGS additionally observes that many glass spheres have slag-wool-like compositions. Those are different from its explicitly iron-rich spheres, illustrating why sorting particles merely by round shape would merge physically distinct classes. 
pubs.usgs.gov

5. Source dependencies and remaining evidence

The December 2003 RJ Lee summary, its May 2004 expert discussion and later articles quoting the 5.87% statistic form one underlying analytical source family, not multiple independent replications. The USGS atlas represents a separate institutional characterization, but lacks a demonstrated matched population and quantitative iron-sphere comparison.

The original 2003 RJ Lee study refers to a May 2003 Insurance Claim Report, Volume III, for more detailed protocols and statistical material (printed pp. 25–26). I did not locate an inspectable copy in the bounded public-source search. Its absence prevents verification of the exact iron-sphere denominator, sample weighting, number of analyzed portions and reported uncertainty.

Those records could test prevalence, phase identification and timing without requiring an unsupported site-wide mass estimate.

R13 disposition

Supported: Iron-rich spherical particles were reported in WTC-associated dust, and RJ Lee found a substantially higher classified fraction in its affected-building samples than in its selected ordinary-office comparison samples.

Not established: Exact reproducible particle denominators; the fraction by dust mass; the metallic or mineral phase of every observed sphere; formation on September 11 rather than before or afterward; an attributable energetic reaction; or whole-site quantities of melted iron.

The present findings are consistent with a complex mixture of building destruction, fires and other thermal or industrial processes, but do not uniquely identify their relative contributions. An intentional energetic source remains a separate, unverified hypothesis requiring diagnostic evidence beyond iron-sphere abundance.

R13 is complete as a published-method and source-provenance desk audit, with quantitative replication and formation-time attribution unresolved. It should remain separate from the completed R12 chip-lineage investigation.
