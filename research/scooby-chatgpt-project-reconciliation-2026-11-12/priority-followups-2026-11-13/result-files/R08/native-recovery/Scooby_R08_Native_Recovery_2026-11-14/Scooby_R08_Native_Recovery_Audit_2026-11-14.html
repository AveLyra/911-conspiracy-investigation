R08 — NIST workbooks recovered and audited

Recovery complete: 6 of 6 original NIST XLSX files. All returned final HTTP 200 after official 302 redirects, passed ZIP CRC verification and parsed as XLSX. The six files total 170,176,394 bytes. The audit covers 30 sheets, nine measurement sheets, 90,340 timed records, and 1,398 header occurrences. Originals were not opened and resaved.

This supersedes the 13 November source-access failure. The separate ambient specimen CB-DA-AMB, CB-DA’s own ambient preload, and CB-DA’s fire phase remain distinct. The official spreadsheets contain published engineering measurements, author-deleted intervals and calculated summary columns; “native” here identifies the distributed NIST files, not untouched continuous DAQ output.

Benchmark decision: the native-file and source-identification gate passes. A fully specified quantitative thermal-and-damage benchmark is not yet ready to run: clocks, physical sensor-validity windows, thermal-input choices and constitutive assumptions must first be fixed in a bounded input specification. Intact ambient and suitable structural-response segments are now available. No structural model, calibration or holdout test ran.

6. Formulas, caches and calibration distinctions

The full inventory preserves all 217,389 source formula cells and cached values, including the two untimed MAX cells. Shared-formula followers can have an empty formula-text node while referring to a stored shared master; that is not a missing formula. Shared masters, group IDs/ranges and follower records are retained separately. Nothing was recalculated or resaved.

For example, CB-DA fire JN2 contains AVERAGE(F2,G2,H2,I2,J2,K2,L2,P2,Q2,R2,S2,T2,U2,V2)*3.9 with stored cache 27.739223571428571. It selects fourteen brace-strain channels and excludes ColW1 and ColE1. Native comments at the force header/list state that negative means compression. This is an author-calculated force estimate, not an independent load-cell measurement. JM2=AVERAGE(IL2,IM2) separately averages VD3 and VD4.

Published expanded uncertainties (k=2), standard uncertainties, sample standard deviations and nominal sensor ranges remain different quantities. The detailed uncertainty sidecar cites the relevant report and operating conditions; a dictionary’s Range Low/High field is not a calibration certificate. The report’s 3.90 kN/µε relation and the workbook’s ×3.9 formula do not themselves establish the adequacy of an independent fixture calibration.

7. Benchmark gate after inspecting the originals

Pass: native evidence intake. The prior missing-byte blocker is resolved, and the complete sheet/header/time/formula evidence is now inspectable. A bounded ambient or structural-response study can use intact segments with explicit exclusions.

Not yet passed: a fully specified quantitative thermal-and-damage run. The central remaining limitation is physical validity, not file access. TN 2055 §3.3.3 (printed p.56; PDF p.59) documents CB-DA beam-thermocouple lead insulation failure around ten minutes after ignition and secondary junctions. Stored numeric values can then represent a different physical temperature. CB-DA-SC and CB-SP have different failure histories; CB-SP-SC has improved routing. A blank-cell mask cannot resolve this. Approximate report times also cannot identify an exact first invalid worksheet cell without an explicit derived rule.

TN 2054 §4.5 and TN 2055 §3.4.3 additionally limit rotation measurements by temperature and cable survival. Load removal is part of the actual experiment: CB-DA’s native event log records unloading at 1802.1081 s from gas flow, before its later cooling and fire-out events. A survival target cannot assume full sustained actuator loading after that event. CB-SP-SC has separate bolt-fracture, unloading, fire-out and late-collapse events, which must remain distinct damage/outcome targets.

Smallest next action: assemble one fixed model-input specification from these recovered files and reports: reconcile event clocks; choose reliable channels and time windows; list reported geometry, load/control and restraint/material inputs; mark selected heat-transfer and high-temperature constitutive laws as assumptions; then freeze target quantities, acceptance tolerances and a whole-specimen holdout. Keep thermal inputs separate from channels used for thermal validation. Do not require deleted pause records before using intact ambient loading segments. No further download, external contact or fee is needed to begin that preparation.
