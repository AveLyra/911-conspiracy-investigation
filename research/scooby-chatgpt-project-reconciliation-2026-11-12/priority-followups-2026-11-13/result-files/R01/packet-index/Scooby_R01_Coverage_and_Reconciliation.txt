        Accounts, copied-row hypothesis and databases
        Portfolio context does not establish account allocation
        The SEC memo describes a U.S. adviser managing several hedge funds with $5.3 billion. It says staff
        interviewed the CEO/trader and reviewed account statements; the FBI also interviewed them. The
        reported airline strategy includes September 6 shorts of 1,800 AMR, 8,700 Continental and 18,500
        Delta shares; later shorts of 3,800 Delta and 500 Northwest; and September 10 purchases of
        115,000 AMR shares alongside 117,000 UAL shares sold short.
        These are agency summaries of interviews and records. They do not show that every position
        belonged to one beneficial account. No visible identifier bridge ties the redacted managed-account
        forms/statements to the principal adviser, the canceled 500 or the separate loss example. Different
        issuers’ share counts cannot be treated as a portfolio offset; prices, allocations and complete
        positions are missing.
        SEC PDF 9–10; printed 8–9 — RSEC 03003652 at memo opening 2.

        the September 6 $5.3 billion adviser example. This is a reported staff finding, not replicated P&L.

          Preserve the SEC/FBI no-evidence conclusion
          The May 15, 2002 SEC memorandum says its review found no evidence of trading based on
          foreknowledge and explains unusual trading as consistent with legitimate strategies. It describes
          over 9.5 million transactions, 103 companies, six industry groups, seven markets and 32
          ETFs/indices, generally August 20–September 11, 2001. Broad foreign requests generally used
          August 27–September 11. It documents DOJ/FBI coordination, referrals and information sharing.
          Where SEC could not determine overseas owners or interview decision-makers, it referred matters to
          the Financial Review Group and records its conclusion after those discussions.
          The May 2004 follow-up records that the numerical corrections did not change the investigators’
          conclusion. Commission staff later supported that conclusion and separately described the
          U.S.-securities and foreign-securities scopes. This audit does not replace those findings with a
          volume or percentile inference, and does not claim to have independently replicated every trade or
          foreign disposition.
