**Important quantitative distinction:** The Chesney Table 1 variables include option-level volume, change in open interest and a model-based payoff/return statistic (`G_t`); none supplies executed account cash flows. An exceptional percentile or the paper's modeled proxy (`1−p_t`) is not the probability that a person had foreknowledge of 9/11. Poteshman's 2006 study independently found unusually high put buying relative to its comparison distributions; that statistical observation and the SEC/FBI's actor-level no-evidence assessment answer *different questions*.

## 4. Which anomaly explanations actually attach to which trade?

**Strongest identified coverage:**

1. **UAL Sept. 6**: SEC interviews of principal adviser CEO and executing trader, examination of account statements and broad airline strategy with both bear and bull exposures; an original SEC-described **2,000** order/trade count whose exact corrected fill allocation still needs a crosswalk. Cella testified the adviser was referred to FBI despite an SEC explanation; Commission monograph p. 149 states FBI also investigated and reached same conclusion. **No account-level 1,500/500 distribution verified.**
2. **AMR Sept. 10 / 20428354-like series:** Sarnoff newsletter issued Sept. 9; 26 interview-based explanations, 27 subscriber-list matches, plus other dispersed accounts investigated by SEC. **Newsletter is directly supported for named interview/subscriber sample, not every series contract**. Missing delivery logs/preexisting trade records mean subscriber presence is not automatically reliance for 27 additional buyers.
3. **AIG, Marsh & McLennan and other insurer anomalies:** SEC HO-9325 pp. 14–18 says it reviewed bluesheets and account statements, with examples involving hedges against long equity holdings; visible public report doesn't give a reproducible account-level trade tape for each anomaly.
4. **Offshore and large index trades:** Cella's 2003 interview pp. 5, 8–11 reports SEC/OIA and FBI referrals and no unexplained suspicious ownership at end of the investigation; it also concedes SEC did not acquire an actual German regulator report and he knew of the domestic trading from interviews and investigations. The Commission monograph later reports additional German law enforcement interviews and BKA lead response (printed pp. 151–152). Neither is a receipt for each individual foreign referral.

## 5. MaST, CD and NYSE MasterList: distinct provenance

**SEC database/analysis:** HO-9325 identifies **Appendix D, "Databases Created"** (printed p. 20), but the appendix rows, database schema, MaST naming, versioning, inclusion rules and completeness were not independently acquired. Treat **MaST** as a known prior-research locator requiring native documentation, not a dataset independently inspected in this audit.

**SEC → DOJ/CD transfer:** Cella's Sept. 16, 2003 interview MFR, printed **p. 9**, explicitly describes a CD containing SEC/SRO identified blue-sheet trading information transferred to **DOJ** and subsequently reviewed at **Seisint** with DOJ's **Paul Johns**, FBI **Gurvais Grigg**, and Cella. This supports a documentary **transfer and review account**, not recovery, hash or preservation of the original CD; it cannot automatically establish that every MaST row was transferred.

**NYSE MasterList:** SEC memo Appendix E is an NYSE report; neither its native MasterList nor its field dictionary was obtained here. Do not map NYSE MasterList row identity onto SEC MaST/CD rows by shared names, counts or a similarly named list.

**Foreign referrals:** SEC memo printed pp. 4–6 and Cella MFR printed pp. 9–11 document the SEC/OIA's requests to foreign regulators and Cella's reported outcomes. The Commission monograph printed pp. 151–152 records UK/German leads, German regulator statements, and the BKA's negative assessment. One December 2001 German statement distinguished **a still-open federal matter pending information** from **separate state-level investigations that had closed**. This demonstrates why neither global closure nor unexplained surviving offshore owner should be inferred without actual case-specific return letters and owner-level joins.

## 6. Investigative conclusion and its scope

**Record:** The SEC's May 15, 2002 investigation, later reaffirmed publicly on July 22, 2004, reports **no evidence developed that persons with advance knowledge traded on the basis of that information**. The review encompassed **over 9.5 million transactions**, securities/derivatives of **103 companies** across specified industries, 32 indices/ETFs, SRO data, short sales, electronic blue sheets, account interviews, broker-dealer canvasses and international cooperation. The Commission monograph Appendix B printed pp. 145–153 documents the FBI's involvement, lower-threshold profitable-trade referrals, supplementary review of staff material and case examples.

**Scope qualification:** This is a substantial negative *investigative finding*, not a probability calculation or a proof that every potential actor or every conceivable market worldwide was exhaustively searched. Cella's assurances of no unresolvable trades are **interview testimony**; source-by-source reproduction of beneficial owners, foreign dispositions, canceled fills and all dataset joins remains blocked.

**Contrary statistical evidence:** Poteshman (2006) reports unusual put-buying metrics; Chesney et al. isolate abnormal contract-level volume/open-interest patterns. These support the existence of unusual market observations, not the identity or intent of any 9/11-informed trader. A rigorous test would need authentic raw datasets, transformations, matched benchmarks and account identifiers; **not performed**.
