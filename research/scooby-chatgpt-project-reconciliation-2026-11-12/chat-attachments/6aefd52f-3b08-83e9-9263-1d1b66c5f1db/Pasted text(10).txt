Yes. If we use foreknowledge as a **working hypothesis**, the most productive leads are the documented handling of information about two future hijackers, warnings about aircraft attacks, and unusual pre-attack trading. Silverstein’s insurance is worth examining too, but the surviving lease terms substantially weaken the inference that obtaining terrorism coverage was itself an unusual preparation for 9/11.

The distinction that matters is **what was known, by whom, and how specifically**. A warning that al-Qaeda might attack the United States is evidence of threat awareness. Evidence that a person knew the date, targets, or method of the actual attacks would be much stronger.

The items in your table also need to be kept separate. A disputed military procedure, a post-attack records search, an alleged chart, and a corporate relocation do not become evidence of a coordinated plan merely because someone could imagine such a plan connecting them. The question is whether records establish an actual link.

## 1. Silverstein’s WTC insurance: real transaction, weak indicator by itself

**What happened.** Larry Silverstein’s entities took 99-year leases of WTC 1, 2, 4, and 5, effective July 24, 2001. They obtained approximately **$3.5 billion in property coverage per occurrence** from multiple insurers. After the attacks, Silverstein’s entities argued that the two aircraft impacts constituted two occurrences for insurance purposes. Litigation followed, with different policy terms producing different results. [Federal court’s account of the leases and insurance](https://www.stblaw.com/docs/default-source/cold-fusion-existing-content/publications/pub681.pdf?sfvrsn=2); [contemporaneous account of the two-occurrence claim](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/business/2001/10/09/insurers-dispute-trade-center-claim/142df029-2625-4717-a60c-edef6340f0a3/).

**Why it looks like a lead under the hypothesis.** The lease and coverage began only weeks before the buildings were destroyed, and the later claim sought more than the stated per-occurrence limit. Those are legitimate facts to investigate.

**The strongest counterevidence:** The court described the leases as requiring property and business-interruption insurance **without an exclusion for terrorist acts**. The leases also obligated the lessees to rebuild even if insurance proceeds were insufficient. And, according to the National Association of Insurance Commissioners, terrorism was generally covered in standard commercial policies before 9/11. So the available record supports a routine commercial explanation for obtaining coverage at the lease closing. [Federal court decision](https://www.stblaw.com/docs/default-source/cold-fusion-existing-content/publications/pub681.pdf?sfvrsn=2); [NAIC background on terrorism insurance](https://content.naic.org/cipr_topics/topic_terrorism_risk_insurance_act_tria.htm).

The approximately **$4.55 billion** eventually made available after litigation and settlements should not be described as Silverstein’s personal profit. A profit calculation would require the proceeds’ allocation, debt, lease obligations, rebuilding costs, later financing, and changes to the development agreements. Nor can I conclude from those figures alone that he personally lost money. [2007 settlement account](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2007/05/23/79970.htm).

**What would strengthen the lead:** A verifiable *pre-September 11* instruction from Silverstein or an associate showing knowledge of the actual attack, an unexplained deviation from the insurance requirements, or a contemporaneous transaction designed around the specific two-impact scenario. The post-attack argument that there were two occurrences does not establish that he anticipated two impacts beforehand.

**Assessment:** The timing is established and warrants inclusion in a chronology. The insurance terms, on the evidence reviewed, are a **weak indicator of foreknowledge**.

## 2. Information about two future hijackers was not passed on promptly

This is a substantially stronger investigative lead because it concerns identifiable people who participated in the attacks and documented decisions made before them.

**What the record says.** In early 2000, the CIA had information linking Khalid al-Mihdhar to suspected al-Qaeda activity and knew he had a multiple-entry U.S. visa. By March 2000, it had information that Nawaf al-Hazmi had flown to Los Angeles. The Justice Department inspector general found that critical information was not passed to the FBI in a timely manner.

The inspector general also described a specific draft report: an FBI employee detailed to the CIA prepared information for the FBI about Mihdhar’s visa and connections, but the report was not sent. A notation indicated that a CIA supervisor had directed that it be placed on hold. Witnesses interviewed later did not recall why. The FBI did not begin its search for Mihdhar until late August 2001 and had not found him by September 11. [DOJ inspector general’s detailed review, especially sections III–IV](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter5.htm).

**What this does and does not show.** This is evidence of a consequential failure to transmit information, including a documented hold on one proposed communication. It is more specific than saying agencies merely “failed to connect dots.” It is **not, by itself, evidence that the supervisor knew the 9/11 plan or intended the attacks to succeed**. The inspector general identified both individual and systemic failures and said the reason for that hold remained unexplained in the available testimony.

**What would distinguish the hypotheses:** The original draft report and routing history; contemporaneous messages explaining the hold; comparable decisions about other suspected operatives; and evidence of what the responsible officials actually knew at the time. A documented operational reason would support one explanation; a contemporaneous instruction to conceal the specific attack would support a very different one.

**Assessment:** **High-priority lead** on the handling of known suspects; the reason for the particular hold remains an important unresolved question in the cited account.

## 3. Earlier warnings about aircraft attacks

**1998.** A September 10, 1998 presidential intelligence brief discussed the possibility that a group associated with bin Laden might fly an explosive-packed aircraft into a U.S. city. The brief also qualified the available intelligence as inconclusive about bin Laden’s intentions. It did not identify the 9/11 date, targets, or hijacking plan. The CIA released this and other presidential briefing materials in September 2026. [CIA’s collection of declassified briefs, including “Bin Ladin’s Location and Intentions”](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/cia-releases-presidents-daily-briefs-in-commemoration-of-the-25th-anniversary-of-9-11/seventy-one-declassified-presidents-daily-brief-products/); [contemporaneous reporting on the release and its qualification](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/cia-declassified-9-11-files-intelligence-reports/).

**August 6, 2001.** President Bush received a brief titled *Bin Ladin Determined to Strike in US*. It discussed bin Laden’s interest in attacks in the United States and referred to information concerning possible hijackings. It did not identify the eventual attacks’ date, targets, or method. [Declassified August 6 brief](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/24279-bin-ladin-determined-strike-us-president-s-daily-brief-central-intelligence-agency).

**Why these matter.** They establish that a broad threat involving aircraft and al-Qaeda was foreseeable and had reached senior officials. They also constrain sweeping later claims that nobody had considered aircraft as weapons.

**Strongest objection to an inference of specific foreknowledge:** Intelligence services receive many reports of possible plots. A warning that resembles a later event in one respect can be compelling in hindsight without having identified that event beforehand. The relevant test is whether there was additional specific information connecting a named person to the date, flights, targets, or operational plan.

**Assessment:** Strong evidence of **general prior warning**; insufficient by itself to establish advance knowledge of the actual 9/11 operation.

## 4. Flight-school warnings and Moussaoui

**What happened.** An FBI agent in Phoenix circulated a July 2001 memo raising concerns about possible terrorist interest in U.S. civil aviation training. In August, a Minnesota flight school reported suspicions about Zacarias Moussaoui to the FBI. Agents suspected he might be planning a hijacking; they were unable to persuade headquarters to pursue a warrant to search his computer and belongings before 9/11. The Commission also reported restrictions on what Minneapolis agents could share with the FAA. [9/11 Commission, chapter 8](https://9-11commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch8.htm); [DOJ inspector general’s Moussaoui review](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/chapter4.htm).

**Why it is connected:** These were contemporaneous warnings about suspicious flight training, a feature relevant to the eventual attacks.

**Limit:** The Phoenix memo did not identify the 9/11 hijackers or their plan. Moussaoui’s precise relationship to the September 11 operation has been disputed; his arrest should not be presented as proof that the FBI had the complete plot in hand.

**Assessment:** A serious, documented **missed-opportunity lead**. The record cited here does not establish intentional protection of the attack.

## 5. Airline put options: a measurable anomaly with competing explanations

**What happened.** Put-option activity increased in the parent companies of United and American Airlines in the days before the attacks. A 2006 peer-reviewed paper by finance scholar Allen Poteshman found abnormal long-put volume consistent with the possibility of trading on advance knowledge. The paper did **not** identify a trader who knew of the attacks; it also found that the option-volume ratios it studied did not themselves establish unusual activity. [Poteshman, *Journal of Business*](https://911inacademia.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/01/a-poteshman-journal-of-business-2006-unusual-option-market-activity-and-the-9-11-attacks.pdf).

**The strongest counterevidence:** The SEC and FBI investigated trading. According to the 9/11 Commission staff, one U.S. investment adviser accounted for **95% of the September 6 United put volume**; investigators said the adviser had a documented bearish airline strategy and also bought American Airlines shares that lost value after the attacks. The staff traced much of the September 10 American put activity to an investment newsletter. The SEC reported reviewing securities and derivatives involving 103 companies. [Commission staff financing monograph](https://www.9-11commission.gov/staff_statements/911_TerrFin_Monograph.pdf); [SEC statement](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2004-98-statement-concerning-sec-terrorist-attack-trading-investigation).

The statistical study and investigation answer different questions: **Was aggregate activity unusual?** versus **Did investigators find a particular trader acting on attack knowledge?** An anomaly can exist without identifying its cause. Conversely, an official finding is only as complete as the transactions, beneficial owners, and records investigators could examine.

**Assessment:** A valid quantitative lead, but the cited evidence does **not establish a knowledgeable trader**. A fair deeper review would compare the paper’s exact dataset and measures with the SEC’s transaction-level work, rather than treating either as automatically dispositive.

## How the original table fits

The items you supplied are useful as leads to **check**, but they currently have different evidentiary status:

- **Military approval rule:** The 1997 predecessor is important counterevidence to the claim that Secretary of Defense approval for military chase-aircraft requests was introduced in June 2001. That document alone does not resolve what happened operationally on September 11. [1997 instruction](https://911facts.dk/resources/FAA/3610_01.pdf).
- **Pentagon videos:** The court opinion describes 85 potentially responsive videotapes identified in FBI databases, not 85 videos of impact. According to the agent’s account quoted by the court, one of those tapes showed the impact; the court assessed the reasonableness of the FOIA search. That finding is not a forensic examination of every possible recording outside the search. [Court opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_05-cv-00475/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_05-cv-00475-0.pdf).
- **Zim:** The April 3 report does establish that the planned September relocation was public months in advance. It does not, standing alone, establish the precise date every employee or asset left the WTC; that would require moving and occupancy records. [April 2001 trade report](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/zim-moves-regional-head-office-from-new-york-to-norfolk).
- **Able Danger:** The post-attack dating of the particular chart described in your table would undermine that chart as proof of an identification made before 9/11. It would not resolve every separate witness claim or establish what may have been on another chart. I have not independently checked the Senate report’s underlying passage in this pass.

My ranking is: **start with the Mihdhar/Hazmi information-sharing record**, then examine the flight-school warnings and the trading data. Include Silverstein’s insurance in the same chronology, but its known contractual and market context makes it less probative than its timing initially suggests. The strongest objection to a unified foreknowledge theory is that these events involve different actors and have independently documented explanations; at present, the sources above do not establish a common communications or decision chain linking them.
