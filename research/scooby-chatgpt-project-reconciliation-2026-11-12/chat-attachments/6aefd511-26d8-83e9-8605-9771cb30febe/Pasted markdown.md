The table’s central corrections are substantially supported, but it compresses several complicated records. The strongest documentary findings are that the Pentagon-approval requirement predates June 2001, the “85 videos” number is being misused, and the Atta chart supplied to Congressman Curt Weldon is described by its source as a post-9/11 product.

The most consequential qualifications are these:

- The 1997 military instruction contained the **immediate-response exception as well as the approval requirement**.
- The FBI’s account of 85 tapes is a **sworn search account in one FOIA case**, not a complete, independently audited inventory of every camera or recording.
- Marvin Bush’s past connection to a security contractor was real. Conflicting accounts remain about the contractor’s precise work at the World Trade Center in 2001; neither account establishes that Bush controlled building security.
- The Odigo report is a real, unresolved lead in the material I found. Its exact wording, sender, and investigative outcome need to be established before its significance can be assessed.
- None of these corrections resolves the separate, well-documented failures to share and act on intelligence before 9/11.

I examined the linked primary documents where available, the 9/11 Commission’s account, inspector-general findings, court opinions, and contemporary reporting. The assessments below distinguish a source’s statement from a conclusion independently established by it.

## 1. The June 2001 military rule and the alleged stand-down

**Assessment: The claim that the June 2001 instruction newly gave the defense secretary approval authority over military chase-aircraft requests is contradicted by the predecessor document. The broader question of why the response failed requires the operational record, not just the directives.**

The [July 31, 1997 Joint Chiefs instruction](https://911facts.dk/resources/FAA/3610_01.pdf), CJCSI 3610.01, expressly instructed the National Military Command Center to forward a request for military chase aircraft to the secretary of defense for approval. Its general responsibilities section also excepted certain “immediate responses” from that approval process.

The [June 1, 2001 replacement](https://www.cia.gov/library/abbottabad-compound/46/4606FCD86093D9FBC8FAE6D67856796D_NORAD_Intercept_Procedure.pdf), CJCSI 3610.01A, retained an approval route and an immediate-response exception. The [1997 underlying Defense Department directive](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/Homeland_Defense/12-F-0663_DOC_01_CONSEQUENCE_MANAGEMENT_PLANNING_FOR_2002_WINTER_OLYMPICS.pdf) defined immediate response in terms of action under imminently serious conditions to save lives, prevent suffering, or mitigate great property damage.

That exception should not be read as a blanket authorization for any particular action. In particular, **scrambling fighters, conducting an escort, and ordering a shootdown are different decisions**. The 9/11 Commission said the existing hijacking protocols anticipated a discreet escort and did not establish a procedure for shooting down an airliner being used as a weapon. [9/11 Commission, Chapter 1](https://www.911commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch1.htm)

### What happened operationally?

The Commission reconstructed the morning using, among other records, FAA and military recordings and radar data:

- Around **8:38 a.m.**, Boston FAA personnel reached the Northeast Air Defense Sector, or NEADS, about American Airlines Flight 11.
- NEADS ordered Otis fighters to battle stations. A commander recalled being told to scramble them and address authority afterward.
- The scramble order came at **8:46**, approximately when Flight 11 hit the North Tower; radar data showed the fighters airborne at **8:53**.
- Langley fighters were ordered scrambled at **9:24**, after NEADS received a mistaken report that Flight 11 was still airborne and heading toward Washington. They were airborne at **9:30**.
- NEADS learned that Flight 77 was missing at **9:34**; it struck the Pentagon at **9:37:46**. [Commission timeline and narrative](https://911commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch1.htm)

Those facts contradict a simple account that fighters were universally kept on the ground waiting for Rumsfeld. They do **not**, by themselves, establish that every delay was unavoidable or that every decision was competent.

There is also a legitimate reason to examine the government’s later accounts critically. The Transportation Department inspector general investigated inaccurate FAA statements about notifications to the military. The office said it did not find evidence sufficient to conclude officials knowingly made false statements, while also finding that three FAA executives failed to correct an erroneous response after learning it was inaccurate. [DOT inspector general, 2006](https://www.oig.dot.gov/library-item/30630)

**What would change the assessment?** A contemporaneous order, authenticated recording, or operational log directing aircraft to remain grounded—or showing that a specific request was held for approval when an available response was otherwise feasible—would warrant separate analysis. The June rule’s existence alone does not establish that.

## 2. The FBI’s 85 Pentagon videos

**Assessment: “The FBI had 85 videos showing what hit the Pentagon” misstates the court record. The court record also cannot prove that no other relevant footage exists anywhere.**

The relevant 2006 [federal district-court opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_05-cv-00475/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_05-cv-00475-0.pdf) recounts an FBI special agent’s search for records potentially responsive to a request for footage capturing the impact. According to her declaration:

| FBI search category                                                                              | Number |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------ |
| Potentially responsive videotapes located                                                        | 85     |
| Ruled out through chain-of-custody and related documentation as not showing the Pentagon on 9/11 | 56     |
| Remaining tapes personally viewed                                                                | 29     |
| Of those viewed, did not show the crash site                                                     | 16     |
| Showed the crash site only after impact                                                          | 12     |
| Described as showing the impact                                                                  | 1      |

These figures are **the agent’s classification as described in a judicial opinion**. The judge accepted the search as adequate under the applicable FOIA standard. That does not mean the court conducted a frame-by-frame forensic authentication of all 85 tapes, established a complete camera census, or proved that no additional recording could exist.

The broader disclosure history also matters. In a separate FOIA case, Judicial Watch obtained the Citgo and Doubletree hotel tapes after court orders and agreements with the government. The appellate opinion concerns disclosure and attorneys’ fees; it should not be treated as a finding that those tapes provide clear views of the aircraft impact. [D.C. Circuit opinion](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2008/04/07-5158-1110396.pdf); [Judicial Watch case history](https://www.judicialwatch.org/cases/judicial-watch-v-federal-bureau-of-investigation-no-06-1135/)

The identification of the aircraft is also a broader evidentiary question than the quality of any one security video. The Commission’s reconstruction draws on flight tracking, communications, and other records; the Justice Department reported recovery of Flight 77’s flight-data recorder at the Pentagon. Those sources do not make every individual video question disappear, but they mean the 85-tape claim is not the only possible route to assessing the crash. [9/11 Commission](https://www.911commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch1.htm); [DOJ remarks, September 20, 2001](https://www.justice.gov/archive/ag/speeches/2001/agcrisisremarks9_20.htm)

**Best remaining test:** Obtain the original Maguire declaration and its referenced inventory or chain-of-custody records, where releasable; map each item to its originating camera, recording period, field of view, and release status. A count without those attributes is a poor measure of what footage could show.

## 3. Able Danger and the Atta chart

**Assessment: The cited chart does not establish that Able Danger identified Mohamed Atta before 9/11. A real dispute over witness recollections and missing material remains, but the documentary corroboration alleged in the table’s first column has not been established.**

Able Danger was a Defense Department effort directed at mapping terrorist networks. Several people later said they recalled seeing Atta identified on a chart before the attacks.

The December 2006 [Senate Intelligence Committee leadership letter](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-filesations-abledanger.pdf) reports that investigators interviewed those witnesses and reviewed documents. They concluded the witness accounts were not reliable, citing inconsistencies with other testimony and, in some cases, the witnesses’ earlier statements.

The particularly important chart is the one connected to Weldon’s September 25, 2001 meeting with Deputy National Security Adviser Stephen Hadley. The analyst who brought a chart to Weldon’s office told investigators that her chart included Atta but was **prepared after September 11**. She also said Weldon brought other charts whose contents she had not seen. Committee staff could not determine exactly which chart or charts were shown to Hadley; they concluded that, if an Atta chart was presented, it had been prepared after the attacks.

That creates an asymmetry:

- The analyst’s account is direct evidence about the chart **she supplied**.
- It does not personally establish the contents of every other chart Weldon carried.
- The committee’s wider conclusion—that Able Danger did not identify any hijacker before 9/11—rests on its broader interviews and document review, not on that analyst alone.

A Defense Department witness at a 2006 hearing stated that the review had not found a pre-9/11 Atta chart or supporting data, while acknowledging that the available evidence did not let investigators prove definitively that no such chart had ever existed. A former participant testified that he was certain he had seen Atta’s name and picture. Those competing accounts deserve to be identified rather than flattened into “everyone agrees.” [Congressional hearing](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-109hhrg24962/html/CHRG-109hhrg24962.htm)

**What would change the assessment?** A demonstrably pre-9/11 chart, dated file with reliable metadata and custody, contemporaneous briefing, or independent record identifying this particular Mohamed Atta would be significant. The name alone would require identity verification.

## 4. Zim’s move from the World Trade Center

**Assessment: The move was planned publicly months before 9/11. That directly contradicts a claim of a wholly secret, spontaneous last-minute departure. It does not independently establish every detail of the lease or the final move.**

On **April 3, 2001**, shipping publication *American Shipper* reported that Zim would move its U.S. corporate office from New York to Norfolk in September. The article attributed the decision in part to business considerations and reported plans to retain and relocate key employees. [Contemporary trade report](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/zim-moves-regional-head-office-from-new-york-to-norfolk)

The distinction is important: **a move can be completed close to September 11 and still have been publicly planned months earlier**. The April report is strong evidence against the claim that the decision was secretly made immediately before the attacks. It is not, by itself, a lease termination agreement, a dated inventory of each employee’s departure, or proof that no one at the company ever had advance information.

To substantiate an advance-warning allegation, one would need evidence connecting an identifiable person with specific foreknowledge to the relocation decision—for example, dated communications or testimony corroborated by business records. Timing alone is weak evidence when a move was already publicly announced and has an ordinary business explanation.

## 5. Marvin Bush and WTC security

**Assessment: The business connection was real, but the leap from board membership to control of security on September 11 is unsupported by the cited evidence. The exact state of the company’s WTC contract in 2001 merits narrower wording.**

Marvin Bush, President George W. Bush’s brother, had served as a director of Securacom, later called Stratesec. Contemporary reporting placed his board service through fiscal year 2000. The company performed electronic-security work associated with the World Trade Center and had other airport or airline-related business. [2003 reporting](https://www.commondreams.org/views/2003/02/04/bush-linked-company-handled-security-wtc-dulles-and-united)

The [July 2001 proxy statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1037453/000092532801500025/defprxy.txt) identifies Wirt Walker as chairman and chief executive and does not list Marvin Bush among the directors or officers. That is useful evidence about the company’s disclosed leadership at the time of the filing. It does not, standing alone, establish the full history of Bush’s ownership, relationships, influence, or any later change.

There are **conflicting reported accounts** about Stratesec’s WTC work:

- A 2003 article quoted then-CEO Barry McDaniel saying the company had a completion contract involving some WTC security through the day the buildings fell.
- A later investigation quoted a former manager saying the company was removed from a particular WTC electronic-security project in 1998, with work taken over by another contractor. [2003 account](https://www.commondreams.org/views/2003/02/04/bush-linked-company-handled-security-wtc-dulles-and-united); [2005 account](https://washingtonspectator.org/family-business-at-the-watergate/)

Those accounts could reflect disagreement, different phases of work, or different contracts; the sources I reviewed do not resolve which. Even a contract active in September 2001 would need its **scope** established. Installing or maintaining electronic systems is not automatically the same as commanding guards, controlling tenant access, or directing airport passenger screening.

**Best remaining test:** Obtain the Port Authority’s contracts, amendments, closeout documents, subcontractor records, and relevant access-control responsibility charts. Then compare the dates and scope to company governance and any documented instructions from Bush. That would answer a more useful question than the word “security” alone.

## 6. Bin Laden’s acknowledgment

**Assessment: The categorical statement that bin Laden never acknowledged responsibility is contradicted by his 2004 public statement.**

In a video broadcast by Al Jazeera on October 29, 2004, bin Laden discussed the decision to strike the American towers and linked his stated motivation to events in Lebanon and Palestine. ABC published a [government translation of the speech](https://abcnews.com/International/story?id=215913); contemporaneous [PBS reporting](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/bin-laden-warns-of-new-attacks-criticizes-bush-in-video-message) also identified the broadcast and its claims.

This is independent of arguments over the provenance or translation of a separate tape released in 2001. A claim can be tested against multiple statements rather than made to depend on the disputed one.

There are two limitations:

1. The linked ABC text is a **translation**, not the Arabic original. If a specific disputed phrase matters, compare the video and Arabic transcript against independent translations.
2. Bin Laden’s own claim is evidence of his claimed role, not proof of every operational detail or a reason to accept all of his statements uncritically.

## 7. Odigo’s reported message

**Assessment: Contemporary reporting supports the narrower account in the table: two recipients were reported in Israel, and a company executive said the message did not mention the World Trade Center. The message’s precise meaning and investigative disposition remain unresolved on the sources I found.**

On September 28, 2001, [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1412803/fbi-probes-advance-im-attack-warnings.html) reported that Odigo executive Alex Diamandis identified two recipients in Herzliya, Israel. He described the message as a nonspecific threat that did not mention the WTC, and said its timing made it unusual. The article reported that the sender’s IP information had been passed through Israeli security services to the FBI.

That is evidence of an executive’s contemporaneous account—not the message itself or the FBI’s final finding. The precise text, timestamp, originating account and address, preservation of server logs, sender identification, and investigative outcome are material unknowns in what I reviewed.

The stronger allegation in the table—that Odigo specifically warned New York employees to stay away from the WTC—does not follow from that report. At the same time, “nonspecific” does not prove the message was a harmless coincidence. The leading competing explanations include a generic threat whose timing acquired significance after the attacks, an unrelated threat, or a message connected to genuine advance knowledge. Without the text and provenance, the alternatives cannot be weighed confidently.

**Best remaining test:** Locate the original message and server records, followed by the FBI and Israeli investigative disposition. Establish the timestamp independently and determine whether the message identified a place, target, method, or time before interpreting its predictive value.

## Connections that are supported—and those that are not

### Real intelligence failures connect to the Able Danger question

The weak Atta-chart claim should not be confused with the much better documented issue of **missed intelligence opportunities**.

The 9/11 Commission found that U.S. agencies had several opportunities in 2001 to focus on Nawaf al-Hazmi and Khalid al-Mihdhar, two eventual Flight 77 hijackers. Information about them was not effectively shared and acted on; the U.S. search began late in August 2001 and failed to locate them before the attacks. The Commission discussed the counterfactual cautiously: closer monitoring might have opened investigative opportunities, but stopping the plot is not something the record can establish with certainty. [Commission, Chapter 8](https://911commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch8.htm); [Commission hearing](https://911commission.gov/archive/hearing7/9-11Commission_Hearing_2004-01-26.htm)

Separately, the Justice Department inspector general investigated the FBI’s handling of the **Phoenix communication**, which raised concerns about individuals associated with flight training, and the **Moussaoui investigation**. Those are concrete records of warnings and institutional response, not proof that officials possessed the complete 9/11 plan. [DOJ inspector-general report](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0606/index.htm)

The useful connection is therefore a research question: *what information was available to which office, when, what did it mean at the time, and what action was feasible?* A contested Able Danger recollection should be evaluated alongside, but not merged with, those documented intelligence failures.

### The Pentagon and stand-down claims share an operational timeline

Flight 77 connects the military-response and Pentagon-video questions: one concerns what defenders knew and could do before impact; the other concerns recordings and identification after impact. They require different evidence. A weakness in a FOIA search does not establish a prior stand-down order, and a mistake in the military’s timeline does not by itself establish what a camera recorded.

### Zim and Odigo do not establish a shared warning network

The two stories are often placed together because both involve companies with Israeli connections. The sources reviewed establish a publicly announced Zim relocation and a separately reported message to two Odigo employees in Israel. **I found no source connecting the Zim relocation decision to the Odigo message or establishing a common warning mechanism.** Nationality, proximity to the WTC, or inclusion in the same later narrative does not supply that missing link.

The most direct disconfirming evidence for such a proposed connection would be a complete Odigo message and investigation identifying an unrelated sender, together with ordinary Zim relocation records. Conversely, dated communications showing a shared, specific warning reaching decision-makers before the move or attacks would materially change the assessment.

## Priority records for further research

If the goal is to move beyond evaluating repeated claims and establish new facts, these are the highest-value records:

1. **Odigo:** Original message, server timestamp and logs, and FBI/Israeli investigative disposition. This is the most visibly unresolved item in the seven.
2. **Stratesec:** WTC contracts and amendments through September 2001, including precise system, access, and personnel responsibilities. These could resolve the conflicting reports about its role.
3. **Pentagon footage:** Maguire’s original declaration and item-level evidence inventory, plus camera locations and fields of view. This would distinguish missing footage from cameras that could not have captured the event.
4. **Military response:** Original FAA/NEADS recordings, dispatch logs, radar tracks, and the bases for revised official timelines. These allow specific delays and disputed decisions to be tested.
5. **Able Danger:** Any preserved pre-9/11 chart, dated underlying files, or contemporaneous distribution records. These are more probative than recollections first recorded years later.

The evidence reviewed supports rejecting several specific claims in the table, with **high confidence** on the 1997 approval provision and the misuse of the 85-tape count. It supports a **more qualified conclusion** on the Atta chart and Stratesec’s exact contract scope. Odigo remains a genuine provenance question: the available report is too nonspecific to establish either advance knowledge of 9/11 or an innocent explanation.
