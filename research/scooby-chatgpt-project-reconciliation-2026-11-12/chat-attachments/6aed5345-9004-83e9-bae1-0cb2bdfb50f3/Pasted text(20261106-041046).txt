## 1. Saudi assistance: a more concrete intelligence connection

**A June 14, 2017 FBI memorandum expressly identifies Omar al-Bayoumi as a paid Saudi intelligence collaborator.** Titled *“Albayoumi / GIP Cooptee,”* it states that he received a monthly stipend through Ambassador Prince Bandar and forwarded information about the Saudi community for Saudi General Intelligence Presidency purposes. The memo says this information confirmed intelligence-affiliation allegations that had remained unconfirmed when the 9/11 Commission reported. The document is reproduced in an official Senate records request, with identifiers **EO14040-002638–002639**. [HSGAC](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023-09-10-Blumenthal-and-Johnson-Letter-to-DOJ-and-FBI-re-Sept.-11.pdf)

That supports a more precise proposition than “a Saudi-connected man helped hijackers”: **the FBI subsequently recorded an intelligence relationship involving the same intermediary.** The source and some supporting details remain redacted, so the public cannot independently audit the underlying intelligence. The memorandum does not itself establish that his assistance was knowingly directed toward the attacks. [HSGAC](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023-09-10-Blumenthal-and-Johnson-Letter-to-DOJ-and-FBI-re-Sept.-11.pdf)

The **August 28, 2025 federal court decision** adds evidentiary weight. Judge George Daniels found sufficient evidence at the jurisdictional stage to support inferences of knowing or deliberately indifferent assistance and government coordination. Among the evidence discussed was Bayoumi’s handwritten aircraft diagram and calculations. Importantly, the judge did not accept every plaintiffs’ interpretation: he rejected treating the apartment gathering as a demonstrated terrorist welcome party or the Capitol video as proven reconnaissance on the showing presented. This was a substantive assessment of evidence, but **not a final liability finding**. [U.S District Court](https://wp.nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/03-md-01570%2011182.pdf)

**A significant subsequent development is the BBC’s September 7, 2026 investigation.** It reported newly public footage showing Bayoumi embracing Anwar al-Awlaki in January 1998 and participating in paintball outings involving him—contradicting Bayoumi’s 2021 testimony that they had not met. According to the BBC, the footage entered the public docket on September 4, 2026. That is corroboration of an actual relationship, rather than a geographical coincidence or telephone-directory association. It does not, by itself, establish knowledge of the eventual attack plan. [AOL.com](https://www.aol.com/articles/videos-man-9-11-lawsuit-050038000.html)

The BBC also interviewed former investigators who described unsuccessful attempts to obtain British-seized evidence and permission to interview Saudi officials. [AOL.com](https://www.aol.com/articles/videos-man-9-11-lawsuit-050038000.html)

The principal contrary official finding remains the FBI’s **May 27, 2021 closure assessment**, which concluded that insufficient evidence existed to prosecute Bayoumi, Thumairy, and Jarrah for knowingly conspiring to assist the attacks. ## 2. The Saudi–CIA liaison hypothesis has a specific evidentiary lead

Your map describes a liaison channel as an inference to test. There is a more specific allegation worth adding: **Donald Canestraro’s July 20, 2021 declaration**, associated with military-commission discovery motion **AE827 (AAA WBA)**. The official motion expressly seeks discovery concerning Bayoumi and a possible recruitment operation. [mc.mil](https://www.mc.mil/Portals/0/pdfs/KSM2/KSM%20II%20%28AE827%28AAA%20WBA%29%29_Part1.pdf)

Canestraro, a defense investigator, reports interviews with former intelligence and law-enforcement personnel. On printed pages **20–21**, a confidential source designated **CS-23**, described as a former FBI special agent, alleges that:

- The CIA maintained operational files on Bayoumi despite telling FBI personnel it had no files.
- Bayoumi was instructed through Thumairy to try to recruit Hazmi and Mihdhar.
- The proposed recruitment was CIA-directed through Saudi intelligence liaison, and subsequent investigation was suppressed.

These are unusually specific allegations concerning an operational relationship and a claimed documentary trail. But **the declaration establishes that Canestraro reported receiving these statements—not that their underlying claims have been independently proved**. The source is unnamed publicly, and the operational files, tasking instructions, and purported witnesses are not supplied in those passages. [Florida Bulldog](https://www.floridabulldog.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Canestraro-Declaration-dated-20-July-2021.pdf)

This makes the recruitment hypothesis more than an inference from suspicious coincidences. It supplies a concrete allegation that can be tested against records. It still does not connect such a recruitment attempt to UMS, explosives, or authorization of the attacks.

**Separately, the DOJ inspector general documented an actual interruption of dissemination.** An FBI detailee drafted a report conveying Mihdhar’s U.S. visa information. ## 3. UMS: support for an intelligence hypothesis, but not yet a demolition role

**Contemporaneous reporting provides a substantive basis for investigating whether UMS was an intelligence front.** In March 2002, *The Forward* reported that an unnamed former senior American intelligence official, who said he received briefings from two law-enforcement officials, described an FBI assessment that UMS was a Mossad front and at least two employees were operatives monitoring local Arabs. The limitation is consequential: the same reporting said the men had been cleared of advance knowledge of 9/11. ## 5. Physical evidence: affirmative findings worth preserving, with narrower implications

**FEMA documented unusual metallurgical damage.** Appendix C of its 2002 building-performance study examined two severely eroded steel members, one believed to be from WTC 7 and another from a tower. It identified oxidation and sulfidation, including localized intergranular melting in the presumed WTC 7 sample. The authors explicitly left the sulfur’s source and the corrosion rate unresolved, and considered both pre-collapse damage and prolonged heating in the debris as possibilities. They recommended further study. [FEMA](https://www.fema.gov/pdf/library/fema403_apc.pdf)

That is genuine physical evidence supporting targeted questions about chemical processes and follow-up testing. It is **not identification of an incendiary charge**, and the appendix itself did not determine whether the phenomenon preceded the collapse. Describing it as “FEMA found thermite” would substantially overstate the finding. [FEMA](https://www.fema.gov/pdf/library/fema403_apc.pdf)

**There is an affirmative published thermitic-material claim.** Harrit and colleagues’ 2009 paper reported examining red/gray chips from four WTC dust samples and interpreted their results as evidence of active thermitic material. Unlike an argument from appearance alone, this is a laboratory claim with described analytical methods. [benthamopenarchives.com](https://benthamopenarchives.com/abstract.php?ArticleCode=TOCPJ-2-7)

The important qualification is that James Millette’s 2012 laboratory investigation of visually similar chips from **different dust samples** identified epoxy coating constituents and did not identify elemental aluminum, reaching a non-thermite interpretation. Because he did not examine Harrit’s actual specimens, it is not a direct same-specimen replication. It nevertheless demonstrates why appearance and elemental overlap alone cannot settle identification. The evidentiary issue is distinguishing the particular specimens, establishing provenance, and obtaining independent analysis—not simply counting papers on either side. [ANETA](https://aneta.org/911experiments_com/Millette/paper/index.htm)

**The UAF study supplies a specific alternative structural analysis.** Its researchers concluded that fire did not cause WTC 7’s collapse and that the observed behavior involved near-simultaneous failure of all columns. The university hosts the report, simulations, and downloadable computational data; the project was funded by Architects & Engineers for 9/11 Truth. This supports the existence of an explicit, inspectable engineering challenge to NIST—not identification of an explosive mechanism or responsible personnel. A modeled failure pattern does not, by itself, establish what physically produced it. [INE University of Alaska Fairbanks](https://ine.uaf.edu/wtc7)

Two NIST acknowledgments also remain relevant. NIST recognizes a **2.25-second free-fall interval of WTC 7’s north face**, while explaining it through prior structural failure. Separately, it states that it **did not test the towers’ steel for explosive or thermite residues**. Thus, its rejection of demolition should not be represented as a negative residue-test result. Neither acknowledgment independently establishes demolition. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation)

None of these physical sources identifies UMS. Even a successful challenge to a collapse model would leave the perpetrator-attribution problem unresolved.

## 6. Iraq: strong contemporaneous evidence of an existing objective and subsequent policy use

**A July 27, 2001 Rumsfeld memorandum to Condoleezza Rice predates the attacks.** Its working paper discusses Iraq policy alternatives, including regime change, and argues that Saddam’s removal would improve the United States’ regional position. Because it also discusses other options, it establishes that regime change was under active consideration—not that a particular invasion or provocation had already been decided. [National Security Archive](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB326/doc06.pdf)

**Stephen Cambone’s September 11 notes record Rumsfeld considering Iraq immediately after the attacks.** They include the question of striking Saddam Hussein alongside bin Laden and language about sweeping in matters both related and unrelated. This is contemporaneous documentary support for the proposition that officials considered using the attack’s aftermath to pursue objectives beyond the identified perpetrators. [National Security Archive](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB326/doc07.pdf)

**The Defense Department inspector general later documented a relevant institutional mechanism.** In 2007 testimony, the acting inspector general said the policy office produced and disseminated alternative assessments of Iraq–al-Qaeda relations that diverged from intelligence-community conclusions without adequately communicating those differences. He characterized the activity as inappropriate, though not illegal or unauthorized. This supports scrutiny of how policy preferences shaped the presentation of intelligence. [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-110shrg35438/html/CHRG-110shrg35438.htm)

For the commercial-benefit component, GAO documented the sole-source Iraq oil-infrastructure contract and examined its justification. But it found that this award complied with the applicable competition exception. The record supports the existence of concentrated commercial opportunity; it does not independently establish corrupt procurement, advance knowledge of 9/11, or participation in the attacks. [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a242676.html)

**The supported inference is “existing objectives influenced the exploitation and interpretation of the aftermath.” The stronger claim—“the attacks were organized to create that opportunity”—still requires pre-attack operational evidence connecting those objectives to the perpetrators.**
