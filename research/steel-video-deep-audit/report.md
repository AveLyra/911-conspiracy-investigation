# FEMA-labeled WTC 7 steel and the west-penthouse timing comparison

**Research-only deep audit — September 12, 2026**

## Bottom line

Two matters often compressed into simple claims have materially different evidentiary status.

1. **Steel.** FEMA's 2002 Appendix D publicly labels four components, across three inventory items, as WTC 7 material. NIST's 2005 inventory says it received two candidates carrying the corresponding identifiers, A/W14A and B/W14B, and lists them as *unidentified* wide-flange pieces. That is a real documentary tension worth resolving. It establishes neither that either item actually came from WTC 7 nor that NIST discarded, concealed, or tested it improperly. It also does not connect either item to FEMA's Sample 1.

2. **Video.** NIST itself reported the west-penthouse comparison: observed descent below the roofline at 9.3 seconds, versus 6.9 and 7.3 seconds in its debris-impact global-model views. The repository's later video work did **not** independently remeasure that event. It audited available derivative recordings and performed a limited roof-corner pixel track. Those results make a fresh independent measurement more necessary, not less, because the access copies' provenance, cadence, calibration, and penthouse visibility remain unresolved.

Neither point is evidence of an intentional collapse or a cover-up. The responsible use of both is to request or locate the specific records that could falsify ordinary explanations.

## 1. Steel: the documented chain and its limit

### Plain account

FEMA's field-era spreadsheet said that pieces A/W14A and B/W14B were from WTC 7. NIST later listed pieces with those same unusually specific labels in its own received-sample inventory, but did not treat them as confirmed WTC 7 steel. That means the proposition “NIST never received a FEMA-labeled WTC 7 candidate” is not supportable. It does *not* mean “NIST possessed confirmed WTC 7 steel and hid it.” The missing step is the physical chain proving that the same pieces moved from the yard to NIST and explaining why their building identification did not survive NIST's review.

### What the primary records say

FEMA Appendix D's March 15, 2002 SEAoNY inventory (page 2 of 13) contains the following WTC#7 labels at the Claremont Terminal:

| FEMA inventory item | Field-era label and record locator | NIST inventory result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | **A or W14A**, WTC#7 W14; trip date Dec. 19, 2001; photo series Jan. 30, 2002, nos. 68–73 | **W-14A or A**, FEMA flag “Y,” heavy wide flange; location 205; unidentified |
| 21 | **B or W14B**, WTC#7 W14; trip date Dec. 19, 2001; photo series Jan. 30, 2002, nos. 63–67 | **W-14B**, FEMA flag “Y,” heavy wide flange; location PL; unidentified |
| 24 | **C12a**, WTC#7 W14 tree/column portion; Jan. 21, 2002 photos 40–45, with attached **C12b** beam portion | No exact C12a/C12b entry found under those names in NIST Table A-1 |

The labels above are not merely generic W-section descriptions. NIST explains that Table A-1 is its complete list of received samples and that it retained SEAoNY identifiers for consistency with FEMA. The two matching labels, together with the FEMA flag, materially support a documentary receipt link. They are stronger than trying to infer a relationship from an aggregate count of steel shipments.

But the same NIST rows leave building, column, floor, and markings blank; Table A-7 classifies both as unidentified. NIST's executive summary says that no piece was unambiguously identified as WTC 7. These propositions can coexist:

    FEMA field attribution ──(identifier continuity)──> NIST receipt
           “WTC#7”            A/W14A and B/W14B        “unidentified”

                 still missing: item-specific physical continuity

The FEMA spreadsheet's “Trip Date” is not automatically a recovery date, shipment date, or NIST intake date. FEMA's aggregate disposition note also cannot allocate these particular items among material sent to NIST, retained at yards, couponed, or processed. Those are reasons not to turn the documentary join into a complete custody narrative.

### What this changes—and does not

**Changed.** It is now reasonable to ask NIST for the item-level basis for treating A/W14A and B/W14B as unidentified, and to ask for their receiving photographs, accession/renumbering sheets, and any disposition record. A public copy of NIST's FOIA log also identifies a specific older request, DOC-NIST-2013-001311 (subject: “column W14B”), which may be an efficient administrative-file locator.

**Not changed.** There is no published member-to-coupon bridge tying either candidate to FEMA Appendix C Sample 1. Sample 1's reported metallurgical condition is therefore a separate provenance question. Nor do the reviewed records show that anyone ordered the candidates destroyed, withheld a result, or knew their origin while representing the opposite.

### Competing explanations the missing records could distinguish

| Explanation | Records that would support or weaken it |
| --- | --- |
| Field misattribution or an uncertain salvage-yard label | Field notes, original Claremont photographs, attribution worksheet, comparison with drawings/markings |
| Correct field label but lost/insufficient identity on later receipt | Receiving images, transport manifest, damage/cutting description, NIST accession and renumbering records |
| NIST had a stronger reason to reject the WTC 7 designation | Item-specific identification analysis and correspondence explaining the blank fields |
| Improper disposition or unexplained reclassification | Dated disposition, transfers, coupon log, contradictions between images/measurements and later catalog entries |

Only the last category could begin to support an evidence-integrity concern, and even that would not establish an intentional-collapse hypothesis without separate evidence of knowledge, purpose, and a causal connection.

### Most probative next records

1. Original Claremont images for W14A (Jan. 30, 2002, 68–73) and W14B (Jan. 30, 2002, 63–67), with the photo index/metadata.
2. NIST receiving/intake images, measurements, accession sheets, original-label photos, transport/renumbering records, and final disposition for each item.
3. The worksheet or correspondence recording why the WTC 7 attribution was not accepted.
4. The full administrative file for DOC-NIST-2013-001311.
5. For C12a/b, a split-component/renumbering and receipt/disposition crosswalk.
6. Separately, any authenticated parent-member-to-coupon/mount record before making a Sample 1 claim.

## 2. West-penthouse timing: what NIST actually compared

### The published numbers

NIST NCSTAR 1A Table 3-1 uses the start of the east-penthouse descent as its zero. Its rows are event times in that relative coordinate, not independent wall-clock timestamps. For descent of the west penthouse below the roofline, the table reports:

| Event | Video observation | Case B with debris impact damage | Case B without debris impact damage |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| West penthouse below roofline | **9.3 s** | **6.9, 7.3 s** | **10.6, 10.9 s** |

The first and second model values correspond to two stated views: northwest and below, then north at the roofline. Thus “6.9 and 7.3” are not two independent simulations or confidence bounds. They are two view-dependent reported detections from the debris-damage run.

This is a genuine published difference: the debris-damage predictions precede the reported observation by roughly 2.0–2.4 seconds. The no-debris run instead follows it by roughly 1.3–1.6 seconds. Neither observation establishes why the difference exists. In particular, this table does not say that 9.3 s is known with zero uncertainty, that 6.9/7.3 were measured from the same native source by the same rule, or that one mismatch identifies a different physical cause.

The neighboring rows show why it should be treated as a specific validation question rather than a slogan:

| Table 3-1 event | Observed | With debris | Without debris |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| North-face roofline's initial downward motion at east section | 6.9 | 6.3 | 9.8 |
| East end of screenwall below roofline | 8.5 | 7.3, 7.7 | 8.7, 9.2 |
| West penthouse below roofline | 9.3 | 6.9, 7.3 | 10.6, 10.9 |

NIST's FAQ emphasizes agreement on the initial north-face descent and says that later global-collapse progression has increased uncertainty. That is the agency's explanation for giving the model overall weight; it is not an answer to the particular west-penthouse row. A sound review should keep both facts in view.

### Did our video work independently test it?

**No. It did not.** The work performed real technical checks, but they had a different scope:

* The Camera 3 pixel-track pilot decoded 232 frames from an access copy and tracked a right roof-corner anchor until the algorithm reached its search boundary at frame 200. The separate penthouse templates rejected far earlier (frames 68 and 71) as the feature changed/was obscured. Those rejection points are algorithm failures, explicitly not measured penthouse-collapse times.
* A frame/cadence review found recurrent near-identical content in the access copy and retained it as recording-process uncertainty. It did not infer an edited timeline, an original exposure rate, or a cause.
* The provenance work found longer derivative Camera 3 files and a saved Tracker project, but did not establish a camera-original source, a tape-to-file chain, or equivalence to NIST's input.
* The team recomputed properties of a printed curve from NCSTAR 1-9; it did not recover NIST's raw point/time data or independently estimate the west-penthouse event.

So the correct description is: **we audited important preconditions for video measurement and carried out a limited roofline pilot. We did not complete the event-specific west-penthouse comparison.** The old wording should be corrected anywhere it implies otherwise.

### What an actual independent timing test needs

1. Obtain the closest available source to NIST's CBS Archive/DV input and preserve its chain of custody, frame timestamps, interlacing/aspect details, and any edits.
2. Predefine what counts as “west penthouse below roofline” in each named view, then have at least two blinded annotators mark first/last ambiguous frames.
3. Report a frame interval and all sources of uncertainty rather than one unqualified decimal time.
4. Use the same event rule on the original model renders or source-result frames; preserve every frame, camera transform, and selected view.
5. Align each series to the same east-penthouse zero using a stated rule; do not silently borrow a time axis from another clip.
6. Test whether the reported difference remains after source, viewpoint, and event-definition uncertainty are included.

The full LS-DYNA results and model-view provenance are particularly relevant to steps 4–6. The current released input materials do not, by themselves, provide that reproduction.

## 3. What the UAF study contributes

### A source-level assessment

The March 2020 report, *A Structural Reevaluation of the Collapse of World Trade Center 7*, is a detailed technical report by J. Leroy Hulsey, Zhili Quan, and Feng Xiao. It identifies two external reviewers and openly discloses that AE911Truth funded the work. Those disclosures should be retained exactly as provenance facts. Funding and an external-review label do not decide whether a calculation is correct; nor do they replace independent replication, a complete model archive, or comparison to competing models.

The report describes three linked activities:

1. local fire-response analyses around Floors 12 and 13;
2. a challenge to NIST's Column 79 / girder A2001 initiation path; and
3. whole-building simulations of hypothesized member failures.

It concludes that fire did not cause the collapse and that near-simultaneous failure of every column best explains the observed behavior. Its strongest useful contribution is narrower: it identifies model-sensitive disputes about connection geometry, shear studs, thermal restraint, the side plate near Column 79, and whether a Column 79 failure would propagate across the core. Those are concrete propositions that can be tested against drawings, connection calculations, and executable models.

### The necessary distinction: prescribed failure versus cause

For its closest visual comparator, UAF removed all core columns over eight stories and then, 1.3 seconds later, all exterior columns over the same eight stories. It reports that this prescribed failure pattern produces behavior close to its selected videos. That establishes a **conditional model result**: if that broad pattern of support removal is imposed in its model, its model can produce a roughly similar exterior descent.

It does not establish what physically caused those removals. The report expressly says it could not simulate the north-face “kink” with its software, and it relies on a prior Chandler motion measurement for part of its velocity comparison. The repository's video audit has not independently validated that measurement or its source/camera chain. A scenario selected because it matches a target trajectory cannot by itself identify the historical initiating mechanism; it must be connected to independently supported loads, temperatures, material failure paths, physical evidence, and a reproducible model run.

This distinction matters equally for any model. NIST's damage transfer is an imposed/model-dependent handoff from its fire analysis; UAF's all-column removal is an imposed/model-dependent collapse initiation. Neither should be described as a direct recording of hidden historical failures.

### What can be borrowed responsibly

| UAF issue | Why it is useful | What is needed before relying on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A2001 side plate / walk-off geometry | A discrete, drawing-testable claim; it can be evaluated with original shop drawings, dimensions, clearances, connection condition, and thermal-displacement calculations | Independent geometry reconstruction and a documented connection/contact model |
| Shear studs and thermal restraint | Identifies a high-sensitivity boundary-condition question rather than a broad intuition about whether steel “can” fail in fire | Exact as-built details, failure properties, slab/beam interaction and sensitivity sweeps |
| Column 79-to-core progression | Tests whether a local initiation could plausibly propagate under a particular global model | Matched NIST/UAF simulations with the same damage, damping, contact, deletion and output conventions |
| Near-simultaneous column removal | A useful mechanical comparator for what a specified global support-loss pattern produces | An independently evidenced mechanism capable of causing that pattern; full input/result release; blinded video comparison |

The UAF website advertised a 256 GB archive of its input, result, and simulation files. In the prior model-access audit, the live project download reached only an 834-byte wrapper pointing to a third-party repaired archive; no substantive UAF model files were inspected or executed. Thus the report's claim of data availability is a lead, not a completed local reproduction. A full archive download is neither necessary nor sufficient: the usable unit is a manifest plus the exact case-entry deck, solver version, custom routines, dependencies, run history, and checksums for the reported cases.

## 4. Other initiatives and the competing-model check

### NIST

NIST's published account is a coupled fire/structural path: video-informed fire reconstruction, thermal/connection response in an ANSYS model, and a later global LS-DYNA collapse calculation. NIST's later technical paper confirms that the fire-stage model used temperature-dependent behavior, user-defined “break elements” for connections, and user intervention to remove failed elements. It also identifies connection type, shear studs, asymmetric framing, bay spans, and slab conditions as sensitivity variables.

That makes the present record problem precise. The public can test whether NIST's proposed sequence is physically possible at a high level, but cannot independently establish how robust the exact handoff and collapse sequence are while the connection calculations, break-element code, full results, damage-transfer logic, and supporting material remain unavailable. That is a reproducibility gap, not evidence that NIST fabricated a result.

### UCL / Orabi et al.

The 2022 peer-reviewed *Fire Technology* paper by Orabi, Jiang, Usmani, and Torero supplies an important disconfirming comparator to the proposition that all credible fire hypotheses have already failed. Using two OpenSees models, it explores whether a possible diesel-fueled mechanical-room fire could compromise transfer structure and remove support for at least two core columns. The authors conclude feasibility, not a completed historical reconstruction: they expressly call the analysis preliminary, say further validation is required, and state that their current study does not account for joint failure.

That work does **not** prove a diesel fire occurred or caused the collapse. It is important because it produces a technically different fire-driven pathway from NIST's floor-12/13 narrative and UAF's rejection of fire initiation. A model portfolio that cannot distinguish these pathways does not yet select a best explanation.

### FEMA and private-litigation studies

FEMA's 2002 study was preliminary, issued before the later NIST investigation, and is not a complete collapse reconstruction. The UAF and UCL reports also discuss the Arup/Guy Nordenson and Weidlinger litigation analyses. Those discussions show that competing technical narratives existed, but this audit has not acquired, source-pinned, or independently evaluated the underlying private reports. Their characterizations must not be cited as established facts through an opponent's summary.

### Comparative claim ledger

| Proposition | Evidence layer | Grade now | Decisive limitation / falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NIST published a fire-to-connection-failure-to-global-collapse account | Directly documented agency conclusion | A | Does not make each unpublished intermediate output independently verified |
| NIST's exact sequence is robust to connection, deletion, handoff and intermediate-fire-state assumptions | Model-dependent inference | D | Release/reproduce full model, then conduct preregistered parameter and control sweeps |
| UAF's prescribed eight-story core/exterior removal produces its reported video-like response | Reported model output | C | Obtain and rerun the precise case; compare all outputs and objective multiview event rules |
| UAF established that fire could not cause WTC 7's collapse | Causal inference from selected models | D | Matched independent fire/connection/global analysis across plausible boundary conditions and alternative fire paths |
| A diesel mechanical-room fire is a feasible transfer-structure failure path | Peer-reviewed model-dependent feasibility finding | C | Establish fuel/fire premises and extend to validated joint failure and global collapse |
| Near-simultaneous support removal demonstrates a controlled demolition | Causal interpretation | E | Requires independent evidence of a capable mechanism, placement/access, initiation, residue/forensics, or authentic operational records |

## 5. The highest-value integrated research program

The practical aim is not to find the animation that looks most convincing. It is to force competing explanations to make comparable, falsifiable predictions.

1. **Create a source register.** Preserve original drawings, fire/video inputs, model decks, custom code, software versions, output files, and every transformation from source to result. Do this separately for NIST, UAF, and any new model.
2. **Build a common geometry/connection benchmark.** Start with A2001/Column 79 and the transfer structure; publish explicit alternative geometries and material/contact assumptions rather than silently selecting one.
3. **Separate initiation from propagation.** Test local fire/connection hypotheses, transfer-structure/fire hypotheses, and prescribed support-removal comparators as distinct questions. A successful propagation model does not establish initiation.
4. **Use matched controls.** For every selected state, run adjacent time/temperature states, retained-versus-deleted members, progressive-versus-instantaneous damage transfer, with/without debris damage, and declared connection alternatives.
5. **Measure model outputs before looking at the preferred video.** Publish element-deletion times, energy/reaction histories, roofline/penthouse trajectories, and camera projections. Score predeclared events against multiple synchronized videos with blinded annotation.
6. **Do not infer a cause from kinematics alone.** Free-fall-compatible intervals or a visually vertical exterior descent constrain resistance and timing; they do not uniquely identify the physical process that removed resistance.

This program incorporates the useful challenges from UAF, the alternative fire pathway from UCL, and NIST's own identified sensitivity variables. It can make the record meaningfully more discriminating without assuming a conspiracy, a particular fire mechanism, or that any one team’s unexplained model choice decides the case.

## 6. Evidentiary assessment and case use

| Lead | What is directly supported | What remains inference | Proper next use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FEMA/NIST W14A–W14B crosswalk | Shared unusual identifiers, FEMA field labels, NIST receipt entries and NIST's unidentified classification | Same physical members, actual WTC 7 origin, later disposition, Sample 1 link | Focused provenance/segregability and record-location question |
| West-penthouse timing row | NIST published 9.3 versus 6.9/7.3 under stated relative-time/view conventions | Measurement uncertainty, model significance, any alternative cause | Specific validation and model-output/video-source request |
| Existing local video work | Access-copy timing/provenance limitations and limited roof-corner tracking | A measured west-penthouse time or reliable acceleration/cause result | Preserve as a reason not to overclaim; build a new event-specific protocol |

For the FOIA litigation, the first lead can be relevant only where it connects to identified responsive NIST records, search terms, withheld categories, or segregable material. The timing lead can support a request for a reviewable account of model-output, camera/view, validation, and simulation-record categories. Neither belongs in a complaint as proof that the collapse was caused deliberately. Both are stronger as precise examples of why generic labels such as “model materials” or “steel inventory” do not make the public record independently auditable.
