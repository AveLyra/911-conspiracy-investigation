# WTC 7 collapse evaluation — consolidated findings

**Prepared:** 2026-09-07

**Status:** Working technical research; not an expert report, factual finding, or pleading text

**Purpose:** Consolidate the collapse-related findings developed in this repository while preserving their provenance, uncertainty, and FOIA-case boundary.

## Bottom line

Two evidence scopes must remain explicit:

- **Broader published analyses, with conditional weight assigned to their disputed calculations:** fire-triggered progressive collapse remains the provisional first-ranked explanation. That judgment depends materially on accepting some evidentiary weight for the case-specific engineering analyses; observing fire does not independently establish that it caused the observed failure.
- **Only the independently checked observations and calculations in this review, excluding the disputed causal outputs:** the present review does not establish a defensible strict ordering between fire-triggered collapse and deliberate support removal. This is underdetermination, not a finding of equal probability or a 50–50 allocation. Nor does this subset support a reliable ranking of explosive against thermitic removal.

NIST supplies a developed, case-specific account, but its exact modeled pathway remains **not independently reproducible from the released package reviewed here**. Neither that gap nor a lack of a closely matched historical office-building comparator proves fire physically impossible. Conversely, a general statement that heated structures can fail does not establish this building's initiating mechanism. Historical rarity is a reason to demand building-specific evidence, not a substitute for it in either direction.

No reviewed item establishes explosives, intentional intervention, fraud, or a state/corporate operation. Lost or unavailable evidence is an evidentiary limitation, not affirmative proof of any one explanation.

**2026-09-11 LS-DYNA-input qualification:** The six supplementary keyword files make the old handoff and 3.5/4.0-hour sensitivity findings more concrete. The live deck is a gravity-stage assembly; thermal and ANSYS-damage steps are later/commented; the 4.0-hour list is deletion-only; a commented 4.1-hour file and the 3.5-hour control are absent. That exacerbates testability gaps. It does not reproduce the collapse, rank causes, or prove concealment. See [content audit](sherlock-wtc7-investigation/lsdyna-supplement-content-audit/report.md) and [discrepancy implications](sherlock-wtc7-investigation/lsdyna-supplement-content-audit/discrepancy-implications.md).

**2026-09-11 audio-method qualification:** The [preserved-audio audit](sherlock-wtc7-investigation/acoustic-audit/report.md) identifies non-average mono mixing, seek/full-decode sample differences and transcript-derived timing limits in the earlier compilation screening. Its repeatable digital measurements do not independently validate the absence of an explosive sequence. Do not treat that compilation as an independently calibrated negative control or its transcript labels as measured event times. NIST's separate blast/detectability analysis has not been reproduced by this audit; any weight assigned to that analysis remains conditional on its own assumptions. This qualification changes the scope of the audio support, not the two overall rankings above.

## Claim ledger

**2026-09-11 visual/edit qualification:** The [compilation correspondence audit](sherlock-wtc7-investigation/acoustic-audit/av-correspondence/report.md) confirms three selected adjacent-frame shot changes and separates foreground turns, camera reframing and background visibility from physical collapse onset. It also finds comparator façade changes before the old approximately13s reference. Digital audio levels around those cuts do not establish historical sound timing or cause. This further narrows the older audiovisual timing claims; it neither validates NIST's mechanism nor supplies affirmative intervention evidence, and does not change the two assessment scopes above.

## What the released and withheld record means

NIST released **25,637 files**, but described released models as excluding connection models/details. Its later response describes **68,500 withheld electronic files**, including remaining ANSYS inputs and all ANSYS results, break-element source code and scripts, a custom ANSYS executable, all LS-DYNA inputs/results, connection properties, and supporting connection-failure calculations/spreadsheets. See `exhibits/processed/exh-007-2025-06-05-nist-interim-response-233.pdf` and `exhibits/processed/exh-008-2025-08-25-nist-final-response-233.pdf` (verify page pins before external use).

These are not merely unspecified “data.” They are the record categories needed to determine whether outcomes depend materially on deletion criteria, connection assumptions, damage mapping, damping, initialization, or output selection. The 2009 public-safety finding supplies NIST's asserted withholding rationale; it does not establish the correctness of the modeled mechanism. See `exhibits/processed/exh-016-2009-07-09-nist-finding-regarding-public-safety-information.pdf`.

The released `WTC 7 Modeling Issues_050609.pptx` describes component softening/removal, script updates, manual intervention, and restarted analyses. Those are numerical implementation choices, not direct observations that physical members vanished. The appropriate question is whether their criteria were pre-specified, documented, physically justified, and outcome-robust.

## Fire-input discrepancies identified in the September 7 review

These are limitations disclosed in NIST's published report and newly highlighted in this review, not newly discovered experimental results or independent measurements of interior temperature. The complete relevant pages of [NCSTAR 1-9](../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1-9.pdf), printed pp. 382–383 / PDF pp. 448–449, were visually checked.

| Source-pinned issue | What NIST reports | What remains to test |
|---|---|---|
| Floor 12 north-face burning duration, p. 382 | Simulated burning lasted longer than the visual evidence indicated. NIST considers burning of interior gasified fuel near windows, excessive input fuel, and partition effects; it considers the first and third explanations more likely than excessive fuel. | Which explanation is supported by independent observations, and how much would correcting it change temperatures at structurally important locations? The alternatives have different spatial heating consequences. |
| Floor 11 temperature-field substitution, p. 383 | NIST used the Floor 12 fire delayed by one hour. Westward spread was faster and burning longer than the photographs indicated; NIST says the combined effects could mildly overestimate north-side heating. | Whether an observation-consistent temperature history changes connection demand, failure time, or the resulting damage state. NIST's characterization as mild has not been independently quantified here. |
| Floor 13 temperature-field substitution, p. 383 | NIST used the Floor 12 fire delayed by half an hour; east-side layout and fuel were highly uncertain. Westward spread was moderately faster and burning longer than the visual evidence; again NIST describes a possible mild north-side heating overestimate. | Whether alternative defensible histories materially alter the proposed initiating pathway near Column 79, including its timing and uncertainty. |

NIST also reports limited fuel-load sensitivity checks in §9.3.3; in particular, lowering Floor 12 fuel load had little effect on simulated progression. The appropriate criticism is therefore not that no sensitivity analysis existed. The unresolved issue is the adequacy and propagation of the tested uncertainties through the full structural chain. Exterior imagery establishes visible flame and smoke patterns, not a complete temperature field; uneven visible burning alone establishes neither mild structural heating nor a uniform interior inferno.

The highest-value expert task is to distinguish **historical reconstruction fidelity** from **reusable component-level predictive capability**. For each discrepancy, an expert should identify the affected inputs, observation uncertainties, defensible alternatives, downstream temperature/connection changes, and whether the capability asserted to justify withholding survives those changes. A mismatch in fire reconstruction does not by itself invalidate a connection-capacity calculation or prove that releasing it is harmless. The legal application is kept in the [model-fidelity withholding analysis](wtc7-model-fidelity-withholding-challenge.md).

## Highest-value technical audit if the record becomes available

1. Reproduce the published baseline from the exact input decks, scripts, solver versions, executable/environment, and documented output settings.
2. Compare physically justified alternatives to instantaneous damage transfer, element-removal criteria, post-buckling behavior, residual connection capacity, contact, and catenary action; do not simply keep every failed member indefinitely.
3. Test documented alternative connection geometries and restraint/contact definitions with source provenance for each.
4. Sweep intermediate fire/damage states between the reported 3.5- and 4.0-hour cases, with predeclared parameter ranges and failure criteria. Include observation-consistent alternatives to the Floor 11–13 heating histories identified above; retain cases in which the proposed initiation or global collapse does not occur.
5. Compare runs with and without debris damage and with alternate damage-handoff schedules.
6. Preserve and publish, subject to valid safety limitations, member-failure/deletion times, roofline displacement/velocity/acceleration, reaction-force and energy histories, run logs, and solver warnings.
7. Pre-register an observation-to-model protocol; conduct blind comparisons against multiple synchronized, calibrated video trajectories not used to choose the preferred run.
8. For each animation, preserve the source asset, hash, source page, retrieval date, source run, output frame/time mapping, camera/viewpoint, and render configuration.

Success in reproducing one baseline would establish that the implementation can be run; it would not alone validate the causal explanation. Robustness across alternatives and out-of-sample observation are separate questions.

## Evidence and retrieval priorities outside the model

- **Native media:** preserve original camera/audio files, time bases, location/metadata, and custody. Compilations and broadcast edits are leads, not independent instruments.
- **Physical evidence:** obtain specimen collection notes, photos, transfer receipts, laboratory notebooks, retained material, and provenance decisions before drawing mechanism inferences from any metallurgical sample.
- **Debris handling:** distinguish documented mishandling or poor preservation from a direction to suppress evidence; retrieve batch, disposition, and instruction records before alleging intent.
- **Warnings/reporting:** reconstruct original source, observation time, transmission path, recipient, and correction history. Treat a prediction as probative only if its content was specific, accurate, and not explainable by visible/emergency information.
- **Operational claim:** require an actor-linked chain of authorization, logistics, access, physical corroboration, and independent confirmation. Positions, access, later benefits, missing material, or engineering disagreement do not substitute for that chain.

## FOIA-case boundary

This synthesis identifies technically distinct categories for a records-access argument: models, source code, scripts, result histories, handoff files, calculation workbooks, validation materials, and visualization provenance. Whether the agency has sufficiently explained exemption fit and segregability is a legal question evaluated in the [model-fidelity withholding analysis](wtc7-model-fidelity-withholding-challenge.md), not a consequence established by modeling uncertainty alone. This synthesis does **not** support pleading that NIST's conclusion is false, that an alternative mechanism is true, or that a conspiracy occurred.

For Vaughn/declaration preparation, use the fields in `briefing/summary-judgment/record-gaps-inventory.md` §F. Keep technical evaluation in research unless a source-pinned proposition is deliberately promoted through the repository's fact/exhibit process.
