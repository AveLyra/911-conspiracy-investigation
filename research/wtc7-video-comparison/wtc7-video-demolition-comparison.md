## Executive assessment

Near-gravitational acceleration is a demanding but conditional force constraint. For an appropriately defined translating mass whose center-of-mass acceleration is measured, with downward positive,

\[
ma = mg + D - U,
\]

where \(U\) and \(D\) are upward and downward nongravitational forces across that mass's boundary. If \(a\) is approximately \(g\), \(U-D\) is approximately zero; this does not require every upward connection force to vanish. A tracked roofline point is not automatically the center of mass of a rigid translating body. Calibration, rotation, deformation and the chosen mass boundary must be justified before converting its motion into a force history. The reported free-fall interval remains a constraint every proposed mechanism must address, not a direct measurement of every hidden support.

It is **not**, by itself, a detector for explosives. Explosive implosion, nonexplosive induced collapse, and a gravity-driven progression that has already removed or disabled support can all produce a near-free-fall interval. The causal question is what produced the widespread loss of capacity before and during that interval.

On the presently reviewed record:

- NIST's fire-driven progressive-collapse account is physically plausible and is the most developed public, case-specific account. Known fires, structural damage, observed pre-façade changes, and a staged internal-to-exterior sequence give it real evidentiary support.
- The **exact NIST pathway** is not uniquely established by the videos. It depends on a multi-model chain, analyst-controlled failure/removal rules, a consequential 3.5-hour/4.0-hour damage threshold, and a global-model handoff in which accumulated damage was applied instantaneously. NIST also acknowledges greater uncertainty and visible divergence after global collapse began.
- Explosive controlled demolition is kinematically compatible with the rapid, largely downward façade motion, and the resemblance is reasonable to investigate. But verticality, dust, façade coherence, and free fall are not cause-specific. The reviewed record lacks the stronger positive indicators normally used to identify an explosive method: documented preparation, a charge sequence on useful audio, unambiguous flashes, residues, or characteristic cut evidence.
- Nonexplosive induced-collapse examples are especially important because they show that "implosion-like" motion does not logically imply explosives. Strategic weakening plus a cable pull or hydraulic action can let gravity do most of the work.
- The fairest bottom line is therefore **underdetermination, not equivalence**. NIST has more case-specific affirmative support than a demolition hypothesis in the presently public record, but the withheld modeling materials materially reduce confidence in independent verification of NIST's exact causal chain.

No defensible numerical probability is assigned. The comparison set is selected rather than statistical, the hypotheses are underspecified, and several decisive evidence types are unavailable.

## Method and source hierarchy

This assessment uses four levels of material:

1. **Primary NIST reports and agency media.** [NIST NCSTAR 1-9, §5.7.1, pp. 261-263](../../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1-9.pdf) reports that its visual database contained 16 collapse videos and that nine, primarily from north of the complex, were selected for detailed analysis. NIST placed those nine clips on a common timeline using shared events, with a stated maximum relative-time uncertainty of 0.2 seconds.
2. **Known-method demolition cases.** The [Capital One/Hertz Tower](https://www.cityoflakecharles.com/egov/documents/1724343064_93841.pdf), [Landmark Tower](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1388884/), and [Cather-Pound](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/civilengdiss/140/) cases supply confirmed explosive comparators. The [HBI case](https://www.appliedscienceint.com/project/hbi-facility/) supplies an engineering-firm description of strategic weakening and cable-pull induced collapse without relying exclusively on explosives.
3. **Fire-associated collapse comparisons.** The [Plasco investigator account](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10694-019-00903-y) attributes that collapse to fire and describes an inferred structural scenario based on limited records, witness accounts, video and engineering judgment. This is relevant evidence, not a newly reproduced causal control.
4. **Web compilations.** The “27 Angles” compilation was used to orient the eye and to locate audio leads. Separate video/audio streams are now preserved and screened in [`27-angles-audio-audit.md`](27-angles-audio-audit.md). Its edits, clocks, crops, compression, and mixed provenance make it unsuitable for new acceleration measurements, physical loudness estimates, or treating its tracks as 27 independent microphones.

The comparison is qualitative. It is not photogrammetry, acoustic forensics, or a statistical classifier.

## What the multi-angle WTC 7 record shows

### 1. The best views are not complete views

The nine NIST-selected cameras were primarily north of the WTC complex. Many show the upper north face well, but foreground buildings, smoke, and then dust obstruct much of the lower structure. That creates an important asymmetry in the evidence: the visible roofline can be measured precisely while the support-loss zone cannot be observed directly in most views.

Claims such as "every column failed simultaneously" or "charges are visible at the base" therefore do not follow from the videos alone. The cameras largely show the response of the upper exterior after internal and lower-story events have begun.

### 2. The visible event is staged, not a single instantaneous onset

Using the east-penthouse descent as \(t=0\), [NIST's synchronized observation timeline, §5.7.6, pp. 289-290](../../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1-9.pdf) reports:

| Relative time | Reported observation |
|---:|---|
| about -3 s | Dust and/or smoke pushed across West Broadway from the east side |
| 0 to 2 s | Roof-structure descent and window changes on the east side |
| 3.8 s | A dust cloud already present near the base in the earliest useful view |
| 6.9 s | North-face global descent begins; eastern roofline/screenwall move together; east end rotates north and a kink develops |
| 8.0 to 9.3 s | More windows open; dust exits upper windows; west roof structures disappear |
| 12.0 s | Upper building begins to break up |
| 13.5 s | Upper building disappears into dust |

Even if roof-penthouse behavior is set aside, the record still contains pre-global window changes, dust, façade rotation, and a kink. The externally coherent descent begins only after several seconds of other visible activity.

### 3. Free fall is real in NIST's own final analysis

[NIST NCSTAR 1A, §3.6, pp. 44-46](../../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1a.pdf) tracked a point near the center of the north roofline through 73.8 m, about 18 stories, in approximately 5.4 seconds. It divided the motion into three stages:

- 0 to 1.75 s: acceleration below \(g\), with about 2.2 m of descent;
- 1.75 to 4.0 s: acceleration approximately \(g\), covering about 32.0 m or eight stories; and
- 4.0 to 5.4 s: acceleration below \(g\), as resistance increased.

The proper inference is neither "nothing unusual happened" nor "explosives are proved." During the reported Stage 2 interval, the tracked point's motion was compatible with gravitational acceleration within that analysis. Any adequate model must address that observation with an explicit mass/force boundary and treatment of projection, rotation, deformation, timing, and measurement uncertainty; it must not silently equate the point with the whole building's center of mass or infer that every connection force was zero.

### 4. "Into its own footprint" is too strong

[NIST's Camera 3/4 analysis, pp. 276-280](../../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1-9.pdf) estimated that, after the roof had descended roughly seven stories, the northeast roof corner was displaced **11 m ± 3 m north** of its original footprint. NIST also described the east edge rotating north, the upper building later tilting sharply west, and the structure breaking apart.

"Predominantly downward" is fair. "Perfectly symmetrical" or "neatly contained within its footprint" is not supported by the same video analysis. The distinction matters because overstatement makes a valid question about widespread support loss easier to dismiss.

### 5. The audio evidence weighs against a conventional explosive sequence, but is not absolute

[NIST analyzed three soundtracks, pp. 289-290](../../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1-9.pdf), from street-level cameras at least about 640 m away, with intervening buildings. It reported no loud explosive sound associated with collapse initiation and accounted for sound-travel time. That is real negative evidence against a conventional column-cutting charge sequence of the magnitude NIST calculated.

It is not equivalent to a close, calibrated blast-array recording. Distance, urban reflections, microphone response, automatic gain control, edits, and occlusion all matter. A serious independent review would need the native audio streams and metadata.

The preserved “27 Angles” access copy sharpens that distinction. At 01:06, “a loud, incredibly loud explosion” is a reporter's spoken retrospective description, not the waveform of the described event. The montage also contains real transients, rumbles, and a later reporter's possible-explosion reference, but it switches among voiceovers, silence, broadcasts, and edited camera tracks. Its uploader-linked source index expressly identifies some circulating versions with different or added audio. By contrast, the preserved confirmed Capital One/Hertz Tower implosion control contains a dense series of large broadband impulses contemporaneous with visible initiation effects. This single control is not a universal classifier, but the difference is material.

The balanced characterization is: **explosion testimony and acoustic leads exist and should be preserved; no consistent, authenticated charge sequence is established across the reviewed WTC 7 recordings; and NIST's negative finding deserves weight proportional to the recordings' limitations.** See [`27-angles-audio-audit.md`](27-angles-audio-audit.md).

### 6. Pre-collapse warnings and premature reports are real, but do not identify the mechanism

There are two different evidentiary categories. First, firefighters, police, camera operators, and reporters expected that WTC 7 might collapse. NIST records concern as early as approximately 11:30 a.m. and a decision to stop firefighting operations and establish a collapse zone at about 2:30 p.m. A post-collapse CNN account likewise said emergency personnel had pulled back and were waiting for the building to fall. Given those facts, audible warnings that the building was “coming down” are relevant evidence of perceived instability, but they are not surprising under a fire-and-damage hypothesis.

Second, broadcasters prematurely reported an event that had not yet occurred. In the preserved CNN segment, at file offset approximately 25:22, the anchor says Building 7 “has either collapsed or is collapsing”; the report is then qualified to say it may collapse. In the preserved BBC segment, at approximately 03:19 and again beginning near 13:28, the broadcast says the Salomon Brothers Building (WTC 7) had collapsed even though the actual exterior collapse was later. The archived segment labels place these statements before NIST's reported collapse time of 5:20:52 p.m.

The premature past-tense reports are a genuine information-chain anomaly worth preserving and tracing. Standing alone, however, they establish neither the source's knowledge nor the physical cause. A fast-moving newsroom can turn a warning, police-scanner report, wire bulletin, or mistaken identification into a false statement of completed fact. A privileged-foreknowledge hypothesis would gain substantial weight if contemporaneous records showed a traceable source conveying a precise, accurate time or method before observable initiation. The current broadcasts instead show hedging, correction, inconsistent wording, and materially inaccurate timing.

A critical submission also identifies a street-level CNN clip in which people turn after an audible event and warn that the building will come down or “blow up.” That sequence must be tested from the uncut, highest-generation recording. A warning made after hearing a structural or impact sound could be contemporaneous recognition of instability or initiation; it is not automatically advance knowledge. Speaker identification, camera location, clock synchronization, sound-propagation correction, and the full lead-in are decisive.

The detailed evidence table and preservation requests are in [`collapse-warning-and-premature-reporting.md`](collapse-warning-and-premature-reporting.md).

## Comparison with known collapse methods

The word "controlled demolition" can include both explosive and nonexplosive engineering. To avoid equivocation, the table separates them.

| Feature | WTC 7 | Explosive implosion | Nonexplosive induced collapse | Ordinary mechanical demolition | Accidental fire-progressive collapse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preparation | No public, case-specific demolition preparation established; long fires and debris damage are established | Extensive access, weakening, charge placement, protection, delay design | Strategic cuts/weakening plus jack, cable, or pull system | Visible top-down or sectional work | No demolition preparation |
| Initiation visible on video | Lower zone mostly obscured; internal/roof/window/dust changes precede façade motion | Often localized flashes/ejecta and a designed support-removal sequence | Motion begins at an engineered weak/hinge level; no blast required | Slow localized removal | Sagging, buckling, local failure, or partial collapse may precede terminal phase |
| Global appearance | Rapid, largely downward north-façade descent; rotation and later breakup | Rapid downward/folding/tilting motion; geometry chosen by designers | Upper block can descend or rotate rapidly and generate an implosion-like dust cloud | Poor visual match to a sudden whole-building fall | Can become rapid after a long damage-accretion period; morphology varies |
| Free-fall interval | Measured by NIST for about 2.25 s / 32 m at one roofline point | Possible after support is removed on the measured path | Possible before the falling block engages lower structure | Not characteristic | Possible if support over the measured path has already failed |
| Sound | No loud onset blast established in NIST's distant recordings | Strong impulsive reports ordinarily expected from conventional cutting charges | Fracture/impact sounds, no charge report required | Machinery | Fracture/impact sounds |
| Dust | Extensive | Extensive | Extensive | Localized/periodic | Extensive in a complete collapse |
| Cause-specific evidence | Fires, damage, structural drawings, model-derived sequence; no recovered WTC 7 steel identified by NIST | Charges, wiring, residues, cut patterns, preparation records | Cuts, jacks/cables, pull records | Equipment and work record | Heat/fire pattern, fracture/deformation, recovered materials |

### Explosive high-rise examples

The confirmed Capital One/Hertz Tower and Landmark Tower videos display the visually intuitive similarities that motivate comparison: abrupt onset, rapid loss of height, a large dust cloud, and substantial downward motion. The Capital One view also shows pronounced deformation/leaning during the fall rather than a rigid, perfectly vertical block.

Cather-Pound is more informative about mechanism than a spectacular broadcast clip. The University of Nebraska study documents charges at selected columns on alternating floors that **initiated a progressive collapse**. That is a useful correction to two common simplifications: engineered implosions do not require literal simultaneous removal of every column, and after initiation gravity and member impacts perform much of the destruction.

Accordingly, resemblance to an implosion is not imaginary, but its strongest shared features are consequences of rapid support loss rather than signatures of the tool that caused the loss.

### Nonexplosive induced collapse

Applied Science International describes industrial structures brought down by strategic weakening cuts and a cable pulled by an excavator, as well as a separate explosive demolition. Hydraulic "vérinage" methods likewise create a weakened story and allow the upper mass to descend and drive collapse.

These cases matter logically. They demonstrate that a fast, dusty, predominantly downward collapse can be intentionally engineered **without** explosive signatures. They also often show a hinge, tilt, or impact deceleration, features that should be measured rather than judged by eye.

### Mechanical dismantling

Ordinary excavator, crane, saw-cut, or top-down demolition is a poor morphological comparator. It is slow and sectional. Its value is mainly negative: not all demolition resembles an implosion, and "demolition" is too broad a category for causal inference.

### Accidental fire-progressive collapse

The [Plasco investigator account](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10694-019-00903-y) describes a 16-story building collapsing about 3.5 hours after fire began, with an inferred causal sequence. Published numerical explanations are assumption-dependent: [Behnam's preliminary analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40999-018-0332-x), for example, reports failure at its estimated 1900 MJ/m² fire load but not at 511 MJ/m², and reports protected columns retaining capacity. These are reported model outcomes, not independently repeated measurements. Plasco is relevant contrary evidence to an absolute assertion of physical impossibility, but does not by itself establish a fire-only mechanism at WTC 7 or a matched precedent; framing, use, protection, loading and exposure require comparison.

## The symmetry and coupling question

The user's intuition is sound in one respect: real connections do not all have identical capacity, temperature, restraint, damage, or load. A credible model should show an asynchronous failure history, not invoke "coupling" as a magic word.

The fires also should not be described as spatially uniform or as heating the entire building. NIST reported separate, traveling contents fires, with the uncontrolled fires most consequential on Floors 7-9 and 11-13. Its mechanism does not require steel to melt: differential thermal expansion of long floor members can impose large connection forces well below steel's melting temperature. For scale, a freely expanding 15 m steel member with \(\alpha \approx 12\times10^{-6}/K\) and a 400 K temperature rise would lengthen about 72 mm. Restraint converts some of that free movement into force, buckling, or connection deformation.

That calculation establishes **mechanical plausibility**, not the actual temperature, restraint, expansion, or connection failure in WTC 7. Those are model- and evidence-dependent quantities. NIST's own [FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation) describes the relevant fires as ordinary building-content fires and thermal expansion as the dominant effect; the exact thermal maps and connection response remain proper subjects for audit.

But asynchronous component failures can still produce a short interval of coherent exterior motion. Three physical effects can compress the apparent timing:

1. **Threshold behavior.** A frame can redistribute load while reserve capacity remains, then lose stability quickly after several columns or braces cross their buckling limits.
2. **Hidden interior progression.** The exterior can remain temporarily coherent while floors and core framing fail out of view. Its visible motion begins after the load path has already changed substantially.
3. **Frame continuity.** Spandrels and connected façade bays can constrain relative motion, so the wall responds as a larger assembly even though individual connections did not fail at the same instant.

NIST's best-fit global analysis did not posit instantaneous east-to-west failure of the entire building. Its [published comparison, Table 3-1, pp. 42-43](../../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1a.pdf), places cascading floor failures around Column 79 about six seconds before roof-structure descent, Column 79 buckling about 1.3 seconds before it, core-column buckling across the building over roughly 3.5 to 6.1 seconds afterward, and north-façade descent at 6.3 seconds in the model versus 6.9 seconds observed.

That sequence answers the bare timing objection: the model supplies several seconds for propagation. It does **not** settle whether the modeled propagation is faithful to the real connection behavior. The decisive questions are whether the connection constitutive laws, failure thresholds, load redistribution, damping, element removal, and damage handoff make that rapid transition robust across reasonable uncertainty.

## Where NIST's explanation is strongest and most vulnerable

### Strongest points

- It is anchored to known long-duration fires, structural drawings, reported debris damage, and a staged visible sequence rather than to façade resemblance alone.
- It models temperature-dependent materials, connection failures, floor-system damage, column buckling, and gravity-driven progression.
- Several pre-global timing features were compared with video, and the best-fit analysis placed the start of exterior descent within roughly 0.6 seconds of the observation.
- A condensed structural-analysis paper was published in the ASCE *Journal of Structural Engineering*.

### Vulnerabilities and limits

- The analysis is a linked chain: fire simulation → thermal mapping → 16-story ANSYS response → 47-story LS-DYNA global collapse. Uncertainty and modeling choices can accumulate at each handoff.
- In the LS-DYNA initialization [described by NIST, pp. 38-39](../../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1a.pdf), debris damage was imposed by instantaneously removing elements; temperatures were then ramped; ANSYS fire-induced damage to beams, girders, and connections was applied instantaneously. This is a numerical transfer procedure, not a literal claim that real fire damage occurred at once, but its dynamic consequences require sensitivity testing.
- NIST's 3.5-hour transferred damage did not initiate global collapse, while the 4.0-hour case did. NIST interpreted the critical state as lying between those times. That is a legitimate bracketing result, but it makes the exact transition sensitive enough that underlying files and alternate parameter runs matter. See [NCSTAR 1A, pp. 41-42](../../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1a.pdf) and [NCSTAR 1-9A, §4.4](../../authority/nist/wtc7/ncstar-1-9a.pdf).
- NIST states that some photographic information helped direct simulations. Its corroboration set was therefore not wholly out-of-sample validation.
- NIST acknowledges greatly increased uncertainty after global collapse began. Its later simulated exterior-wall deformation was not seen in the video, which NIST attributes partly to random breakup and omitted nonstructural stiffness. NIST addresses that discrepancy in its [WTC 7 FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation).
- [Journal peer review of a condensed methodology](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%29ST.1943-541X.0000398) establishes that the work was examinable and professionally presented. It does not establish that independent reviewers reran the unreleased model, reproduced the failure chain, or tested all reasonable alternatives.

These points support skepticism about **verification strength**, not a finding that the model is fraudulent or physically impossible.

### Physical-evidence limitation

NIST states that WTC 7 steel was removed before its investigation began and could not later be clearly identified. That is a substantial forensic limitation because recovered members could, in principle, help discriminate among heat deformation, overload fracture, mechanical cutting, and explosive damage. It is fair to say that this evidence was lost for WTC 7-specific forensic examination. It is **not** fair, on the present record, to infer that it was intentionally destroyed to conceal a cause; NIST says debris was removed rapidly for recovery and emergency operations. See [NIST FAQ questions 21-22](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation).

## Independent challenges and what they do—and do not—establish

The [University of Alaska Fairbanks project](https://files.wtc7report.org/) concluded that fire did not cause the collapse and that a near-simultaneous failure of every column was needed to reproduce the observed motion. Its public data are a substantial transparency advantage and its model deserves technical engagement. Two cautions are essential:

1. the project was funded by Architects & Engineers for 9/11 Truth, which should be disclosed but does not itself decide validity; and
2. prescribing a near-simultaneous column-removal pattern demonstrates that the pattern is kinematically sufficient. It does not identify what caused the pattern or prove it occurred.

The [2022 UCL/*Fire Technology* study](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10146056/) is important in the opposite direction. It explored another fire-driven pathway and described differing conclusions among prior expert teams. That supports the proposition that even within fire causation, NIST's exact path is not necessarily unique. Case-use framing (records lead, not merits claim; NIST did consider diesel fires): [Orabi records-lead memo](../orabi-2022-alternative-fire-records-lead.md).

Taken together, these studies make "NIST is one plausible model among competing mechanisms" a fair research conclusion. They do not make every mechanism equally supported.

## How to verify the video claims independently

A defensible new analysis should be designed before measuring outcomes:

1. **Acquire and preserve sources.** Obtain the nine NIST-analyzed camera files and any additional native views, with metadata, frame rate, interlacing status, edit history, hashes, and source chain. Preserve relevant secondary copies immediately when they are the best presently obtainable material, but do not confuse them with native originals. Do the same for known-method comparators.
2. **Synchronize independently.** Use multiple common visual and audio events, propagation-time corrections, and uncertainty bands. Preserve both raw and deinterlaced versions.
3. **Calibrate cameras.** Solve camera geometry from fixed building edges and surveyed dimensions. Quantify lens distortion and camera motion.
4. **Track multiple points.** Track center roofline, both visible roof corners, façade kinks, fixed foreground references, window lines, and dust fronts. Do not infer the whole structure from one pixel track.
5. **Report displacement before derivatives.** Publish raw point coordinates, scaling, smoothing choices, residuals, and confidence intervals. Acceleration is extremely sensitive to frame noise and smoothing.
6. **Normalize comparators.** Compare dimensionless time \(t/\sqrt{H/g}\), rotation, lateral/vertical displacement, visible initiation level, and dust onset. Do not compare raw seconds across different heights.
7. **Analyze native audio.** Correct for distance and clock offset; inspect impulsive events, spectrum, clipping, microphone response, and automatic gain control. Use known demolition recordings as positive controls.
8. **Blind the morphology test.** If experts classify short clips, remove labels and match framing/resolution. Then report inter-rater reliability and false-positive rates.
9. **Integrate non-video evidence.** Video cannot substitute for physical fracture evidence, residues, cut patterns, access/preparation records, structural drawings, fire observations, or validated model runs.
10. **Pre-register decisive tests.** State what findings would raise or lower confidence in each hypothesis before examining additional frames.

Until that work is done, compilation-video comparison should remain hypothesis generation, not expert proof.

## Complaint-use boundary

The current amended-complaint architecture correctly excludes a scientific merits dispute. Importing claims about free fall, demolition resemblance, or NIST's engineering conclusion would let the government characterize a FOIA case as an attempt to relitigate WTC 7.

If counsel later decides that a short relevance paragraph is needed, the safest form is procedural and agnostic:

> NIST characterizes its conclusion as a probable collapse sequence derived substantially from linked computational models. Its published materials identify sensitivity to modeled damage states and acknowledge that uncertainty increased after global collapse began. Plaintiff does not ask this Court to determine the collapse cause. These features explain the public interest in access to nonexempt, reasonably segregable model inputs, outputs, scripts, and supporting calculations needed to evaluate the agency's published work independently.

Even that paragraph should be used only if it advances a live FOIA element and does not conflict with the pleading's disciplined focus. The stronger present use is in background research, expert consultation, a declaration explaining reproducibility, or public-interest context—not as a new merits count.

## Bottom line

The videos make a serious question visible: visible pre-facade changes preceded a rapid exterior descent, including a reported roofline interval compatible with gravitational acceleration. This does not directly measure every hidden support or separate connection force. Documented explosive and nonexplosive demolition operations offer comparisons, but the particular recorded operation must be authenticated and its preparation and structural differences retained. Visual resemblance alone does not identify the initiating mechanism.

On merits alone, NIST's broad proposition—fire and accumulated damage produced a progressive internal failure followed by global exterior collapse—is physically credible. Confidence should be lower in the uniqueness and precise details of its published sequence, particularly where the conclusion depends on sensitive model transfers and unreleased files. The rational next step is not to convert resemblance into an allegation; it is to demand the records and perform a reproducible, uncertainty-aware audit.
